Agility Robotics Closes SPAC Deal, Set to Trade on Nasdaq as AGLT on November 5

Agility Robotics completed its business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI on November 4, 2026, and the combined company is expected to begin Nasdaq trading November 5 under AGLT and AGLTW, with an expected gross capital raise of about $465 million and Digit humanoids already deployed with GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Agility Robotics announced on November 4, 2026 that it had completed its business combination with Churchill Capital Corp XI, a Nasdaq-listed special purpose acquisition company. The combined company, Agility Robotics, Inc., is expected to begin trading on Nasdaq on November 5, 2026 under the ticker symbols AGLT for common stock and AGLTW for warrants, while Churchill XI's previous symbols cease trading and its outstanding units are separated into their underlying securities.

The closing followed Churchill XI shareholders' approval of the combination on November 2, 2026, with more than 84% of votes cast in favor. An October 30, 2026 announcement put the expected gross capital raise at approximately $465 million before transaction expenses, comprising approximately $164 million from Churchill XI's trust account, approximately $201 million from a common-stock private investment committed at $10 per share and led by Foxconn, and $100 million of equity funding already received after the combination was announced. The June 2026 transaction announcement set Agility's pre-money equity value at $2.5 billion. All existing Agility shareholders will roll 100% of their equity into the combined company, and shares held by those shareholders and by the Churchill sponsor are subject to a 180-day lock-up, with early release possible on specified stock-price performance measures.

Agility enters the public market with operating deployments rather than prototypes alone. Its flagship humanoid Digit is commercially deployed in manufacturing, distribution and logistics with GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada, with more than 65,000 hours of real-world operation. GXO recently expanded its agreement to deploy Digit at two additional U.S. facilities beginning in early 2027, and AGCO has signed a commercial agreement to pilot Digit at its Hesston, Kansas facility beginning in 2027. The next-generation Digit 5, designed for cooperative safety so robots can work close to people, carries more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders, with initial deliveries to early-access customers targeted for the first half of 2027 and general availability targeted for the end of 2027. Supporting infrastructure includes RoboFab, a U.S. facility designed to produce up to 10,000 robots annually, and Agility Arc, a cloud platform for deploying and managing robot fleets.

WHY IT MATTERSA public listing tests whether humanoids are a business yet

Agility's claim to be the only U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with proven, active commercial deployments is the point of the listing. Until now, humanoid exposure in public markets came through diversified giants or suppliers. A pure-play quote forces a different question: can 65,000 hours of real-world operation and a $300 million Digit 5 order book convert into recurring fleet revenue, or does the hardware business stay lumpy and milestone-gated?

The capital structure tells the same story as the deployment numbers. A $465 million gross raise, down from more than $620 million anticipated when the merger was announced in June, reflects redemptions and a cooler SPAC market rather than a failed deal. The 180-day lock-up on all existing holders aligns insiders with the first two quarters of public trading, which will cover the Digit 5 early-access delivery window.

WHY IT MATTERSWhat to discount and what to test

Discount the $300 million order figure until deliveries convert it: it is committed multi-year value subject to milestones and specifications, not revenue. Discount the $465 million figure the same way: it is a gross, pre-expense estimate from October 30, not a final net-proceeds number from the completion release.

Test three things: whether AGLT begins trading on November 5 as expected, whether Digit 5 early-access deliveries begin in the first half of 2027, and whether the GXO expansion and AGCO pilot start on schedule in 2027. RoboFab's designed capacity of 10,000 robots a year is a ceiling to measure against, not a production result.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe humanoid question moves from demo to quarterly earnings

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Agility's listing matters because it puts a pure-play humanoid maker on a public tape with real deployments to defend. The bull case is 65,000 operating hours and a $300 million Digit 5 order book; the bear case is that both numbers are still promises about 2027 deliveries. Watch the first two quarters of trading, which the 180-day lock-up conveniently frames, for conversion of orders into shipped robots.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this report rest on Agility Robotics' official November 4, 2026 press release announcing completion of the business combination, independently covered the same day by Unite.AI and securities.io. The $465 million capital figure is the October 30, 2026 expected gross raise before transaction expenses; the completion release did not replace it with a final net-proceeds figure. The "only U.S. publicly listed pure-play humanoid" characterization is the company's own claim. Trading under AGLT and AGLTW is expected November 5, 2026 and remains subject to market mechanics.
