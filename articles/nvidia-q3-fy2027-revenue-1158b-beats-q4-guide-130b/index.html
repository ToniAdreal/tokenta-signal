Nvidia Beats Big in Its First Jensen-Free Quarter: $115.8B Revenue, $130B Guide

Nvidia reported Q3 FY2027 revenue of $115.84B, up 103% YoY, beating the $108.71B consensus by about $7.1B, with non-GAAP EPS of $2.68 vs $2.47 expected and Data Center revenue of $107.6B, up 110%. Q4 guidance is revenue of $130.0B plus or minus 3%, above the roughly $125.3B consensus, with margins guided to 71-72%. Vera Rubin NVL72 has shipped, and the board created a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee on results day. First print since Jensen Huang resigned on November 12; interim CEO Colette Kress leads the call.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Nvidia reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 results after the close on November 17, the first print since founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on November 12, and the numbers beat on every line that matters. Revenue reached $115.84 billion, up 103 percent from a year ago and 20 percent from the previous quarter, about $7.1 billion above the $108.71 billion consensus and above the company's own $108.0 billion guidance. Non-GAAP earnings per diluted share were $2.68 against $2.47 expected, with GAAP earnings of $2.70. Data Center revenue hit $107.6 billion, up 110 percent year over year, against roughly $101.9 billion expected. Gross margins held at 74.8 percent on a GAAP basis and 74.9 percent non-GAAP. GAAP net income was $65.3 billion, up 105 percent. Shares rose about 2 percent in after-hours trading.

The outlook, not the quarter, is what the market was waiting for, and it came in strong. Nvidia guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, above the roughly $125.3 billion consensus, in a 14-week quarter that includes an additional week. GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins are expected between 71.0 and 72.0 percent, down from this quarter's 74.9 percent non-GAAP as memory costs bite, with GAAP operating expenses of about $11.0 billion. The outlook assumes no Data Center compute revenue from China. Interim chief executive Colette Kress, who remains chief financial officer, said: "Our momentum is real. Nvidia is an exceptional technology leader, powered by the relentless innovation of our people. Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater."

The balance sheet tells the other half of the story. Operating cash flow was $48.3 billion and free cash flow $43.8 billion in the quarter. Nvidia returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through repurchases and dividends, and about $227.3 billion remained under its repurchase authorization at quarter end; the $150 billion increase to that authorization was approved in September 2026, not today. A quarterly dividend of $0.25 per share will be paid on December 23, 2026. The commitments keep climbing with the demand: supply and capacity commitments rose to $390 billion from $279 billion a quarter earlier, total future commitments reached $518 billion, and maximum gross exposure on guarantees was $118.0 billion, including $105.0 billion of credit support for SB Energy. Accounts receivable stood at $81.0 billion at 64 days sales outstanding, up from 60 days, and inventory rose to $39.7 billion from $31.6 billion.

Governance moved on results day too. Earlier on November 17, Nvidia's board established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, a new standing committee chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, with Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri as members, to oversee national security policy, export control compliance and global trade risk. Kress leads the earnings call at 5 p.m. ET, the first since the leadership change, with the board still searching for a permanent chief executive among internal and external candidates. One caveat frames the whole print: the quarter ended October 25, before Huang resigned, so the results describe the old leadership's quarter, while the outlook is the first written under interim leadership. Shares had fallen about 20 percent since the August 26 report before tonight's release.

WHY IT MATTERSThe beat is real, and so is the bill

Demand is not the question anymore. Data Center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110 percent year over year, breaks down into Hyperscale at $60.1 billion, up 71 percent, and AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise at $47.5 billion, up 195 percent. Vera Rubin NVL72, which the company calls the biggest advanced computing platform ever built, has shipped, and the quarter's beat came with gross margins still near 75 percent. The guidance for $130.0 billion next quarter says the order book is intact through the leadership break.

The bill for serving that demand is growing just as fast. Supply commitments jumped $111 billion in a single quarter to $390 billion, total future commitments reached $518 billion, and the guarantee book now carries $105 billion of credit support for a single tenant's energy campus. Margins are guided down to 71 to 72 percent next quarter on memory costs. None of that overturns the beat, but it changes what the beat proves: Nvidia can sell everything it can build, and the cost of building it, financing it and standing behind its customers keeps rising with it.

WHAT TO WATCHThe call is the event now

Kress's first call as interim chief executive is where the quarter's meaning gets decided. Watch whether she holds the preliminary fiscal 2028 line from August, roughly 70 percent revenue growth, and how she frames the $518 billion commitment book and the 64 days of receivables against three direct customers worth 14, 11 and 11 percent of revenue. The permanent chief executive search, the new export-control committee's posture, and any word on China will all be read as signals about whether the board intends steady continuity or a break with the Huang era.

The week stays crowded after tonight: Anthropic prices its IPO on November 18, and the market will read Nvidia's guide as the demand backdrop for that debut. The next test is not a number but a sentence, whatever Kress says about fiscal 2028 on the call, and whether the board's search for a permanent chief executive produces a name the market trusts.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA decisive quarter, an open question

Tokenta Signal's judgment: this is the cleanest possible answer to the question the market has asked since November 12, whether the demand story survives the founder. Revenue beat by $7 billion, Data Center grew 110 percent, the guide clears consensus, and Rubin is shipping. Nothing in the print suggests the machine slowed when the leadership changed. But the quarter ended before the resignation, so the print is evidence about demand, not about the new leadership, and the guide is only the first sentence of that story. The numbers to keep watching are the ones that did not make the headline: $518 billion of future commitments, $105 billion of credit support for one tenant, receivables stretching to 64 days, and margins sliding toward 71 percent. Kress has now delivered the quarter; the call, the fiscal 2028 line, and the permanent chief executive search will decide whether the market believes the next one.
