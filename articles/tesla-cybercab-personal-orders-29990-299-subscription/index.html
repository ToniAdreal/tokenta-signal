Tesla Opens Cybercab Orders at $29,990, but the Car Won't Move Without a $299 Monthly Subscription

Tesla has invited select customers to buy the Cybercab for personal use at $29,990 before taxes, with a $1,000 non-refundable deposit and estimated December 2026 delivery in Austin, Texas. The car will not operate without a $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription, pre-checked on the order page, and only works inside Tesla's Robotaxi service areas in eight Texas and Florida cities. Dual-verified: Tesla's official Robotaxi account on X (Nov 13, 23:27 UTC) + Electrek read in full. NHTSA has not ruled on the no-brake-pedal safety review; Tesla answered Oct 30 and asked for confidentiality.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Tesla has started selling the Cybercab to private buyers. Its official Robotaxi account posted on X on Friday, November 13, 2026 at 23:27 UTC: 'Select customers are now being invited to purchase Cybercab for personal use.' The order page in the post lists the two-seat car with no steering wheel or pedals at $29,990 before taxes, with an estimated delivery of December 2026 in Austin, Texas, and a $1,000 non-refundable deposit due on ordering.

The price is not the whole bill. The order page pre-checks a $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription, with one month included, and the fine print reads: 'Subscription and regular vehicle maintenance required to operate.' The invitation email is blunter: 'Only available in supported service areas. Subscription required for operation.' Without an active subscription the car will not operate, and there is no manual mode. Electrek calculates the subscription at $3,588 a year, about $47,600 over five years on top of the purchase price, before insurance, maintenance, taxes and charging.

The car only works where Tesla's Robotaxi service already runs: eight cities, Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth and San Antonio in Texas, and Miami, Orlando and Tampa in Florida. Outside those geofences it cannot drive itself. The Verge reports the order terms prohibit commercial use, so owners cannot put the car to work in ride-hailing or add it to Tesla's own Robotaxi network. Tesla has not said how invited customers are chosen, or when orders open to the general public.

WHY IT MATTERSThe headline price keeps Musk's promise, the subscription rewrites it

Musk promised a Cybercab 'below $30,000' when he unveiled it in 2024, and $29,990 technically hits the number. The subscription is the part he did not mention on stage. At $299 a month, a buyer pays $3,588 a year just to keep the car moving, and Electrek's five-year arithmetic puts the real cost near $47,600 before insurance, maintenance, taxes and charging. Tesla is selling a car that cannot drive without a recurring payment it controls and can reprice, with The Verge reporting Tesla can change the subscription price with 30 days' notice.

The invitation-only, Austin-first rollout reads as a proof point rather than a launch. Tesla is delivering the first cars in December 2026, the same year it has been promising unsupervised vehicles, and only to selected customers in the one city where Cybercabs already run in its Robotaxi service. The constraint is deliberate: a small, controlled fleet lets Tesla claim deliveries of an unsupervised vehicle in 2026 while keeping the cars inside geofences it already operates.

WHAT TO WATCHThe regulator has not cleared the car Tesla is now selling

The open question is the safety review. Electrek reports that the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration ordered Tesla to prove under oath that a car with no brake pedal meets federal safety standards that require one, that Tesla answered on October 30 and asked that the entire response be kept confidential, and that NHTSA has not ruled. Tesla is taking $1,000 non-refundable deposits while that review is open, so the first deliveries in December depend on a regulator that has not yet spoken.

The second watchpoint is who gets invited and what happens after Austin. Tesla has published no criteria for the invitations, no date for general orders, and no plan for other countries. The Cybercab runs on the same AI4 computer as the current Model Y, which Tesla already operates unsupervised in the same geofences, so the question Electrek raises is why Model Y owners who paid for Full Self-Driving cannot get unsupervised operation in those zones while buyers of a new two-seater can. How Tesla answers that, and whether the December deliveries actually happen, sets the terms for the whole product.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISBelow $30,000, on paper only

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Tesla kept the letter of Musk's promise and broke its spirit in the same order page. $29,990 is below $30,000, and the car that costs it cannot move without a $299 monthly payment that Tesla controls, inside geofences Tesla defines, with no commercial use allowed. The December deliveries in Austin are real progress for a vehicle with no steering wheel, and taking deposits while the federal safety review is still open is a gamble on a regulator that has not ruled. The Cybercab is for sale. Whether it is a car you own, or a subscription you rent with a $30,000 deposit, is the question the first buyers will answer.
