Snap Swings to a $56M Q3 Profit as Revenue Misses by $8M

Snap reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $1.722 billion, up 14% year over year but just below the roughly $1.73 billion expected, while net income swung to $56 million and adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $383 million, above guidance; daily active users reached 495 million and reported fourth-quarter guidance was $1.95 billion to $1.99 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Snap reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $1.722 billion on November 4, 2026, up 14% from $1.507 billion a year earlier but just below the roughly $1.73 billion analysts expected, a miss of about $8 million. Net income swung to $56 million from a net loss of $104 million a year earlier, and diluted earnings of $0.03 per share beat the roughly $0.00 consensus, improving from a loss of $0.06 per share a year earlier and $0.10 per share in the second quarter.

Profitability, not the top line, carried the quarter. Adjusted EBITDA more than doubled to $383 million from $182 million a year earlier, finishing $33 million above the high end of the company's $300 million to $350 million guidance range. Operating cash flow rose to $210 million from $146 million, and free cash flow rose 76% to $164 million from $93 million.

Users grew slowly but monetized better. Daily active users reached 495 million, up 3.8% from 477 million a year earlier and up from 493 million in the second quarter, while average revenue per user rose 10.1% to $3.48 from $3.16. Chief executive Evan Spiegel pointed to AI as a significant advantage for Snap's teams and advertising platform, and to the launch of Specs as its long-term augmented-reality bet. MarketCatalyst reported shares fell about 10% in after-hours trading after closing up 5.8% on the day.

WHY IT MATTERSA profit built on discipline, not acceleration

Snap's swing from a $104 million loss to a $56 million profit, with adjusted EBITDA up 110% year over year, is a cost story first. Revenue grew 14% and operating income turned positive at $77 million against a $128 million loss a year earlier, which means the quarter's leverage came from holding the cost line while the ad business grew, not from a demand surge.

That framing matters for the next test. Revenue landed inside the company's own $1.70 billion to $1.74 billion guidance range, so the miss against consensus is a forecasting gap, not a guidance miss. The question is whether profit stays positive when revenue growth stays in the mid-teens rather than re-accelerating.

WHY IT MATTERSThe user metric itself is about to change

Buried in the release is a reporting change with real consequences for how Snap will be judged. Beginning with the first-quarter 2027 Form 10-Q, Snap plans to switch to global monthly active users as its primary audience metric, replacing daily active users and discontinuing regional daily-user and per-user figures.

Snap's stated reason is that daily engagement has been stable as a share of monthly engagement, and that optimizing for daily use can work against long-term value. Readers should therefore treat this quarter's 495 million daily users as one of the last prints on the old yardstick, and expect future comparisons to be made on a different base.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISProfit arrived before growth did

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Snap's quarter is a profitability print, not a growth print. The profit swing and the doubling of adjusted EBITDA are real, but they were bought with discipline while user growth added only 2 million in the quarter. The story to test is whether the next quarter holds profit positive on mid-teens revenue growth, and how the coming switch to monthly users reframes a user base that is barely growing on the daily count.
