Qualcomm beat the totals and still lost the phone quarter

Qualcomm's fiscal fourth-quarter handset revenue fell 26% to $5.14 billion, missing the $5.25 billion estimate, even as adjusted sales of $10.44 billion and adjusted profit of $2.17 per share beat estimates. The company guided first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $10.4 billion to $11.2 billion, said the forecast includes no further Apple sales, and saw shares fall more than 4% after hours.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Qualcomm reported fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results after the U.S. market close on November 4, and the print split in two directions: the totals beat, the handset line missed. Adjusted sales fell 7% to $10.44 billion, beating the $10.11 billion estimate, and adjusted profit came in at $2.17 per share against a $2.16 estimate. GAAP revenue fell 8% to $10.32 billion, beating a $10.12 billion expectation, with GAAP profit of $1.25 billion reversing a $3.12 billion loss a year earlier.

The miss sat in the handset unit. Handset revenue fell 26% to $5.14 billion, below the $5.25 billion analysts polled by Visible Alpha expected, dragging total chip-segment revenue down 8% to $9.02 billion. Reuters attributed the shortfall to sluggish smartphone demand and declining Apple-related revenue, and cited Counterpoint Research data showing global smartphone shipments fell 20% in the September quarter to a 14-year low.

The offset came from outside phones. Automotive revenue rose 89% to $1.99 billion and internet-of-things revenue grew 4% to $1.89 billion, while the licensing segment fell 7% to $1.3 billion. For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, Qualcomm guided revenue of $10.4 billion to $11.2 billion, with a midpoint above the $10.48 billion average estimate, chip-segment sales of $9 billion to $9.6 billion against an $8.88 billion estimate, and adjusted profit of $2.15 to $2.35 per share against a $2.27 estimate. Shares fell more than 4% in extended trading, with The Wall Street Journal reporting a 4.9% fall to $164.78.

WHY IT MATTERSThe beat and the miss are both real, on different lines

Reading this quarter as either a beat or a miss alone misstates it. The company-level totals cleared estimates on both the GAAP and adjusted bases, yet the handset line, the business that still supplies more than half of chip-segment revenue, fell short of its own estimate and fell 26% year over year. The two facts coexist because the growth lines and the legacy line are moving at very different speeds.

That split is the quarter's real story: totals held up by segments that are still smaller than the shrinking one. Automotive at $1.99 billion and IoT at $1.89 billion together remain below handset revenue of $5.14 billion, so the offset is directional rather than complete.

WHY IT MATTERSApple is out of the forecast, by Qualcomm's own statement

CEO Cristiano Amon told Reuters the first-quarter fiscal 2027 forecast includes no further sales from Apple, and said design wins rose 70% year over year in fiscal 2027 with Apple out of the model. He added that Qualcomm expects non-handset revenue to replace Apple entirely, with 60% year-over-year growth in the non-handset business across fiscal 2027.

The statement reframes the handset decline from a cyclical dip into a planned exit from a customer. Whether the replacement arrives on schedule is now the testable claim: two custom-silicon programs with hyperscalers begin generating revenue in the current quarter, and Qualcomm continues to target $5 billion in data center revenue for fiscal 2027, on the way to a stated $15 billion annual data center target by fiscal 2029.

WHY IT MATTERSMemory prices are the quiet squeeze on the whole handset chain

Reuters noted that demand for high-bandwidth storage for AI servers has pushed up memory chip prices, raising the cost of consumer electronics and squeezing smartphone processor designers such as Qualcomm. Amon said Qualcomm called the memory pressure early and prepared for it, and that Android handset sales are ticking upward again sequentially.

The mechanism matters beyond one quarter: AI infrastructure demand is now bidding up an input that consumer devices also need. Handset weakness and data center strength are not separate stories here; the same AI build-out lifts one side of Qualcomm's business while raising costs on the other.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe totals beat; the phone business is being replaced, not repaired

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Qualcomm's quarter marks a transition stated out loud. The handset line is no longer the engine to be fixed; it is the legacy to be replaced by automotive, IoT and data center revenue. The guidance assumes that replacement arrives on schedule, and the $5 billion data center target for fiscal 2027 is the number that will prove or break that assumption.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The figures in this report mix two bases that must not be interchanged: GAAP revenue of $10.32 billion, down 8% year over year, and adjusted sales of $10.44 billion, down 7%, against an estimate of $10.11 billion. Adjusted earnings of $2.17 per share compare with an estimate of $2.16; GAAP profit of $1.25 billion compares with a $3.12 billion loss a year earlier. First-quarter fiscal 2027 guidance is forward-looking and actual results may vary. The CEO's statement that the forecast includes no further Apple sales is a company statement about its own model, not an Apple disclosure.
