Teradata Q3 Revenue Falls 4% to $399M as Cloud ARR Grows 11%

Teradata reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $399 million, down 4% but above consensus, with public cloud ARR up 11% to $700 million, non-GAAP EPS of $0.70 beating estimates, and a raised full-year earnings guide; shares fell 5.5% as the AI product push has yet to lift reported revenue.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Teradata reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $399 million on November 5, 2026, down 4% year over year from $416 million but slightly above the $395.7 million consensus. Recurring revenue fell 2% to $359 million, still 90% of total revenue. Total annual recurring revenue rose 1% to $1.512 billion, and public cloud ARR grew 11% to $700 million. GAAP diluted earnings per share were $0.40 against $0.42 a year earlier, while non-GAAP diluted EPS of $0.70 beat the $0.58 consensus. Cash flow from operations was $91 million and adjusted free cash flow $96 million.

The company raised its full-year 2026 earnings outlook: GAAP diluted EPS is now expected at $4.63 to $4.67 and non-GAAP diluted EPS at $2.86 to $2.90. It narrowed its revenue outlook to the high end of the prior range, with total revenue down 1% to flat for the year, and reaffirmed cash flow from operations of $665 million to $685 million, which includes a $315 million after-tax net benefit from its settlement with SAP. For the fourth quarter it guided revenue down 4% to 6% year over year.

The quarter's product story was AI. Teradata released Teradata Factory for general availability, an integrated GPU system for analytics and AI that keeps data inside the customer's own walls, turned its Tera assistant into an agentic coworker for enterprise data work, and launched Tera Context Engine and Tera Harness. It also put its Data Analyst Agent on AWS Marketplace and integrated its Autonomous Knowledge Platform with Microsoft OneLake. Shares fell 5.5% in the regular session on November 5, closing at $32.72.

WHY IT MATTERSCloud ARR is growing; the legacy revenue base is still shrinking

Public cloud ARR grew 11% to $700 million, now nearly half of total ARR, while total revenue fell 4% and consulting services dropped 21%. The shape of the business is clear: the cloud and AI platform is growing, the legacy on-premises and services base is contracting, and the net result is a company treading water on the top line while its mix improves. Recurring revenue at 90% of the total keeps the base stable, but stability is not growth.

The raised earnings guide is the other half of the story. Non-GAAP EPS of $0.70 beat consensus by about 21%, and the full-year non-GAAP guide moved up, which says management believes cost discipline and the higher-margin recurring mix can carry earnings even while revenue is flat to down. The question is how long earnings can outrun a flat top line before the cloud ARR growth has to show up in reported revenue.

WHY IT MATTERSTeradata is pitching itself as the governed home for enterprise AI agents

The product announcements are all pointed at the same buyer: enterprises that want AI agents working on their own data without moving it. Teradata Factory, Tera Context Engine and Tera Harness are a bet that the differentiator in enterprise AI is governance, context and cost per task, not raw model capability. The company even cites an internal benchmark claiming Tera delivers a 53% lower cost per reliably solved task than Snowflake Cortex Code.

That framing puts Teradata in direct competition with the cloud data platforms on their own turf, and the 11% cloud ARR growth suggests the pitch is landing with some customers. But the 5.5% share drop after a consensus beat shows the market is not yet convinced that AI positioning converts into revenue growth. The fourth-quarter guide, revenue down 4% to 6%, gives the skeptics the near-term numbers.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA cloud ARR story that still needs a revenue story

Tokenta Signal's judgment: $700 million of public cloud ARR growing 11% is the real signal in Teradata's quarter, and the Teradata Factory and Tera agent launches show a coherent pitch for governed enterprise AI. But $399 million of revenue, down 4%, with a fourth-quarter guide of down 4% to 6%, means the AI story is still a promise on the income statement. The raised earnings guide buys time; the cloud ARR has to start converting into reported growth before the market stops discounting it.
