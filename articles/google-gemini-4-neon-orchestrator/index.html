Four models in five weeks, and Neon takes the orchestrator's seat

The selling point this time is the division of labour. Neon's pricing and benchmarks point at the same position: it acts as the lead agent, splitting tasks, assigning work and checking results, while cheaper Hydrogen runs the volume underneath. The frontier race is shifting from who is smartest to whose team gets the work done.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Argon led off on October 13, Carbon on November 10, Hydrogen on November 12, and Neon arrived on November 18. The Gemini 4 family shipped four models in five weeks, all named from the periodic table, and the model picker in AI Studio is laid out as a periodic table itself. The division of labour was written into the launch: Carbon takes the hardest long-horizon projects, Hydrogen handles fast everyday execution, and Neon fills the high-frequency complex work in between.

Neon's spec sheet is restrained. Input costs $3 per million tokens, cached input $0.15, output $15, with a 1 million-token context window and four thinking levels. There is no free tier; the API opens on the paid tier only, while Pro and Ultra subscribers get it directly in the Gemini app and Antigravity. The official model card shows it was adapted from the Carbon line, with knowledge current to March 2026.

THE ORCHESTRATORThe lead agent's job is the one it does best

Google states Neon's positioning plainly in the official blog. It can run long tasks on its own, or serve as the orchestrator, directing parallel Hydrogen sub-agents inside Antigravity. The supporting engineering detail is fewer repeated file reads, proactive progress reports while it works, and stable context compression across long sessions.

In the official demo, a team of Neon agents broke one image down into a spreadsheet of 100,000 cells, running more than 500,000 live formulas for edge detection, depth calculation and relighting, without embedding the image or writing a script. Chubby, a tester who received the model a day early, ran 17 interactive demos in Antigravity; 13 sessions at the xhigh level triggered 762 tool calls in total. Three buggy programs passed all tests after its fixes, all four planted bugs in a code review were found, and it flagged two more edge cases on its own, leap-second validation and folder replacement.

THE SCORECARDOn the scorecard, it loses only three

Google's official comparison table puts Neon against Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol. Across 19 items, Neon loses three: Terminal-Bench 4.1 at 65.2 against Opus at 65.8, FrontierCode at 46.2 against 47.3, and Chartography at 73.5 against Sol at 75.1. It leads on most of the rest. SWE-sweep scores 39.0 inside Google's own Antigravity harness, nearly four times the best rival score. Switch to the neutral mini-SWE harness and the lead shrinks to 12.8 against 11.6. The same model on a different harness can differ by a factor of three, and that row is the most revealing line in the table.

The results beyond coding are equally clear. Vending-Bench 2, which simulates running a business over the long term, puts Neon first with $16,843, and the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index puts it first at 58.6. Cursor published same-day measurements on its default tier: CursorBench 4.1 at 63.7, averaging $6.54 per task, on the cost-effectiveness frontier. Security is a separate track: Neon for cyber scores 90.5 on SEC-bench Pro and 51.3 on WebExploitBench, and opens over the coming days through the Fairwind Program to trusted defenders.

ALSO TODAYAlso today, Anthropic prices at $83

On the same day, Anthropic set its initial public offering price at $83 per share, above the earlier $70 to $78 range, expanding the offering to 980 million shares and raising about $81.3 billion, a record for an IPO. The shares list on Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker ANT. The Amazon and Google ownership stakes covered in yesterday's piece are unchanged; only the price moved, and the market paid more than the company asked.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM SINGLE MODELS TO MODEL TEAMS
From single models to model teams
A lead agent plus a cluster of sub-agents is now the officially recommended architecture. Models are released by job description, and the orchestrator seat gets its own price and its own scorecard.
FROM TOKEN PRICE TO TASK COST
From token price to task price
Google has changed the terms of the pitch. Neon's token price still sits above Carbon's promotional rate; the selling point is a 32% lower cost per completed task. The buyer's arithmetic becomes what one finished task actually costs.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISSelling the team, not the soloist

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Neon's specs do not dazzle; its position is exact. Google has laid out four models by job description, and the orchestrator seat now stands on its own. The frontier race is shifting from which model is smartest to whose agent team can actually finish a long task. What matters next is whether this division of labour produces real retention inside Antigravity. Demo videos do not count.
