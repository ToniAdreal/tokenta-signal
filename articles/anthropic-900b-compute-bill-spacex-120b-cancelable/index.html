Anthropic's $900.9B Compute Bill, Filed: SpaceX Gets $120.5B, and Either Side Can Walk Away

Anthropic's SEC filings disclose about $900.9 billion in purchase and lease commitments, substantially all for compute, with about $451 billion due by the end of 2030; Google is owed at least $121.3 billion, Amazon at least $110 billion and Microsoft up to $49.2 billion, all non-cancelable, while the up-to-$120.5 billion SpaceX deal is cancelable on 90 days' notice after early 2027.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic's IPO filings put its aggregate purchase and lease commitments at about $900.9 billion, substantially all of it for compute and infrastructure, stretching out to 2045. About $451 billion of that total falls due by the end of 2030, and about $70.7 billion falls due in the twelve months after September 30, 2026. The numbers were in the filing all along; the market spent the second day of trading reading them, and Anthropic's shares closed that Friday down 8.5% at $88.91, as covered in flash 447.

The breakdown shows how the bill is spread. Google is owed at least $121.3 billion from April 2026 to July 2033, Amazon at least $110 billion from May 2026 to April 2036, and Microsoft $47.2 billion to $49.2 billion from November 2026 to June 2033. All three are non-cancelable except for an uncured material breach, with true-up payments if Anthropic spends less than agreed. Broadcom accounts for about $161 billion in TPU equipment leases, and the neoclouds Nscale and Lambda about $86 billion combined.

The SpaceX line is the exception, and it is the one that moved the story. Anthropic agreed to pay SpaceX's AI unit up to $120.5 billion through 2029, but after initial non-cancelable periods ending in January and March 2027, either side can walk away on 90 days' notice. That makes the largest single relationship in the filing also the softest. SpaceX's own third quarter shows what the other side of that table looks like: net profit of $4.4 billion on $14.58 billion of revenue, up 161% year over year, with Anthropic contributing about $5 billion, roughly 34.5% of the total.

The same week, The Information reported that Anthropic is offering large customers discounts of more than 30%, plus credits of up to $2,000 per user, to win bigger spending commitments for newer products such as Claude Cowork and Claude Managed Agents. Anthropic did not respond to a request for comment. SpaceX chief executive Elon Musk, speaking at the DealBook summit, said Anthropic told him its highest-probability outcome is wanting an incremental 10 gigawatts of compute from SpaceX, and wrote on X that he thinks the opposite of cancellation is going to happen.

WHY IT MATTERSThe bill is the business model, stated plainly

The $900.9 billion figure is not a footnote to the IPO; it is the other half of it. Anthropic raised about $81.3 billion at pricing and about $93.5 billion with the greenshoe, and it owes roughly ten times that in future compute payments. The filing ties the raise to the bill directly: the customers financing the supplier are also the suppliers Anthropic owes, with Google, Amazon and Microsoft on both sides of the table. That circularity is the frontier business stated plainly, a company buying intelligence by the token, forward, at scale.

The structure of the bill matters more than its size. The hyperscaler deals are locked in, non-cancelable, with true-ups. The SpaceX deal, the one with the largest single counterparty and the largest share of the counterparty's revenue, is the one either side can exit with 90 days' notice in early 2027. If Anthropic's demand grows as Musk says it will, SpaceX holds the flexible capacity; if it does not, the cancelable deal is the first pressure valve. Either way, the next quarterly report will show whether the revenue line is catching the compute bill, or the bill is still running ahead of it.

WHAT TO WATCHDiscounts are the tell on demand

Watch the discounting. Offering more than 30% off to lock in bigger commitments is what a company does when it needs the revenue line to keep pace with a $451 billion wall of payments due by 2030. It is also what a company does when it believes the volume will come. The test is whether the discounted commitments convert into usage at the rates the filing assumes, or whether the next filing shows the same bill with slower revenue behind it.

Watch the SpaceX dates. The initial non-cancelable periods end in January and March 2027, and the 90-day notice window after that is the first moment either side can renegotiate or walk away. Musk's public confidence that Anthropic will want 10 more gigawatts is a statement of intent, not a contract. The contract says either side can leave. The first quarterly report as a public company, and any movement on the SpaceX terms, will show which of those two documents describes the relationship.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe $900 Billion Bill Has a Soft Spot, and It Is SpaceX

Tokenta Signal's judgment: The headline number is $900.9 billion, but the number that decides the story is $120.5 billion with a 90-day exit. The hyperscaler deals are locked and the revenue has to catch them; the SpaceX deal is the one relationship where either side can walk away in early 2027, and it is also the one where Anthropic is a third of the counterparty's revenue. Musk says Anthropic will want 10 more gigawatts. The filing says either side can leave. Both can be true at once, and the next quarterly report will show which one the market believes.
