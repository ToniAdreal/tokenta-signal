Rival Turned Supplier: OpenAI to Buy Google's Chips

Reuters relayed The Information on October 27: OpenAI plans to buy Google's AI chips, with price and details undisclosed. Cooperation between the two rivals is extending from cloud services to chip procurement.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 27, Reuters relayed a report from The Information: OpenAI plans to buy Google's AI chips. The report disclosed no price or details; Google did not respond and OpenAI declined to comment. The report itself is unconfirmed by either side, but its direction is clear: the commercial cooperation between two companies that compete in products and search is deepening.

A week earlier, OpenAI had announced it was putting its frontier models into Google Cloud's Model Garden. Earlier still, in June 2025, Reuters reported that OpenAI had begun renting Google chips. Across renting, using and buying, the relationship is moving from testing the waters to procurement.

The backdrop is the scale of the compute bill: Amazon, Microsoft and Google parent Alphabet together will spend up to $600 billion on AI infrastructure this year. OpenAI is one of the largest buyers of Nvidia GPUs, and Google's TPUs, a cheaper alternative, are winning outside customers.

THE RELATIONSHIPFrom Competitor to Supplier and Customer

OpenAI and Google appear on three relationship charts at once: competitors in search and chat products, supply and demand counterparts in cloud services, and buyer and seller of chips. The same pair of companies no longer holds mutually exclusive roles at different layers.

The pattern is not unique: Anthropic is also a Google TPU customer. Google sells chips to rivals on one side and puts rivals' models into its own cloud on the other; chips and models are decoupling into separate businesses.

THE CHIPThe TPU's Selling Point Is Price, Not a Performance Myth

Reuters' phrasing was restrained: TPUs are seen as a cheaper alternative to Nvidia's dominant chips. Google only began selling TPUs directly to some customers this year, and Alphabet expects revenue from TPU sales agreements to be only a small share by the end of this year, with the bulk arriving in 2027.

If OpenAI does order, what it buys is not compute itself but bargaining power: a second supplier reduces dependence on a single vendor and hedges against GPU supply and price swings.

THE TIMINGThe Timing, on the Eve of Earnings Season, Is Worth Noting

The report surfaced a day before Alphabet's quarterly earnings, as markets watch for when AI spending converts into cloud growth. Progress in external TPU sales is one of the numbers most likely to be pressed on the earnings call.

For OpenAI, the purchase report itself carries signal value: it shows the market that its compute sources are diversifying, even before a final contract is signed.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM BUNDLED TO STANDALONE
From picking a cloud means picking its chips, to chips flowing across vendors
TPUs are leaving Google's data centres for a rival's purchase order; the binding between chips and cloud is being undone.
FROM SINGLE SOURCE TO SECOND SOURCE
From a single supplier to second-source bargaining
What OpenAI's purchasing move buys is optionality and bargaining power beyond Nvidia.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Chip Business Is Tearing Down the Cloud Vendors' Walls

If reports that OpenAI is buying Google chips prove true, the significance lies not in a single purchase but in a structural shift: chips are leaving the cloud vendors' walled gardens to become standalone goods traded across companies. Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: the next round of competition in the compute market will be about who can unbundle chips, cloud and models and sell them separately.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain
