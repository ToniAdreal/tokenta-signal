Microsoft 365 Outage Passes 18 Hours as 11:01 UTC Update Reports Recovery Completed in Parts of Asia Pacific

Update to flashes 331 through 341: at 11:01 UTC on November 13, Microsoft said recovery actions have been completed across portions of the affected infrastructure in the Asia Pacific region, traffic is being gradually restored, and it is seeing improvements in key customer scenarios, including mailbox access and delayed email processing, though some users may still experience intermittent issues; its focus is now on extending the same recovery actions across affected infrastructure in other regions, while continuing stabilization and adding supporting capacity; reducing email queues and backlogs remains a priority, the email timeout window remains extended to 48 hours, and it has no evidence of malicious activity, with a post-incident report promised once mitigated. The University of Pennsylvania mirror of the admin-console feed, crawled at 11:32 UTC, carries the same current-status text in both incident records, MO1550821 at 11:01:59 UTC and EX1550543 at 11:01:26 UTC; a second fetch confirmed the text, and Petri's live article logs the same update at 6:11 AM ET quoting the Asia Pacific sentence verbatim, with AppleInsider publishing at 11:19:33 UTC quoting the same update. The 09:57 UTC update had said only that recovery was progressing in phases with incremental improvement and capacity below baseline; this is the first update naming a region where recovery actions are complete. The outage, open since 16:17 UTC on November 12, is past 18 hours and 44 minutes with no root cause published and no fix declared; the next update is due by 12:00 UTC. Note: the 11:01 time comes from the admin-console feed via the University of Pennsylvania mirror, verified by two independent reads of the mirror plus two independent press sources quoting the same update.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Microsoft's global Microsoft 365 outage passed 18 hours and 44 minutes with still no fix declared, and its newest update named a recovered region for the first time. At 11:01 UTC on November 13, 2026, Microsoft posted a fresh service-health update for incidents EX1550543 and MO1550821: recovery actions have been completed across portions of the affected infrastructure in the Asia Pacific region, traffic is being gradually restored, and Microsoft is seeing improvements in key customer scenarios, including mailbox access and delayed email processing. Some users may still experience intermittent issues. The focus is now on extending the same recovery actions across affected infrastructure in other regions, while continuing stabilization efforts and adding supporting capacity. Reducing email queues and backlogs remains a priority, and the email timeout window stays extended to 48 hours to protect delayed messages. It said it has no evidence of malicious activity, and that once mitigated it will publish a full post-incident report with the root cause and defense-in-depth actions. The next update is due by 12:00 UTC.

The admin-console feed, mirrored by the University of Pennsylvania and crawled at 11:32 UTC, carries the same current-status text in both incident records, MO1550821 updated at 11:01:59 UTC and EX1550543 at 11:01:26 UTC. A second fetch of the mirror minutes later confirmed the update text is present. Petri's live outage article logs the same update at 6:11 AM ET, quoting the Asia Pacific sentence verbatim, and AppleInsider, publishing at 11:19:33 UTC, quotes the same update. The incident opened at 16:17 UTC on November 12, when Exchange Online users lost mailbox access, and widened at 18:26 UTC into a Microsoft 365 advisory covering Outlook, Teams, SharePoint Online, Copilot, Defender, Purview, Planner, To Do, Intune and Power BI. Microsoft has twice reported servers returning to service and then becoming unresponsive again once they started taking traffic, at 00:00 UTC and again at 04:22 UTC. No root cause has been published, and no time for full recovery has been given.

The 11:01 UTC update matters because it is the first update in which Microsoft names a region where recovery actions are complete, rather than describing recovery as in progress everywhere. The 09:57 UTC update had said only that recovery actions were progressing in phases, with incremental improvement in several workloads and capacity still below baseline. The Asia Pacific completion is the first concrete regional milestone in an incident now in its nineteenth hour, and the update pairs it with a named next step: extending the same actions to other regions. But the qualifiers are unchanged in kind: some users may still experience intermittent issues, capacity work continues, and earlier improvements proved temporary. The outage also continues to reach beyond Microsoft's own assistant: Reuters reported it hit the connectors OpenAI and Anthropic use to reach Microsoft 365.

WHY IT MATTERSA first recovered region, still no recovered service

Every update since 04:22 UTC has carried the same shape: a mechanism named, a mitigation described, and no sustained recovery to point at. The 11:01 UTC update breaks the shape in one respect: it names the Asia Pacific region as the place where recovery actions are complete, with traffic gradually restored and improvements visible in mailbox access and delayed email processing. The grade has moved step by step, from marginal success for patches applied after reboots at 06:57 UTC, to positive results for an authentication and routing patch at 07:29 UTC, to confirmed stability across the patched portions at 08:09 UTC, to early signs of stabilization at 09:03 UTC, to incremental improvement in several workloads at 09:57 UTC, and now to completed recovery actions in part of one region. The change is a named region and a named next step, not a declared end, and it lands more than eighteen hours after the first mailbox failures.

For the AI stack the exposure is unchanged and still open. Copilot Chat may not load or answer and may not reach email, calendar or files, and the OpenAI and Anthropic Microsoft 365 connectors remain affected, Reuters reported. Companies that layered AI assistants on top of Microsoft 365 are still waiting on the same mailbox layer as everyone else. Completed recovery actions in part of Asia Pacific are not the same as a restored service, and the next update will show whether the regional recovery holds and extends, or repeats the pattern of the two recoveries that froze again at 00:00 and 04:22 UTC.

WHAT TO WATCHThe 12:00 UTC update, then the post-incident report

The next fact is the 12:00 UTC update: whether the Asia Pacific recovery extends to a second region, or whether the update repeats the same watching brief with a new region named. Watch whether Microsoft names the traffic influx as a cause or a symptom, and whether it gives a time for full recovery.

The post-incident report will carry the lasting answers: why servers failed their health checks, why restored servers froze again under traffic, what the network configuration change under review actually changed, and what the traffic influx was. Until it lands, treat every cause as unconfirmed. The earlier Teams incident TM1550488 was resolved the same day, with a preliminary post-incident report promised within two business days.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISEighteen hours in, a first region is named recovered, but the service is not

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Eighteen hours and forty-four minutes in, Microsoft has named a region where recovery actions are complete, the Asia Pacific, with traffic gradually restored and improvements visible in mailbox access and delayed email processing. That is the first concrete regional milestone in this incident, and the first update in which recovery is described as finished somewhere rather than merely in progress everywhere. But the substance is still short of an end: some users may still experience intermittent issues, capacity work continues, and earlier improvements proved temporary. What is still missing is everything that would close the incident: a root cause, a recovered service, and a time for full restoration. Until the 12:00 UTC update shows the recovery extending to a second region and holding under Friday traffic, treat the outage as ongoing.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this update rest on the University of Pennsylvania's mirror of Microsoft's Service Health admin-console feed, crawled at 11:32 UTC on November 13, 2026, which carries the newest update for MO1550821 at 11:01:59 UTC and for EX1550543 at 11:01:26 UTC, with identical current-status text in both records, and on a second fetch of the same mirror minutes later confirming the update text is present. A second independent source, Petri's live outage article, logs the same update at 6:11 AM ET and quotes its key sentences verbatim, and AppleInsider's article, published at 11:19:33 UTC, quotes the same update. The incident IDs, start times, and the next-update time of 12:00 UTC come from the admin feed. Downdetector counts are user-submitted reports, not a count of affected accounts. The root cause is unconfirmed: Microsoft has described failing health checks, an overwhelmed secure-connection process, a network configuration change under review, a surge in outbound call volume from its routing services, machines stuck in a bad state, and a traffic influx it is still working to explain, and has said there is no indication of malicious activity.
