Uber Books $59.8 Billion in Q3 and Puts a Number on Autonomy: 700 Driverless Vehicles by Year-End

Uber's third-quarter Gross Bookings rose 20% to $59.8 billion and non-GAAP EPS rose 35% to $0.88, with Adjusted EBITDA up 31% to $3.0 billion. The company said approximately 700 fully driverless vehicles are expected on its platform by year-end and over 2,000 in 2027, and guided Q4 Gross Bookings of $63.25 billion to $65.25 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Uber's third-quarter 2026 results, released on November 4, 2026, put Gross Bookings for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 at $59.8 billion, up 20% year over year, the company's fifth consecutive quarter of 20% or higher Gross Bookings growth. Revenue was $14.8 billion, up 10% year over year, while trips grew 16% to 4.1 billion and Monthly Active Platform Consumers rose 14% to 215 million.

Profitability expanded faster than revenue: GAAP income from operations rose 30% to $1.4 billion, Adjusted EBITDA rose 31% to $3.0 billion, and non-GAAP operating income rose 38% to $2.3 billion, a record 3.9% of Gross Bookings. Non-GAAP EPS rose 35% to $0.88. GAAP net income was $512 million and GAAP diluted EPS was $0.25, down from $6.6 billion and $3.11 a year earlier, a comparison distorted by the year-earlier tax benefit and this quarter's $476 million pre-tax equity revaluation loss.

The autonomy line is the forward-looking hook: CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said Uber's focus on autonomous is now on commercial execution, with approximately 700 fully driverless vehicles expected on the platform by year-end and over 2,000 in 2027. For the fourth quarter, Uber guided Gross Bookings of $63.25 billion to $65.25 billion, non-GAAP EPS of $0.95 to $0.99, and Adjusted EBITDA of $3.13 billion to $3.23 billion.

WHY IT MATTERSThe profit story is cleaner than the GAAP headline

A 92% fall in GAAP net income looks like a collapse until the base is stated: the year-earlier quarter carried a $4.9 billion tax benefit, and this quarter carries a $476 million pre-tax loss on equity revaluations. Strip those two items out and the operating picture runs the other way, with operating income up 30%, Adjusted EBITDA up 31%, and non-GAAP operating margin at a record 3.9% of Gross Bookings.

That distinction is the whole quarter in one line: the platform is converting growth into operating profit more efficiently, while the GAAP bottom line is dominated by tax and revaluation items that say little about the operating business.

WHY IT MATTERS700 driverless vehicles is a deployment target, not a fleet yet

The autonomous commitment deserves the same discipline as the earnings numbers: approximately 700 fully driverless vehicles by year-end and over 2,000 in 2027 are targets stated on the earnings call materials, not vehicles already on the road. The test is whether those counts materialize and what share of trips they carry.

What makes the target worth tracking is the framing shift: Khosrowshahi described autonomy as moving from pilot to commercial execution. If the 2027 count lands, Uber's autonomy story stops being a partnership slide and becomes a measurable operating line.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is autonomy moving from pilot language to a counted target

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the number that matters in Uber's print is not the 20% bookings growth, which is now a five-quarter routine, but the counted autonomy target. Approximately 700 driverless vehicles by year-end and over 2,000 in 2027 give the market something checkable, and the record 3.9% non-GAAP operating margin shows the core business can fund that build. The watchpoint is delivery: a missed vehicle count will read as a missed promise, not a rounding error.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this report rest on Uber's official third-quarter 2026 earnings release of November 4, 2026, as distributed by BusinessWire, and Reuters' same-day brief of the results. The earnings figures are unaudited. GAAP EPS of $0.25 and non-GAAP EPS of $0.88 are two different bases and must not be interchanged: GAAP net income fell 92% year over year largely because the year-earlier quarter included a $4.9 billion tax benefit, while the current quarter includes a $476 million pre-tax loss on equity revaluations.
