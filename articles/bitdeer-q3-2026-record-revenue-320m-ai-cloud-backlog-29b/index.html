Bitdeer Q3 Revenue Hits Record $320.7M as AI Cloud Backlog Reaches $2.9 Billion

Bitdeer Technologies Group reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $320.7 million, up 89% year over year and ahead of the $258.2 million estimate. Adjusted EBITDA rose 173% to $69.6 million, while net loss widened to $125.5 million and gross margin fell to 3.4%. AI Cloud revenue grew to $20.4 million from $1.8 million, and the AI Cloud revenue backlog reached approximately $2.9 billion, anchored by a $4.7 billion 16-year Tydal, Norway lease and more than $2.5 billion of contracted Malaysia capacity.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Bitdeer Technologies Group (NASDAQ: BTDR) reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $320.7 million, up 89% year over year from $169.7 million and up 40% sequentially from $228.8 million, beating the $258.2 million analyst estimate by 24%. Adjusted EBITDA rose 173% to $69.6 million, up 124% from $31.1 million in the second quarter. Self-mining revenue led the quarter at $217.5 million, up 66% year over year, with co-mining contributing $59.9 million in its first full quarter.

The AI pivot is now measured in contracts, not just capacity. AI Cloud revenue was $20.4 million, up from $1.8 million a year earlier, and the AI Cloud revenue backlog grew to approximately $2.9 billion under signed customer commitments. The Tydal, Norway phase 1 site, 60.5 MW due in the fourth quarter of 2026, carries a $4.7 billion 16-year AI/HPC data center lease with Volta, with 121 IT MW configured to run NVIDIA GPUs for a leading AI lab and a potential total of about $8.0 billion if the tenant exercises its one-time 8-year extension.

The record top line came with a thin margin and a wider loss. Gross profit fell to $10.8 million from $39.7 million a year earlier, a 3.4% margin against 23.4%, as electricity and depreciation costs rose with the expanded mining fleet. Net loss widened to $125.5 million from $30.1 million, including a $54.6 million non-cash loss on the change in fair value of a digital assets loan, and interest expense rose to $34.3 million from $20.2 million on convertible senior notes and related-party borrowing. Cash and restricted cash stood at $213.7 million, with $387.0 million in digital assets and receivables.

WHY IT MATTERSThe $2.9 billion backlog is the quarter's real number

Revenue of $320.7 million is a record, but most of it is still Bitcoin mining: self-mining at $217.5 million and co-mining at $59.9 million dwarf AI Cloud's $20.4 million. The number that reframes the company is the $2.9 billion AI Cloud backlog, signed commitments that convert mining power into long-duration AI service contracts.

The Malaysia pipeline shows how the backlog is built. The Johor Bahru A201 site has offtake commitments covering more than 70% of capacity, worth more than $1.7 billion over five years, with over 100 GB300 NVL72 racks ordered ahead of a second-quarter 2027 go-live. The Cyberjaya A102 site is 100% contracted under five-year commitments worth more than $800 million. Backlog becomes revenue only when sites energize on schedule, so the Tydal phase 1 delivery this quarter and the two Malaysia go-lives in 2027 are the conversion tests.

THE HONEST CHECKRecord revenue, 3.4% gross margin

The profit picture cuts against the headline. Gross margin collapsed to 3.4% from 23.4% a year ago, the net loss quadrupled to $125.5 million, and the adjusted loss of $0.46 per share came in wider than analysts expected. The loss is flattered and distorted at once: a $54.6 million non-cash digital-assets loan revaluation sits inside it, while adjusted EBITDA of $69.6 million strips out the depreciation on a mining fleet that more than doubled its hashrate to 86.4 EH/s.

The funding question is open. Management said customer prepayments plus project-level financing are designed to fund AI capacity as it is contracted, and named delivering Tydal, completing financings and bringing Malaysia online as the priorities. With $213.7 million of cash against a 3,121 MW global capacity plan, the $2.9 billion backlog is only as bankable as the financings behind it.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA mining company repricing itself as an AI landlord

Tokenta Signal's judgment: The $2.9 billion AI Cloud backlog is the story, and the 3.4% gross margin is the warning. Bitdeer is converting its power and mining footprint into long-duration AI contracts faster than any neocloud peer this quarter, with Tydal and Malaysia locking in multi-year, multi-billion-dollar commitments. The honest check is execution and financing: the backlog pays only when sites energize on time, the loss widened even as revenue hit a record, and the build-out is funded by prepayments and project debt that still have to close. A backlog this large against cash of $213.7 million is either the cheapest AI infrastructure pipeline in the market or a financing cliff, and the Tydal delivery this quarter will say which.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this report rest on Bitdeer Technologies Group's third-quarter 2026 results press release, published November 10, 2026 at 07:00 a.m. ET and read in full via GlobeNewswire, with AlphaStreet's earnings summary as a second source confirming revenue of $320.7 million against a $258.2 million estimate and an adjusted loss of $0.46 per share. AI Cloud revenue backlog is a company-defined, non-GAAP measure of expected revenue under signed customer commitments, not recognized revenue, and its timing depends on data center energization, GPU deployment and customer performance. The $4.7 billion Tydal lease value is the contracted value over 16 years, subject to the tenant's no-fee termination right at 10 years.
