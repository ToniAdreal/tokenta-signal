Anthropic Closes the Record IPO at $93.5 Billion as the Stock Slides Toward Its Issue Price

Anthropic closed its IPO on Friday, November 20, 2026, after Morgan Stanley exercised the full 147-million-share greenshoe at the $83 offer price, lifting gross proceeds to roughly $93.5 billion, the largest IPO on record. A $26 billion private placement to strategic investors including AMD, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia closed alongside. The shares fell 8.5% the same day to $88.91, after touching $83.05 intraday, and the stabilization support is now gone. This flash closes the Anthropic IPO arc in flashes 440, 441, 443, 447 and 451.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic closed its initial public offering on Friday, November 20, 2026, after underwriters exercised the full greenshoe option, lifting total gross proceeds to roughly $93.5 billion. Morgan Stanley, the stabilization agent, bought all 147 million additional Class A shares at the $83 offer price, adding about $12.2 billion to the $81.3 billion raised from the base offering of 980 million shares.

In total, 1,127,000,000 Class A shares were sold at $83 each. Anthropic sold 1,103,351,354 of them and received about $91.6 billion before underwriting discounts and expenses; existing stockholders sold 23,648,646 shares, worth just under $2 billion, which does not go to the company. Alongside the offering, Anthropic closed a $26 billion private placement of 313,253,007 Class N shares at the same $83 price to strategic technology investors including AMD, Broadcom, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC.

The closing landed on a brutal day for the stock. Anthropic shares fell 8.5% on Friday to close at $88.91, after trading as low as $83.05 intraday, five cents above the offer price. The stock had opened at $111.50 on Thursday and closed its debut at $97.17, about 17% above the offer price. The Nasdaq fell more than 5% on Friday, its worst stretch of the year, and the S&P 500 dropped for a seventh straight session.

Exercising the full greenshoe one day after the debut also removes a safety net. Underwriters normally have up to 30 days to exercise the option, and they can instead buy shares in the open market, which supports the price. With the option now fully exercised at the offer price, Morgan Stanley can no longer use open-market purchases to cover any remaining overallotment if the shares fall below $83. Berenberg's Carsten Hesse called the move positive for Anthropic, which keeps the extra proceeds, but less positive for investors losing a potential source of buying support.

WHY IT MATTERSThe largest IPO in history, closed in one day

Anthropic's offering is the largest IPO on record, and the greenshoe decision compressed its entire stabilization window into a single day. The underwriters sold more shares than the base deal, then bought the extra 147 million directly from the company at $83 rather than in the market. That is the standard move when a stock trades above its offer price, and it was available because Thursday's debut closed at $97.17.

The speed is the signal. A stabilization agent normally keeps the option open for up to 30 days as insurance against a slide. Using it in full on day one, while the stock was already falling toward the offer price, tells the market the banks would rather lock in the proceeds than defend the price. One person familiar with the matter told Reuters that market participants questioned the timing given Thursday's volatility.

WHAT TO WATCHThe floor is now the offer price itself

With the greenshoe gone, nothing stands between the stock and its $83 offer price except buyers. Friday's intraday low of $83.05 showed how close that floor came to being tested on the second day of trading. The close at $88.91 leaves only about 7% of headroom above the price every IPO buyer paid.

Watch whether the shares hold above $83 in the coming sessions, whether Anthropic's first quarterly report as a public company steadies the story, and whether the broader AI selloff that drove the Nasdaq down more than 5% on Friday pauses. The IPO arc is now complete: a record raise, a 17% debut pop, and a second day that gave most of it back.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe record raise is banked; the price defense is spent

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Anthropic got what the IPO was for, about $91.6 billion before fees plus a $26 billion strategic placement, and the banks got their deal closed in a day. What the company no longer has is a buyer of last resort. Exercising the full greenshoe while the stock slid toward the offer price was the rational trade for the underwriters and a cold signal for everyone else: the stabilization window is closed, the $83 floor is now defended only by demand, and Friday's $83.05 low showed how thin that defense is. The IPO proved Anthropic can raise money at historic scale. The next test is whether it can hold a public-market price without help.
