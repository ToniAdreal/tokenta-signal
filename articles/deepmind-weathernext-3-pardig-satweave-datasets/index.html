DeepMind Opens the Datasets Behind Its AI Weather Models

Google DeepMind released two foundational Earth-science datasets on November 13, 2026: SatWeave and PARDIG, the observational backbones behind its recently released WeatherNext 3 model. SatWeave merges geostationary satellite imagery (GOES-East, GOES-West, Meteosat 0, Meteosat Indian Ocean, Himawari) into a global mosaic across 17 channels, updating hourly with a decade-plus archive. PARDIG is an AI global precipitation dataset at 15-minute intervals and ~10 km resolution, trained on NASA GPM radar with SatWeave and ECMWF inputs; it improves hourly precipitation detection skill over NASA IMERG-Final by more than 30% on light-to-moderate rain, validated against three continental radar networks and 36,000+ stations, with archives 1999-2026. Both are openly available on Google Cloud Storage; PARDIG also in Google Earth Engine. Separately, the UK Met Office and Google signed a multi-year agreement for Met Office scientists to assess WeatherNext 3 in experimental sandboxes. Verified against the DeepMind blog read in full plus the Met Office blog and Xenospectrum coverage.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Google DeepMind released two foundational Earth-science datasets on November 13, 2026: SatWeave and PARDIG, the observational backbones behind its recently released WeatherNext 3 forecasting model. Both datasets are now openly available, with live feeds and historical archives, so researchers and developers can build weather, hydrology and climate applications on the same inputs the model uses.

SatWeave is a global mosaic of geostationary satellite imagery that gives WeatherNext 3 a live view of developing clouds and storms. It combines imagery from GOES-East, GOES-West, Meteosat 0, Meteosat Indian Ocean and Himawari into a single grid across 17 visible, water-vapour and infrared channels. It updates hourly, letting WeatherNext 3 issue new global predictions on an hourly cycle instead of waiting for analysis data refreshed every six hours, and DeepMind is releasing the hourly live feed plus a historical archive spanning more than a decade.

PARDIG (Precipitation AI Reanalysis: Densely Inferred GPM) is an AI-based global precipitation dataset built on an architecture similar to WeatherNext 3. It is trained directly on sparse radar observations from NASA's Global Precipitation Measurement Core Observatory, using SatWeave imagery and atmospheric fields from ECMWF as inputs. The result is a global precipitation map at 15-minute intervals and roughly 10-kilometre resolution, updated with an average latency of about five hours. DeepMind evaluated it against three continental radar networks across the US, Europe and Japan and more than 36,000 weather stations worldwide; it improves hourly precipitation detection skill over NASA's IMERG-Final by more than 30% on light-to-moderate precipitation and proved particularly effective at capturing extreme precipitation. Historical PARDIG archives run from 1999 to 2026.

WHAT TO WATCHA national forecaster signs on to test it

The second half of the story is institutional. The UK Met Office and Google have signed a new multi-year agreement under which Met Office scientists will assess WeatherNext 3 in experimental sandboxes, building on earlier collaborative research on tropical cyclone prediction. The Met Office says AI models are a complement to physics-based forecasting, not a replacement, and that new techniques pass strict validation before any operational use. Watch whether that assessment moves from sandboxes to operational trials, and whether other national services follow.

The watchpoints for the datasets themselves are adoption and independent verification. The live feeds are now public on Google Cloud Storage, and PARDIG's catalogues are also in Google Earth Engine, so independent teams can test the claimed 30%-plus detection gain and the extreme-rainfall performance on their own regions and events. DeepMind also points to downstream uses beyond forecasting, including solar power and wildfire monitoring from SatWeave and flood, landslide and drought modelling from PARDIG.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe model was the headline; the data is the durable part

Tokenta Signal's judgment: WeatherNext 3 drew attention as a model, but the datasets are what other people can build on. SatWeave and PARDIG solve the unglamorous problem underneath AI weather forecasting, turning incompatible satellite feeds and sparse rainfall measurements into a coherent, hourly, global picture, and DeepMind has now handed that picture to everyone with live feeds attached. The Met Office agreement is the credibility test to watch: a national forecaster putting the model through experimental sandboxes is a stronger signal than any benchmark chart, and whether it graduates to operational trials will say more about AI forecasting than the release itself.
