Vistra Posts $1.14B Q3 Profit as Power EBITDA Jumps Over 50%, Lifts 2026 Outlook

Vistra reported third-quarter 2026 net income of $1.14 billion, up from $652 million a year earlier, with Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA up more than 50% to $2.378 billion; it raised and narrowed 2026 guidance, initiated 2027 guidance above the 2026 range, and closed the Cogentrix acquisition adding about 5,500 MW of gas generation.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Vistra reported third-quarter 2026 net income of $1.14 billion on November 6, 2026, up from $652 million a year earlier, an increase of $488 million. Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA rose more than 50% year over year to $2.378 billion, from $1.581 billion. Shares rose about 5.7% in premarket trading, according to Finnhub data, after falling 6.15% in the prior session.

The profit includes a $559 million unrealized gain from commodity hedges expected to settle in future years, so the headline net income overstates the quarter's cash economics. The cleaner read is the operating line: adjusted EBITDA rose $797 million, driven by higher realized energy and capacity prices, three months of contribution from the plants acquired from Lotus, and the return of Martin Lake Unit 1 to service. Texas delivered $1.227 billion of segment EBITDA, up from $784 million, and the East segment $979 million, up from $719 million.

Vistra raised and narrowed its 2026 guidance, lifting the midpoint of Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA to a $7.1 billion to $7.5 billion range, from $6.8 billion to $7.6 billion, and initiated 2027 guidance of $7.7 billion to $8.5 billion. The board authorized an additional $1.5 billion of share repurchases, leaving about $2.5 billion of authorization, expected to be used by year-end 2028. The company has repurchased about $6.7 billion of shares since November 2021, cutting shares outstanding by roughly 30%.

The quarter also closed the growth leg Vistra has been building toward. In October, Vistra completed the Cogentrix Energy acquisition, adding about 5,500 MW of natural gas generation, paying about $2.2 billion in cash and repaying about $1.7 billion of assumed debt. It started commercial operations at the 405 MW Pulaski Solar facility in Illinois, under a power purchase agreement with Microsoft, and the 100 MW Deer Creek solar and storage facility in California, and signed a 20-year, 207 MW power purchase agreement with New Era Energy and Digital at its Odessa combined cycle gas plant in Texas.

WHY IT MATTERSThe AI power trade, priced into a utility print

Vistra is the power supplier behind the data-center buildout, and the quarter reads like a demand story rather than a cost story. CEO Jim Burke cited one of the hottest calendar third quarters on record, with all-time peak demand records in July in both ERCOT and PJM, and fleet commercial availability of nearly 95% through it. The Cogentrix gas fleet, the Microsoft-backed solar plant and the New Era agreement all point the same way: contracted, long-duration load.

The 2027 guidance range is the number that matters most. A $7.7 billion to $8.5 billion EBITDA range starts above the top of the raised 2026 range, which means Vistra is telling the market the demand step-up is structural, not a hot-summer spike. The test will be whether the Cogentrix fleet and the new contracts convert into realized prices at the same rate when weather normalizes.

WHY IT MATTERSGuidance is the real beat; the headline profit needs a haircut

More than $550 million of the $1.14 billion net income is an unrealized mark-to-market gain on hedges that settle in future years, and the release says plainly that the year-over-year profit increase was driven partly by a $375 million rise in unrealized derivative gains. Investors who read the headline as cash earnings will be disappointed on the next print if those marks reverse.

What is durable is the operating leverage: $2.378 billion of adjusted EBITDA on higher realized energy and capacity prices, a narrowed 2026 range with a higher midpoint, and a buyback program that has already retired about 30% of the share count. The quarter's signal is the guidance and the fleet, not the accounting gain.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA power supplier pricing the data-center step-up into 2027

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the $1.14 billion headline flatters the quarter, since more than $550 million of it is an unrealized hedge gain, but the operating print is genuine. Adjusted EBITDA up more than 50%, a raised and narrowed 2026 range, and a 2027 range that starts above the 2026 top end say Vistra expects the AI power demand to hold through weather normalization. The Cogentrix fleet and the Microsoft and New Era contracts are the evidence. The watchpoint is whether realized energy prices stay high enough to defend that 2027 range once the summer heat fades.
