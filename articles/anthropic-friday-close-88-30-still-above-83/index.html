Anthropic Closes Friday at $88.91, Down 8.5% on Day Two but Still Above $83 IPO Price

Anthropic shares closed their second Nasdaq session at $88.91 on Friday, November 20, 2026, down 8.5% from Thursday's $97.17 close, after dipping to $83.05, five cents above the $83 IPO price. The Nasdaq fell 5.3%, wiping out its 2026 gains, and the S&P 500 fell 3.6% for a seventh straight down day. The offering closed with the greenshoe exercised in full, about $93.5 billion. WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic shares closed their second Nasdaq session at $88.91 on Friday, November 20, 2026, down $8.26, or 8.5%, from Thursday's $97.17 close, according to Finnhub's session table. The stock opened at $89.86, touched $92.56, and fell as low as $83.05, five cents above the $83 IPO price, before recovering into the close. The Associated Press reported the shares at $85.16 around midday and Reuters at about $85 at 12:20 p.m. ET, so the close sits about $4 above the midday level.

The slide came on the worst day of a brutal week for the AI trade. CNN reported the Nasdaq Composite down 5.3% on Friday, wiping out its gains for 2026 and leaving it down almost 2% for the year, while the S&P 500 fell 3.6% for a seventh straight down day, its longest losing streak since February 2020. CNN also reported the S&P 500 and the Dow both dipping into correction territory, down 10% from their peaks, and the VIX jumping to 33, its highest level in 19 months. Nvidia fell 20% on the week, its worst week since November 2018, and has shed $2.5 trillion in market value since its October 6 peak.

The offering itself closed on Friday. Anthropic announced the underwriters had exercised the greenshoe in full, taking the deal to 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83 for about $93.5 billion in gross proceeds, about $91.6 billion of it to the company, plus a $26 billion private placement of Class N shares to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC Partners, as covered in flash 440. From the $111.50 debut open to Friday's $88.91 close, the stock has given back about 20% in two sessions, and the $83 offer price is now the line the market has to defend.

WHY IT MATTERSThe $83 line held, by five cents

The low of $83.05 is the most important number of the session. A break below $83 in the first week would have turned the largest IPO on record into a broken deal, and the market tested the line and, for now, did not break it. The close at $88.91 keeps buyers from the offering in profit by 7.1%, but the 17% first-day gain has shrunk to 7.1% in a single session.

The level matters beyond Anthropic. Index-tracking funds are expected to buy as much as 30% of the company's stock by year-end, according to analytics firm Intropic, cited by the New York Post. That bid is the next test: if it arrives while the broad selloff is still running, $83 may hold; if the selloff deepens first, the line gets tested again.

WHAT TO WATCHThe selloff, not the stock

The story is no longer Anthropic-specific. The Nasdaq gave back its entire 2026 gain, the S&P 500 is on its longest losing streak since 2020, and the Wall Street Journal reported the Nasdaq's members lost more than $4 trillion in market value this week. Every member of the Magnificent Seven closed lower on Friday, and neoclouds and chip makers took double-digit losses.

Watch whether ANT holds above $83 on Monday, whether the broader AI selloff finds a floor after a week that erased the year's Nasdaq gains, and Anthropic's first quarterly report as a public company, which will be the first test of whether the $33.24 billion nine-month revenue line is catching the compute bill.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe pop is mostly gone; the line held

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the Friday close says the debut's excitement has washed out in two sessions, but the deal has not broken. At $88.91 the stock is still above the $83 price the deal was struck at, and the $83.05 low shows the market tested that line and stopped five cents short of it. The bigger signal is the tape around it: a Nasdaq that gave back its year, a VIX at 33, and a $4 trillion weekly wipeout say this is a market repricing the AI trade, not a verdict on one lab. The first earnings call will decide whether $88.91 was a floor or a pause.
