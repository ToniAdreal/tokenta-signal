Siemens Closes Fiscal 2026 With Record €34.8 Billion in Orders as AI Data Centers Drive Demand

Siemens reported fourth-quarter orders of 34.77 billion euros, up 58% year on year to a quarterly record, with industrial profit up 22% to a record 3.89 billion euros, beating the 3.71 billion euro consensus, and revenue up 11% to 23.68 billion euros. Full-year orders hit a record 108.2 billion euros and the backlog reached 143 billion euros. Siemens raised its mid-term growth target to 7% to 11% a year and guided fiscal 2027 revenue growth of 9% to 11%, while planning a spin-off of 34.1% of Siemens Healthineers.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Siemens reported fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 results on November 12, 2026, with orders of 34.77 billion euros, up 58% year on year to a quarterly record, revenue of 23.68 billion euros, up 11% and ahead of the 23.39 billion euros forecast, and industrial profit of 3.89 billion euros, up 22% to a record, beating the 3.71 billion euro company-gathered consensus. Net income for the quarter was 2.43 billion euros, and the industrial margin widened to 16.8% from 15.3% a year earlier.

Two engines drove the order surge. Mobility's order intake more than quadrupled to 10.7 billion euros on large rail contracts, including a 2.4 billion euro high-speed train order in Germany. Smart Infrastructure posted all-time quarterly highs, with orders up 40% to 8.7 billion euros, revenue up 16% to 7.2 billion euros and profit up 26% to 1.5 billion euros at a 20.3% margin, on numerous larger data-center contracts, predominantly in the United States and Europe. Digital Industries lifted fourth-quarter profit 50% to 1.2 billion euros at a 20.9% margin, with software revenue up 13% to 2.1 billion euros.

For the full fiscal year, orders rose 25% on a comparable basis to a record 108.2 billion euros and comparable revenue grew 8% to 83.4 billion euros, with the order backlog reaching a record 143 billion euros and a book-to-bill ratio of 1.30. Industrial profit rose 13% to a record 13.3 billion euros at a 16.3% margin, and free cash flow hit a record 12.1 billion euros. Net income fell 9% to 9.5 billion euros because fiscal 2025 had included a 2.1 billion euro gain on the sale of Innomotics; earnings per share before purchase price allocation of 11.76 euros beat the 11.20 to 11.50 euro range Siemens set after its third quarter. The boards will propose a dividend of 5.50 euros per share, up from 5.35 euros.

Siemens raised its mid-term target to comparable revenue growth of 7% to 11% a year, up from 6% to 9%, with double-digit growth in earnings per share before purchase price allocation. For fiscal 2027 it expects comparable revenue growth of 9% to 11% and a book-to-bill ratio above one, with earnings per share before purchase price allocation of 10.20 to 10.80 euros, against 9.27 euros in fiscal 2026 excluding Siemens Healthineers. Siemens plans to transfer 34.1% of its stake in Siemens Healthineers to its own shareholders in a direct spin-off during fiscal 2027, at one Healthineers share for every two Siemens shares, and then deconsolidate the medical technology company; the fiscal 2027 guidance excludes Healthineers. Chief Executive Roland Busch said industrial AI is an exceptional growth opportunity and Siemens is built to win in this space.

WHY IT MATTERSThe order book is the story, not the quarter's profit

Orders of 34.77 billion euros against revenue of 23.68 billion euros put the quarter's book-to-bill at 1.47, and the 143 billion euro backlog now covers well over a year of sales. That is the durable part of the release: Siemens is booking the physical layer of the AI buildout, electrical distribution, switchgear, building controls and grid equipment for data centers, plus trains, and the demand is arriving faster than the company can ship.

The mix also explains why the profit beat is not a one-off. Smart Infrastructure's 20.3% margin and Digital Industries' 20.9% margin both improved year on year, and the full-year industrial margin widened to 16.3% from 15.4%. The quarter's profit was helped by U.S. tariff refunds, which will not repeat, but the order intake is a forward indicator that does not depend on them.

WHAT TO WATCHThe Healthineers spin-off redraws the guidance

Fiscal 2027 guidance excludes Healthineers, so the 9% to 11% revenue growth and 10.20 to 10.80 euro earnings range describe a smaller, more industrial Siemens. The segment guides set the bar: Smart Infrastructure at 11% to 13% growth with a 19.5% to 20.5% margin, Digital Industries at 8% to 12% with a 20% to 22% margin, and Mobility at 6% to 8% with an 8% to 10% margin.

The question from here is whether the data-center order flow sustains. Smart Infrastructure's full-year orders already rose 34% on a comparable basis to 31.4 billion euros, and the backlog gives Siemens time, but the segment is now the swing factor for the whole group. If data-center electrification orders cool, the raised mid-term target of 7% to 11% annual growth becomes the number to hold management to.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISSiemens is selling the physical layer of the AI buildout, and the order book proves it

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Siemens' quarter is a demand story, not a cost story. A 1.47 book-to-bill and a 143 billion euro backlog mean the company is booking work faster than it can deliver it, and the drivers, data-center electrification and rail, are multi-year programs rather than one-quarter orders. The Healthineers spin-off sharpens the picture: the Siemens that remains is an industrial AI infrastructure play, and the raised 7% to 11% mid-term growth target is the commitment to judge it by. The caveat is concentration: Smart Infrastructure is now the swing factor, so any cooling in data-center buildout would show up here first.
