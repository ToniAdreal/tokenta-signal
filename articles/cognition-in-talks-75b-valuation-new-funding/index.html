Cognition in Talks for New Funding at a $75 Billion Valuation

Cognition AI is in talks for a new funding round at a $75 billion valuation, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, November 20, 2026, with the company considering raising $2 billion or more. The valuation would be about 56% above the $48 billion of its September Series E, and sits inside the $70 billion to $80 billion range of investor offers Business Insider reported in October. No round has been announced and the talks could still change.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Cognition AI, the maker of the Devin coding agent, is in funding talks at a $75 billion valuation, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, November 20, 2026, citing people familiar with the matter. Reuters carried the report the same day. The company is considering raising $2 billion or more in the round, and the talks are ongoing, with details still subject to change.

The new figure would mark a steep step up from Cognition's last round. About two months earlier, the company raised $2 billion in a Series E at a $48 billion valuation, announced on September 8, 2026. A $75 billion valuation is about 56% above that mark.

The $75 billion figure did not come out of nowhere. Business Insider reported on October 28 that Cognition had received investor offers valuing it at $70 billion to $80 billion and was seriously considering a round before the end of the year, with talks then still early. Bloomberg's reported $75 billion sits inside that range.

No round has been announced, and no lead investor or final terms have been reported. Cognition has not publicly confirmed the talks. The September round made Cognition one of the most valuable private AI coding companies; a $75 billion valuation would extend that lead.

WHY IT MATTERSThe step-up is the signal, and it was pre-announced by the market

A 56% valuation step-up in two months, without a new product launch in between, is a bet on the coding-agent market itself rather than on any single release. Investors are pricing Devin's position in enterprise coding workflows and the pace at which AI coding tools are becoming infrastructure.

The October offers matter as much as the November number. When Business Insider reported the $70 billion to $80 billion range on October 28, the talks were described as early and the targets as changeable. Bloomberg's $75 billion, landing inside that range three weeks later, reads as those early offers hardening into a round rather than as a new price discovery.

WHAT TO WATCHWhat would confirm the round, and what would change the read

Watch first for a company announcement or a second wire confirming terms: the size of the raise, the lead investor and whether the $75 billion valuation holds. Bloomberg's own reporting stresses the talks are ongoing and details could change.

Watch second for what the money is for. Cognition's September raise was followed by product expansion, including Devin on macOS infrastructure; a second $2 billion-plus raise in three months would test whether the company is buying growth, talent or compute capacity at a moment when every AI lab is competing for the same resources.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe coding-agent repricing is happening in public

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the Cognition number is less interesting as a valuation than as a sequence. In September, $48 billion. In October, offers at $70 billion to $80 billion. In November, talks at $75 billion. Each step was reported while the talks were still open, which means the market is watching the repricing of AI coding agents happen in real time rather than learning about it after a close. The claim to discount is the valuation itself until a round closes, because talks at this stage can still move or fall apart. The claim to test is whether a second $2 billion raise in three months buys Cognition durable distance from its rivals, or simply buys the same engineers and compute everyone else is bidding for.
