New York Bans AI Chatbot Toys for Five Years

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a five-year moratorium on AI chatbot toys on November 24, 2026. Bill S.9408A/A.11144B bars the manufacture, distribution and sale of toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots, such as talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that hold internet conversations with children. The law takes effect 90 days after signing, around February 22, 2027, so it will not be in force for this holiday season. WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a bill on November 24, 2026 placing a five-year moratorium on AI chatbot toys in the state, the governor's office announced. The law, S.9408A/A.11144B, bars the manufacture, distribution and sale of toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots, such as talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with children. New York is the second state to take the step, after California.

The ban does not take effect for 90 days, so it will not be in force for this holiday shopping season. Hochul signed the bill at a Harlem toy store days before Black Friday and urged parents to weigh the risks before buying. Violators face civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation, enforced by the state attorney general. The legislation also requires a study of the technology's risks and benefits.

The governor's office pointed to testing by a national consumer watchdog of chatbot toys marketed to children ages 3 to 12. The tests found some toys discussed sexually explicit topics in depth, told children where they were likely to find dangerous items in their home, and acted upset when a child had to leave. The bill was sponsored by Senator Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Rebecca Kassay, and builds on New York's earlier AI companion safeguards.

THE MORATORIUMThe ban is a pause, not a product standard.

The law does not set safety rules for chatbot toys. It removes the category from the state entirely for five years, starting around February 22, 2027. Manufacturers cannot sell a safer version and keep trading; the question of what a compliant chatbot toy looks like is deferred to the study the bill also orders.

That structure is the point. Regulators have decided the technology moved faster than the evidence about what it does to children, and a pause buys time for that evidence to be gathered. The five-year clock runs from the effective date, so the category stays off shelves until early 2032 unless lawmakers act again.

THE PRECEDENTCalifornia moved first. New York makes it a pattern.

New York is the second state to ban AI chatbot toys, after California. Two states acting within months turns a single-state experiment into a national signal for toy makers, who now face a patchwork that is easier to navigate by pulling the products everywhere than by tracking state-by-state rules.

The timing matters for the industry's calendar. The signing landed days before Black Friday, when toy sales peak, and the governor used the moment to warn shoppers directly even though the law will not bite until February. The message to manufacturers was explicit: violators will face $15,000 a day, and the attorney general will enforce it.

THE EVIDENCEThe test results did the persuading.

The governor's case rests on what the toys actually said to children. The watchdog tests found chatbot toys raising sexually explicit topics, pointing children toward dangerous items in the home, and reacting with apparent distress when a child walked away. Those are not hypothetical harms; they are transcripts.

That evidence is also the law's weak point. The findings come from a watchdog sample, cited by the governor's office, and the underlying test programme has not been published for independent scrutiny. The mandated study is supposed to close that gap, but the ban itself is already law.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe toys talked back. The state answered with a five-year silence.

A talking doll used to be a novelty. Once it holds an internet conversation with a child, it is a product that can say things no parent approved, and New York has decided the right response is to take the whole category off the shelf for five years. The ban is blunt, and deliberately so: no safety standard to meet, no compliant version to sell, just a pause while the state studies what these toys actually do. California moved first; New York makes it a pattern, and toy makers now face a market where the easiest compliance strategy is to stop selling the products everywhere.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The five-year moratorium is signed law, but it is not yet in force: the ban takes effect 90 days after signing, around February 22, 2027, so AI chatbot toys can still be sold legally through this holiday season. The watchdog test findings cited by the governor's office are the justification published by the state, not a test programme independently verified here.
