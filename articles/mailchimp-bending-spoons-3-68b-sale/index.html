WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On November 19, 2026, Bending Spoons announced a definitive agreement with Intuit to acquire Mailchimp for $3.68 billion. Intuit bought the email-marketing company in 2021 for about $12 billion; five years later, the sale price is barely 30% of that.

The deal is $3.31 billion in cash plus $368 million in Bending Spoons stock. The target is all equity of The Rocket Science Group, the Mailchimp operating entity. The transaction is expected to close in early 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

The buyer is Milan-based Bending Spoons, which in the past year has also bought AOL, Eventbrite, Airtable and Miro. CEO Luca Ferrari said the company will invest heavily in the business after closing. Intuit said the sale lets it focus on AI tools for small businesses, with proceeds going to share buybacks.

WHY IT MATTERSThe Math of Selling at 70% Off

Bought for $12 billion, sold for $3.68 billion: the math here is damage control. The email-marketing growth story has stopped working, and Intuit would rather exit at a discount than keep money and attention on AI tools.

For Intuit, Mailchimp is no longer a growth engine but a mature business that needs sustained investment. Selling it amounts to admitting that the 2021 price carried too much optimism about synergies.

WHAT TO WATCHThe Buyer's Collection Logic

Bending Spoons' playbook is to buy mature software in sequence and make money by operating it more efficiently. After Evernote, Vimeo and Meetup, Mailchimp is the latest piece on its shelf.

Mailchimp's users and brand are still there; the value is in the installed base. The buyer is betting that cash flow from a mature product is worth more than a growth story, and the next question is how it renovates the old tool.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM PREMIUM BUY TO DISCOUNT SALE
From premium buy to discount sale
The 2021 premium purchase was built on growth expectations; five years later the expectations are gone and the price is back to 30%. The bill for an acquisition is ultimately paid by the business itself.
FROM GROWTH STORY TO OPERATING BUSINESS
From growth story to operating business
Mailchimp has gone from a growth asset to an operating asset. The buyer's game is not to tell another growth story, but to run a mature product's cash flow well.
