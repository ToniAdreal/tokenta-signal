Zeta Global's 21st Straight Beat-and-Raise: Revenue Up 46% as GAAP Profit Turns Positive

Zeta Global reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $492.7 million, up 46.1 percent year over year and ahead of the $471.2 million consensus, with GAAP net income of $38 million, or $0.14 per diluted share, its first positive GAAP profit on a year-to-date and trailing-twelve-month basis. Adjusted EBITDA reached $121 million at a 24.6 percent margin, and the company raised full-year revenue guidance by $37 million to $1,855 million at the midpoint, its 21st consecutive beat-and-raise quarter.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Zeta Global reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $492.7 million on November 9, 2026, up 46.1 percent from a year earlier and ahead of the $471.2 million consensus, a 4.6 percent beat. GAAP net income reached $38 million, or $0.14 per diluted share, against a $0.05 consensus, and the company achieved positive GAAP net income on a year-to-date basis and over the last twelve months for the first time. Non-GAAP net income was $103 million, or $0.38 per diluted share, and adjusted EBITDA rose to $121 million, a 24.6 percent margin, up 140 basis points year over year. Combined with 46 percent growth, that margin gives the Rule of 71 the company cites.

The growth is increasingly organic. Revenue excluding the Marigold acquisition and political candidate revenue rose 30 percent year over year, accelerating from 28 percent last quarter, and billings reached $498.9 million, up 47.9 percent. Chief executive David A. Steinberg credited rising adoption of Athena, the company's AI platform, and chief financial officer Chris Greiner said adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow are growing faster than revenue. For the fourth quarter, Zeta guided revenue of $521 million to $525 million, and for the full year it raised revenue guidance by $37 million to $1,853 million to $1,857 million, growth of 42 percent, while lifting full-year GAAP earnings per share guidance to $0.22 to $0.24 from $0.09 to $0.11.

The honest caveat sits in what the profit costs. Stock-based compensation was $50 million in the quarter and $155 million year to date, more than the GAAP net income the quarter produced, so the first GAAP profit arrives while equity pay still exceeds it. A federal court also denied the motion to dismiss an ongoing securities class action on July 8, 2026, moving the case to discovery. The streak itself is the headline: this is Zeta's 21st consecutive quarter of beating and raising guidance.

WHY IT MATTERSThe first GAAP profit is the milestone

Zeta has grown fast for years while losing money on a GAAP basis, so the turn to positive GAAP net income, year to date and over the trailing twelve months, is the number that changes how the story is read. It is not a one-quarter artifact: the fourth-quarter guide implies 32 to 33 percent growth, and the full-year guide now points to 42 percent growth with a 22.3 to 22.4 percent adjusted EBITDA margin. A company growing above 40 percent while expanding margins is doing something the market rarely sees at this scale.

The 21-quarter beat-and-raise streak matters because it is the mechanism behind the print. Guidance raised every quarter for five years means the bar keeps moving up and the company keeps clearing it. The streak is also the risk: a single miss would break a narrative the valuation leans on, and the guide for the fourth quarter, while raised, implies growth easing from the 46 percent just printed.

WHY IT MATTERSGrowth is real, but check what it costs

Strip out the acquisition contribution and the picture stays strong: 30 percent organic growth excluding Marigold and political revenue, and 27 percent guided for the full year on the same basis. That is the defense against the claim that Zeta is buying growth. Athena adoption, the AI platform the company says is driving the acceleration, is the engine the bull case rests on, and the CFO's point that adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow are growing faster than revenue supports it.

The cost side deserves the same attention. Stock-based compensation of $155 million year to date exceeds GAAP net income, so shareholders are paying for the growth in dilution as well as in cash. The unresolved securities class action, now in discovery after the July dismissal denial, is a second overhang. Neither invalidates the quarter, but both belong in any honest read of a first GAAP profit that still leans on equity pay.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA genuine inflection, with the cost of growth still on the bill

Tokenta Signal's judgment: This is the strongest print in Zeta's history as a public company, and the first GAAP profit is the number that matters, not the revenue beat. Forty-six percent growth with a 24.6 percent adjusted EBITDA margin is a combination few companies sustain, and the 30 percent organic growth rate says the acceleration is not bought. The honest caveats are the cost of that growth: stock-based compensation of $155 million year to date still exceeds GAAP net income, and a securities class action is now in discovery. The 21-quarter streak is both the proof and the pressure, because the next quarter has to clear a bar the company keeps raising itself. For now, the evidence points to a real inflection, with dilution as the price of admission.
