Rockwell Buys Austria's AGILOX; Mobile Robotics More Than Doubles

This round of industrial robotics M&A is not buying capacity but the ticket into the factory. Whoever gets handling robots onto customers' warehouse floors first takes the next round of automation orders.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On November 18, 2026, Rockwell Automation signed a definitive agreement to acquire Austrian mobile robot maker AGILOX. The price was not disclosed, and the deal is expected to close in the second quarter of fiscal 2027, January to March 2027, subject to regulatory approval. The seller is private equity firm Carlyle, which took a majority stake in 2021 and saw AGILOX revenue quadruple during its hold.

AGILOX makes forklift-style autonomous mobile robots that move pallets and heavy loads around factories and warehouses without fixed guide tracks. Its robots run on in-house AI and swarm networking, coordinating fleets without a central scheduler. More than 2,500 units are deployed worldwide, with over 400 customers across 35 countries and more than 40 partners.

Rockwell's own mobile robotics base is OTTO, a flatbed line. Together, forklift plus flatbed, Rockwell says its mobile robotics business will more than double in scale. AGILOX joins the Intelligent Devices division. In 2023, Rockwell paid more than $600 million for Clearpath and OTTO; this is the second deal on the same line.

WHAT TO WATCHWhat is being bought is not capacity but the ticket into the factory

AGILOX robots are already inside the warehouses of automotive, consumer goods, pharmaceutical, and logistics customers. Buying it hands Rockwell direct access to more than 400 customers' factory floors, onto which it can layer its own control systems and software.

Rivals are not standing still. In the same week, OMRON began taking orders for the LD-150 and LD-300, with payloads up to 300 kilograms, 2.5 times the previous generation. This round of factory robotics is a contest over who lifts heavier loads first.

WHY IT MATTERSA signed agreement is not a closed deal

A definitive agreement is only the first step; regulatory approval and closing conditions are still ahead. Carlyle's own completion window is the first half of 2027, while Rockwell's is the second fiscal quarter, and both framings leave slack.

With the price undisclosed, no valuation can be calculated. What can be counted is scale: more than 2,500 robots in service and more than 400 customers, the only public measure of this deal's size.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM ONE ROBOT TO A FULL FLEET
From a single robot to a full handling solution
Rockwell used to sell the OTTO flatbed base alone; with AGILOX's forklift-style robots added, the handling solution goes from one flatbed piece of the puzzle to a full set. Customers no longer need to piece together two suppliers.
FROM NORTH AMERICA TO EUROPE TOO
From the North American market to a European foothold as well
OTTO's base is in North America; AGILOX's base is in Europe. With this deal, Rockwell's mobile robotics has customers and channels on both sides at once.
