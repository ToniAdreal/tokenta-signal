A $1 Billion Series A for a Lab That Wants AI to Do the Research Itself

The money buys an experiment result that has yet to be independently verified. No valuation was disclosed, and the model has no third-party evaluation; the bet is that agents doing research is a road that leads somewhere.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On November 12, Mirendil announced a $1 billion Series A, co-led by Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake Capital, with Battery Ventures, Doerr Capital, Gil & Co, Google, M13, Pear VC and S32 among the participants. The company did not disclose a valuation for the round.

The company only emerged publicly on June 24, with a $200 million seed round at a valuation of about $1 billion, led by Andreessen Horowitz and Kleiner Perkins, with Nvidia joining later. The founding team of about 20 came from Anthropic, xAI, Google DeepMind and OpenAI; chief executive Behnam Neyshabur and chief technology officer Harsh Mehta are both Anthropic alumni.

An October 21 research disclosure set up this round directly. One researcher directing agents pre-trained the coding model Mirendil-Base from scratch in three and a half months, using under $10 million of compute, with agents writing more than 99% of the code. The company then post-trained the base model into Fern, with context reaching 10 million tokens. Lor, launched on October 28 and still in invite-only testing, lets agents write code, run experiments and request compute on their own, for days at a time.

WHAT TO WATCHThe $10 Million Figure Covers Only the Smallest Model

The under-$10-million compute figure covers only Mirendil-Base, at 100 billion to 500 billion parameters. The two larger versions were still training when the October 21 disclosure landed, and their costs were not disclosed.

Agents writing 99% of the code is not the same as a model growing on its own. The company itself states that an experienced researcher directed the work throughout, judging which results could be trusted. Autonomous recursive improvement, the company says, has not been achieved.

WHY IT MATTERSNo Valuation Was Disclosed; $5 Billion Is Only a Rumored Figure

Bloomberg reported in September that the company was discussing a raise at a $5 billion valuation, and the Wall Street Journal cited people familiar with the matter as saying this round was priced at $5 billion. The company's announcement confirmed neither, and no figure should be treated as a price.

The $1 billion Series A is the largest US financing of the week. The size says one thing: investors are willing to pay early for the proposition that agents can do research, whether or not the evidence has arrived.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM TEAM RESEARCH TO ONE PERSON PLUS AGENTS
From a team doing research to one person plus agents doing research
What the company is selling is not a model but a change in how research is done. One person directing hundreds of agents does the work of what used to be a team; Lor is that workflow turned into a product.
FROM MODEL CAPABILITY TO RESEARCH COST
From model capability to research cost
The case for this round rests not on a benchmark but on a bill. Three and a half months and under $10 million — if it can be repeated, the barrier to frontier research is no longer the compute budget.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSIS$1 Billion Buys an Experiment, Not a Product

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Mirendil's Series A is a classic conviction bet — 141 days from seed to Series A, with the money arriving faster than the evidence. If the claim of training a model in three and a half months holds up, the cost structure of AI research gets rewritten; if it is only a lab-condition anecdote, $1 billion becomes the most expensive optimism of this cycle. No valuation has been disclosed and no third-party evaluation exists; the real test lies beyond Lor's invite-only list.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

Mirendil-Base's training cost, the share of code written by agents, and Fern's context length are all company claims, none independently verified. No valuation for this round was disclosed; the $5 billion figure is a rumored number cited by the press. This does not constitute investment advice.
