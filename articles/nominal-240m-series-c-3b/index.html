Nominal Triples Valuation to $3B in Eight Months

Nominal announced a $240 million Series C on November 9 at a $3 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed with Insight as co-lead. At the Series B extension in March the valuation was $1 billion, a threefold rise in eight months. The platform serves aerospace and defense hardware testing, with more than 80 customers.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Nominal builds the AI layer for hardware testing. Aircraft, satellites, nuclear reactors, and factory equipment generate vast test data, and the platform ties sensor readings, telemetry, and flight logs to engineering context, helping engineers find anomalies and compare results.

The customer list underpins the valuation. More than 80 commercial and government customers use the platform, including defense contractor Anduril. In October, the Air Force selected Nominal for a $71.2 million STRATFI investment to support collaborative combat aircraft capability development.

The founders come from the front line. CEO Cameron McCord is a former U.S. Navy submarine officer and an Anduril alumnus. The company is based in Austin, is four years old, and has raised more than $395 million in total.

3× IN 8 MONTHSThreefold in Eight Months

In March, Nominal announced a $155 million Series B extension at a $1 billion valuation, led by Founders Fund's Trae Stephens. That round was a preemptive deal initiated by the investors.

The November 9 Series C pushed the valuation to $3 billion. Lightspeed took over as lead, Insight co-led, and Sequoia, General Catalyst, Lux Capital, and Founders Fund joined in.

CONTRACTS BEFORE VALUATIONDefense Orders First, Valuation Follows

The STRATFI program requires matching funds beyond the Air Force, so winning it amounts to passing the government's commercialization review. At $71.2 million it is not the largest figure, but it carries the hardest endorsement.

Lockheed Martin Ventures joined this round as well. With Anduril as a customer and Lockheed Martin as an investor, Nominal has worked its way into the core of the U.S. defense supply chain.

SOFTWARE, NOT HARDWARESelling Software, Not Hardware

Nominal builds no aircraft and no chips. It sells software that connects test data to engineering judgment, hardware-neutral, so any company that tests physical products is a potential customer.

The company's stated expansion directions are energy, robotics, and advanced manufacturing. Testing in those industries is equally data-heavy and tool-fragmented, and replicating the defense playbook there is the next thing to prove.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM TEST DATA TO INTELLIGENCE
From testing tool to engineering intelligence platform
Nominal's positioning moves from organizing test data to using AI to answer engineers' questions, with test results traceable to every measurement.
FROM ROUND TO ENDORSEMENT
From a funding round to a defense endorsement
In the same period the valuation tripled, the Air Force contract and Lockheed Martin's investment landed one after another, capital and orders confirming each other.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISValuation Is the Effect, Orders Are the Cause

Tokenta Signal's verdict: $3 billion is not a market mirage, it is a price built on an Air Force contract and more than 80 customers. The risk is equally visible: the valuation is running ahead of revenue, and the next round will need hard numbers on how much engineering time the AI layer actually saves, or the threefold gain will not hold.
