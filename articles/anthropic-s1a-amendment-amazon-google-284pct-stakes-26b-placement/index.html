Anthropic's Final Filing Before Pricing: Amazon and Google to Own 28.4%

Anthropic filed Amendment No. 3 to its S-1 on November 17, 2026, the final filing before pricing expected November 18. Amazon will own 14.7% and Google 13.7% of the company after the IPO and a concurrent $26 billion private placement, a combined 28.4%. The placement is led by Google and Nvidia at $10 billion each. The $70 to $78 range for 825 million shares is unchanged, Nasdaq has approved the ANT listing, and net proceeds are estimated at $84.6 billion at the $74 midpoint.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic filed the final amendment to its IPO prospectus on November 17, 2026, one day before the offering is expected to price. Amendment No. 3 to the Form S-1 keeps the deal terms unchanged, 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, and confirms Nasdaq has approved the listing under the ticker ANT, with trading expected to begin on November 19. The filing's new disclosure is who will own the company once it is public.

After the IPO and a concurrent $26 billion private placement, Amazon will hold 14.7% of Anthropic's outstanding capital stock and Google 13.7%, a combined 28.4%. Amazon's stake is about 1.92 billion Class N shares and Google's about 1.79 billion, each roughly a third of that non-voting share class. Amazon could reach 23.8% if its convertible notes converted in full, but the filing says Amazon has elected not to waive the ownership cap that stops that happening at the IPO.

The private placement is where the supplier overlap becomes explicit. Seven chip and cloud companies agreed to buy $26 billion of Class N shares at the IPO price: Google $10 billion, Nvidia $10 billion, AMD $2.5 billion, and Broadcom, Micron and Microsoft $1 billion each, with TSMC at $500 million. The placement closes straight after the listing and is contingent on the offering. At the $74 midpoint, Anthropic estimates net proceeds of $84.6 billion from the IPO and placement combined, or $93.7 billion if the underwriters' option to buy up to 123.75 million more shares is exercised in full.

The filing also records a roughly $3 billion stock-compensation charge tied to a vesting date that moved by one day. Behind the numbers, the prospectus shows the scale of the bet the money must carry: Anthropic generated $33.24 billion of revenue in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, with an annual revenue run rate of $81 billion at October 31, against more than $900 billion of spending commitments, about $71 billion of which falls due in the year through September 30, 2027. Its minimum commitments include $110 billion to Amazon between May 2026 and April 2036 and about $121.3 billion to Google between April 2026 and July 2033.

WHY IT MATTERSThe backers are also the suppliers

The 28.4% figure is the story of this filing, not the unchanged price range. Amazon and Google are not passive shareholders: they are Anthropic's largest cloud and chip suppliers, its distribution partners through their marketplaces, and, as the filing itself warns, its competitors. The prospectus states the three companies simultaneously serve as investors, customers, distribution partners and competitors, and warns of conflicts that may be difficult to anticipate or mitigate.

The $26 billion placement sharpens the point. Close to three dollars in every ten of the money Anthropic raises at the top of the range comes from companies it pays for compute and chips. That is not a flaw in the deal on its own, capacity is scarce and these are the companies that have it, but it means the financing is partly circular: the suppliers are funding the customer that funds them. The outside demand is real, Bloomberg reported orders close to seven times the shares on offer by November 15, with multiple orders of $10 billion or more, and Altimeter's Brad Gerstner publicly aims to invest $5 billion. The question is how much of the book would hold if the suppliers stepped back.

WHAT TO WATCHWednesday's price is the first verdict

Books were scheduled to close at 4 p.m. ET on November 17, and pricing is expected on November 18, with the first trade on November 19. Bloomberg reported bankers guiding investors toward the top of the $70 to $78 range or above, which would value the company at about $1.4 trillion fully diluted and make the offering the second-largest IPO on record. A price above the range would confirm the demand; a price at $70 would say the book was thinner than the headlines suggested.

After pricing, the test moves from the deal to the balance sheet. About $71 billion falls due in the year through September 30, 2027, against an $81 billion revenue run rate measured at October 31, and the filing's own warning is that Anthropic has significantly underestimated its compute requirements in the past. The first quarterly filings as a public company will show whether revenue can carry those payments, and whether the supplier-funded structure holds when the market, not the filing, sets the price.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe IPO's biggest shareholders are also its biggest suppliers

Tokenta Signal's judgment: this filing turns the Anthropic IPO from a valuation story into a structure story. A combined 28.4% in the hands of Amazon and Google, plus a $26 billion placement funded by the very companies Anthropic pays for compute, means the deal's success is partly self-referential. That does not make it fragile by itself: the order book ran close to seven times covered, and outside buyers like Altimeter want in at scale. But it does mean the first public test is not the price on November 19, it is whether a company whose backers are also its suppliers and competitors can keep revenue growing faster than the roughly $71 billion that falls due in the next year. If it can, the structure looks like smart alignment. If it cannot, the circularity becomes the story.
