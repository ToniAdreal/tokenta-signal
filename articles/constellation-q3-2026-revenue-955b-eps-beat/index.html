Constellation Q3 Revenue Jumps 45% to $9.55B as Calpine Powers EPS Beat

Constellation Energy reported Q3 2026 revenue of $9.55 billion, up 45% year over year, with adjusted EPS of $5.06 beating the $3.90 consensus, and raised full-year guidance to $12.50-$12.80 as its Calpine acquisition and Amazon and Google power deals turn it into the AI buildout's key electricity supplier.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Constellation Energy reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $9.55 billion on November 9, 2026, up 45% year over year from $6.57 billion and above the $9.23 billion consensus, driven by favorable market and portfolio conditions and the addition of Calpine, which Constellation acquired in January 2026. GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 75% to $5.19 from $2.97, while adjusted operating EPS increased 66% to $5.06 from $3.04, beating the $3.90 consensus by about 30%. GAAP net income attributable to common shareholders nearly doubled to $1.835 billion from $930 million.

Operating expenses rose 29% to $7.07 billion, but revenue grew faster, so GAAP operating income more than doubled to $2.492 billion and operating margin widened to 26.1% from 16.5%. The nuclear fleet produced 46,677 GWh in the quarter, about 0.4% more than a year earlier, with the capacity factor for owned plants improving to 97.2% from 96.8%. Higher interest expense, up 62% to $217 million, and a larger diluted share count of 354 million versus 313 million tempered the per-share gain, while a lower effective tax rate of 18.2% versus 33.4% helped GAAP earnings.

The company raised the midpoint and narrowed its full-year 2026 adjusted operating EPS guidance to $12.50-$12.80, its second midpoint increase of the year, with an implied midpoint of $12.65. Constellation also disclosed it agreed to acquire the 609 MW Rhode Island State Energy Center natural gas-fired facility for $715 million, subject to approvals. Its AI-era power deals kept building: a 20-year, 690 MW agreement with Amazon at Calvert Cliffs supporting more than $3 billion of investment, a 20-year Google agreement supporting more than $4.3 billion across 11 nuclear units and about 890 MW of new capacity, a 15-year load-following agreement covering 2,700 MW, and a first Peak+ transaction covering 270 MW for 20 years from 2028. A conditional DOE commitment could guarantee up to $5 billion of 30-year financing for nuclear uprates. Shares rose 4.3% on Friday, November 6, closing at $249.95 ahead of the print.

WHY IT MATTERSCalpine turns Constellation into the power supplier of the AI buildout

The 45% revenue jump is mostly a bigger company, not a better quarter at the same company: Calpine's generation assets, added in January, are doing the heavy lifting, and the 13% rise in diluted share count shows the deal was partly paid for in stock. What matters for the AI story is what the bigger base is now selling into. Constellation's nuclear fleet plus Calpine's gas fleet is the exact product hyperscalers are buying in bulk: firm, around-the-clock power with a clean-energy label, which is why Amazon and Google have now signed multi-billion-dollar, 20-year agreements with the same supplier.

The raised guidance is the signal management is confident the demand is durable, not a one-quarter market windfall. Adjusted operating EPS guidance of $12.50 to $12.80 implies a full year roughly a third above 2025. The risk is execution: the Google uprates do not arrive until 2028, the Calvert Cliffs expansion lands in 2030 to 2032, and the DOE loan guarantee is conditional, not final. Constellation is collecting long-dated promises faster than it is delivering new megawatts.

WHY IT MATTERSThe beat is real, but the offsets are worth watching

Two numbers temper the headline beat. Net interest expense jumped 62% to $217 million, and management named unfavorable interest impacts as a partial offset, which means the larger, more leveraged post-Calpine balance sheet is now a recurring drag. The nuclear production tax credit portfolio was the other offset, leaving results exposed to policy-linked economics that management cannot control.

The other watchpoint is concentration: growth is increasingly tied to a handful of hyperscaler customers and to PJM capacity revenues. If data-center power demand pauses or capacity prices soften, the same operating leverage that widened margins to 26.1% works in reverse. For now the print answers the question the market asked before the open: whether the Calpine bet and the AI power deals are showing up in earnings. On revenue, EPS and guidance, the answer is yes.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe AI power trade is now showing up in earnings

Tokenta Signal's judgment: $9.55 billion of revenue and a $5.06 adjusted EPS print, 30% above consensus, is the first quarter where Constellation's Calpine acquisition and its hyperscaler power deals show up together as earnings rather than announcements. The raised $12.50 to $12.80 full-year guide says management believes the demand is durable. The open question is delivery: the Google uprates arrive in 2028, Calvert Cliffs in 2030 to 2032, and the $5 billion DOE guarantee is still conditional. Constellation is selling the future faster than it is building it, and the next test is whether the megawatts arrive on schedule.
