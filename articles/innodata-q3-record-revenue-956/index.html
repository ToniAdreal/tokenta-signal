Innodata's AI Data Revenue Jumps 53% to a Record $95.6M

Innodata reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $95.6 million, up 53% year over year and about 15% above consensus, its 13th straight quarter of growth, and raised full-year guidance to 42% or more while pitching a new physical AI data business; shares fell 10.8% despite the beat.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Innodata reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $95.6 million on November 5, 2026, up 53% year over year and about 15% above analyst consensus, or $12.3 million. It was the company's 13th consecutive quarter of year-over-year growth. Adjusted EBITDA was $17.7 million, 18.5% of revenue and also about 15% above consensus, while net income rose 24% to $10.3 million and fully diluted earnings per share of $0.29 beat consensus by about 70%.

The quarter also diversified the customer base. A customer that represented less than 5% of revenue a year ago became Innodata's largest customer in the quarter, giving the company two customers of comparable scale, and Innodata has already signed an additional $18 million program with that customer for the fourth quarter. It won five new programs with a large hyperscaler from which it generated about $3.1 million in the trailing twelve months, and now forecasts more than $20 million of revenue from that customer in 2027. Five new logos were added in the quarter, including model builders and an enterprise data platform.

Chief executive Rahul Singhal, in his first earnings report as CEO, raised full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance to 42% or more, up from 40% or more, even after absorbing the wind-down of a program at one large customer. Cash and equivalents stood at $164.0 million at September 30, 2026, up from $82.2 million at the end of 2025. Shares fell 10.8% in the regular session on November 5, closing at $38.62, despite the beat.

WHY IT MATTERSThe AI data supplier is now selling the next wave, not just the last one

Innodata built its growth supplying the data that trains large language models. This quarter's release is explicit that the next demand is elsewhere: physical AI. The company launched a motion capture lab in September for humanoid robot and world model training, is in pilots with leading frontier and robotics labs, and says a single successful pilot could yield about $7 million of revenue in 2027. The thesis is that robot foundation models improve with data the same way language models do, and the data supplier that fed the first wave is positioning to feed the second.

The customer diversification matters as much as the new market. A year ago the company depended on a small set of large customers; now a former sub-5% customer is its largest, and a hyperscaler relationship is scaling from $3.1 million toward more than $20 million. That breadth makes the 42%-plus growth guide more credible than a single-customer surge would be.

WHY IT MATTERSA beat that fell 10.8% is a warning about expectations, not the quarter

Innodata beat consensus on revenue by 15% and on earnings per share by about 70%, raised guidance, and still fell 10.8% in the session. The stock had already fallen 18.95% over the prior five days, and the pattern after the second quarter was similar: record results, then a decline. The market is not doubting the quarter; it is doubting the multiple on a business whose growth depends on project-based work from a handful of frontier labs.

The test ahead is the physical AI pipeline. The motion capture pilots, the $18 million fourth-quarter program and the verbal $5 million-a-year federal win, expected to be signed by year-end, are all forward-looking. If the pilots convert, the 2027 story is a second growth engine; if they stall, Innodata remains a fast-growing but concentrated supplier to the LLM labs, and the market will keep discounting it that way.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISRecord growth, a new physical AI bet, and a market that wants more

Tokenta Signal's judgment: $95.6 million, up 53%, with a raised guide and a diversifying customer base is a strong quarter by any measure, and the motion capture lab puts Innodata in front of the physical AI data wave rather than behind it. The 10.8% drop after a 15% revenue beat says the market is pricing the concentration risk and the project-based model, not the quarter. The pilots with frontier and robotics labs are now the story: one conversion at about $7 million would start a second growth engine.
