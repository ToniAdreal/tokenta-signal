Chip Design Software Starts Running Its Own Models

On October 26 Cadence launched Maestro, a chip-design AI model suite based on Nvidia Nemotron that can run inside customer firewalls, a head-on fork from Synopsys' OpenAI-hosted route.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 26, Cadence launched the Maestro model suite: a set of AI models tuned for chip design, partly based on Nvidia's open-weight Nemotron models and hardened with Nvidia's OpenShell security technology.

Maestro's selling point is not just the models but deployment choice: customers can run them on Cadence's own cloud, on any public cloud, or entirely inside their own firewalls and data centres. Chip design files never have to leave the building.

The early results are company-reported: up to 30% better quality than general-purpose models and up to 5x token efficiency. On launch day, Cadence shares rose 3.9%.

THE FORKThe Fork with Synopsys Is the Real Story Here

A month earlier, Synopsys had announced a partnership with OpenAI to develop GPT-Synopsys, with models running on OpenAI-hosted infrastructure and revenue shared with OpenAI. Cadence's Maestro runs exactly the other way: the models can be taken home.

The two routes rest on two trust assumptions: trust cloud hosting, or trust the firewall. Chip design files are a semiconductor company's most core asset, and deployment location is itself a product feature.

THE AGNOSTICModel-Agnostic Is Cadence's Defensive Wall

A remark by Cadence senior vice president Paul Cunningham is worth recording: there are more AI model vendors than chip design software vendors. Maestro is positioned as model-agnostic: frontier models, open weights and specialised models can be mixed, routed and combined.

The implication is that Cadence does not want to be locked in by any single model vendor, and does not want its customers locked in either. The platform's value sits in the orchestration and evaluation loop, not in any one model.

THE CAVEATSelf-Reported Numbers Need Customers' Own Verification

The 30% and 5x figures come from Cadence's early testing, with no reproducible test set and no public definition of quality. Analysts have already noted that, depending on the token mix, the total cost reduction can differ completely.

The first models go only to selected early customers, with delivery starting in the fourth quarter of 2026. The real verification will not be in the launch release but in customers' own design flows.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISOwnership of Chip Design Files Is Becoming a Product Feature

The fork between Maestro and GPT-Synopsys is not about model capability but about where design files live. Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: in the next round of AI competition in EDA, the deciding factor is deployment trust, not parameter scale.
