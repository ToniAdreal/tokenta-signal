Dell Revenue More Than Doubles on AI Servers, Backlog Hits a Record $132.3 Billion

Dell Technologies reported record fiscal third-quarter 2027 revenue of $54.94 billion, up 103% year over year and well above the $49.32 billion analysts expected, with adjusted earnings of $9.45 a share against estimates of $6.59. The company closed the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI server backlog after $60.2 billion in new orders, and raised its full-year revenue forecast to $202 billion and its AI server revenue target to $78 billion. Shares rose in extended trading.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Dell Technologies reported fiscal third-quarter 2027 results after the market closed on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, and the numbers doubled the company in a year. Revenue reached $54.94 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and well above the $49.32 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings were $9.45 a share, against estimates of $6.59. Net income on a GAAP basis was $5.67 billion, or $8.82 a diluted share, up from $1.55 billion, or $2.28 a share, a year earlier.

The growth came from the infrastructure business. Revenue at the Infrastructure Solutions Group, which houses Dell's servers and storage, nearly tripled to $41.97 billion in the quarter. The PC unit grew far more slowly, with revenue up about 3% to $12.89 billion, down from 20% growth in the previous three months. Dell closed the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI server backlog after booking $60.2 billion in new AI orders during the three months ended October 30.

Dell raised its outlook for the third time this year. For fiscal 2027 the company now expects revenue of $202 billion, up from a prior target of $192 billion and above Wall Street expectations of $194.48 billion, with adjusted earnings of $29.68 a share, up from $25.50. It also raised its forecast for AI-optimized server revenue to $78 billion from $74 billion. For the current fourth quarter Dell forecast revenue of $56.3 billion and adjusted earnings of $8.40 a share, both above analyst estimates.

Shares rose in extended trading after the release. The quarter makes Dell the latest hardware supplier to show that the AI buildout is still converting into orders faster than the skeptics expected, even as the market keeps asking how much of that demand turns into profit. Dell returned a record $4.9 billion to shareholders in the quarter, and chief financial officer David Kennedy said the team "executed exceptionally well, driving record revenue of $54.9 billion, record EPS and a record $4.9 billion returned to shareholders."

WHY IT MATTERSThe beat is real, and it is concentrated in one business.

Dell more than doubled its revenue in a single year, and almost all of the growth sits in servers and storage. ISG revenue nearly tripled to $41.97 billion while the PC business grew 3%, which means the quarter is a read on enterprise AI infrastructure spending, not on a broad computing recovery. The $132.3 billion AI backlog, after $60.2 billion of new orders in one quarter, says customers are still committing to capacity at a pace that keeps breaking Dell's own forecasts.

That is why the guidance matters more than the beat. Dell has now raised its annual revenue forecast three times this year, to $202 billion from $192 billion, and lifted its AI server revenue target to $78 billion. The company is telling the market the order book is not a one-quarter spike. The open question is the one Hewlett Packard Enterprise answered badly the day before: how much margin survives when AI servers become a bigger share of sales.

THE WATCHPOINTBacklog is the number to watch next quarter.

A record backlog is only as good as its conversion. Dell's fourth-quarter guidance, $56.3 billion in revenue and $8.40 in adjusted earnings, both above estimates, sets the bar for turning those $60.2 billion of new orders into shipped machines. Memory shortages and component costs are already squeezing server margins across the industry, and HPE's shares fell about 12% on Tuesday after it warned on exactly that pressure.

The next data points are Dell's fourth-quarter report in February 2027 and, before that, whether the backlog keeps growing or starts to plateau as customers digest the capacity they have already ordered. If the backlog holds near $132 billion while margins stay intact, the AI infrastructure cycle still has room to run. If orders slow and margins compress, Dell's third raise of the year will look like the peak.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM BEAT TO THIRD RAISE
From beating estimates to raising the bar three times in one year
The $54.94 billion quarter beat the $49.32 billion consensus by a wide margin, and Dell raised its full-year revenue forecast to $202 billion, its third increase this year. The question moves from whether demand exists to whether margins survive as AI servers dominate the mix.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA demand story, with the margin question still open.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Dell's quarter is the strongest evidence yet that enterprise AI infrastructure spending is still accelerating, not topping out. Revenue more than doubled, the backlog hit a record, and guidance went up for the third time this year. But the number that decides whether this is a durable cycle or a peak is not in the headline figures. It is the margin on those AI servers, and the industry's answer so far, delivered by HPE the day before, is that the margin is thinning. Dell has earned the benefit of the doubt on demand; it has not yet answered the profitability question.
