Anthropic's Shorter Riemann Proof Cuts 17 Million Lines to 76,000

Luke Sernau, with colleagues at Anthropic and Claude, posted a much shorter Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions on November 25, 2026. The compact edition runs to about 76,000 lines against about 17 million in the original, with identical theorem statements, and independent checks including a paranoid lake comparator run have passed.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Luke Sernau, with colleagues at Anthropic and Claude, posted a much shorter Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions on November 25, 2026. The repository, lsernau/grh-lean, rewrites the Alpöge–Claude development in about 76,000 lines in its compact edition and about 300,000 lines in its readable edition.

The theorem statements are identical to those of the original proof, which Levent Alpöge announced on November 20, 2026 as a formal Lean development of 175,187 files and about 17 million lines. The shortened proof covers the same five theorems: the Riemann Hypothesis as Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions.

Independent verification is already under way. The Waterloo check page records that the compact edition's full compile, axiom check and kernel replay are complete with no errors, and that the multi-kernel check is running. Andrew Sutherland's checks of the shortened proof, both editions, include lake comparator --paranoid on the compact edition, which passed on two machines.

The proof depends only on Lean's three standard axioms, propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, and is released under Apache 2.0 with copyright held by Anthropic, PBC. Its README states plainly that it has passed machine checks only, that no mathematician has read it through, and that no paper comes with it.

WHY IT MATTERSThe 99 percent cut is the story, not a new theorem.

Nothing new has been proved. The five theorems are the same ones Alpöge and Claude formalised on November 20, and the shortened proof does not extend them to Dedekind zeta functions, Artin or automorphic L-functions. What changed is the size of the artifact: a 17-million-line development that no human could read is now a 76,000-line compact edition and a 300,000-line readable edition with a guide.

That matters because verification is only half the problem. A machine-checked proof that nobody can read is a black box with a green light. At 76,000 lines, the proof enters the range where human review becomes conceivable, which is the step the original development could not offer. The shorter proof does not settle whether the mathematics is right in the human sense; it makes that question answerable.

WHAT REMAINSMachine-checked is not human-verified, and the authors say so.

The honest boundary is written into the repository itself: machine checks only, no mathematician has read it through, no paper. The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative's verification of the original proof, the independent kernel checks, and Sutherland's paranoid comparator runs all establish that the formal artifact is internally consistent, not that the argument is understood.

Watch for the human-readable account, which the Waterloo guide is building toward, and for the multi-kernel check on the shortened proof to finish. The next real event is a mathematician reading the compact edition and saying, in public, whether the argument holds together as mathematics rather than as code.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA shorter proof does not prove more. It lets humans check it.

The temptation is to read this as a second breakthrough. It is not. The theorems are unchanged, and the authors are candid that no mathematician has read the shortened proof through. What Sernau and colleagues did is arguably more useful than a new theorem: they turned an unreadable 17-million-line artifact into something a person could actually review. Machine checks say the code is consistent. Only human reading can say the mathematics is understood. Until that reading happens, the correct status is verified by machine, unverified by mathematicians, and the shorter proof is valuable precisely because it makes the second half possible.
