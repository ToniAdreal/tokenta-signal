TeraWulf's AI Leases Now Drive 83% of Revenue as Bitcoin Mining Fades

TeraWulf reported Q3 2026 revenue of $76.2 million on November 6, 2026, with HPC lease revenue of $63.0 million, about 83% of the total, while bitcoin mining revenue fell 69% to $13.2 million. Net income of $709.9 million was driven by a $728.4 million non-cash warrant gain; adjusted EBITDA was negative $12.8 million. Lake Mariner has 102 MW online with 336 MW under construction, and the 20-year, $19 billion Anthropic lease at Justified now carries a Google credit backstop and a Fluor construction agreement.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

TeraWulf reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $76.2 million on November 6, 2026, up 51% from $50.6 million a year earlier and above the roughly $70.4 million consensus. High-performance computing lease revenue reached $63.0 million, about 83% of the total, up from $7.2 million a year earlier. Bitcoin mining revenue fell 69% to $13.2 million from $43.4 million.

The headline profit needs a footnote. Net income attributable to shareholders was about $709.9 million, but it rests on a $728.4 million non-cash gain from the change in fair value of warrants, plus a $95 million gain on the sale of the company's 50.1% interest in the Abernathy joint venture. GAAP earnings per share were negative $0.03, adjusted EBITDA was negative $12.8 million against positive $18.1 million a year earlier, and the operating loss widened to $88.2 million from $24.7 million.

The buildout behind the revenue mix is moving. Lake Mariner in New York had 102 MW of revenue-generating critical IT capacity online at September 30, 2026, with another 336 MW under construction across the CB-4 and CB-5 buildings, and management expects to exit the first quarter of 2027 at about a $700 million annualized leasing run rate. The 20-year Anthropic lease at the Justified campus in Kentucky covers about 401 MW and about $19 billion of contracted revenue, and on November 4 TeraWulf announced a Google credit backstop for its project financing plus a definitive Fluor engineering, procurement and construction agreement for three data center buildings. Contracted power at the Muskie campus doubled to 1 GW, and the company ended the quarter with about $2.4 billion in cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash.

WHY IT MATTERSThe profit is paper, the revenue mix is real

Read the quarter the way the release asks to be read: the $709.9 million net income is an accounting entry, not cash earned. Warrant revaluations swing with the share price, and the Abernathy sale is a one-time gain. The operating line tells the cleaner story, and it is still loss-making: an $88.2 million operating loss and negative $12.8 million adjusted EBITDA as the company scales ahead of capacity coming online.

What changed for real is the mix. A year ago HPC leases were 14% of revenue; this quarter they are 83%, with mining down to $13.2 million. That is the pivot the company has been promising, now visible in the reported numbers rather than in guidance. The question for the next two quarters is whether the contracted capacity at Lake Mariner converts to cash flow fast enough to close the gap between leasing revenue and the cost of building it.

WHY IT MATTERSThe financing question that haunted AI infrastructure got an answer this week

The sector's open question all year has been how developers finance multi-billion-dollar campuses when credit spreads move and AI demand is the only collateral. TeraWulf's answer, assembled in one week, is a stack: a $19 billion contracted lease with Anthropic, a Google credit backstop behind the project financing, a Fluor agreement with guaranteed maximum pricing, $2.4 billion of cash on hand, and a $400 million revolving credit facility.

That stack is why the quarter matters beyond one company's numbers. If the Justified campus funds on project-level debt with investment-grade support, it becomes a template other developers can point lenders to. The watchpoint is execution: pilings are underway, phased ready-for-service deliveries are targeted from late 2027 into the second quarter of 2028, and the Muskie expansion to 1 GW still needs Kentucky Public Service Commission approval.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA bitcoin miner became an AI landlord on paper, and the lease book now has to prove it in cash

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the revenue mix is the real event and the profit is not. HPC leases at 83% of revenue, up from 14% a year ago, mark the point where TeraWulf's pivot stops being a story and starts being a reported fact. But the business still loses money on operations while it builds, so the next two quarters are a conversion test: CB-4 and CB-5 coming online, the $700 million run rate landing, and the Justified campus funding on the Google-backed terms announced this week. If that chain holds, TeraWulf is a template for how AI infrastructure gets financed. If it slips, the warrant gain will look like the quarter's only profit.
