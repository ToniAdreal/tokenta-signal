Synaptics Q1 Revenue Misses at $294M as Onsemi Deal Goes Quiet

Synaptics reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of $294.0 million, up 1% but about 7% below consensus, with non-GAAP EPS of $1.26; no guidance or call was given because of the pending all-cash acquisition by onsemi.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Synaptics reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $294.0 million on November 5, 2026, up 1% year over year from $292.5 million but below the $315.6 million consensus. The GAAP net loss was $17.7 million, or $0.45 per share, while non-GAAP net income was $51.5 million, or $1.26 per diluted share, just above the $1.25 consensus. GAAP gross margin was 47.8%. The company cited supply constraints and memory-related demand pressure, plus softness in smartphone touch and wireless products.

The quarter introduced the new Astra SR200 xMCU product line and Coralboard offerings, the company's edge AI push for the internet of things. But Synaptics gave no forward outlook and held no earnings call, because of its pending acquisition by onsemi. The deal was first announced on June 25, 2026 as an all-stock transaction and was restructured on October 1, 2026 into an all-cash acquisition. Shares fell 0.9% in the regular session on November 5, closing at $116.40.

WHY IT MATTERSA revenue miss the company cannot explain on a call

Revenue of $294.0 million missed consensus by about 7%, and the stated reasons, supply constraints, memory-related demand pressure and soft smartphone touch, are the same pressures hitting the broader semiconductor chain. The non-GAAP earnings beat of a single cent does not offset a top-line miss of that size, and with no earnings call and no guidance, investors have no management narrative to weigh against the numbers.

The silence is structural, not evasive. A pending acquirer in an all-cash deal has no incentive to guide the market, and Synaptics' remaining public quarters are now placeholders until the transaction closes. The quarter's information value is therefore mostly about the demand environment it reveals, not about Synaptics' own trajectory.

WHY IT MATTERSThe onsemi deal turns Synaptics into a closing timetable

The October restructuring from all-stock to all-cash changed the deal's meaning for Synaptics holders: the outcome is now a fixed cash price rather than a stake in the combined company. That makes the share price a function of the closing timetable and the spread to the offer, not of quarterly results. A 0.9% move on a 7% revenue miss is the market treating the quarter as noise against the deal.

What remains relevant is the Astra platform. The SR200 xMCU line and Coralboard are Synaptics' bet on AI-native edge devices, and their fate inside onsemi will say something about whether edge AI is consolidating into the big analog and power players. The product line was introduced this quarter; its first design-win evidence will now arrive under a different owner.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA miss that barely moved the stock, because the deal owns the price now

Tokenta Signal's judgment: $294.0 million against a $315.6 million consensus is a real miss, driven by the same supply and memory pressures hitting the chip chain. But with the onsemi acquisition now all-cash and no guidance or call, the quarter is a footnote to a closing timetable. The Astra SR200 launch is the one piece of the quarter that still matters, as a test of whether edge AI is consolidating into the big analog players.
