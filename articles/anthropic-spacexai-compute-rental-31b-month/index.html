Anthropic Pays xAI $3.1 Billion a Month for Compute

Anthropic now pays roughly $3.1 billion a month to rent computing capacity from xAI, The New York Times reported on November 24, 2026, making the Claude maker the largest customer of a rival lab. Google pays about $800 million a month for the same service. Anthropic's SEC filing discloses up to $120.5 billion in agreements with SpaceXAI through 2029, cancellable on 90 days' notice after lock-in periods ending in January and March 2027. Renting out capacity now accounts for more than half of xAI's revenue, and the unit has cut its own products, including the Grok Imagine team, to serve customer orders.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic has become the largest customer of the company built to compete with it. The New York Times reported on November 24, 2026 that Anthropic now pays roughly $3.1 billion a month to rent computing capacity from xAI, Elon Musk's AI unit, making the Claude maker the biggest source of revenue for a rival lab. Google pays about $800 million a month for the same service. Either contract can be cancelled on 90 days' notice.

The numbers are confirmed by Anthropic's own filing. Its Form S-1, filed with the SEC ahead of its Nasdaq listing, discloses agreements signed between May and October 2026 with SpaceXAI LLC, formerly X.AI LLC, to access compute powered by Nvidia GPUs. Anthropic agreed to spend up to $120.5 billion over terms extending into 2029, about $3.1 billion a month in aggregate once all capacity is delivered, which the company expects in December 2026. Initial non-cancelable periods end in January and March 2027.

The deal has already reshaped xAI. Renting out capacity now accounts for more than half of the unit's revenue, and Musk has pushed engineers for 'revenue per GPU' since August, reportedly renaming an internal chat 'Sell compute because we can't compete.' In November, unit chief Mike Nicolls told staff the 60-person team behind the Grok Imagine image and video generator would be disbanded, and xAI shut down its AI avatars in September. SpaceX, which absorbed xAI in February 2026, reported $7.56 billion of revenue from its Super Intelligence segment in the third quarter of 2026, up from $813 million a year earlier.

THE INVERSIONA frontier lab is now its rival's landlord.

The usual story of the AI race is labs competing for the same scarce chips. This deal inverts it: the lab that cannot get enough compute is buying it from a rival that could not make its own models competitive. Anthropic needs the capacity so badly it signed a $120.5 billion commitment; xAI needs the revenue so badly it is cutting its own products to serve a competitor's orders.

The structure of the deal shows who holds the leverage. Anthropic's payments are locked in only until January and March 2027. After that, either side can walk away with 90 days' notice, which means xAI's largest revenue line is contingent on a customer that is itself loss-making and preparing for life as a public company.

THE PAPER TRAILThe filing is the story; the Times report is the confirmation.

Both halves of this story are on the public record. Anthropic's S-1 spells out the $120.5 billion ceiling, the $3.1 billion monthly rate, the December delivery date and the cancellation terms. SpaceX's third-quarter filing shows the Super Intelligence segment swinging to a $431 million operating profit on $7.56 billion of revenue. The Times reporting adds what the filings cannot: the internal shift in priorities, the disbanded Imagine team and the 'revenue per GPU' pressure from Musk.

The open question is durability. Anthropic's total commitments across all suppliers run to about $900.9 billion, nearly all of it for compute and infrastructure, and its revenue is primarily consumption-based. If demand for Claude slows, the company is left holding fixed compute bills. If Anthropic walks away after the lock-in periods, xAI loses more than half its revenue overnight.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM RIVALS TO LANDLORD AND TENANT
From model rivalry to landlord and tenant
Anthropic and xAI compete on models, but the money now flows the other way: the Claude maker is its rival's biggest customer, paying $3.1 billion a month for the GPUs it cannot get elsewhere.
FROM PRODUCTS TO CAPACITY
From building products to selling capacity
xAI is cutting its own AI products, disbanding the Grok Imagine team and shutting down avatars, to free up capacity for paying customers. Revenue per GPU has replaced model progress as the unit's organizing metric.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe compute shortage has turned rivals into landlord and tenant.

This is the clearest sign yet that the AI race is now a capacity race first and a model race second. Anthropic would rather pay a rival $3.1 billion a month than wait for its own supply, and xAI would rather rent out its GPUs than keep building products on them. Both sides are making a rational trade, and both sides are exposed: Anthropic to fixed bills against consumption-based revenue, xAI to a single customer that can cancel with 90 days' notice. The December delivery date and the January and March 2027 lock-in expiries are the next checkpoints.
