Sequoia Raises ~$10 Billion and Rewinds 20 Years of Expansion

Sequoia Capital announced in a Wednesday memo that it has raised roughly $10 billion in new funds while closing its London office, sunsetting its Arc accelerator and Scouts program, merging its early-stage and growth teams into one, flattening its partnership to seven general partners, and ending its Monday partner meeting cycle.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Sequoia Capital posted a memo to X on Wednesday morning titled 'The Sequoia of Tomorrow,' announcing that it has raised roughly $10 billion in new funds while unwinding much of its 20-year expansion. TechCrunch published the memo in full on November 4, 2026, and Reuters reported the same day that the firm is closing its London office and has raised about $10 billion in new funding.

The overhaul closes the London office Sequoia opened in 2020, with several people leaving the firm. The memo says Sequoia sees no need for a local presence and believes Europe will keep producing outlier founders that it can support from the Bay Area. Luciana Lixandru, hired in 2020 as Sequoia's first partner in Europe, declined a move to San Francisco and remains a partner keeping her board seats while investing personally in European founders.

Sequoia is also sunsetting two programs that put it closest to young founders: Arc, its batch accelerator launched in March 2022 offering $500,000 to $1 million up front, and Scouts, launched around 2009, which gave small checks to founders and operators to invest on the firm's behalf. The early-stage and growth teams are merged into a single team, the partnership is flattened to seven general partners who own and operate the firm, and the Monday partner meeting cycle is ended.

The memo frames the moves as the end of a roughly 20-year expansionary phase during which Sequoia built multiple businesses in multiple geographies, with hierarchy as an unintended side effect. It follows Andreessen Horowitz shuttering its London office last year and Coatue closing its own in 2024, and it comes a year after Sequoia's generational leadership transition.

WHY IT MATTERSA Reversal, Not a Retreat From Capital

The striking combination is contraction plus firepower. Sequoia is closing offices and programs while announcing roughly $10 billion in new funds, and the memo insists talent density and capital base are stronger than ever. That pairing rejects the usual reading of a pullback: the firm is not shrinking its ambitions, it is concentrating them.

The test of that claim is where the $10 billion goes. A smaller, Bay Area-centered team with one investment team and no Monday meeting cycle must still find and win the same outlier founders, including in Europe, without the local presence and the early-stage programs it just closed.

WHY IT MATTERSThe Center of Gravity Moves Back to the Bay Area

For European founders, the message is the sharpest part of the memo. Sequoia says it will keep backing European outliers, but from the Bay Area, after Andreessen Horowitz and Coatue already left London. The practical pressure on founders to move to, or open an office in, the Bay Area just rose again.

The same logic runs through the program closures. Arc and Scouts were bets on being early and being everywhere; the memo replaces them with high-conviction, highly selective investing by fewer people. In a market where AI, space, biology, and reindustrialization are all shifting at once, Sequoia is betting that concentration beats coverage.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISConcentration is the strategy; coverage was the cost

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Sequoia is betting that a smaller, flatter, Bay Area-centered firm with $10 billion behind it beats a global one. The risk is not the money, it is the pipeline: the London office, Arc, and Scouts were how Sequoia found founders early and far away, and their replacements are not yet named.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

This item is based on Sequoia Capital's own Wednesday memo, 'The Sequoia of Tomorrow,' as published in full by TechCrunch, and on Reuters' same-day report of it. The memo does not break the roughly $10 billion in new funds between fund vehicles or name the vehicles, and it does not give a number for the people leaving with the London office closure. This article is a summary with links only and does not reproduce the memo's full text. This does not constitute investment advice.
