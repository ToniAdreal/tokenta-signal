Ex-OpenAI Engineer Accused of Bugging Meetings Released on $1 Million Bond

U.S. Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse ordered former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan released on a $1 million bond pending trial, reversing his October detention as a flight risk. Alfozan, who pleaded not guilty to a single Wiretap Act count, must wear an ankle monitor and stay in Northern California; he is due out of Santa Clara jail on Wednesday morning. Trial is set for January 20, 2027.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

U.S. Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse ruled on Tuesday, November 24, 2026 that Abdulrahman Alfozan, the former OpenAI engineer accused of bugging a company conference room, will be released from jail on a $1 million bond pending trial. The judge determined Alfozan did not present an immediate flight risk, reversing the detention order that has kept him in custody since his arrest on October 9, 2026.

The release carries conditions. Alfozan must wear an ankle monitor, is restricted from leaving Northern California, and will stay at his home in Millbrae, California. He is set to be released on Wednesday morning, November 25, from Santa Clara jail. Business Insider reported that $500,000 of his own cash goes toward the bond, with friends acting as sureties.

Federal prosecutors had argued Alfozan should stay in jail, saying the 35-year-old Saudi national has weak ties to the United States and could flee to Saudi Arabia. They said in a November 23 filing that investigators found hundreds of audio files on his personal computer, including dozens of recordings of OpenAI meetings from September 2026, and that he used OpenAI's own coding tool, Codex, to add voice detection to a hidden recording device and to organize the files. Prosecutors also said he photographed what appeared to be a confidential term sheet for a strategic partnership.

Alfozan has pleaded not guilty to a single count under the Wiretap Act for allegedly intercepting oral communications, which carries up to five years in prison. His lawyer, David Rizk, argued that prosecutors have presented no evidence of espionage or trade-secret theft, and that most of the recordings contain noise and silence. Jury selection and trial are set for January 20, 2027, before U.S. District Judge Vince Chhabria in San Francisco.

WHY IT MATTERSA judge decided the flight risk can be managed, not that the case is weak.

The ruling is a reversal of the October detention order, and it turns on conditions rather than on the merits. Judge Tse kept Alfozan in custody in October as a flight risk; on Tuesday he concluded that a $1 million bond, a $500,000 cash posting, an ankle monitor and a Northern California travel limit are enough to manage that risk while the case proceeds. The defense argument that no heavier charge has been brought, despite weeks of investigation, clearly weighed in the balance.

The release does not resolve the underlying allegations. Prosecutors say the investigation is ongoing, and the November 23 filing shows they believe the recordings likely captured trade secrets, including a meeting with representatives of a foreign government. The question the trial will answer is what Alfozan did with what he recorded, and whether the government can prove a purpose beyond the single wiretapping count.

THE WATCHPOINTThe recordings are still the case.

What prosecutors found is now on the record: hundreds of audio files, AI-generated transcripts sorted by date and renamed by content, and a recording device enhanced with the company's own tools. That record is what makes this more than a routine bail fight, and it is also what the defense is attacking, arguing the bulk of the material is noise and that no evidence ties Alfozan to a foreign intelligence service.

The next dates are December 8, when the next status conference is scheduled, and January 20, 2027, when jury selection begins. Prosecutors must also turn over the recordings and video footage by November 24 under an earlier court order. Whether a heavier charge follows, or the case stays a single wiretapping count, decides how much of this story is about a security failure at OpenAI and how much is about one engineer's misconduct.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA procedural turn, not a verdict on the recordings.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the bail ruling changes where Alfozan waits for trial, not what he is accused of doing. The court split the difference between the prosecutors' flight-risk argument and the defense's point that no espionage or trade-secret charge has been filed, and it did so with money and monitoring rather than with a finding. The case still turns on the recordings themselves, and on whether the government's ongoing investigation produces a heavier charge before January 20.
