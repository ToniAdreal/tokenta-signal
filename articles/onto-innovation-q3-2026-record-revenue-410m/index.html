Onto Innovation's Record Quarter: Revenue Jumps 88% as AI Chip Inspection Demand Surges

Onto Innovation reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $409.7 million, up 87.8 percent year over year, with non-GAAP earnings of $2.44 per diluted share, as demand for process control in AI and high-performance computing pushed its backlog past $1.3 billion. Management guided full-year 2026 revenue growth above 45 percent and set fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $430 million to $450 million.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Onto Innovation reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $409.7 million on November 9, 2026, up 87.8 percent from $218.2 million a year earlier and up 19 percent sequentially, with revenue, operating margin, and earnings per share all above the high end of its previously announced non-GAAP guidance. Non-GAAP diluted earnings per share reached $2.44, against $0.92 in the third quarter of 2025, while GAAP diluted earnings per share were $0.95. Gross margin widened to 54.8 percent from 50.7 percent a year earlier.

The growth came from both ends of the fab. Advanced Nodes set a quarterly revenue record on strong adoption of the Atlas G6 optical critical dimension system, while Advanced Packaging and Specialty Devices also set records, driven by the Dragonfly G5 and Firefly inspection systems. Chief executive Mike Plisinski tied the demand to increasingly complex device architectures for next-generation AI and high-performance computing, which drive greater process-control requirements. In August, the company closed its purchase of a 27 percent ownership interest in Rigaku.

The order book gives the quarter its forward weight. Backlog increased to more than $1.3 billion, and management said customer investment plans extend out multiple years. For the fourth quarter, Onto guided revenue of $430 million to $450 million and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $2.59 to $2.79, and said it expects to finish 2026 with revenue growth above 45 percent year over year. Shares rose about 12 percent to $194.04 on the day.

WHY IT MATTERSInspection is where the AI buildout shows up first

Onto does not sell chips. It sells the metrology and inspection tools that fabs need before advanced chips can ship, and that position makes its quarter a read-through on the whole AI hardware cycle. When device architectures get more complex, process-control spending rises faster than wafer starts, and Onto's 88 percent revenue jump says customers are buying that control at a record pace rather than deferring it.

The backlog matters more than the quarter. At more than $1.3 billion against $409.7 million of quarterly revenue, the order book covers roughly three quarters of sales at the current run rate, and management pointed to multi-year visibility into customer investment plans. That is the difference between a one-off surge and a durable demand trend, and it is why the company could guide fourth-quarter revenue above the third quarter's record.

WHY IT MATTERSA 45 percent year, priced for a strong 2027

Nine-month revenue of $1.045 billion, up 41.5 percent year over year, puts Onto on track for the full-year growth above 45 percent that management now expects. The fourth-quarter guide of $430 million to $450 million implies another record, and non-GAAP operating margin of 32.6 percent in the quarter, up from 21.1 percent a year earlier, shows the growth is carrying operating leverage rather than just volume.

The watchpoint is concentration. Advanced Nodes, Advanced Packaging, and Specialty Devices all set records in the same quarter, which is strength today and a reminder that the same AI capital-spending cycle drives all three. If fab customers slow expansion plans, Onto's backlog cushions the turn, but the growth rate would compress fast. For now, the company's own visibility, multi-year customer plans and a backlog above $1.3 billion, argues the cycle still has room to run.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISOnto's record quarter is a proxy for the AI fab cycle, and the backlog says it is not peaking yet

Tokenta Signal's judgment: The headline number, revenue up 88 percent, is striking, but the number that carries the story is the backlog above $1.3 billion. A record quarter can be a spike; a backlog worth three quarters of sales, paired with multi-year customer visibility and a fourth-quarter guide above the record just set, is a trend. Onto sits one layer beneath the chipmakers, selling the inspection and metrology that complex AI devices cannot ship without, so its order book is an early read on fab spending rather than a lagging one. The risk is the same concentration that powers the growth: all three segments set records together, and they would slow together if AI capital spending pauses. For now, the evidence points to durability, not a peak.
