Z.ai Ships GLM-5.4, Its First Loop Transformer Flagship, With Vision

Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on November 23, 2026, its first flagship built on a Loop Transformer: 28 layers of the roughly 750-billion-parameter GLM-5 base run twice, which Z.ai says reaches intelligence comparable to 2-trillion to 3-trillion parameter models. The model adds vision to the mainline flagship for the first time, supports a 1-million-token context, and is live for GLM Coding Plan subscribers with 50% more weekly quota than GLM-5.3. API access and open weights are pending safety review, and no benchmark scores for GLM-5.4 itself have been published yet.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Z.ai released GLM-5.4 on November 23, 2026, its first flagship model built on a Loop Transformer architecture, and made it available to all GLM Coding Plan subscribers, along with ZCode and Z.ai Chat. API access is listed as coming soon and will open after a safety evaluation, while the open weights are planned for release once safety hardening is complete.

The model loops 28 layers of the roughly 750-billion-parameter GLM-5 base model twice, reusing the same weights for a second, deeper pass. Z.ai says the result reaches intelligence levels comparable to models in the 2-trillion to 3-trillion parameter class. GLM-5.4 also adds vision to the mainline flagship for the first time, so developers can hand it a screenshot of a broken interface alongside the code, and it supports a 1-million-token context window. Zixuan Li said subscribers on weekly quotas get 50% more GLM-5.4 allowance than they had on GLM-5.3, at lower credit multipliers.

Z.ai frames the release as the start of Generalized Coding, where code becomes the foundation for tasks well beyond software development. Its demos include a self-narrated music video, a 6-axis robotic arm modeled in FreeCAD, a complete game, and an interactive 3D bird-watching scene built from pure geometry and custom shaders. The company is open about one limitation: the model tends to get caught in overly long "marathon thinking" loops, though Z.ai says the extra self-correction still lifts accuracy.

WHY IT MATTERSDepth instead of size, at the moment rivals are racing to trillions of parameters.

The loop is a bet that compute depth can substitute for parameter count. Where the recent frontier race has been won by adding parameters, with Alibaba's Qwen4-Max at a reported 2.4 trillion, Z.ai claims 2-trillion to 3-trillion level intelligence from a 750-billion base by running the same 28 layers twice.

If the claim survives independent benchmarks, it moves the efficiency frontier for everyone building at this scale. Until then it is a company claim, not a result: Z.ai has published no benchmark scores for GLM-5.4 itself, and a team member said they will come later. The honest read is to watch the scores, not the slogan.

THE EXPERIMENTThree engineering changes make the loop affordable at 750B scale.

A naive loop would multiply memory cost with each pass. Z.ai's blog describes three fixes: independent per-token gates that write each pass's updates back into the residual stream in proportion, a hybrid KV cache where the extra loop costs one sliding window instead of a full cache, and reuse of the first pass's sparse attention indices at zero extra indexing cost.

The design was validated progressively on 1-billion, 4-billion, 30-billion and 750-billion base models rather than trained from scratch as a looped model, which Z.ai says still trails unshared models on the same compute budget. On a 30-billion ablation with about 10% more training compute, the looped version showed its largest gains in math, scientific reasoning and long-context tasks, and cut average output length by 10 to 14% on benchmarks such as GPQA-Diamond and MMLU-Pro.

WHAT IS NEXTSubscription first, open weights later.

GLM-5.4 reaches developers first inside the paid Coding Plan, where it runs at up to 200 tokens per second off-peak and 70 to 80 at peak load, and where it can be selected as glm-5.4[1m] in Claude Code. The general API and the weights both wait on safety work, with no dates attached.

Watch for three things: published benchmark scores for GLM-5.4 itself, the API opening after safety evaluation, and the larger next model Z.ai says is coming soon, which it describes as carrying forward everything learned from looping.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM BIGGER MODELS TO DEEPER PASSES
From adding parameters to reusing them
The frontier race has been won by stacking parameters. GLM-5.4 runs the same 28 layers twice instead, trading parameter count for compute depth and claiming the intelligence of a model three to four times its size.
FROM CODING TO GENERALIZED CODING
From code-only models to code as a general medium
Vision joins the mainline flagship, and Z.ai frames the release as Generalized Coding: code as the starting point for video, games, 3D scenes and CAD, not just software.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA Real Efficiency Bet, With a Claim Still Awaiting Its Scores.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the engineering is concrete and the headline is not yet proven. Per-token gates, a hybrid KV cache and sparse-index reuse are specific answers to the real cost of looping at 750-billion scale, and the 30-billion ablation gives the design a measured basis. The 2-trillion to 3-trillion comparison, by contrast, arrives with no published scores for GLM-5.4 itself. Treat the release as a usable coding upgrade with cheaper quota and new vision, and the scale claim as a test awaiting its benchmark.

The facts are as stated in Z.ai's technical blog post "Toward Generalized Coding with Our First Loop Transformer" (z.ai, November 23, 2026), in Z.ai lead Zixuan Li's announcement on X the same day, and in independent coverage by Winbuzzer and TestingCatalog on November 23, 2026.
