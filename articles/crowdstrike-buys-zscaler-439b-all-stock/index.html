CrowdStrike to Buy Zscaler in $43.9B All-Stock Deal to Build AI-Era Security Platform

CrowdStrike will acquire Zscaler in a $43.9 billion all-stock deal announced November 23, 2026, paying 1.135 shares per Zscaler share, a 34% premium, to add zero-trust network security to its Falcon endpoint platform. The combined company starts with more than $10 billion in ARR and targets $1 billion in revenue synergies; close is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, pending shareholder and regulatory approval.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On Monday, November 23, 2026, CrowdStrike said it will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valuing Zscaler at approximately $43.9 billion, the two companies announced in a joint statement. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each Zscaler share they own, an implied $260.41 per share based on CrowdStrike's closing price on November 20, 2026, a premium of about 34%. Existing CrowdStrike shareholders will own about 84.1% of the combined company after the transaction closes.

The deal unites the leader in endpoint security with the leader in zero-trust network security. Zscaler's Zero Trust Exchange sits inline between users, agents and applications; CrowdStrike's Falcon sensor sits on endpoints and cloud workloads. The combined company will start with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue, with Zscaler contributing $3.9 billion, and CrowdStrike targets $1 billion in annualized revenue synergies within 30 months of closing.

The market split on the news: Zscaler shares jumped more than 13.4% in premarket trading, while CrowdStrike fell 5%, Reuters reported. The transaction, unanimously approved by both boards, is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028, subject to Zscaler shareholder approval and regulatory clearance. Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry, whose affiliates hold about 34% of Zscaler shares, has agreed to vote for the deal and will lead the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike's board after closing.

WHY IT MATTERSThe deal is priced on AI agents, not on firewalls.

CrowdStrike's own pitch makes the AI angle explicit. Chief executive George Kurtz said safety is now required to let AI in, because the network that once connected people to places now connects agents to data. Zscaler's chief executive Jay Chaudhry called AI agents the new weakest link. The acquisition is a bet that the traffic that matters most in the next decade is agent traffic, and that the company controlling both where agents run and where they go owns the security layer for it.

The data argument is the second half of the thesis. Falcon generates trillions of events daily from endpoints and cloud workloads; Zscaler's Zero Trust Exchange processes more than 750 billion transactions a day. Merged into one data set, that telemetry trains CrowdStrike's SafeMind frontier security models on the adversary's full attack path, from device to network. That is the moat CrowdStrike is buying: not two product lines, but one training set neither company could assemble alone.

WHAT TO WATCHA distant close and a premium the market has to believe.

The close is far away, the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which leaves more than a year for the all-stock price to move, for regulators to weigh in and for the integration thesis to be tested. CrowdStrike shares fell 5% on the announcement while Zscaler rose more than 13%, the market's first verdict on who got the better of the exchange ratio.

The synergy targets are promises, not results: $1 billion in annualized revenue synergies within 30 months of closing and $500 million in cost synergies within 18 to 24 months. Watch the Zscaler shareholder vote, where Chaudhry's voting agreements cover about 34% of shares, the antitrust review of a combination that would create one of the world's largest pure-play cybersecurity providers, and whether the roughly 8,000 Zscaler customers who are not yet CrowdStrike customers start buying Falcon products on a single contract. If cross-sell stalls, the 34% premium becomes the story.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM ENDPOINT TO NETWORK
From guarding the device to guarding every connection
CrowdStrike owned the endpoint. Zscaler owns the connections between users, agents and applications. The combined platform controls both ends of every agent's path.
FROM KEEPING AI OUT TO LETTING AI IN
From blocking the adversary to enabling the agent
The deal's framing is not about keeping attackers out but about letting AI in safely. Security becomes the permission layer for enterprise agent deployment, not just the wall around it.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA 34% Premium to Own the Network Where AI Agents Travel

Tokenta Signal's judgment: CrowdStrike is paying a full premium to buy the network layer its endpoint platform never owned, and justifying it as the operating system for AI-era security. The logic holds if agent traffic really becomes the traffic that matters, and if one data layer beats two vendors selling separately. The price is real, the close is distant and the market's first reaction split the difference. The test is whether the combined platform wins deals neither company could win alone.
