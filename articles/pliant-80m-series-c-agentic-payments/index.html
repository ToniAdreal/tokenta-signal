Pliant Raises $80 Million to Open Its B2B Payment Rails to AI Agents

Pliant announced on November 4, 2026 an $80 million Series C led by Seaya Nova and Speedinvest to open its B2B payment infrastructure to AI agents. The Berlin fintech, which says it passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue after three years of doubling, will expand in the US and Canada; third-party agents connect to its card issuing, payment accounts, and FX rails via Model Context Protocol.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Pliant announced on November 4, 2026 that it has raised an $80 million Series C funding round led by Seaya Nova and Speedinvest, with participation from Illuminate Financial, Motive Partners, and Molten Ventures. The company described the round as oversubscribed.

The Berlin-headquartered fintech will use the capital to expand in the United States and Canada, prepare entry into further international markets, and accelerate its AI roadmap as businesses connect AI agents directly to financial infrastructure.

Pliant's platform combines commercial card issuing, payment accounts, foreign exchange, and spend management for businesses and AI agents. Its own AI agents help customers automate recurring finance work, and third-party agents, from assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT to a company's own automations, connect to the platform via Model Context Protocol (MCP) and embed payment workflows into business processes.

The round follows three consecutive years in which Pliant says it doubled its business, and comes shortly after the company surpassed $100 million in annual recurring revenue. Pliant employs more than 300 people across eight offices and processes billions of dollars in annual payment volume. It follows an April 2025 Series B of $40 million, which at the time brought its total funding above $100 million.

Seaya Nova general partner Vinoth Jayakumar framed the bet in one line: when agents move money, someone still has to be licensed, auditable, and liable, and that is the layer Pliant owns. Pliant, founded in 2020, is a principal member of Visa and Mastercard and issues commercial cards in 13 currencies across 32 countries under its European e-money license and through regulated partners in the UK and US.

WHY IT MATTERSAgentic payments need a licensed layer, not just an API

The pitch is deliberately boring, and that is the point. An agent that can browse and click still cannot legally move a company's money without a licensed, auditable rail underneath it, with permissions, controls, and an audit trail built in. Pliant's claim is to own that layer: principal-member card issuing, payment accounts, and FX in one modular platform that businesses, banks, and now agents can build on.

The MCP connection is the distribution bet. If third-party agents can embed Pliant payment workflows directly into business processes, the platform stops being a dashboard a finance team visits and becomes infrastructure an agent calls. The open question is how much real agent-driven volume flows through those connections versus human-initiated workflows with AI assistance.

WHY IT MATTERSThe funding pattern marks a shift in fintech capital

An $80 million Series C for a B2B payments platform, oversubscribed, on the back of $100 million in annual recurring revenue, reads differently from the model-era rounds around it. Investors are paying for regulated infrastructure with existing volume, not for a model waiting for a market.

Pliant's three years of doubling and its North America push also set the next test: whether a European-licensed platform can convert US and Canadian expansion into the same growth rate, in markets where its regulated-partner structure, rather than its own license, does the heavy lifting.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe licensed rail is the product

Tokenta Signal's judgment: agentic commerce will be decided less by which agent is smartest than by which rail is licensed to move the money. Pliant's round bets that the winning layer is the auditable one, and its next proof point is whether US expansion converts agent connections into real payment volume.
