Alphabet's Books Show $548 Million of Qualcomm

Alphabet's third-quarter 13F filing discloses that it held 2.977 million Qualcomm shares worth $548 million as of September 30. It is Alphabet's sixth-largest position. The stake sits under GV, Alphabet's venture arm, and most likely came from a share swap when Qualcomm acquired Modular, not a fresh purchase.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE SWAP — On July 28, Qualcomm completed its all-stock acquisition of Modular; GV received Qualcomm shares.
  • 02THE FILING — On November 6, the 13F filing disclosed a Qualcomm position of $548 million.
  • 03THE MARKET — Qualcomm rose about 3% premarket, a day after falling more than 9% on weak results.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The 13F filing is dated November 5 and appeared on SEC EDGAR on November 6. The previous filing, as of June 30, had no Qualcomm line, so the position appeared in the third quarter.

The position ranks sixth in Alphabet's 13F portfolio. The portfolio totals $88.3 billion; Qualcomm is 0.62% of it.

The Modular deal closed on July 28, 2026. GV, an investor in Modular, received Qualcomm shares in the swap, and the timeline matches the position's appearance in the 13F exactly.

GV, NOT A NEW BETThe Stake Came From a Swap, Not a New Bet

The 13F filing lists the position under GV Management Company, Alphabet's venture arm. The shares most likely came from Qualcomm's July 28 all-stock acquisition of AI startup Modular, a deal of about $3.1 billion to $3.9 billion, in which GV funds received Qualcomm stock.

A 13F filing states no purchase price, no purchase date, and no motive. Reading it as Google newly buying $548 million of Qualcomm is a misreading.

THE $15B TARGETQualcomm's Data-Center Bet

Qualcomm has set itself a target of $15 billion in data-center chip revenue by fiscal 2029, with $5 billion first in fiscal 2027.

The Amazon agreement announced on September 8 covers multi-generation custom AI inference chips plus 1.6T optical cooperation. The $60 billion figure is a procurement ceiling, not an order. Amazon received warrants for up to 25 million shares at a strike price of $161.26, with only 3.75 million vested at signing.

MORE BUYERSMicrosoft and Meta Are Ordering Too

Reuters reports that Microsoft and Meta have also ordered data-center processors from Qualcomm. Qualcomm's customer list is expanding from phones into data centers.

Qualcomm shares rose about 3% premarket after the 13F disclosure. The day before, they had fallen more than 9% on weak results, so the rebound looks more like a reaction to the bad news being out.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISDo Not Read a Holding as an Investment

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the $548 million is real; Google newly investing in Qualcomm is not. The real new story is Qualcomm's data-center turn. The Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon orders carry more weight than a 13F filing.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

A 13F filing discloses no purchase price, date, or motive. The share-swap origin of the position is inferred from the deal timeline and is unconfirmed by Alphabet. The $60 billion figure is an Amazon procurement ceiling, not a signed order. This does not constitute investment advice.
