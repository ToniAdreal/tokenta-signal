Varonis Q3: SaaS Near 98% of Revenue, ARR at $774.6 Million, No Guidance While Acquisition Is Pending

Varonis's third-quarter total revenue was $189.5 million, up 17.3% year on year and above expectations. SaaS ARR reached $774.6 million, up 42%, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.09 beat expectations by $0.06. With the Proofpoint acquisition pending, the company provides no guidance and holds no earnings call.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Varonis's third-quarter 2026 results, released after the close on November 3, 2026, put total revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 at $189.5 million, up from $161.6 million a year earlier. SaaS revenue was $185.1 million, up from $125.8 million a year earlier, while legacy term-license subscription revenue shrank from $24.8 million to $1.2 million, with most of the decline coming from customers migrating to the SaaS platform.

On key metrics, SaaS ARR at period end was $774.6 million, up 42% year on year, or 24% excluding the effect of migrations. The GAAP operating loss was $47.7 million, against $35.9 million a year earlier; non-GAAP operating profit was $7.6 million, against just $0.2 million a year earlier.

Seeking Alpha confirmed the same day that non-GAAP EPS of $0.09 beat expectations by $0.06, and that revenue of $189.5 million beat expectations by $2.56 million, up 17.3% year on year.

The release also states that, because of Proofpoint's pending acquisition of the company (Proofpoint is a Thoma Bravo portfolio company), Varonis will provide no forward guidance, will not update prior guidance, and will hold no earnings call. The merger agreement was signed on October 15, 2026, and the transaction still requires shareholder approval and regulatory clearance.

WHY IT MATTERSMigration completion matters more than the revenue growth rate

SaaS revenue is now close to 98% of total revenue, with legacy term-license subscriptions down to just $1.2 million. The substance of this report is not how much the company grew, but that an established data-security vendor's business-model migration is nearly complete, with the revenue mix shifting decisively from one-off licenses to subscriptions.

But the migration dividend is diminishing: excluding migrations, SaaS ARR grew 24% year on year, well below the 42% headline that includes migration. The next question is no longer how many customers move over, but whether those who moved renew and expand — and with the company providing no guidance while the acquisition is pending, the answer must wait for new disclosure once the deal lands.

WHY IT MATTERSAn earnings report with no call carries higher information density

Providing no guidance and holding no call usually means management is unwilling, before the transaction closes, to give any number that could be read as a basis for pricing the deal. The hard information still verifiable in the release is cash: cash and marketable securities totaled $937.8 million at period end, while operating cash flow for the first nine months was $108.3 million and free cash flow $94.8 million, both below the year-earlier period.

For the AI security field, Varonis's positioning narrative is worth noting on its own: the company describes itself as protecting the data AI uses, securing AI deployment from code to runtime. As AI agents begin accessing enterprise data warehouses in bulk, governance of data access is moving from a compliance accessory to a precondition of AI deployment. This earnings report is an operating footnote to that judgment, not proof of it.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is migration completion, not the size of the beat

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the signal in Varonis's results is not the $2.56 million revenue beat, but that after SaaS reached close to 98% of revenue, the growth story switches from migration-driven to retention- and expansion-driven. The longer the silence lasts while the acquisition is pending, the more the market's pricing of the company depends on the transaction itself rather than on operating numbers.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this report rest on Varonis's official earnings release of November 3, 2026 and Seeking Alpha's same-day reporting. The earnings figures are unaudited. SaaS ARR is the annualized value of effective contracts at period end, not a forecast of future revenue. The non-GAAP basis excludes items such as stock-based compensation and is not interchangeable with the GAAP loss basis. Proofpoint's acquisition still requires shareholder and regulatory approval; closing timing and final terms will follow official disclosures. The company has stated explicitly that it will provide no forward guidance and hold no earnings call; this article makes no earnings forecast.
