Government Claude Pricing Moves from $1 to 40% Off

On October 27, the GSA expanded its OneGov agreement with Anthropic: Claude's consumption pricing is discounted 40%, covering state, local and tribal governments for the first time, with no minimum purchase, succeeding the $1 offer expiring October 31.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 27, the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) announced an expanded OneGov agreement with Anthropic: Claude's token-based pricing is discounted 40%, covering federal, state, local and tribal governments, with no minimum purchase and pay-per-use billing.

The agreement succeeds the $1 offer expiring on October 31: over the past year, federal agencies used Claude Enterprise and Claude for Government at $1 per user. The $1 era is ending, replaced by a long-term consumption discount; the agreement runs 27 months, through January 31, 2029.

Coverage is this round's real change: for the first time the discount extends from federal agencies to state, local, tribal and territorial governments, across Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the API. Anthropic says about 500 U.S. federal, state and local agencies now use Claude, and weekly active users of the government edition have grown about eightfold since February.

THE PRICING SHIFTFrom $1 to Pay-Per-Use: the Shape of the Discount Changed

The $1 price was an acquisition price: get agencies using it, with bills close to zero. A 40% consumption discount is a retention price: agencies pay for actual usage, the discount holds long-term, and procurement shifts from "grabbing a bargain" to "doing the maths."

The GSA's framing shifted the same way: OneGov has cumulatively saved the federal government more than $1.8 billion, about $1.6 billion of it from superintelligence agreements. The discount war's battlefield has spread from enterprise API pricing to government procurement catalogues.

THE CITY HALL FRONTStates and Localities Enter for the First Time: the Discount War Reaches City Hall

State transport departments, county election offices and tribal government IT teams were not covered by federal discounts before. This agreement writes them into the discount list for the first time, with purchase paths including direct procurement, resellers and major cloud platforms.

For Anthropic, the step expands government customers from a "federal showcase" to a "national network." For states, the threshold drops from "whether they qualify" to "pay for what they use."

THE TWO-HORSE RACEOpenAI's 50% Came First: Government Procurement Is Now a Two-Horse Race

Earlier this month, OpenAI reached a similar OneGov agreement for 50% off token-based pricing, also for 27 months. The two labs now benchmark directly against each other inside the government procurement catalogue: 50% off versus 40% off, through the same purchasing channel.

Government customers thereby gain a rare bargaining position: two top-tier models competing inside a single procurement framework, with switching costs compressed by the structure of the agreements themselves.

$1 was hospitality; 40% off is business. Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: when two top-tier models benchmark against each other inside a single government procurement framework, the real winner is the government customer holding bargaining power for the first time.
