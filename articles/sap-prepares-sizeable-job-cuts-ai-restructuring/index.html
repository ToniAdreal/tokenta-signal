SAP Prepares Sizeable Job Cuts in AI Restructuring

SAP is preparing to cut a sizeable number of jobs in an AI-driven restructuring, Reuters reported on November 27, 2026, citing two people familiar with the matter. Bloomberg News reported late on November 26 that SAP is considering cutting at least 10,000 jobs, citing people familiar with the matter. The works council had not yet been informed about the plans. SAP has not announced the restructuring or a job figure publicly.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

SAP is preparing to cut a sizeable number of jobs in an AI-driven restructuring, Reuters reported on November 27, 2026, citing two people familiar with the matter. The works council had not yet been informed about the plans, one of the sources said.

Bloomberg News reported late on November 26 that SAP is considering cutting at least 10,000 jobs in the restructuring, citing people familiar with the matter. Reuters said it could not independently verify the Bloomberg report when it first covered it, and its own November 27 report confirmed that job cuts are being prepared without putting a number on them.

The reports land as Europe's largest software company reorients itself around artificial intelligence, with AI reshaping how enterprise software is built, sold and serviced. SAP has not announced the restructuring or a job figure publicly, and no timeline for the cuts has been reported.

WHY IT MATTERSAI restructuring is now a headcount story, not just a product story.

For two years the AI story at enterprise software companies has been told in product launches and partnerships. SAP's reported restructuring tells it in headcount. When a company of SAP's scale ties job cuts directly to an AI reorganisation, it signals that the technology is changing which roles the business needs, not only which features it ships.

The reported scale is what makes this land. Ten thousand roles would be one of the largest AI-linked restructurings in European tech, and even the unnumbered version, a sizeable cut confirmed by Reuters' own sources, puts SAP alongside the wave of AI-driven workforce resets sweeping the software industry.

WHAT REMAINSTwo reports, one unnamed plan.

The reporting is careful and so should the reading be. Bloomberg's number rests on unnamed sources, Reuters could not independently verify it, and Reuters' own confirmation is deliberately vague, a sizeable number, with the works council not yet informed. That is the shape of a plan still being formed, not a finished decision.

What to watch next is SAP itself: whether the company confirms a restructuring, what figure it gives, which functions are affected, and whether the cuts are framed as AI displacement or as a broader efficiency programme. German labour law gives the works council a formal role, so the council's involvement will be the first public sign the plan has moved from consideration to execution.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISSAP's AI story just became a jobs story.

Enterprise software spent two years selling AI as a feature. SAP is now, reportedly, restructuring its workforce around it. The numbers are still sourced, not announced, and the works council has not been told, so this is a plan in motion rather than a decision delivered. But the direction is unmistakable: AI is no longer just changing what SAP sells, it is changing who SAP employs. Watch whether SAP confirms a figure and which functions it names; the split between displaced roles and new AI roles will say more about the industry than the headline number.
