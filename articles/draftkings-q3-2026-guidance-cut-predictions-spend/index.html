DraftKings Q3 Loss Widens as It Slashes 2026 Outlook to Fund Prediction Markets

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

DraftKings reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $1.329 billion, up 16% year over year, but below the roughly $1.40 billion analysts expected, according to the company's official results release published through Business Wire on November 5, 2026. The net loss widened to $468.7 million, or $0.94 per share, from $256.8 million a year earlier, and adjusted EBITDA swung to a loss of $316.9 million from a $126.5 million loss.

The company slashed its full-year 2026 outlook to pay for a bigger push into prediction markets. DraftKings cut its 2026 revenue guidance to $6.35 billion to $6.55 billion and its adjusted EBITDA guidance to $175 million to $275 million, down from the $700 million to $900 million it maintained in August, citing $650 million to $700 million of investment in DraftKings Predictions. Shares fell 24% in after-hours trading.

WHY IT MATTERSThe core is fine; the bet is Predictions

The underlying sportsbook business is still growing. Sports revenue rose 31.5% to $783.6 million, monthly unique payers increased about 13% to 4.1 million, and the sports net revenue margin improved to 5.6% from 5.2%. More than 1.6 million customers have engaged with Predictions so far this year, and the product has grown fivefold since July, reaching $56.8 billion in annualized volume traded.

The problem is the price of the pivot. Sales and marketing expense jumped 68% to $605.7 million in the quarter, and the company expects to spend $175 million to $225 million more on Predictions in the fourth quarter alone. DraftKings says its core business will still generate $875 million to $925 million of adjusted EBITDA in 2026; the entire guidance cut is the Predictions investment. The bet is that a prediction-market leader is worth a year of profits.

WHY IT MATTERSA guidance cut the market read as a strategy tax

The 24% after-hours fall is the market pricing the pivot, not the quarter. Revenue missed by about 5% and the adjusted loss per share of $0.53 was far worse than the roughly $0.21 to $0.36 loss analysts expected, but the guidance cut is what reset the valuation: three-quarters of the year's expected adjusted EBITDA, gone in one release.

Management tried to soften the blow by introducing 2027 guidance at the same time: revenue of $7.35 billion to $7.75 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $800 million to $1.0 billion, including $650 million to $750 million of Predictions revenue. Whether the market buys the 2027 story depends on the earnings call on Friday morning and on whether Predictions revenue starts to show up in the fourth quarter instead of being erased by promotions.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA profitable core paying for a prediction-market land grab

Tokenta Signal's judgment: DraftKings is making the most expensive strategic bet of its history in public. The sportsbook is healthy, with revenue up 31.5% and margins improving, but the company is spending roughly three-quarters of a year's expected profit to buy leadership in prediction markets before rivals lock up the space. The 24% after-hours fall says investors are not yet convinced the prize is worth the price. The 2027 guidance, $800 million to $1.0 billion of adjusted EBITDA, is the number that has to survive contact with the next two quarters.
