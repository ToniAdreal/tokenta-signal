Meta Opens Books on First Euro Bond Sale: Six Tranches, Longest Tenor 38 Years (Update to Flash 190)

Reuters reported on November 4, 2026 that Meta has launched its first euro-denominated bond issue: six tranches with tenors of 4 to 38 years, books open, underwritten by Citigroup and Morgan Stanley. This is the follow-on to flash 190's dollar leg, which priced $15 billion the previous day. Tranche sizes and pricing for the euro leg have not been published, and the $20–25 billion overall financing target remains unconfirmed.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Reuters reported on November 4, 2026 (republished by TradingView, 08:18 UTC) that Meta launched its first euro-denominated bond sale on Wednesday. The lead-manager memo shows the euro bonds in six tranches with tenors from 4 to 38 years, and the books already open. This is Meta's first euro bond issue; all of its previous bonds were denominated in dollars.

Finimize followed on November 4, 2026 (08:54 UTC, relaying Reuters), confirming that Meta has opened books for its first euro bond, with Citigroup and Morgan Stanley running the book. It noted that Alphabet issued €6 billion of bonds this week with demand above €17 billion, indicating European buying interest persists even as sentiment toward tech stocks has cooled somewhat.

Link to flash 190: on November 3, 2026, Meta raised $15 billion through five dollar tranches, with the 10-year spread at 1.60 percentage points, markedly wider than the previous round's 0.90 points, and the bonds settling on November 5. At that time the euro portion had not yet filed final terms. CNBC, relaying Bloomberg, put the overall financing target at $20–25 billion, roughly two-thirds dollars and one-third euros. With the euro leg's books now open, the overall financing's size is beginning to take shape, but pricing is still not complete.

WHY IT MATTERSFrom "possible issuance" to "books open" in a single day

The speed of the euro leg is itself a signal. The preliminary prospectus of November 3 said only that euro notes "may" be issued at about the same time, with no guarantee of issuance. By the morning of November 4 the books were open, showing Meta did not wait for the dollar bonds to settle, but pushed the second leg to market the day after the dollar leg priced.

For the financing as a whole, the dollar leg's $15 billion is secured; euro-leg pricing will decide whether the total can reach the $20–25 billion target range. On the roughly one-third-euro basis, the euro leg would be on the order of several billion dollars — but that is arithmetic on a target basis, not a published issue size, and this item does not state it as fact.

WHY IT MATTERSThe 38-year tranche is the real test

The longest of the six tranches runs 38 years, well beyond the dollar leg's longest 30 years. Ultra-long-dated bonds test European pension and insurance money's confidence in the credit of an AI-heavy issuer decades out, and test the market's patience with AI infrastructure payback periods.

If the long tranche prices smoothly, it gives other US tech giants a template for funding multi-year AI build-outs in euros. If demand for the long tranche falls short and spreads are forced wider, then together with the dollar leg's widening spreads it would signal that debt-market funding costs for AI infrastructure are rising systemically. The next verifiable milestone is the euro leg's pricing result and final terms filing.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is the euro leg landing, not the headline total

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the signal in this update is not the $20–25 billion target figure, but that the euro leg has actually opened its books. Dollar-leg spreads have already widened noticeably; whether the euro market will take 38-year duration is the next calibration point. Books being open is only the start — until pricing lands, the financing's final size and cost remain undetermined.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

This item is an update to flash 190: the dollar leg was priced on November 3, 2026, while the euro leg had not yet filed final terms at that time. This item records only the euro leg's move from "possible issuance" to "books open." Both pieces of evidence trace back to the same lead-manager memo seen by Reuters: the Reuters article as republished by TradingView and Finimize's piece are both accounts of that memo, and Finimize is not independently reported. The independence of this item is limited to the two accounts agreeing on tranche count, tenors and book status. The size, coupon and spread of each euro tranche have not been published, and books being open is not the same as pricing. Whether the total reaches the $20–25 billion target awaits the pricing result. The figures for Alphabet's €6 billion euro bond and demand above €17 billion are Reuters' account as relayed by Finimize, not Meta's own filing. This article is a summary with links only and does not reproduce the reports' full text. This does not constitute investment advice.
