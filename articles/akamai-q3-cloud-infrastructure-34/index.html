WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Akamai Technologies reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $1.122 billion on November 5, 2026, up 6% year over year. The growth came from the businesses Akamai is pivoting toward: Cloud Infrastructure Services revenue rose 34% to $109 million, and security revenue grew 9% to $622 million. Delivery and other cloud applications revenue declined 3% year over year, continuing the fade of the legacy content-delivery business.

Earnings went the other way. GAAP net income per diluted share fell 21% to $0.77, and non-GAAP earnings per share fell 5% to $1.77. GAAP income from operations fell 35% from the prior-year quarter and adjusted EBITDA decreased 5%, as the cost of scaling cloud infrastructure compressed margins. Cash from operations reached $433 million, or 39% of revenue, in the quarter.

Management paired the results with an aggressive forward outlook. Akamai projects 2027 Cloud Infrastructure Services revenue of $1.1 billion to $1.3 billion and total company revenue growth of 17% to 22% when adjusted for foreign exchange, and expects 2028 total revenue to exceed $7.0 billion. Shares fell 9.4% in the regular session on November 5, closing at $77.84, as the market weighed the margin compression against the growth guide.

WHY IT MATTERSThe pivot is working on the top line and costing margin on the bottom

The strategic story is intact: the cloud infrastructure line Akamai built to serve AI workloads grew 34%, and security, its largest business, grew 9%. Those two lines are now the company. The problem is the transition cost. GAAP operating income fell 35% while revenue rose 6%, which means the infrastructure build is eating profit faster than the new revenue replaces the old delivery margins.

The 2027 guide is the bet that the trade reverses. Projecting 17% to 22% total revenue growth, against 6% this quarter, implies the cloud line scales from $109 million a quarter toward a $1.1 billion to $1.3 billion annual run rate. If the build costs level off as that scale arrives, margins recover; if they do not, Akamai will have grown into a larger, less profitable company.

WHY IT MATTERSA 9.4% drop says the market priced the guide, not the quarter

Akamai beat nothing and missed nothing dramatic on the quarter itself: revenue up 6%, cloud up 34%, earnings down on investment. The sell-off is a verdict on the forward numbers. A 17% to 22% growth guide for 2027, plus a $7 billion-plus revenue target for 2028, reads as a promise that the pivot accelerates sharply, and the market discounted it against a quarter in which margins moved the wrong way.

The tension is familiar across the AI infrastructure buildout: the companies supplying the capacity are spending heavily before the demand fully arrives. Akamai's cash generation, $433 million from operations in the quarter, gives it room to keep investing. The question for the next two quarters is whether cloud infrastructure revenue keeps compounding at 30%-plus rates while the margin decline flattens.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe cloud pivot is real, and the market wants proof it pays

Tokenta Signal's judgment: a 34% jump in cloud infrastructure revenue confirms Akamai's AI-era pivot is finding demand, and security growing 9% keeps the core healthy. But GAAP operating income fell 35% in the same quarter, and the 9.4% share drop is the market asking for proof that the 2027 guide, 17% to 22% growth and a $7 billion-plus 2028, comes with recovering margins rather than just more scale at a lower return.
