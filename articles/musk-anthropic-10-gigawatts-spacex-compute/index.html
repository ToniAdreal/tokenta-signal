Musk: Anthropic Wants Another 10 Gigawatts of SpaceX Compute

At the New York Times DealBook Summit on November 18, 2026, Elon Musk said Anthropic told him it is extremely likely to keep renting compute from SpaceX and that its highest-probability outcome is an incremental 10 gigawatts, more than 30 times its current Colossus 1 deal of over 300 megawatts. Anthropic has not publicly confirmed the figure. The existing agreements run to up to $120.5 billion through 2029, with either side able to terminate after January and March 2027.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On the eve of Anthropic's Nasdaq debut, Elon Musk told the New York Times DealBook Summit on November 18, 2026 that the Claude maker is likely to want far more computing power from SpaceX. Speaking to columnist Andrew Ross Sorkin, Musk praised Anthropic's models first: "They make a great AI. Opus and Fable are great products."

Musk said his conversations with Anthropic pointed away from the speculation that it might end its SpaceX contracts. "I think that it's unlikely that they will do so," he said. "So when I talk to Anthropic, they tell me it is extremely likely that they will continue to rent compute from SpaceX. And in fact that they want significantly more."

The number he put on it: "But they told me that their highest probability outcome is that they would like an incremental 10 gigawatts of compute from SpaceX. So that's a big number." Ten gigawatts is 10,000 megawatts. In May 2026, Anthropic agreed to take all of the capacity at SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center in Memphis, more than 300 megawatts powered by over 220,000 Nvidia chips. An extra 10 gigawatts would be more than 30 times the size of the current deal.

Musk was explicit that nothing is locked in. The deals are "relatively short term deals that either party can terminate," he said, adding: "If I feel Anthropic is doing something really terrible for the world, obviously I would end those contracts." Anthropic's prospectus puts the existing agreements with SpaceX's AI unit at up to $120.5 billion through 2029, with payments reaching about $3.1 billion a month once all contracted capacity is delivered, anticipated in December. The initial periods end in January and March 2027, after which either side can cancel with 90 days' written notice. Anthropic has not publicly confirmed the 10-gigawatt figure.

WHY IT MATTERSA demand signal that cuts against the week's doubt

Investors have spent the week questioning whether AI spending can keep growing at its current pace, with chip stocks under pressure and Nvidia at a 16-month low. A demand signal of this size, from one of the largest buyers of computing power, cuts directly against that worry. It also lands on the eve of the largest IPO on record: Anthropic priced at $83 a share on November 18, raising $81.3 billion, with trading starting November 19 under the ticker ANT.

The signal has to be handled as what it is: Musk's account of a customer's plans, not a signed contract, from a man with an obvious interest in talking up demand for SpaceX's data centers. SpaceX owns xAI, an Anthropic rival, and Musk has been a vocal public critic of the Claude developer, which makes the praise and the disclosure stranger, and more interesting. A desired 10 gigawatts only becomes revenue once it is financed, powered, and built, and power supply is one of the tightest constraints facing the industry.

WHAT TO WATCHConfirmation, contracts, and power

Watch whether Anthropic confirms the figure, or says anything at all about it, once its shares start trading. Markets are likely to want confirmation from Anthropic itself before treating 10 gigawatts as firm, and the first day of trading will be driven more by IPO demand than by second-hand remarks. Watch too whether the 10 gigawatts ever becomes a signed agreement rather than a stated preference.

Watch the contract calendar. The existing agreements' initial periods end in January and March 2027, after which either side can walk away with 90 days' notice. And watch power: SpaceX ended September with 1.7 gigawatts of nameplate compute capacity and expected 2.5 to 3 gigawatts by the end of 2026. Ten gigawatts is nearly six times the September figure, and no region hands out grid capacity on that scale quickly.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe number is huge; the confirmation is missing

Tokenta Signal's judgment: 10 gigawatts is a demand signal, not a deal. Musk is describing a customer's stated preference on the eve of the largest IPO on record, and the scale is real enough to matter: 30 times the current Colossus 1 arrangement, nearly six times SpaceX's entire September compute capacity. But the figure comes from the seller, not the buyer, Anthropic has not confirmed it, and the contracts on the books are short-term and terminable. The honest read is that the world's biggest AI buyer wants vastly more power than anyone can currently supply, and that the binding constraint is no longer demand but grid capacity, financing, and time. Watch for Anthropic's own words, not Musk's retelling of them.
