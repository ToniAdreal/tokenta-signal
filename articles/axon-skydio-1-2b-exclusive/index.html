Axon Bets $1.2 Billion to Become US Police's Only Drone Supplier

On November 5, Axon announced a further $1.2 billion investment in drone company Skydio at a $5.2 billion valuation, raising its stake to 37% and taking exclusive distribution rights for public-safety channels in the US and Canada.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE ANNOUNCEMENT — On November 5, Axon announced the additional investment alongside third-quarter earnings.
  • 02FIRST CLOSE — The first $254.9 million has closed, buying 25 million newly issued Series G preferred shares.
  • 03THE REST — The remaining tranches are expected to close in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Six months ago Skydio had just closed a $110 million Series F at a $4.4 billion post-money valuation. The CEO had wanted to keep the round small. Existing shareholders who wanted to put in more were not let in. This time, Axon's money gives those shareholders an exit.

Skydio stays independent and keeps serving defense and commercial markets. Axon is buying a 37% stake and a channel, not an acquisition.

The US police drone market is shifting. With Chinese makers shut out, a domestic supply chain has become a scarce good. The $1.2 billion buys more than shares. It buys pricing power in this lane.

NOT ALL NEW MONEYOnly Part of the $1.2 Billion Is New Money

The investment splits in two: part buys new shares to fund Skydio, part buys old shares from existing shareholders. The first $254.9 million to close was new shares, at $10.19617 per share.

Axon raised $1.15 billion of zero-coupon convertible notes to fund the deal. The money came from the bond market, the shares from existing holders. Only part of it reaches Skydio's balance sheet.

THE CHANNEL IS THE PRIZEThe Exclusive Channel Is Worth More Than the Equity

Once the deal closes, police, fire and other public-safety agencies in the US and Canada can buy Skydio drones only through Axon. Skydio will no longer sell direct into that channel.

Axon is folding the business into its Drone as First Responder program. Skydio expects about $1 billion in total orders this fiscal year, and Axon puts the US-Canada market at about $13 billion.

SAME DAY, TWO SIGNALSGood News and Bad News on the Same Day

The same day's earnings showed Axon third-quarter revenue of $909 million, up 28% year over year, with software and services revenue up 43%.

On the other side, full-year growth guidance was cut from 32%-34% to about 30%, after three federal counter-drone deployments slipped and about $130 million of revenue moved out of 2026. The shares fell about 14% after hours.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: in the $1.2 billion, the equity is the visible ledger and the exclusive distribution right is the hidden one. When US police buy a drone, there will be only one counter left.
