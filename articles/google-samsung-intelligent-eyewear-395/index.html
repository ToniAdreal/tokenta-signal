Google and Samsung's Intelligent Eyewear Opens for Pre-Order, From $395

The selling point of the glasses is not the hardware specs, it is Gemini living inside the frames. Translation, navigation and recognition without reaching for a phone is what separates them from ordinary glasses. The launch covers only the US and South Korea, and users in China can only watch for now.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The price and style split is clear. The Warby Parker collection has three styles, Alden, Reid and Wren, in 14 variants, starting at $395. The Gentle Monster collection has six styles in 16 variants, priced from $449 to $489. Together the two collections offer 30 designs, split between everyday and fashion lines.

The hardware is a complete carry-with-you setup. A 12MP ultra-wide front camera, open-ear speakers, four microphones, a Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 chip, 2GB of memory and 64GB of storage, running Android XR. It supports Gemini and Bixby, with Bixby available only when paired with a Galaxy phone. Compatible phones include Pixel 7 and newer, Galaxy S and Z series on One UI 9.0 or later, and iPhone 13 Pro and newer.

The battery and weight figures come from the makers. Google says the glasses weigh about 41 grams and run up to 9 hours, with the charging case recharging the frames up to seven times. SamMobile's math puts the total at up to 72 hours with the case, roughly 10 hours a day for a week. On privacy, Gemini blurs bystanders' faces before processing what the glasses see, captured images are not saved to Gemini history and are not used for model training or advertising. An indicator light shows when photos or video are being taken, and covering or switching it off stops recording; each pair also has a physical power switch.

WHY IT MATTERSTranslation and Navigation Are the First Killer App for Glasses

Real-time translation in 70+ languages with automatic language detection, and turn-by-turn navigation in the ear, are nothing new on a phone. On glasses they mean something different. Out on the street, eyes on the road, translation in the ear, hands free of the phone.

Landmark and object recognition completes the set. See a sign, a building, a product, ask a question and get an answer. The value of the glasses is not what they can see, it is that they can answer at once what you are looking at.

WHAT TO WATCHTwo Countries at Launch, and China Can Only Watch for Now

Two things come next. First, real battery life and wearing comfort after the December 10 launch, since the makers' numbers only count once users have the glasses on. Second, whether the launch range widens; activation requires binding a US or South Korean Google account, and users in mainland China cannot use the glasses directly for now.

The privacy terms deserve a closer read than the hardware specs. Bystanders' faces are blurred before processing, captured images stay out of history, training and advertising, and Play Store policy bars using the glasses' camera for social livestreaming and real-time facial recognition. The rules were written first, so the product dares to be worn out the door.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM PHONE TO FACE
From pulling out a phone to lifting the eyes
Translation, navigation and recognition used to mean pulling out a phone; now they happen while wearing glasses. The glasses moved AI from the pocket into the line of sight.
FROM GADGET TO GLASSES
From gadget to everyday glasses
The price covers two collections, everyday and fashion. The strategy is clear: first make a pair of glasses people will actually wear every day, then talk about the AI inside.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISAI Moves Into the Frames, and the Phone Can Stay in the Pocket

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the point of the smart glasses is not another wearable, it is Gemini getting its first entry point that sits on the face. Real-time translation in 70+ languages, in-ear navigation and recognition that answers what you are looking at are each unremarkable on their own; worn together on the face, they are the real change. The hardware is mid-range, 41 grams, 9 hours of battery, a Snapdragon AR1, all the makers' claims and not yet independently tested. The real threshold is the launch range: outside the United States and South Korea, even account activation is out of reach.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The 9-hour battery life, 41-gram weight and translation in 70+ languages are the makers' claims, not independently tested. The launch covers only the United States and South Korea, and users in mainland China cannot use the glasses directly. This does not constitute investment advice.
