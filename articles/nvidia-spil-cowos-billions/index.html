Packaging Ran Short, So Nvidia Is Paying the Supplier Directly

Reuters reported on November 6 that Nvidia is providing financing worth several billion dollars to expand CoWoS advanced packaging capacity at ASE subsidiary SPIL. The AI chip bottleneck has moved from wafer fabrication to packaging.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Reuters reported on November 6 that Nvidia is providing financing worth several billion dollars to expand CoWoS advanced packaging capacity at Silicon Precision Industries (SPIL), a subsidiary of Taiwan's ASE Technology Holding. The information came from an industry executive familiar with the matter, speaking anonymously.

The money will fund new factory buildings and new equipment. Reuters noted that the scale of the support had not previously been reported. CoWoS is a packaging technology that mounts processors, memory, and other chips on a single substrate so they work together at high speed; Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom all use it.

The backdrop is that TSMC's CoWoS capacity is no longer enough to go around. Reuters interviewed more than a dozen industry executives and analysts and concluded that packaging, once an unremarkable link in the chain, is becoming the bottleneck for AI chip shipments.

THE BOTTLENECK MOVESThe Bottleneck Moved From Wafers to Packaging

A chip that is made but cannot be packaged still cannot ship. CoWoS bonds compute chips to high-bandwidth memory; without it, even the strongest GPU is just a bare die.

Research firm Yole forecasts advanced packaging capital spending of nearly $28 billion in 2026, with advanced packaging revenue overtaking traditional packaging for the first time this year. The definition of the bottleneck is being rewritten.

NVIDIA PAYS UPNvidia Is Paying Up Itself

Nvidia is not queuing for capacity; it is directly funding its supplier's expansion. A buyer fronting money for upstream expansion is uncommon in the chip industry, a sign the shortfall can no longer wait for the normal pace of expansion.

The same report has AMD investing more than $10 billion in Taiwan, covering packaging partnerships with ASE, SPIL, and Powertech. Two rivals pouring money into the same link in the chain makes the location of the bottleneck very clear.

BEYOND TSMCNew Supply Beyond TSMC

TSMC still dominates the most advanced CoWoS capacity, but its ceiling is already pushing chip designers to look for a second source. SPIL is that second source.

TSMC is also developing panel-level packaging, CoPoS, and said in April it had built a pilot line, with volume production still years away. CoWoS remains the mainstream technology for the foreseeable future, so the expansion money has to go here first.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISWhoever Solves Packaging First Gets the Shipments

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Nvidia fronting money for a supplier's expansion shows the packaging shortfall can no longer wait for the normal pace. The next battleground of the compute race is not in the fab; it is in the packaging plant.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The financing figure is only described in general terms as several billion dollars, with no precise number. Nvidia did not respond to a request for comment, and ASE said it does not comment on market rumors involving clients. Packaging capacity is only one of the bottlenecks for AI chips, not the whole story. This does not constitute investment advice.
