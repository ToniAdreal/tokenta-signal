Mitsui Chemicals Swallows JSR for $11.6 Billion

On November 9, Mitsui Chemicals announced it will acquire semiconductor materials maker JSR for ¥1.84 trillion, together with the Development Bank of Japan. JSR's photoresist ranks second globally; Mitsui's EUV pellicles and packaging materials complement the other half. The goal is Japan's largest semiconductor materials producer.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

JSR's fiscal year ended March 2026 brought revenue of ¥440.7 billion, core operating profit of ¥62.3 billion, and 6,951 employees.

The target close is the second half of the fiscal year ending March 2028, still subject to competition and foreign investment approvals. The timeline is long, and the variables are many.

Mitsui Chemicals' board approved the deal on November 9 and signed the share purchase agreement the same day. The move was fast, and the market reaction was fast too.

PHOTORESIST × PELLICLEPhotoresist Plus EUV Pellicles

JSR's photoresist ranks second globally, with strengths in both ArF and EUV photoresist. Mitsui Chemicals' EUV pellicles and packaging materials fill exactly the other half.

Together, the goal is Japan's largest semiconductor materials producer. The two companies' product lines barely overlap, so the synergy story holds together.

THE FUNDING STACKWhere the Money Comes From

The total price is ¥1.84 trillion: equity value of ¥1.3921 trillion plus ¥447.9 billion of interest-bearing debt and other items. The Development Bank of Japan is investing ¥200 billion in non-voting preferred shares; Mitsui Chemicals takes all voting rights.

The funding plan is a ¥1.64 trillion bridge loan plus up to ¥150 billion in share issuance, bank loans, and mezzanine financing. Mitsui Chemicals' market value is only ¥664.6 billion, nearly three times smaller than the deal.

THE MARKET OBJECTSThe Market Voted No First

On the day of the news, Mitsui Chemicals shares fell 8.5%. Reuters reports that investors questioned the deal logic and the debt burden.

JSR's life sciences business and non-core businesses will be sold, with a target of restoring a sound financial position within three years. In 2024, JIC took JSR private for about ¥900 billion; two years later it is changing hands at more than double the price.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Synergy Adds Up. The Balance Sheet Does Not.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the product synergy is real, and so is the financial strain. A company a third the size of its target is swallowing a $11.6 billion deal with loans and share issuance. The 8.5% drop on the day wrote the doubt into the price.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

Some aggregated reports carry wrong figures in their text; this article uses Reuters' figures only. The deal still needs competition and foreign investment approvals, and the timetable may change. This does not constitute investment advice.
