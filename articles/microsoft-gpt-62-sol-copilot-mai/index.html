Microsoft Pushes GPT-6.2 Sol to Copilot Worldwide While Its Own MAI Takes Over Traffic Behind It

Microsoft has pushed OpenAI's newest everyday model into Copilot worldwide, while letting its own models take over more traffic behind it.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On November 20, 2026, Microsoft announced on the Copilot blog that OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol and GPT-6.2 Astra are joining Microsoft Copilot. Sol reaches eligible users worldwide, rolling out across Copilot Chat, Cowork, Studio, Word, Excel and PowerPoint.

Astra's rollout is far narrower. Full rollout covers only Cowork and Studio; reaching Chat and Office apps requires joining the Frontier early-access program. Administrators control the switches in the Microsoft 365 admin center, and progress varies by region and organization.

The two-track setup follows the partnership agreement revised on April 27, 2026. Microsoft no longer pays OpenAI revenue share, and the exclusivity has ended. At the Ignite conference the same week, Microsoft introduced its own models including MAI Code 2, starting to keep more traffic on its own models.

WHY IT MATTERSSol for Everyone, Astra for Power Users

Sol enters every Copilot surface under a standard license, while Astra stays in Cowork, Studio and Frontier users' hands. The split is drawn on cost: Astra's API price is 4 times Sol's.

OpenAI's public price list puts Sol at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and Astra at $8 and $40. Heavy models are placed only in scenarios where users pay by usage.

WHAT TO WATCHTraffic MAI Takes Over Pays No Share

Microsoft's own MAI series debuted at Ignite, and MAI Code 2 is already in GitHub Copilot. Microsoft says it reaches about 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's performance at 40% of the cost. Those numbers come from the vendor's own claims, with no independent review.

Every request that moves from an OpenAI model to an MAI model saves Microsoft one revenue-share payment. Copilot still looks like OpenAI's newest models on the surface, while the traffic split underneath is shifting bit by bit.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM EXCLUSIVE CHANNEL TO TWO TRACKS
From Exclusive Channel to Two Tracks
Microsoft is no longer OpenAI's exclusive distribution channel. OpenAI's newest models still enter Copilot as before, while Microsoft's own MAI takes over the traffic it can behind them.
FROM SEAT LICENSES TO METERED USE
From Seat Licenses to Metered Use
Astra enters Cowork and Studio on usage-based billing, with heavy use charged directly per token. Copilot's bill is moving from fixed licenses toward metered pricing.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISMicrosoft Pushes GPT-6.2 Sol to Copilot Worldwide While Its Own MAI Takes Over Traffic Behind It

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Microsoft has pushed OpenAI's newest everyday model into Copilot worldwide, while letting its own models take over more traffic behind it. Two tracks running in parallel, and the accounting matters more than the models themselves.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

Sol's rollout is still under way, and Microsoft has given no completion date; the model choices tenants see may lag the blog post. MAI performance and cost figures come from Microsoft's own claims, without independent verification. Revenue-share arrangements follow the revised agreement between the two sides; Microsoft still pays OpenAI revenue share until 2030, with a cap.
