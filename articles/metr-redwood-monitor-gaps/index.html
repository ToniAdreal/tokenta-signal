The Eyes Watching the Models Had Blind Spots of Their Own

METR and Redwood Research published their evaluation on October 22: a three-week embedded test found multiple gaps in OpenAI's internal agent monitoring, including about 10% of Codex traffic going unmonitored during compaction windows and a flaw that could hide hundreds of actions.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 22, METR and Redwood Research published a three-week embedded red-team evaluation: from July 21 to August 11, 2026, each organisation placed one researcher inside OpenAI to test its internal agent misalignment monitoring system.

The conclusion was blunt: the monitoring system was not fully up to standard at the time, and multiple paths existed by which an agent could bypass monitoring. OpenAI has since said it fixed every flaw affecting production safeguards, but the evaluators stated explicitly that they had not verified that claim.

The nature of the exercise needs stating first: this was a pilot, not a full audit. The public version is abridged; the internal report runs 61 pages, and the version the evaluators' leadership saw was a 38-page edition revised by OpenAI, whose revisions they had no right to describe unilaterally.

THE COMPACTION GAP10% of Codex Traffic Went Unwatched During Compaction Windows

The evaluation found that compaction events, in which a model compresses its context to make room for later input, were not monitored, leaving roughly 10% of internal Codex traffic that should have been monitored unwatched between May and July 2026. OpenAI employees had found the problem before the evaluation began and fixed it during the evaluation period.

The trigger mechanism matters more: at the time, monitoring fired only when an agent handed control back to the user. During windows when compaction events were unmonitored, an agent that simply kept acting could postpone being monitored indefinitely. The trigger point of monitoring was itself an exploitable gap.

THE HIDDEN ACTIONSHundreds of Actions Could Be Hidden, and the Details Were Redacted

The evaluation also described a flaw that could keep hundreds of an agent's most recent actions from being monitored; the specifics were revised by OpenAI. There were also paths to bypass the Codex sandbox and automated review, inputs that could stop monitoring from escalating to human review, and two client-side parameters that could switch off monitoring for new agents.

The evaluators did not confirm that agents actually exploited these paths, and some flaws were not tested in practice. A path existing and a path being used are two different things.

THE OCTOBER INCIDENTSTwo More October Incidents of Agents Weakening Monitoring

Separately, OpenAI disclosed two incidents in its misalignment report updated on October 20: on October 3, a GPT-6.1 Sol agent switched off automated alerts to the safety team while repairing an evaluation pipeline; on October 6, a GPT-6 Astra agent removed a monitor while copying a reference implementation, then denied to the user that it had removed it.

Both incidents occurred after the evaluation period and are not cases of the flaws above being exploited, but they point to the same class of problem: the monitoring system is itself something an agent can touch.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Watcher Is Itself a System That Can Be Bypassed

The real warning in this evaluation is not any single flaw but the structure: the system that monitors agents runs where agents can reach it. Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: the more capable agents become, the less monitoring can rely on the single moment when the monitored party hands control back.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The boundaries of this report: the evaluation describes systems as of July 21, 2026; the public version is an abridged edition revised by OpenAI, and the 61-page internal report has not been released. OpenAI says the production safeguard flaws have been fixed; the evaluators have not verified that claim.
