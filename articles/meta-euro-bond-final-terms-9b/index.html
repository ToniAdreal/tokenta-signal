Meta Prices Its Debut Euro Bond at 9 Billion Euros, Closing the Record 53 Billion Euro Book (Update to Flashes 193 and 202)

Meta priced its debut euro bond sale at 9 billion euros on November 4, 2026, per its final term sheet reproduced by AIStockWire, against record 53 billion euro orders. The six tranches run from 2031 at 4.30 percent to 2065 at 6.40 percent, settle November 12, and bring the two-day dollar-plus-euro total to about 25.1 billion dollars. This closes the open pricing item in flashes 193 and 202.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Meta priced its debut euro bond sale at 9 billion euros on November 4, 2026, according to its final term sheet as reproduced by AIStockWire, closing the pricing item left open in flashes 193 and 202. The sale drew record orders of 53 billion euros, Bloomberg News reported, as carried by Reuters, nearly six times the amount sold.

The euro leg is split into six tranches: 1.75 billion euros due 2031 at 4.30 percent (103 basis points over mid-swaps), 1.25 billion euros due 2033 at 4.65 percent (135 points), 1.00 billion euros due 2035 at 4.90 percent (160 points), 1.75 billion euros due 2038 at 5.40 percent (203 points), 1.75 billion euros due 2044 at 6.05 percent (263 points), and 1.50 billion euros due 2065 at 6.40 percent (333 points). The notes settle on November 12 and carry the same ratings as the dollar notes.

Combined with the 15 billion dollar five-part sale priced on November 3, the two-day financing totals about 25.1 billion dollars at the European Central Bank reference rate of 1.1267 dollars per euro, by AIStockWire's calculation, at the top of the 20 to 25 billion dollar range CNBC reported. AIStockWire calculates the euro notes will cost Meta about 479 million euros a year in interest, against about 990 million dollars a year for the dollar notes, which settle on November 5.

AIWeekly, relaying Bloomberg, independently confirms the 9 billion euro priced size against the 53 billion euro order book, and reports investors demanded roughly 1.4 times the risk premium Alphabet paid on a similar euro deal this week. Citigroup and Morgan Stanley managed the euro transaction, as in the book-opening account.

WHY IT MATTERSPricing, not orders, is the verdict on the debt market's mood

The order book said Europe's bond market wanted Meta paper. The pricing says what it charged for it. The shortest tranche priced at 103 basis points over mid-swaps against initial guidance of about 140, and the longest at 333 against about 370, so the record book did tighten spreads from guidance, but the absolute coupons still run from 4.30 percent to 6.40 percent on a debut euro credit.

That is the same pattern as the dollar leg a day earlier, which priced 57 to 88 basis points wider than Meta's April sale across comparable maturities. Demand is deep and the price of that demand is rising at the same time, which is exactly the repricing story flashes 193 and 202 flagged as the thing to watch.

WHY IT MATTERSA 38-year tranche asks buyers to underwrite AI spending for a generation

The 2065 tranche, 1.50 billion euros at 6.40 percent, extends eight years beyond the dollar leg's longest 30-year note and asks European long-duration buyers to hold an AI-heavy credit for nearly four decades. Its 333-basis-point spread is the widest in the deal, and it is the tranche that most directly prices uncertainty about whether today's AI capital spending turns into durable cash flow.

For Meta, the two-day total of about 25.1 billion dollars lands on top of 84 billion dollars of bonds already outstanding at September 30, plus the joint-venture data-center debt and roughly 355.73 billion dollars of not-yet-started leases AIStockWire tracks from the third-quarter filing. The euro debut diversifies the funding base, but it does not change the leverage direction.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISDeep demand, rising price: both halves of the story are now confirmed

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the final terms confirm both halves of the story at once. Europe's bond market genuinely wants Meta paper at record scale, and it is charging more for it than it did in April, in dollars and in euros alike. The 38-year tranche is the honest test of long-horizon confidence, and its 6.40 percent coupon says that confidence is real but no longer cheap. The open question moves from whether Meta can fund the AI build-out to what the rising coupon does to the returns on that spending.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

This item is an update to flashes 193 and 202: it closes the open pricing item those flashes left unresolved. The 53 billion euro figure is the order book, not the amount sold; the amount sold in the euro leg is 9 billion euros. The combined 25.1 billion dollar figure is AIStockWire's calculation at the European Central Bank reference rate of 1.1267 dollars per euro, not a figure Meta itself published. An earlier single account putting the euro leg at 8.25 billion euros is superseded by the final term sheet figures reproduced here. The dollar leg's 15 billion dollars and the euro leg's 9 billion euros use different benchmarks (Treasuries versus mid-swaps) and their spreads are not directly comparable.
