NiCE AI Revenue Jumps 46% to $392 Million as Q3 Beats Estimates and Workforce Is Cut 5%

NiCE's third-quarter revenue rose 8.0% to $790.7 million and non-GAAP EPS of $2.84 beat estimates, with AI annual recurring revenue up 46% to $392 million. The company raised full-year EPS guidance to $11.12-11.32 while announcing a workforce reduction of approximately 5% as it streamlines around AI.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE ANNOUNCEMENT — NiCE released third-quarter 2026 results on November 4, 2026: revenue of $790.7 million, up 8.0%, and AI ARR of $392 million, up 46%.
  • 02THE CONFIRMATION — Seeking Alpha confirmed the beat on revenue and non-GAAP EPS and the raised full-year EPS guidance to $11.12-11.32.
  • 03THE WATCHPOINT — Whether AI ARR keeps growing near 46%, whether the approximately 5% workforce reduction delivers the promised efficiency, and whether full-year EPS lands in the raised $11.12-11.32 range.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

NiCE's third-quarter 2026 results, released on November 4, 2026, put total revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 at $790.7 million, up 8.0% year over year from $732.0 million. Cloud revenue was $622.7 million, up 10.6%. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $2.84, down from $3.18 a year earlier, while GAAP diluted EPS was $1.60, down from $2.29.

The AI line is the standout: AI annual recurring revenue reached $392 million, up 46% year over year and equal to 16% of cloud revenue, with international revenue up 33% year over year. Seeking Alpha confirmed the same day that revenue of $790.7 million beat estimates by $4.96 million and non-GAAP EPS of $2.84 beat by $0.06, and that the company raised full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance while reiterating revenue guidance.

NiCE raised full-year 2026 non-GAAP diluted EPS guidance to $11.12-11.32 and reiterated revenue guidance of $3,170 million to $3,190 million. In the same release it announced a plan to streamline its operating model by leveraging AI, reducing its global workforce by approximately 5%, with pre-tax restructuring charges of approximately $15-20 million expected primarily in the fourth quarter of 2026. Excluding an atypical $10 million customer credit, revenue growth would have been 9.4% and cloud revenue growth 12.2%.

WHY IT MATTERSAI is now 16% of cloud revenue, and growing six times faster than the total

Total revenue up 8% is solid but unremarkable; AI recurring revenue up 46% to $392 million is the number that changes the shape of the company. At 16% of cloud revenue, AI is no longer a feature line, it is a material revenue stream, and its growth rate is roughly six times the company's overall pace.

That mix shift is also the logic behind the workforce cut: NiCE said it is streamlining by leveraging AI, cutting management layers and shifting to partner-led delivery. The company is, in effect, using AI both as a product it sells and as a reason to run leaner, and the $15-20 million restructuring charge is the price it booked for that transition.

WHY IT MATTERSA profit beat with a profit decline needs both halves stated

NiCE beat the high end of its own guidance ranges and raised full-year EPS guidance, yet non-GAAP EPS fell from $3.18 to $2.84 and GAAP net income fell 34% to $95.1 million. Both facts are true, and either alone misleads.

The gap is partly explained by the $10 million atypical customer credit, which shaved about 1.4 points off revenue growth, and by margin compression: GAAP gross margin fell to 62.4% from 66.8%. The raised EPS guide signals management confidence in the back half, but the year-over-year profit decline shows the AI transition is not yet paying for itself in earnings.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is AI paying for the restructuring, not yet for earnings

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the signal in NiCE's print is that AI revenue is growing fast enough to justify a restructuring, but not yet fast enough to lift earnings. At $392 million and 46% growth, AI is now a real engine inside a $790.7 million quarter. The test for the next two quarters is whether that engine, plus a leaner 5%-smaller workforce, turns the raised EPS guide into actual profit growth rather than another quarter of beating guidance while year-over-year earnings fall.
