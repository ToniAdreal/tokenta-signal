WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Amazon is in advanced talks to acquire Israeli AI startup Decart for about $7.5 billion, with a deal possible as early as next week, the Financial Times reported on Saturday, November 21, 2026. The Information reported the same $7.5 billion figure the same day. Neither Amazon nor Decart has confirmed the talks, and the Financial Times said the discussions could still fall apart.

The price has climbed since the talks first surfaced. The Wall Street Journal reported on October 11, 2026 that Amazon was negotiating a purchase of about $7 billion. Calcalist reported on November 18, 2026 that the structure exceeds $7 billion, combining cash, stock and payments tied to Decart's performance, with the three founders staying for five years and a large payout deferred to year five.

Decart was founded in September 2023 by Moshe Shalev and brothers Dean and Orian Leitersdorf. The company was valued at about $4 billion in May 2026 after raising $300 million backed by investors including Sequoia Capital and Nvidia, and has raised more than $400 million in total. Its main software product helps developers run AI workloads across different chip architectures, and it is developing the Lucy and Oasis world models, which can live-edit video and create interactive virtual worlds.

WHY IT MATTERSThe price is a chip-strategy premium, not a revenue multiple

Decart's value to Amazon is technical, not commercial scale. The startup was an early adopter of Amazon's Trainium3 chips, the processors Amazon built to rival Nvidia's GPUs, and its software helps developers move AI work between chip architectures. For AWS, owning that tooling would make its home-grown silicon easier to adopt at a moment when customers are desperate for alternatives to Nvidia supply.

The $7.5 billion figure is roughly double Decart's May valuation, and it sits above the roughly $6 billion acquisition Anthropic negotiated before those talks fell through in September 2026. Amazon has been a strategic customer of Decart since 2025, so the buyer already knows the product. The premium reads as payment for a scarce bridge between AWS silicon and the wider AI software stack, not for Decart's current revenue.

WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWNThe structure says Amazon is buying the founders as much as the code

The reported structure, cash plus stock plus earnouts with the founders locked in for five years, is designed to keep the team that built the chip-portability software inside AWS while the payout depends on what Decart achieves afterwards. That makes the headline number conditional: part of the $7.5 billion is only paid if performance targets are met.

What is not yet known is whether the talks close at all, whether the announcement lands at AWS re:Invent on November 30, 2026, or how much of the price is earnout rather than upfront. The Financial Times frames the deal as strengthening AWS's bet against Nvidia; the test of that framing will be whether Trainium adoption measurably widens once Decart's tooling is inside AWS.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISAWS is buying its way around Nvidia, and paying up for it

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the consequential part of this deal is not the $7.5 billion, it is what the money buys. AWS has spent years building Trainium as an alternative to Nvidia GPUs, and the hard part was never the silicon, it was getting developers' software to run well on it. Decart's chip-portability tooling is the missing layer, and Amazon, already a customer since 2025, is now paying roughly double the startup's May valuation to own it. The claim to discount is any version that treats the price as settled, because part of it is earnouts and the talks can still collapse. The claim to act on is the direction: every major cloud provider is now buying or building its way out of single-supplier dependence on Nvidia, and the next test is whether Trainium adoption actually widens once Decart is inside AWS.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

Amazon and Decart have not confirmed the deal, and the Financial Times reports the talks could still fall apart; the $7.5 billion figure is a reported price, not a confirmed one, and part of it may be tied to performance targets rather than paid at closing. The Wall Street Journal reported about $7 billion on October 11, 2026, and Calcalist reported on November 18, 2026 that the structure exceeds $7 billion with cash, stock and earnouts. Whether the deal closes, and on what terms, is not yet known. This does not constitute investment advice.
