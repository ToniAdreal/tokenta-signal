Arm Posts Record $1.44B Quarter as Licensing Jumps 50% and Hyperscaler Share Passes 50%

Arm reported record fiscal second-quarter revenue of $1.44 billion, up 27% year over year, beating the $1.38 billion estimate, as licensing revenue jumped 50% to $773 million while royalty revenue of $670 million missed estimates. Arm said its architecture now powers more than 50% of top-hyperscaler server deployments, up from 15% in 2024, and guided third-quarter revenue of $1.54 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Arm reported second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $1.44 billion on November 4, 2026, up 27% year over year and the company's highest ever second quarter, marking a fifth consecutive quarter of total revenue growth above 20%. Adjusted profit of $0.52 per share beat the $0.47 estimate, and revenue beat the $1.38 billion average analyst estimate compiled by LSEG. Shares slipped about 2% in extended trading despite the beat.

The split inside the beat ran in opposite directions. Licensing and other revenue reached $773 million, up 50% year over year and well above the $625.1 million estimate, a leading indicator of future royalty growth. Royalty revenue reached $670 million, up 8% year over year but below the $720.8 million estimate, even as data center royalties doubled year over year. Arm's partners shipped more than 10 billion Arm-based chips in the quarter for the first time, up 1.5 billion year over year, on a platform used by more than 30 million developers.

The quarter's strategic headline was servers. Arm said its architecture now accounts for more than 50% of deployments at the top hyperscalers in calendar 2025, up from 15% in calendar 2024, and expects that share to exceed 60% by the end of calendar 2026, citing ramps across AWS Graviton, Google Axion, Microsoft Cobalt and NVIDIA platforms. Reuters noted the same claim and the reason behind it: power efficiency as operators seek to rein in the energy and cooling costs of running large AI models. Arm-based PCs have now reached approximately 20% of global PC shipments.

Arm's newer silicon business also moved forward. Demand for the Arm AGI CPU is described as extremely strong, Arm's confidence in achieving up to $2 billion in AGI CPU revenue over fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028 has increased over the past 90 days, purchase orders extend through calendar 2027, and the first commercial deliveries are on track for the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027. Oracle Cloud Infrastructure plans to offer AGI CPU instances in 2027, and independent testing by Signal65 showed approximately 2x the performance per rack versus AMD EPYC 9755. Arm also introduced Neoverse CSS N4, delivering up to 2x performance per socket and 25% better performance per watt than the prior generation, and launched Arm Total Design for Physical AI with more than 80 companies. Physical AI royalty revenue grew 50% year over year.

For the third quarter of fiscal 2027 Arm projected revenue of $1.54 billion, against the $1.53 billion average analyst estimate. Reuters added one legal footnote from the same period: last month a Delaware jury found Arm breached parts of its licensing agreement with Qualcomm and interfered with Qualcomm's relationship with Meta, but awarded only $2 in damages, leaving key disputes over royalties and future licensing terms to be decided by a judge.

WHY IT MATTERSLicensing is the leading indicator, royalties are the lagging proof

The quarter's tension is the gap between the two revenue lines. Licensing up 50% says customers are committing to Arm designs at a record pace, which should convert into royalty streams as those chips ship. Royalties up only 8% and below estimate says that conversion has not yet arrived at the same speed, even with data center royalties doubling. The stock's after-hours slip reads as the market pricing that gap rather than the headline beat.

The server-share claim is the number to test. Moving from 15% of top-hyperscaler deployments in 2024 to more than 50% in 2025, with more than 60% projected by the end of 2026, would make Arm the default CPU architecture of the AI data center. It is Arm's own characterization of top hyperscalers, not the whole server market, and it rests on four named ramps: Graviton, Axion, Cobalt and NVIDIA's Vera. If those ramps hold, the royalty line should follow the licensing line upward in coming quarters.

WHY IT MATTERSWhat to discount and what to test

Discount the headline beat until the split is stated: total revenue beat because licensing overwhelmed a royalty miss, and adjusted profit excludes items the release does not detail in the summary. Discount the hyperscaler share claim the same way: it is a deployment share at top cloud providers, not a revenue share of the server market.

Test three things: whether third-quarter revenue lands near the $1.54 billion projection, whether AGI CPU commercial deliveries begin in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027 as planned, and whether royalty growth accelerates toward the licensing growth rate as the new designs ship. The Qualcomm licensing dispute is a separate overhang: a $2 jury award looks trivial, but the unresolved royalty and licensing terms to be decided by a judge are not.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is licensing racing ahead of royalties

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Arm's quarter matters less as a beat and more as a timing story. Licensing up 50% is customers buying into Arm's roadmap at a record pace, and the hyperscaler share claim, if it holds, puts Arm at the center of AI data-center compute. The claim to discount is any single revenue number without its split, and the claim to test is conversion: whether royalty growth catches up to licensing growth as Graviton, Axion, Cobalt and Vera ramps ship, and whether AGI CPU deliveries begin on schedule. The Qualcomm dispute remains an unresolved overhang despite the $2 award.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this report rest on Arm's official November 4, 2026 newsroom summary of its second-quarter fiscal 2027 results and on Reuters' same-day report of those results. The quarterly figures are preliminary and unaudited. Licensing and royalty revenue are two different streams and must not be conflated: licensing is upfront fees for access to Arm technology, royalty is payment per chip shipped. Third-quarter guidance and all market-share projections, AGI CPU revenue expectations and delivery dates are forward-looking and actual results may vary. The Delaware jury item is reported as Reuters described it and is not part of Arm's results release. This article is a summary with links only and does not reproduce either source's full text. This does not constitute investment advice.
