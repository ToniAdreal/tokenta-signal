GlobalFoundries Posts Record 31.4% Margin as AI Packaging Deals Stack Up: $2B TSMC Pact, $21B U.S.-Japan Fabs

GlobalFoundries reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $1.899 billion, up 13% year over year, with a record non-IFRS gross margin of 31.4% driven by AI and data-center demand. The release also disclosed a $2 billion TSMC silicon-interposer agreement, a $955 million CHIPS award reallocation, and a U.S.-Japan fab project with up to $21 billion in funding, while guiding fourth-quarter revenue of $2.0 billion plus or minus $25 million.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

GlobalFoundries reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $1.899 billion on November 4, 2026, up 13% year over year from $1.688 billion and up 6% sequentially from $1.786 billion. Non-IFRS gross margin hit a record 31.4%, up 540 basis points year over year, while IFRS gross margin was 30.1%. Non-IFRS net income was $298 million and non-IFRS diluted EPS was $0.53, up 29% year over year, while non-IFRS adjusted EBITDA reached $647 million at a 34.1% margin.

The IFRS bottom line tells a different story and the release explains why: a net loss of $270 million and diluted EPS of negative $0.49, driven primarily by a $441 million non-cash charge recorded in September 2026 when GF issued 9.9 million ordinary shares to the U.S. government. Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities ended the quarter at $3.2 billion, operating cash flow was $341 million, and non-IFRS adjusted free cash flow was $136 million.

CEO Tim Breen pointed to record year-over-year revenue growth in communications infrastructure and data center, plus strong growth in automotive and home and industrial IoT, as the mix shift behind the record margin. Earnings-call coverage put numbers on that shift: communications infrastructure and data center revenue up 81% year over year, its eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth, at roughly 17% of total revenue, with silicon photonics revenue expected at $500 million in 2026 and exiting 2027 at a $1 billion annualized run rate.

The release's business highlights read as a U.S. advanced-packaging build-out. In October 2026 GF was selected as technology provider and operator for two new semiconductor fabs at its Malta, New York site under a U.S.-Japan initiative providing up to $21 billion in funding, with an initial investment of approximately $10 billion accelerating construction of a new 300mm fab already underway and capacity expected online in 2029. The same month GF and the U.S. government agreed to reallocate $955 million of its CHIPS Act award to Malta and Burlington, Vermont, for silicon photonics, silicon germanium and advanced packaging, including the first U.S.-based source of silicon interposers.

Also in October 2026, GF signed a multi-year, $2 billion manufacturing agreement with TSMC to establish a U.S.-based supply of silicon interposers for TSMC's CoWoS advanced packaging ecosystem, adding capacity at Malta with volume production expected to ramp in the first half of 2028. In September 2026 GF expanded its multi-year agreement with Marvell to add silicon germanium capacity at Burlington for pluggable optical transceivers, near-packaged optics and co-packaged optics, and signed a long-term agreement with Monolithic Power Systems to deploy MPS process technology at its Singapore 300mm fab, with volume production planned for early 2027.

For the fourth quarter GF guided revenue of $2.0 billion plus or minus $25 million, non-IFRS gross margin of approximately 31.5% plus or minus 100 basis points, and non-IFRS diluted EPS of $0.59 plus or minus $0.05. Earnings-call coverage added that GF plans approximately $4 billion of net capital expenditure in 2027, with about one-third supported by customer prepayments. The board also approved a dividend of $0.12 per share payable on January 14, 2027 to shareholders of record as of December 22, 2026.

WHY IT MATTERSThe margin record is a mix story, not a volume story

Revenue grew 13% while non-IFRS gross profit grew 36%, and that gap is the whole quarter. GF is selling more of its differentiated technologies, silicon photonics, silicon germanium and advanced packaging, into AI and data-center connectivity, and less of its legacy smart-mobile-device base. Earnings-call coverage noted that revenue outside smart mobile devices passed two-thirds of the total for the first time, with that segment falling to about 32% of revenue. A foundry that once lived on handset volumes is re-rating itself on AI interconnect content.

The TSMC agreement is the sharpest proof point. A $2 billion multi-year pact to make silicon interposers in the United States for TSMC's CoWoS ecosystem puts GF inside the packaging chain that every leading AI accelerator depends on, and it pairs with the $955 million CHIPS reallocation aimed at the same capability. Combined with the U.S.-Japan fab project, GF is positioning Malta as a domestic source for the layer of AI hardware that sits between chips, not just as another fab site.

WHY IT MATTERSWhat to discount and what to test

Discount the headline loss and the headline margin equally until the basis is stated: the IFRS loss is dominated by a one-off non-cash government-share charge, and the record margin is a non-IFRS figure that strips out share-based compensation, structural optimization and acquisition charges. The cleaner read is the direction of travel, both bases improved sequentially, with IFRS gross margin up 180 basis points and non-IFRS gross margin up 150 basis points from the second quarter.

Test delivery on three dates: whether the Malta 300mm fab capacity comes online in 2029 as planned, whether TSMC interposer volume ramps in the first half of 2028, and whether fourth-quarter revenue lands in the $1.975 billion to $2.025 billion guided range. The 2027 capital plan of approximately $4 billion, as described on the call, is the funding test behind all three.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is a foundry re-rating itself on AI interconnect

Tokenta Signal's judgment: GlobalFoundries' quarter matters less as an earnings beat and more as evidence that the AI bottleneck has moved into packaging and interconnect. The record margin came from selling photonics, silicon germanium and interposers into data centers, and the TSMC pact plus the U.S.-Japan fabs lock that mix shift into multi-year capacity. The claim to discount is any single margin number without its basis, IFRS or non-IFRS, and the claim to test is delivery: Malta capacity in 2029, interposer volume in the first half of 2028, and a fourth quarter inside the guided range.
