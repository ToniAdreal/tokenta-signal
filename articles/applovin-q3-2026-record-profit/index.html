AppLovin Q3: Revenue Up 55% to $2.185B as 81% of New Sales Becomes Profit

AppLovin reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of about $2.185 billion, up 55% year over year, with net income up 72% to about $1.439 billion and earnings of $4.30 per share beating expectations, as about 81% of added revenue became operating income; the company guided fourth-quarter revenue of $2.365 billion to $2.395 billion and added $3.2 billion to its buyback authorization.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

AppLovin reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of about $2.185 billion on November 4, 2026, up 55% from $1.41 billion a year earlier and above the roughly $2.1 billion analysts expected. Net income climbed 72% to about $1.439 billion, from $836 million, and diluted earnings of $4.30 per share beat the roughly $4.03 analysts projected. Adjusted EBITDA reached $1.842 billion, an 84% margin, and free cash flow rose 35% to about $1.42 billion, from $1.05 billion. Shares jumped in after-hours trading after ending the regular session up more than 10%.

The quarter's standout ratio is operating leverage: about 81% of the added revenue became operating income, even as research spending more than doubled. Total expenses rose only about $150 million, because running more ad spend through the same software costs little extra. That leverage is what funded the heavier research budget without squeezing margins. The board also approved a $3.2 billion increase to the share repurchase authorization, bringing the total available to $3.6 billion as of the end of October.

Cash tells a slightly different story than profit. Free cash flow grew about half as fast as net income, with cash paid for income taxes in the first nine months more than five times last year's level and rising receivables tying up cash as sales grow. For the fourth quarter AppLovin guided revenue of $2.365 billion to $2.395 billion, implying roughly 9% sequential growth, with adjusted EBITDA margin expected to stay near this quarter's level.

WHY IT MATTERSSoftware economics, with a tax bill attached

AppLovin's quarter is a textbook software-margin print: revenue up 55%, expenses up barely at all, and 81 cents of each new revenue dollar falling to operating income. That is the AI-advertising flywheel working as pitched, with the company's models placing ads more efficiently and the incremental cost of serving more impressions staying near zero.

The gap between net income growth of 72% and free cash flow growth of 35% is the number to watch rather than a contradiction. A fivefold jump in cash taxes and receivables growth are normal growing pains of a company scaling this fast, but they cap how much of the paper profit converts to cash in the quarter. The $3.2 billion buyback increase signals management's confidence that the conversion gap narrows rather than widens.

WHY IT MATTERSWhat to discount and what to test

Discount the 84% adjusted EBITDA margin as a cash proxy: it excludes stock-based compensation and other items, and the release gives no revenue split showing how much growth comes from outside gaming, a disclosure gap The Motley Fool flagged. Discount after-hours share moves the same way: outlets printed different magnitudes for the same session.

Test three things: whether fourth-quarter revenue lands in the $2.365 billion to $2.395 billion guided range, whether free cash flow growth catches up to earnings growth next quarter, and whether the company starts breaking out advertiser mix beyond gaming.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe ad machine prints profit faster than it prints cash

Tokenta Signal's judgment: AppLovin's quarter is the purest software-leverage print of this earnings season, with 81% of new revenue reaching operating income. The story to test is not demand, which the guidance covers, but conversion: whether cash flow catches up to earnings once the tax and receivables drag normalizes, and whether growth beyond gaming gets disclosed.
