Microsoft 365 Outage Passes 21 Hours as 14:00 UTC Update Reports Email Queues Nearing Normal

Update to flashes 331 through 344: at 14:00 UTC on November 13, Microsoft said it is continuing to see improvements in service availability, with email queues significantly reduced and approaching normal levels, although some users may still experience intermittent access issues or delayed email delivery as recovery continues; it has implemented additional mitigation measures and is restoring the remaining affected infrastructure while investigating residual service issues, closely monitoring service health and validating recovery across different connection methods to ensure sustained service stability; no evidence of malicious activity, with a post-incident report promised once mitigated. Microsoft's public status page, read live between about 14:08 and 14:14 UTC, shows "Last updated: Friday, November 13, 2026 at 2:00:09 PM UTC"; a second mirror, Pingoru, carries the identical text, and Naples News quoted the same 9 a.m. ET update. The University of Pennsylvania mirror generated at 14:05 UTC still carried the 12:59:33 UTC entry, its refresh not yet having picked up the new one. The 12:58 UTC update had said broader improvements in North America, sustained improvements across much of Europe and encouraging signals in Asia Pacific; this is the first update to quantify the email queues draining. The outage, open since 16:17 UTC on November 12, is past 21 hours and 43 minutes with no root cause published and no fix declared; the next update is due by 15:00 UTC.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Microsoft's global Microsoft 365 outage passed 21 hours and 43 minutes with still no fix declared, and its newest update changed the yardstick: for the first time it quantified the email backlog instead of naming another region. At 14:00 UTC on November 13, 2026, Microsoft posted a fresh service-health update for incidents EX1550543 and MO1550821: it is continuing to see improvements in service availability, with email queues significantly reduced and approaching normal levels. Some users may still experience intermittent access issues or delayed email delivery as recovery continues. Microsoft said it has implemented additional mitigation measures and is restoring the remaining affected infrastructure while investigating residual service issues. It is closely monitoring service health and validating recovery across different connection methods to ensure sustained service stability. It said it has no evidence of malicious activity, and that once mitigated it will publish a full post-incident report with the root cause and defense-in-depth actions. The next update is due by 15:00 UTC.

Microsoft's public status page, read live between about 14:08 and 14:14 UTC, carries the update with the timestamp "Last updated: Friday, November 13, 2026 at 2:00:09 PM UTC". A second mirror, Pingoru, carries the identical update text, and Naples News quoted the same 9 a.m. ET update. The incident opened at 16:17 UTC on November 12, when Exchange Online users lost mailbox access, and widened at 18:26 UTC into a Microsoft 365 advisory covering Outlook, Teams, SharePoint Online, Copilot, Defender, Purview, Planner, To Do, Intune and Power BI. Microsoft has twice reported servers returning to service and then becoming unresponsive again once they started taking traffic, at 00:00 UTC and again at 04:22 UTC. No root cause has been published, and no time for full recovery has been given.

The 14:00 UTC update matters because it is the first to describe the email queues themselves draining rather than another region improving. The 12:58 UTC update had named broader improvements in North America, sustained improvements across much of Europe and encouraging signals in Asia Pacific; this one says the queues are significantly reduced and approaching normal levels. But the qualifiers are unchanged in kind: some users may still experience intermittent issues or delayed email, additional mitigation measures are unnamed, and residual service issues are still under investigation. The outage also continues to reach beyond Microsoft's own assistant: Reuters reported it hit the connectors OpenAI and Anthropic use to reach Microsoft 365.

WHY IT MATTERSEmail queues, not regions, are the new yardstick

Every update since 04:22 UTC has carried the same shape: a mechanism named, a mitigation described, and no sustained recovery to point at. The 14:00 UTC update breaks that pattern in one respect: it names a throughput measure, email queues significantly reduced and approaching normal levels, rather than another region joining a recovery map. The step-by-step grade that began with marginal success for patches applied after reboots at 06:57 UTC, positive results for an authentication and routing patch at 07:29 UTC, confirmed stability across the patched portions at 08:09 UTC, early signs of stabilization at 09:03 UTC, incremental improvement in several workloads at 09:57 UTC, completed recovery actions in part of Asia Pacific at 11:01 UTC, encouraging recovery progress in Europe at 12:00 UTC, and broader improvements in North America at 12:58 UTC now adds a backlog number to the story. The change is a measurable drain, not a declared end, and it lands more than twenty-one hours after the first mailbox failures.

For the AI stack the exposure is unchanged and still open. Copilot Chat may not load or answer and may not reach email, calendar or files, and the OpenAI and Anthropic Microsoft 365 connectors remain affected, Reuters reported. Companies that layered AI assistants on top of Microsoft 365 are still waiting on the same mailbox layer as everyone else. Queues approaching normal is not normal service, and the next update will show whether the backlog keeps draining under Friday traffic or repeats the pattern of the two recoveries that froze again at 00:00 and 04:22 UTC.

WHAT TO WATCHThe 15:00 UTC update, then the post-incident report

The next fact is the 15:00 UTC update: whether the email queues reach normal levels, whether any region or service is declared recovered, whether Microsoft names the traffic influx as a cause or a symptom, and whether it gives a time for full recovery. Watch whether the additional mitigation measures get described, and whether the residual service issues under investigation turn out to be a new failure mode or the same one.

The post-incident report will carry the lasting answers: why servers failed their health checks, why restored servers froze again under traffic, what the network configuration change under review actually changed, and what the traffic influx was. Until it lands, treat every cause as unconfirmed. The earlier Teams incident TM1550488 was resolved the same day, with a preliminary post-incident report promised within two business days.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISTwenty-one hours in, the queues are draining, but the service is not back

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Twenty-one hours and forty-three minutes in, Microsoft now describes email queues significantly reduced and approaching normal levels. That is the first concrete throughput signal in this incident, and a sharper measure than another region joining the recovery map. But the substance is still short of an end: some users may still experience intermittent access or delayed email, the additional mitigation measures are unnamed, residual issues are still under investigation, and earlier improvements proved temporary. What is still missing is everything that would close the incident: a root cause, a recovered service, and a time for full restoration. Until the 15:00 UTC update shows the queues reaching normal and holding under Friday traffic, treat the outage as ongoing.
