Google Launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen, Its Fast Everyday Model for Agentic Work

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12, 2026, a speed-focused model for everyday coding and agentic workflows, rolling out globally today across the Gemini app, Google Search, the Gemini API, AI Studio and Antigravity. It scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at default settings, one point above frontier model Argon, finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, and costs a flat $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday, November 12, 2026, a speed-focused model built for everyday coding and agentic workflows. The company positions Hydrogen as the fast execution tier of the Gemini 4 family, while Carbon and Argon handle deep, long-horizon reasoning. Hydrogen is rolling out globally starting today across the Gemini app, Google Search, the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, Gemini for business, and Google Antigravity for paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.

Hydrogen carries a 1 million-token context window and is priced flat at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scores 51 at its default high thinking level, one point above the frontier model Argon at 50, and reaches 54 at its highest xhigh setting. Google says it finishes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at its high default setting, more than triples Terminal-Bench 4.1 scores to 57% from 18.2%, and cuts fabricated claims by more than five times.

The model offers four thinking levels, low, medium, high by default, and a new xhigh tier, so users trade reasoning depth for speed and cost. Google is also introducing Orchestrator in Antigravity, which switches between models such as Carbon and Hydrogen inside one session; on Terminal-Bench 4.0 it reached near-Carbon task success rates while using 24% less quota on average and finishing 19% faster. In parallel, Google Search is upgrading to Hydrogen for all users globally, which Google calls the biggest model upgrade to Search yet.

Hydrogen replaces the Flash branding Google used for its fast tier. The model was tested in the open under the name gemini-3.8-flash from late October, and its pricing appeared in Google Cloud's public catalog earlier on November 12 before the official announcement. The launch comes weeks after Carbon and Argon, completing the three-model Gemini 4 lineup.

WHY IT MATTERSA speed tier, not a smaller frontier model

Google is splitting Gemini 4 into roles rather than sizes. Carbon and Argon do the deep architectural judgment; Hydrogen does the fast, disciplined execution, writing code, running tests, verifying changes. The Orchestrator feature makes the split automatic, switching models mid-session, which is Google betting that multi-model workflows, not one model for everything, are how agentic work gets done.

The numbers that matter are the ones users will feel: 2.5 times faster than 3.8 Flash, a Terminal-Bench jump from 18.2% to 57%, and fabricated claims cut by more than five times. Those are Google's own benchmarks, and independent verification will come from live use. The flat $1.50 and $7.50 pricing is simple enough to budget against, and it undercuts the frontier tier while beating it on one public index at default settings.

WHAT TO WATCHSearch is the widest distribution move

The consumer story is bigger than the developer story. Hydrogen now powers Google Search for all users globally, with more visual responses, better intent understanding, and wider query fan-out. Every search becomes a Gemini 4 demonstration, and every answer shapes what users expect from the model in the app and the API.

Watch whether the xhigh thinking level, which pushes the index score to 54, becomes the default for hard tasks, and whether Orchestrator's multi-model switching spreads beyond Antigravity. The next test is live usage: whether Hydrogen's speed holds up outside Google's benchmarks, and whether the safety gains it claims, up to 81% less overreach in agentic tasks, survive contact with real users.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISGoogle now sells speed as a tier, and Search is the showroom

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Hydrogen is Google finishing the Gemini 4 lineup with a workhorse, not another frontier bid. The positioning is deliberate: Carbon and Argon think, Hydrogen executes, and Orchestrator switches between them automatically. The benchmark that counts is the one Google publishes for itself, so treat the 51-versus-50 edge over Argon as a claim, not a verdict. What is not a claim is the distribution: Hydrogen in Search for every user on Earth is the widest model rollout Google has ever done, and it makes the fast tier the default way most people will meet Gemini 4.
