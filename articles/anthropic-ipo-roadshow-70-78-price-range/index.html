Anthropic Launches IPO Roadshow at $70–$78, Targeting Up to $64.4 Billion

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow on November 10, 2026, pricing 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78 each, a deal that could raise up to $64.4 billion. A separate $26 billion private placement from seven strategic partners takes total fundraising to as much as $90.4 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic launched the roadshow for its initial public offering on November 10, 2026, the company announced. The offering covers 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78 each, with Anthropic selling 801.35 million shares and existing stockholders selling 23.65 million. At the top of the range, the offering raises up to $64.4 billion.

The launch came a day after Anthropic sent banks a filing showing 867 million shares at $70 to $80, then retracted it within hours, saying the pricing details were inaccurate. The corrected range lowers the top by $2 a share and cuts the maximum raise by about $4.9 billion.

Seven strategic partners plan to buy $26 billion of stock in a separate private placement: Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC. Combined with the offering, total fundraising could reach $90.4 billion. Anthropic has applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT, with pricing expected the following week and trading as early as next Thursday or Friday.

WHY IT MATTERSThe correction cost $4.9 billion off the top

The retracted Monday filing showed 867 million shares at $70 to $80, a maximum raise of $69.3 billion. The corrected filing shows 825 million shares at $70 to $78, a maximum of $64.4 billion. The bottom of the range did not move; the top came down $2, and the share count fell by about 42 million. Anthropic did not explain the error, and Reuters could not determine what was wrong, so whether the cut reflects weaker demand or a clerical correction is not established. What is established is the direction: the deal got smaller, not bigger.

The underwriters also hold a 30-day option to buy up to 123.75 million additional shares. If exercised in full at the top of the range, that adds about $9.7 billion, which would take the offering above the retracted figure. The option is the safety valve; the base deal is the smaller number.

WHY IT MATTERSSeven chip and cloud giants are buying in alongside

The $26 billion private placement is the more telling number. Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC plan to buy non-voting Class N stock right after the offering closes. These are the companies that sell Anthropic its compute and its customers' cloud capacity, and they are putting $26 billion of their own money in beside the public offering.

The placement also frames the valuation debate. Anthropic's amended S-1 earlier this month showed revenue of almost $17 billion for the three months ended September 30, though its quarterly growth rate has already declined sharply. At $70 to $78 a share, the implied market value sits well below the $1.25 trillion to $1.75 trillion range reported earlier, though the final share count and valuation depend on the pricing. The roadshow, which runs this week, is where that gap gets tested.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA smaller deal, with the industry buying in beside it

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the retraction is the noise; the corrected range is the signal. A $64.4 billion maximum, down from $69.3 billion, is still the largest offering ever attempted by an AI company, and the $26 billion that seven chip and cloud giants are putting in beside it is the stronger vote. The roadshow now has to answer one question: whether public buyers will pay $70 to $78 for a company whose quarterly revenue was almost $17 billion and whose growth rate is already slowing.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The figures come from Anthropic's roadshow announcement and the corrected S-1/A Amendment No. 2, filed November 10, 2026, cross-checked against Reuters' report on the latest draft prospectus. The retracted Monday filing showed 867 million shares at $70 to $80, a maximum raise of $69.3 billion; those figures were disowned by Anthropic and are not used as the deal terms. The registration statement has not yet become effective, the final price and offering date are not set, and the underwriters' 123.75 million-share option is not included in the $64.4 billion maximum.
