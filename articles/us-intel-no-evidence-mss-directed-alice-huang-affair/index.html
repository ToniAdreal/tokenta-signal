WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

U.S. intelligence officials from the FBI, the CIA, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence told congressional aides on November 24, 2026 that they found no evidence China's Ministry of State Security directed Alice Huang to initiate an affair with Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, The New York Times reported, citing people told about the briefing. The session was held for staff members of the House and Senate intelligence committees and aides to congressional leaders.

The briefers did not dispute that Alice Huang passed intelligence back to China's spy agency after the affair began, and officials told aides they still do not fully understand the role she played at Megaspeed, the Singapore data-center firm suspected of diverting advanced Nvidia AI chips to China. Officials described Kang Wei, often introduced as Alice Huang's husband, as a more significant figure who had been an MSS asset at times and had more significant contacts with the agency.

The briefing omitted the most sensitive U.S. intelligence collected on Alice Huang and a highly classified March 30 memo overseen by Secretary of State and National Security Adviser Marco Rubio detailing Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang and her ties to Chinese intelligence services. The session came a day after President Donald Trump publicly declared that Jensen Huang was not a national security threat or facing criminal prosecution.

WHY IT MATTERSThe briefing narrows the espionage claim without closing it.

The central question in the Huang affair has always been whether the relationship was a directed intelligence operation or a personal relationship that later became an intelligence channel. The briefing answers the first half: the agencies found no evidence the MSS ordered Alice Huang to start the affair. It leaves the second half open, with officials conceding they do not fully understand her role at Megaspeed and not disputing that intelligence flowed to Beijing after the affair began.

The shift of focus to Kang Wei matters for the same reason. If the more significant MSS contact was the man introduced as her husband rather than Alice Huang herself, the intelligence picture around Megaspeed looks less like a single honeytrap and more like a network the agencies are still mapping, which is consistent with the Entity List designation of Megaspeed and the continuing customs and export-control probes.

THE SECOND MOVEWhat Congress heard was a tempered summary, not the full file.

The briefing's omissions are as important as its conclusions. Lawmakers' aides were not shown the most sensitive intelligence on Alice Huang or the Rubio memo, so the session gives Congress a narrowed account without the evidence needed to judge the risk independently. That gap is likely to shape the December hearings, where Jensen Huang is due to appear before House members on December 1 and the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.

The timing also matters. The briefing followed Trump's public clearance of Huang by a day and Nvidia's board vote to reinstate him on November 23. The sequence, clearance, reinstatement, then a briefing that softens the directed-operation claim, points toward the scandal's political temperature falling even as the underlying export-control and Megaspeed investigations continue.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM DIRECTED TO UNDIRECTED
From a directed operation to an undirected affair
The original reporting framed Alice Huang as an MSS asset recruited to target Jensen Huang. The briefing reframes the affair as one the MSS did not direct, while leaving open that intelligence flowed to Beijing after it began.
FROM ONE PERSON TO A NETWORK
From one figure to a wider network
The briefing shifts attention from Alice Huang alone to Kang Wei, described as a more significant figure with deeper MSS contacts, and to Megaspeed itself, where the export-control investigations continue regardless of how the affair began.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe directed-spy claim narrows, but the Megaspeed file stays open.

This briefing does real work for Jensen Huang: the agencies themselves told Congress there is no evidence the affair was an MSS-directed operation, which undercuts the sharpest version of the scandal a day after Trump cleared him and the board reinstated him. But the concession is narrow by design. The briefers would not dispute that intelligence reached Beijing, they pointed to a second figure in Kang Wei, and they kept the most sensitive material, including the Rubio memo, out of the room. Congress now has a tempered summary and a December hearing schedule, not the underlying evidence. The reputational case for Huang is improving; the export-control case around Megaspeed is untouched.
