Alibaba Is Already Training Its Next Qwen Model on Its Own T-Head Chips

Alibaba CEO Eddie Wu said on the November 19 earnings call that Qwen is already training its next-generation model on T-Head chips and that Bailian, known as Model Studio, already runs inference on Chinese-made chips. Wu said most new AI chip capacity after 2027 will be domestic, with T-Head first in line for training. The next chip, the Zhenwu V900, enters mass production in the first quarter of 2027.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Alibaba CEO Eddie Wu disclosed on the company's earnings call on Thursday, November 19, 2026 that Qwen, Alibaba's flagship model family, is already training its next-generation model on chips designed by T-Head, Alibaba's semiconductor subsidiary. In his prepared remarks, Wu said most of the incremental AI chip capacity the company adds will be primarily domestic or proprietary domestic chips, and that its own flagship proprietary chips will be used as a first priority to support the training and iteration of its foundation models. The company's Bailian platform, known internationally as Model Studio, is also already providing inference services on domestic chips.

The disclosure extends T-Head's role across both stages of AI computing. The Zhenwu M890 processor, which entered service in August and has moved into large-scale commercial use, is the chip now training Qwen; Alibaba says it delivers three times the performance of its predecessor, the Zhenwu 810E, with 144 gigabytes of on-chip memory and 800 gigabytes per second of bandwidth between chips. T-Head has shipped more than 560,000 Zhenwu chips in total, with more than 400 outside customers across 20 industries. The next chip, the Zhenwu V900, is due to enter mass production in the first quarter of 2027 with three times the computing capacity of the M890, followed by a V900 supernode cluster in the second quarter and the J900 in 2028.

The timing is shaped by Washington. New US rules announced on November 16 require licenses for data centers in countries including Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil if they supply substantial computing power to Chinese companies, closing a route Chinese firms used to rent advanced compute abroad. Wu said the rules will affect Alibaba's products and services in 2027 and that it was too early to know how they might affect Qwen's development. Alibaba's AI cloud and computing services revenue reached 60.97 billion yuan in the September quarter, up 53 percent year on year, with AI product revenue of 17.9 billion yuan growing at a triple-digit rate for a 13th consecutive quarter.

WHY IT MATTERSTraining on its own chips is the answer to the export controls

Until now, Alibaba's chip story was about inference and cloud capacity: the M890 in commercial use, the V900 on a roadmap, a confidential Hong Kong listing filing for T-Head. Putting Qwen's next model on T-Head silicon moves the claim from the data center to the model itself. Training is the most demanding workload in AI, and a flagship model training run is the hardest test a chip can face. If T-Head chips can carry it, Alibaba has a domestic answer to the question every Chinese AI company now faces: where does the compute come from when Nvidia parts and foreign data centers are both restricted.

The business case is already visible. Alibaba's AI cloud revenue grew 53 percent to 60.97 billion yuan in the September quarter, and Bailian's annual recurring revenue passed 20 billion yuan in August, with a target of 30 billion by the end of the fiscal year. Wu said the link between the company's own chips and models could lower computing costs and make application development more efficient. Capital spending reached 50.5 billion yuan in the quarter, up 60 percent, and Alibaba has pledged more than $53 billion over three years for AI models, chips and cloud infrastructure.

WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWNNo benchmark, no chip count, no cost saving

Nothing in the disclosure lets an outsider check how well the T-Head chips actually perform. Alibaba has not said how much of the next Qwen model's training runs on T-Head chips, how much computing capacity the work requires, or what cost savings it has achieved. No performance or yield figures for the V900 were given on the call, and no independent benchmark of T-Head chips against Nvidia parts has been published. The M890's three-times claim is Alibaba's own, and the 560,000-chip shipment figure counts all Zhenwu chips, not the ones training Qwen.

Supply is the other open question. Wu expects computing supply in China to remain tight through 2027 and 2028, and Alibaba's own plans depend on whether chip production and supporting infrastructure can grow as intended. The company will first make sure its own models have enough capacity, then consider important partners, which could matter to cloud customers if demand keeps outpacing processor supply. The 20-gigawatt data-center target may also be revised. The practical test is the V900: mass production in the first quarter of 2027, and a supernode cluster in the second.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA domestic training run is a claim; the V900 is the test

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the disclosure matters because it is specific and checkable in a way chip roadmaps are not. A flagship model either trains on domestic silicon or it does not, and Alibaba has now said it does. The claim to discount is the performance story, because Alibaba publishes no benchmark, no chip count and no cost figure, and the M890's three-times improvement is its own number. The claim to watch is the schedule: V900 mass production in the first quarter of 2027 and a supernode cluster in the second. If those land on time and the next Qwen model ships without a compute bottleneck, Alibaba will have shown that a Chinese AI stack can train at the frontier without Nvidia. If they slip, the training disclosure will read as a stopgap, not a strategy.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

Alibaba has not disclosed the next Qwen model's name, how much of its training runs on T-Head chips, the computing capacity involved, or any cost saving. No independent benchmark of T-Head chips against Nvidia parts has been published, and the M890 and V900 performance figures are Alibaba's own. The V900 mass-production date and the supernode schedule are company plans, not completed deliveries. This does not constitute investment advice.
