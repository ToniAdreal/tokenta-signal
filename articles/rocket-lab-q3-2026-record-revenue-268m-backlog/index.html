Rocket Lab Posts Record $268M Quarter as Backlog Jumps 52% to $3.57B

Rocket Lab reported record third-quarter 2026 revenue of $267.8 million, up 73 percent year over year and ahead of consensus, with a record $3.57 billion backlog, up 52 percent in one quarter, as Neutron secured more than $515 million in new contracts. The net loss widened to $79.7 million and missed earnings expectations, sending shares lower after hours despite an 11 percent gain in the regular session.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Rocket Lab reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $267.8 million on November 9, 2026, up 73 percent from $155.1 million a year earlier and ahead of the roughly $258 million consensus and the top of its own $250 million to $265 million guide. Gross profit rose to $90.1 million, a 33.6 percent margin, and the company posted its first positive GAAP operating cash flow, of $7.2 million. The net loss widened to $79.7 million, or $0.12 per share, against the $0.07 loss analysts expected, and the adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $18 million, inside the guided range.

The backlog is the quarter's real story. It jumped 52 percent in a single quarter to a record $3.57 billion, up $1.2 billion from the end of June, with about $1 billion in new launch contracts and $470 million signed for Space Systems. Total launch backlog reached a record 117 launches. Neutron, the reusable medium-lift rocket due for its first flight no earlier than 2027, secured more than $515 million in contracts from constellation operators in the quarter, growing its backlog to 16 missions and lifting the base launch price to $70 million for several of them as industry launch capacity tightens.

Rocket Lab also fully funded its pending acquisition of Iridium Communications through a completed ATM equity offering, with Iridium shareholders approving the deal. Twelve Rocket Lab-built spacecraft reached orbit and are now operating in the quarter, including eight satellite platforms for Globalstar's direct-to-device constellation, twin spacecraft conducting Mars science for NASA, and the responsive-space VICTUS HAZE mission for the United States Space Force. The company guided fourth-quarter revenue of $270 million to $290 million, GAAP gross margin of 34 to 36 percent, and an adjusted EBITDA loss of $14 million to $20 million. Shares closed up 10.9 percent at $58.86 in Monday's regular session, then slipped in after-hours trading on the earnings miss.

WHY IT MATTERSThe backlog is doing the talking

A $1.2 billion backlog jump in one quarter is not a rounding effect. It says constellation operators are locking in launch capacity years ahead, and Rocket Lab is capturing a meaningful share of that demand across Electron, HASTE, and Neutron. The Neutron detail matters most: $515 million in a single quarter, a $70 million base price, and 16 missions booked before the rocket has flown once. Customers are paying a premium for capacity that does not exist yet, which is a vote of confidence in the vehicle and a sign of how tight the launch market has become.

The test is conversion. Backlog is a promise, not revenue, and Neutron's first launch is still at least a year out. If the rocket flies on schedule and the manifest holds, the $3.57 billion backlog turns into the multi-year revenue ramp the valuation assumes. If Neutron slips, the backlog becomes a liability of expectations. For now, the order book is the strongest evidence that Rocket Lab's end-to-end space strategy, launch plus spacecraft plus components, is winning work that used to go to a single provider.

WHY IT MATTERSA record top line, a wider loss, and a split market reaction

Read honestly, this is a mixed print wearing a record headline. Revenue beat by a clear margin and growth of 73 percent is real, but the net loss more than quadrupled year over year to $79.7 million, and the $0.12 per-share loss missed the $0.07 consensus by a wide margin. The market's two-part reaction tells the story: shares rose nearly 11 percent in the regular session on the revenue and backlog numbers, then gave some back after hours once the earnings miss landed.

The watchpoint is the path to profitability. Positive operating cash flow is a first, and the fourth-quarter guide points to record revenue and expanding gross margins, but operating expenses are still running well ahead of gross profit, and the Iridium acquisition adds integration risk on top of a heavy Neutron development bill. Rocket Lab is proving it can win the work. The next two quarters decide whether it can do the work at a margin the market will pay for.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe order book is the story; profitability is still the open question

Tokenta Signal's judgment: The headline is a genuine record, and the $1.2 billion backlog jump in a single quarter is the strongest demand signal Rocket Lab has ever printed, with Neutron commanding a $70 million base price before its first flight. But the loss widened sharply, the per-share miss was wide, and the after-hours slide shows the market noticed. Positive operating cash flow is a real milestone, yet operating expenses still dwarf gross profit and the Iridium deal adds execution risk. Rocket Lab has proven it can win the work. The next two quarters, and Neutron's path to a 2027 debut, decide whether the work pays.
