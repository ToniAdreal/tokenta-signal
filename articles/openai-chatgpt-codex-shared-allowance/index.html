From December, ChatGPT and Codex Share One Allowance

OpenAI's help center article states that from December, usage allowances for Plus, Pro and Business subscriptions will be shared between ChatGPT and Codex, metered in tokens, with Instant chat exempt; once used up, users can wait for a reset, upgrade or buy credits.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

An updated article in OpenAI's help center states that from December, usage allowances for Plus, Pro and Business subscriptions will be shared between ChatGPT and Codex. The allowance is metered in tokens; the stronger the model and the more complex the task, the faster it drains.

The substance of the change is two pools becoming one. Previously, ordinary chat had its own limit, while Codex and Work shared another allowance. From December, a long conversation in ChatGPT and a coding task in Codex both draw from the same allowance.

The sole exception is Instant chat. The company states explicitly that Instant chat does not count toward usage. Once the allowance is used up, users can see the combined remaining usage and reset time in Settings, and choose to wait for the reset, upgrade their plan or buy credits.

ONE POOL, TWO JOBSAn Afternoon of Coding Will Eat the Chat Allowance

For subscribers who use both products, December means budgeting afresh. One long Codex session could directly consume the day's allowance for advanced-model chat, and the reverse is equally true.

OpenAI's reassurance is that most users are not expected to need to wait for resets more often than now. That sentence comes with no numbers attached. Reassurance without figures should be read as a forecast, not a guarantee.

WORK JOINS CHATWork Merges Into Chat, and the Interface Converges Too

Alongside the allowance merger, OpenAI is also folding the Work tab into regular chat, testing it first with a small group on the desktop app before rolling it out gradually. Codex itself remains a separate view in the desktop app.

Interface convergence and allowance convergence are two sides of the same thing. As product boundaries disappear, usage boundaries disappear with them. Subscribers are no longer buying two products, but one unified agent workbench.

THE MISSING NUMBERSThe Unpublished Numbers Are the Real Variable

The help center article gives no allowance size for any plan tier, and no conversion ratios between models. Whether the five-hour window survives, and how weekly limits are computed, is left unclear by the company.

Until the numbers are published, the only reliable basis for subscribers' capacity planning is their own actual consumption. From December, the usage panel in Settings will show the combined balance; watch a few weeks of one's own consumption curve first, then decide whether to upgrade.
