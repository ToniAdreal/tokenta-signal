Two Cracks in 1024-Bit RSA; 2048-Bit Still Safe for Now

On November 6, DeepMind announced that AI agents, guided by human cryptographers, had found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA. Keys of 2048 bits and above are unaffected.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE BENCHMARK — In September 2026, researchers published a study forging 1024-bit RSA signatures.
  • 02THE PAPER — On November 5, DeepMind's batch-factoring working paper was posted.
  • 03THE ANNOUNCEMENT — On November 6, DeepMind announced both results on X.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The research was led by DeepMind's Dj Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta, with collaborators including Nadia Heninger of UC San Diego and Emmanuel Thome of Inria.

DeepMind stressed repeatedly that the models worked under human guidance, not on their own. Cryptographers set the direction; agents handled derivation, coding and debugging.

The September forgery study used 1,380 single-core years spread over five months, querying devices about 4.3 billion times. Agents cut the same benchmark's CPU cost to 391 core-years. The speedup is on the table; so is the boundary of the conclusion.

PADDING STOPS PROTECTINGFirst Weakness: Padding No Longer Protects

The work targets PKCS#1 v1.5 padding. Once an attacker controls more than half of a signature block, padding no longer adds forgery difficulty. For 32-byte short digests, the team estimates the new method is about 1,600 times faster than the previous best. That is a theoretical estimate.

In a sandbox, agents reproduced the benchmark key from the September study using the same algorithm and parameters, spending 391 core-years of CPU, 3.5 times less than the original study. Two collaborating agents completed the design, launch and debugging in four days.

BATCH FACTORING GETS CHEAPERSecond Weakness: Batch Factoring Gets Cheaper

The second working paper is called Fan Factories. One precomputation costs about as much as factoring a single key; after that, each additional 1024-bit key costs about 1/58 of the traditional method, roughly 30 times lower than the previous best batch method.

That is also a first-order theoretical estimate, with no full implementation. The paper's abstract says so, and DeepMind's announcement repeats the qualification.

2048-BIT STAYS SAFE2048-Bit Is Out of Range

Both results target 1024-bit keys only. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology has not allowed RSA signatures below 2048 bits for new signatures since the end of 2013.

1024-bit keys still live in old hardware, long-lived certificates and embedded systems. The systems at risk are the slow-to-upgrade legacy ones, not services deployed today.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISAI Is Settling Cryptography's Old Accounts Faster

Tokenta Signal's judgment: this is not RSA broken. It is the old standard's ledger being settled faster. 1024-bit should have retired long ago; the places slow to retire it now have an account to settle.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The 1,600x and 30x figures are theoretical estimates, not verified by full implementation. The working papers have not been peer reviewed, and the correspondence between the formal statements and the original conjectures still awaits confirmation by the field.
