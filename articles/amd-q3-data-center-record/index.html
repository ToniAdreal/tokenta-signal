AMD Posts Record Q3 Revenue as Data Center Nearly Doubles to Over 60% of Sales

AMD's third-quarter revenue was $13.377 billion, up 45% year on year and above the FactSet consensus of $13.1 billion; non-GAAP EPS of $2.09 beat the expected $1.95. Data center revenue was $8.5 billion, up 97%, driven by EPYC demand and the Instinct MI450 series GPU launch; fourth-quarter guidance is about $17 billion, up roughly 66% at the midpoint.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

AMD's official press release, published on 2026-11-03, said third-quarter revenue was $13.377 billion, up 45% year on year and 16% sequentially, a quarterly record that came in above the top of guidance; non-GAAP earnings per share were $2.09, up 74% year on year.

MarketWatch confirmed the same day that revenue beat the FactSet consensus of $13.1 billion and adjusted EPS beat the expected $1.95, with CEO Lisa Su attributing the strength to the data center business.

By segment: data center revenue was a record $8.5 billion, up 97% year on year, driven by EPYC processor demand and the launch of Instinct MI450 series GPUs; client and gaming revenue was $3.7 billion, down 8%, with gaming down 40% on weaker semi-custom and graphics revenue; embedded revenue was $1.1 billion, up 29%.

GAAP net income was $2.428 billion, or $1.46 per share; the gap between GAAP and non-GAAP included a $1.097 billion change in the fair value of warrant liabilities, which the WSJ reported was tied to last year's large deal with OpenAI, and was largely offset by income tax provisions.

Fourth-quarter guidance was equally strong: AMD expects revenue of about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%, implying roughly 66% year-on-year and 27% sequential growth at the midpoint, with non-GAAP gross margin of about 55%; CFO Jean Hu said the outlook reflects accelerating revenue and earnings growth driven by demand across the data center portfolio.

The release also listed third-quarter highlights: Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure are already running models and workloads on the first AMD Helios rack-scale solutions, powered by Instinct MI450 series GPUs and sixth-generation EPYC CPUs; ROCm 10 has been released; and AMD announced agreements to acquire World Labs and Taalas.

WHY IT MATTERSData Center Becomes AMD's Clear Center of Gravity for the First Time

Data center revenue of $8.5 billion accounted for more than 60% of total quarterly revenue, up from less than half a year ago. AMD is turning from a PC and gaming chip company into a data-center-first company, a shift the WSJ's headline called out directly.

The drivers are specific: continued strengthening EPYC processor demand, plus the launch of the Instinct MI450 series GPUs. This is not a generic AI-boom narrative; two product lines are ramping at the same time.

WHY IT MATTERSGuidance Deserves More Attention Than the Quarter Itself

Ahead of the results, the market had just gone through the October 30 semiconductor selloff, questioning whether AI demand could support high valuations. AMD answered the past with record quarterly numbers, but answered the future with roughly $17 billion of fourth-quarter guidance, a midpoint up about 27% sequentially.

The other side to watch: GAAP profit is still disturbed by non-operating items such as warrant revaluations, so GAAP and non-GAAP figures need to be read separately. The earnings call is at 17:00 ET, and what management says about the Helios production ramp and Venice will be the next evidence on whether the guidance can be delivered.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Signal Is in the Guidance, Not the Single-Quarter Beat

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the signal in AMD's results is not the revenue beat of a little over $200 million, but data center passing 60% of revenue at the same time as roughly $17 billion of fourth-quarter guidance. A single-quarter beat can be explained by product cycles; accelerating guidance means management believes the Helios and Venice production ramps are already in the order book, not still on a launch stage.
