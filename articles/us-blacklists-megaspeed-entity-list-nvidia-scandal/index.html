US Puts Megaspeed on the Entity List, Cutting Off the Firm at the Center of the Nvidia Scandal

The United States moved on November 23, 2026 to add Megaspeed, the Singapore chip firm at the center of the Nvidia scandal, to the Commerce Department's Entity List, Reuters reported. A Bureau of Industry and Security rule filed the same day adds seven Megaspeed-linked companies in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand; licenses are now required for all items subject to US export rules, under a presumption of denial. The rule cites the significant risk of diversion of advanced computing commodities, not a finding that any company diverted anything.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The United States moved on November 23, 2026 to add Megaspeed, the Singapore chip firm at the center of the Nvidia scandal, to the Commerce Department's Entity List, Reuters reported, citing a source. The Bureau of Industry and Security rule, filed the same day and scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on November 25, adds seven companies in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, including Megaspeed International and affiliates.

Anyone exporting, reexporting or transferring items subject to US export rules to the listed companies now needs a license, and those applications will be reviewed under a presumption of denial. The reason given is the significant risk of the entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end users.

US authorities have investigated Megaspeed since 2024 over possible violations of export controls related to alleged shipments of Nvidia AI chips to China, the Justice and Commerce departments said last week. Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US authorities suspect of working for Chinese state intelligence, is the woman at the center of Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia chief executive on November 12. Huang was reinstated by Nvidia's board on November 23.

WHY IT MATTERSA risk finding, not a verdict.

The rule does not say any of the seven companies diverted chips or committed a crime. It does not name Nvidia or Jensen Huang, and it does not mention any specific product. What it does is formal: it cuts the Megaspeed network off from American technology unless Washington grants a license it is expected to deny.

The listing is the first formal Commerce action against the firm at the center of the scandal, and it lands on the same day Nvidia's board brought Huang back. The two events move in opposite directions: the chief executive returns, while the company linked to his downfall is placed behind a license wall.

THE CONTEXTThe legal net widens while Huang returns.

The listing sits alongside a set of probes that the reinstatement does not close. California Attorney General Rob Bonta's office said on November 23 that Huang's return does not diminish the need for its probe into what Nvidia told investors about its China ties, Reuters reported. The Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's accounting disclosures, and the Justice Department's criminal investigation into Megaspeed continues.

Huang is due to be questioned by three House committees on December 1. The Entity List rule makes no reference to any of those investigations, and whether anyone will be charged remains unknown.

The listing covers Megaspeed entities and affiliates in three countries: MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix in Malaysia; Apex Enterprise Solutions, Megaspeed International and Swiftdata in Singapore; and Megaspeed (Thailand) in Thailand.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe perimeter is now drawn in law.

The Entity List move turns a two-year investigation into a concrete restriction, and it lands on the same day Nvidia's board brought Huang back. The listing does not prove diversion, and it does not touch Nvidia itself. What it does is draw the legal perimeter: the firm at the center of the scandal can no longer buy American technology without a license Washington is expected to deny. The scandal's center of gravity has shifted from the boardroom to the docket.
