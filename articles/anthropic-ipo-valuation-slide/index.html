Roadshow Goes Ahead, Valuation Cut First as Anthropic's Listing Hits a Cold Wind

Anthropic's IPO roadshow starts next week as planned, targeting a listing in the week of November 16. But prospective buyers are discussing a valuation in the $1.1 trillion to $1.3 trillion range, far below the $2 trillion discussed in early October.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic's IPO roadshow starts next week as planned, targeting a listing in the week of November 16. The Information and CNBC confirmed the timetable on November 6, days after Reuters reported investors doubting the roadshow could proceed on schedule.

The price moved first. Bloomberg reported on November 6 that prospective buyers are discussing a valuation in the $1.1 trillion to $1.3 trillion range, below the $1.5 trillion discussed in mid-October. The Wall Street Journal wrote that three major investors view $1.5 trillion as unrealistic, with one putting fair value at about $1 trillion.

A month ago, Anthropic's target was still $2 trillion. The amended prospectus filed on November 2 showed third-quarter revenue of $17 billion, up about 47% quarter on quarter, alongside a return to a $567 million operating loss, and compute commitments and leases totaling about $895 billion.

ROADSHOW ON, PRICE DOWNRoadshow On, Price Down

The roadshow was not delayed; the price gave way first. That is Anthropic and its underwriters' choice: protect the timetable first, then talk price.

The Nasdaq had just suffered its deepest single-day fall in 19 months on November 5, and in the same week Anthropic's prospective buyers cut expectations by nearly half. The timing could hardly be worse.

THE SLIDEFrom $2 Trillion to $1.1 Trillion

$2 trillion in early October, $1.5 trillion in mid-October, $1.1 trillion to $1.3 trillion on November 6. In one month, expectations were cut by nearly half.

The Wall Street Journal's account was harsher still, with one investor putting fair value near $1 trillion. Anthropic's May private round valued it at $965 billion; $1 trillion is only slightly above that.

THE ODDS SLIPThe Listing Odds Are Slipping Too

Kalshi's market pricing shows the odds of Anthropic announcing a listing this year falling from above 85% to 57% within a week. On Polymarket, the odds of a November listing fell from about 88% to about 51%.

Investors also want more data, above all the October revenue figures. The amended prospectus did not include that monthly number. Once the roadshow starts, the first verifiable signal will be a new amended S-1 filing.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISList First, Haggle Later

Tokenta Signal's judgment: a roadshow going ahead shows Anthropic does not want to wait, and the valuation cut shows the market does not want to chase. List first, then haggle: the timetable matters more than the price.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

There is no final pricing, and Anthropic declined to comment. The roadshow timetable may still change, and a November listing is not a settled fact. The valuation range comes from people familiar with the matter speaking to different outlets, and the accounts are not fully consistent. Market volatility does not constitute any investment advice.
