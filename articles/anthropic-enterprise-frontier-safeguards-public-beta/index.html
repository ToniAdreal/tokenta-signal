WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic said on November 23, 2026, that Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) is now in public beta. EFS adds automated safety monitoring for sophisticated misuse while remaining compatible with zero data retention: neither Anthropic nor any cloud provider retains an enterprise customer's data. The system is available on the Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock, and Claude on Google Cloud, with support for Microsoft Foundry coming soon.

The mechanism puts storage in the customer's hands. Inputs and outputs are written to storage the customer controls — Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage in the customer's own account — and deleted promptly from Anthropic's side. The customer configures its own access policies and audit logging and encrypts safety data with its own keys. Anthropic's automated safety monitoring reads that customer-held data to look for misuse patterns across many requests. No operator personnel at Anthropic or the cloud provider access inputs and outputs, and the data is never used for model or classifier training.

When the monitoring finds a risk, it creates a Safety Case for the customer's team to review. The case carries an ID, a harm category, a status and flagged transcript IDs, but no inputs or outputs, and can be read from the customer's cloud storage or through an API, or fed into security information and event management systems. Anthropic first announced EFS in September and developed it with more than 100 customers, including a quarter of the Fortune 100; Bridgewater, Salesforce and Stripe evaluated it in private beta. Eligible customers will be notified with instructions on how to join.

WHY IT MATTERSThe safety layer that holds none of the data.

Enterprise AI adoption has run into a hard conflict: safety monitoring needs to see traffic, and regulated customers need nobody to hold it. Anthropic's answer is to split the two — monitoring runs on data that lives only in storage the customer owns and controls.

That split matters because the threat EFS is built for is not a single bad prompt. Anthropic's September threat report describes an operation against roughly fifty organizations in which an AI agent divided an attack among many parallel agents and saved harvested credentials between sessions. Viewed one request at a time, the attack looked benign. Catching it requires seeing across requests, which is exactly what zero-retention customers previously could not get.

WHAT CHANGEDFrom 30-day retention to customer-held storage.

Until now, Anthropic's most advanced models required the company to retain customer prompts and responses for at least 30 days so safety monitoring could run. EFS removes that trade-off: the monitoring still runs, but the data it reads sits in the customer's own cloud account, under the customer's keys.

The design also draws a line around human access. Only automated safety monitoring can touch the data — no person at Anthropic or the cloud provider reviews inputs and outputs, and nothing is used for training.

WHAT IS NEXTWatch adoption, and the Microsoft Foundry follow-through.

The near test is uptake among the regulated customers EFS was built with — healthcare, financial services and law firms, where data-visibility rules are strictest. Anthropic developed the system with more than 100 customers, so the beta starts with a real user base rather than a blank slate.

The other test is breadth. Microsoft Foundry support is promised but not yet shipped, and the beta is limited to eligible business customers who are notified directly. Whether EFS becomes the default way enterprises run Claude under zero retention depends on both landing.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISSafety Monitoring Without Holding the Data.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: this is the right fix for the right conflict. Regulated enterprises would not accept a safety system that required handing over their data, and a safety system that could not see across requests could not catch the multi-agent attacks Anthropic itself documented. EFS resolves the tension by moving the monitoring to the customer's storage rather than moving the data to the monitor. The honest limits are the rollout's: a beta for eligible customers only, Microsoft Foundry still to come. If adoption among the banks, hospitals and law firms it was built with follows, zero-retention monitoring becomes the enterprise default rather than the exception.
