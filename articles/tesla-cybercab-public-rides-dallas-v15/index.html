Tesla Opens Public Cybercab Rides in Dallas, Its Second City, With a Revised V1.5 Car

Tesla opened public Cybercab rides in Dallas on November 19, 2026, the second US city after Austin where riders can hail the purpose-built two-seat robotaxi with no steering wheel or pedals. The Dallas cars are a revised V1.5 version, with early riders spotting a Starlink dish built into the roof and a camera in the trunk lid. Tesla registered 200 more Cybercabs in Texas on November 17, its largest single-day batch, lifting the state's registered Cybercab total to 773.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Tesla opened public rides in its purpose-built Cybercab robotaxi in Dallas on November 19, 2026, making the Texas city the second in the United States where riders can hail the two-seat vehicle with no steering wheel and no pedals. Tesla's official Robotaxi account announced the launch on X with a short video and the words: "Howdy Dallas. Cybercab has arrived in your city." Riders book through the Tesla Robotaxi app, where the Cybercab appears alongside the Model Y.

The Dallas cars are not identical to the ones in Austin. Cybercab chief engineer Eric Earley called them "Cybercab V1.5" on X, and early riders have already spotted new hardware. One rider found a Starlink dish integrated directly into the rear of the roof; another spotted a camera in the rear trunk lid. Tesla has not said what else changed in V1.5, and has not said whether the Starlink terminals are switched on in Dallas. The cars remain gold, the same colour as the Austin fleet.

The first public rides were already happening within hours. One early rider described a 20-minute loop from near Southern Methodist University to the Knox district and back, saying the car arrived in two minutes and the trip cost $11. Tesla is also notifying people in and around the Dallas service area by app and email, inviting them to "be one of the first to take a Cybercab ride in Dallas."

Dallas is not a cold start. Tesla has run unsupervised Model Y robotaxis there since April, and expanded the Dallas geofence from 31 square miles to 81 in late August. The Cybercab now joins that network, which has run around the clock in every unsupervised city since November 11. The launch matches the timeline Tesla set in public: on the October 21 earnings call, AI software chief Ashok Elluswamy said Dallas would be the next Cybercab city after Austin, where public hailing began in early September 2026.

WHY IT MATTERSFrom one-city pilot to a second city in ten weeks

The Austin launch in early September was a pilot: a limited area, a single city's streets, a vehicle most people had only seen on a stage. Dallas is the first proof that the same purpose-built car and the same software stack transfer to a new environment. That is the step every robotaxi programme has to take, and it is the step where most of them stall. Tesla took it ten weeks after the first public rides, with a hardware revision already in the fleet.

The V1.5 detail matters as much as the city. A Starlink terminal built into the roof and a new camera in the trunk lid suggest Tesla is still iterating the vehicle itself, not just scaling a finished design. The open question is what the hardware is for: connectivity resilience, fleet telemetry, or something the company has not explained. Until Tesla says, the sightings are evidence of iteration, not of a capability.

WHAT TO WATCHFleet growth, and the personal-order question

Watch the fleet numbers. On November 17, Tesla registered 200 more Cybercabs with Texas in a single day, its largest batch, lifting the state's registered Cybercab total to 773 and the combined automated-vehicle register to 1,507. The new batch carries the first 2027 model-year vehicle identification numbers. Registered vehicles are not the same as vehicles in service, but the registration pace is the clearest public signal of how fast Tesla intends to scale.

Watch second whether Dallas gets a stated service area and fleet count, and whether more cities follow before the end of 2026, as executives have described. Separately, Tesla has begun inviting select customers to buy Cybercabs for personal use at $29,990 before taxes plus a $299 monthly Autonomy Subscription, with deliveries targeted for December and focused on Austin. The personal-order programme and the Dallas fleet expansion are the same vehicle going in two directions at once: one sold to drivers, one kept in Tesla's own network. Which direction grows faster will say a lot about what the Cybercab business becomes.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe pilot is over; the second city is the test

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Austin proved Tesla could run a purpose-built robotaxi in one city. Dallas is the first test of whether that was a pilot or a programme. Ten weeks from first public rides to a second city, with a hardware revision already in the fleet, is a fast cadence by robotaxi standards, and the registration numbers back it up: 200 cars in a day is not a demonstration fleet. The honest limit is what Tesla has not said. No Dallas service area, no fleet count, no explanation of what V1.5 actually changes. A second city is a real milestone, but the milestone that matters next is the third, and whether the cars in Dallas are operating at a scale anyone can measure.
