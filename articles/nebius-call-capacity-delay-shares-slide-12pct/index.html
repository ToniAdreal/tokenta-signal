Nebius Blames Third-Party Capacity Delay as Shares Slide 12% After Earnings Call

Nebius shares deepened their slide to 12% in premarket trading after the earnings call, as the company said third-party capacity expected online this year will now arrive in the first half of 2027, pushing some December revenue into February. The contracts behind the delayed capacity remain in place, and the company still expects to add more than 1 gigawatt of new capacity a year. About 23% of the free float is sold short, per Barron's.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The earnings call answered the question Flash 320 left open: why the revenue slipped. The Wall Street Journal reports that some third-party capacity Nebius had expected to come online this year is now scheduled for the first half of 2027, pushing a portion of this year's revenue into next year. The contracts behind that capacity remain in place, the company said, and it still expects to bring more than 1 gigawatt of new compute capacity online each year. The delay is a timing problem, not a demand problem, on the company's own account.

The market sold anyway, and harder. Nebius shares fell 12% to $136.66 in premarket trading, according to The Wall Street Journal, and 11% to $137.15, according to Barron's, deepening the 8.3% drop to $141.90 recorded in Flash 320. Barron's notes that about 23% of Nebius's publicly available shares are sold short, and that the stock had already tumbled 58% from its all-time closing high on June 18 through last Thursday.

The financing backdrop is unchanged and unforgiving. Nebius entered a $3.5 billion secured-debt facility from Citi last month and has about $10 billion in outstanding convertible bonds, Barron's reports, while the chief executive still expects the company to break even on a pre-tax basis by the end of 2027. A $40-45 billion spending year leaves no room for timetables to slip, and the call's message was that one already has.

WHY IT MATTERSA timing problem, not a demand problem

The distinction matters because the two kinds of slip have opposite cures. Lost demand would threaten the $12-13 billion 2027 sales projection; a delayed data center only moves revenue across the year boundary, and the contracts behind the delayed capacity remain in place, according to the company. With $1,962.1 million banked in nine months, the low end of the $3-3.4 billion 2026 range implies about $1.04 billion in the fourth quarter, so the slip narrows the room for any further delay but does not break the year.

The neocloud comparison sharpens the point. Barron's notes that CoreWeave told analysts on Tuesday it would rely on delayed-draw loans backed by customer contracts rather than high-interest unsecured bonds, a deliberately different answer to the same financing question. Nebius is answering with prepayments and a secured facility; the call showed the prepayment defense is intact, and the capacity timetable is the part that slipped.

WHAT TO WATCHWhat still has to be proven

Watch whether the delayed third-party capacity actually lands in the first half of 2027. A second slip would turn a timing story into a delivery story, and delivery is the whole neocloud thesis: capacity sold before it exists has to exist on schedule.

Watch the short interest. About 23% of the free float sold short, per Barron's, means a large part of the market is betting Nebius cannot generate the revenue to justify its capital-intensive model. The next checkpoint is the year-end annualized run-rate revenue target of $7-9 billion, which is the number that has to survive contact with the delayed capacity.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe call gave a reason, and the market sold anyway

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the call gave the market the best version of bad news, a delay with the contracts intact, and the market still sold. That is the honest read of a company asking investors to fund a $40-45 billion year while its own timetable slips by a quarter or two. The prepayment defense remains real, and the demand story is unchallenged. But until capacity lands on schedule, every guide number carries a timing discount, and a 23% short interest says a large part of the market is pricing exactly that discount.
