WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 28, South Korean inference chip company FuriosaAI announced it had closed a $620 million Series D at a $2.9 billion post-money valuation, roughly four times the previous round's $735 million. The round was led by U.S.-based Infinitum Partners, with South Korea's National Growth Fund contributing $280 million and international institutions including Cisco Investments taking part.

The background to the raise was written in March 2025: FuriosaAI rejected an acquisition offer of about $800 million from Meta. A year and a half later, the company raised at a $2.9 billion valuation, and the ledger of that refusal has a public number for the first time.

The product is the second-generation inference chip RNGD: a 180-watt air-cooled PCIe card that entered mass production in January 2026, with plans to produce 20,000 units this year and more than double to over 40,000 in 2027. Samsung SDS uses it to provide NPU cloud services, and LG Group uses it to run its EXAONE models.

THE STATE CAPITALA State Fund Leading Half the Round: the National-Team Moment in Chip Financing

The $280 million from South Korea's National Growth Fund is close to half the round's total. South Korea counts AI semiconductors as part of its national compute strategy: the fund steps in, and international institutions follow.

This is not purely financial investment: the state fund's backing is itself a form of credit guarantee, helping an inference chip company cross the trust threshold of "can it actually manufacture at scale."

THE INFERENCE BETThe Inference Chip Playbook: Skip Training, Compete on Power per Unit

FuriosaAI's RNGD uses tensor contraction as its basic compute unit rather than the 2D matrix processing of Nvidia GPUs. The company claims its design power is under half that of Nvidia hardware.

The logic of the inference market differs from training: customers are not buying peak compute but how many requests each watt and each dollar can run stably. A 180-watt air-cooled card targets precisely the layer of the data centre that is easiest to replace.

THE GEN 3 ROADMAPA Third-Generation Chip with Broadcom: the Next Hand Is Already Dealt

The stated uses of the funding are two: expand RNGD's global commercialisation, and develop a third-generation hyperscale AI infrastructure solution with Broadcom. Producing one generation while developing the next, on two parallel tracks.

The company is also reportedly planning an IPO as early as 2027. The Series D's real examination is not the valuation but whether the 40,000-unit capacity planned for 2027 can be delivered on schedule.

Rejecting an acquisition is not pride; it is a bet on manufacturing capability. Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: the inference chip war will be decided not at launches but in 2027's delivery numbers.
