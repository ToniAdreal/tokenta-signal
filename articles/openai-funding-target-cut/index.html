Target Cut by $10 Billion: OpenAI's Fundraise Meets the Sell-Off

According to the Financial Times on October 30, 2026, OpenAI cut this round's funding target by $10 billion to about $20 billion and its target valuation from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion, with the first closing expected below $20 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The cut came in the same week as a technology stock sell-off. The Nasdaq fell 2.9% that Friday, down 7.8% from its record closing high on October 6. Reports said some investors scaled back their subscription amounts, and OpenAI had originally set Friday as the deadline for investor commitments.

A UAE consortium is reportedly set to become the largest participant, including the state-backed technology fund MGX. As part of the deal, OpenAI would gain an option to lease newly built local compute capacity. Other expected participants include Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz, with wealthy individuals able to join later in a retail tranche at the same valuation.

Time pressure is another thread. OpenAI plans to list in 2027 and reportedly wants to complete this round before Anthropic's listing, because the two companies are competing for the same pool of investors. Anthropic has filed to list and is expected to begin trading in mid-November.

A PRICE RESET, NOT A VERDICTA Valuation Pullback Does Not Mean the Company Is in Trouble

$1.25 trillion remains one of the largest private-company valuations in human history. Cutting the target by $10 billion reflects market sentiment and a shift in investor bargaining power more than any revenue collapse.

What really deserves attention is the actual amount at the first closing. Reports say the first closing is expected to come in below $20 billion, and the money actually received says more about investors' real attitude than the target figure.

COMPUTE AS A TERMCompute Options Are Becoming Part of Financing Terms

A condition attached to the MGX consortium's participation is a compute leasing option. Money and compute are starting to arrive as a package: investors provide not only cash but also usage rights to future data centers.

The structure is a double-edged sword for OpenAI. Compute is secured, at the cost of binding its future cost structure more deeply to partners and reducing its freedom at the negotiating table.

THE IPO CLOCKThe IPO Calendar Has Become Financing's Invisible Deadline

OpenAI's rush to close before Anthropic lists shows the two companies' financing windows are squeezing each other. Investor money is finite; whoever closes first gets certainty first.

If Anthropic lists smoothly in mid-November, public-market pricing will in turn influence the anchor for OpenAI's 2027 listing. This round's $1.25 trillion may be only the starting point of the next negotiation.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Valuation Pullback Is the Market Recalculating, Not AI Receding

Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: the $1.25 trillion target remains extremely high; what matters is how much investors actually wire in, and whether compute terms become standard in the next round of financing.
