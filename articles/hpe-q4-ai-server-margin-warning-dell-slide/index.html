HPE Beats on an AI Server Surge, Warns Margins Will Thin, and Dell Slides Before Its Own Results

Hewlett Packard Enterprise beat its fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 estimates with revenue of $14.5 billion, up 50%, and non-GAAP earnings per share of $1.43, then warned that AI server margins will thin: Cloud and AI operating margin is guided to about 14% for fiscal 2027, down from 18.4%. HPE shares fell about 16% on Tuesday and Dell fell about 12% ahead of its own results after the close.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Hewlett Packard Enterprise reported fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results after the market closed on November 23, 2026, and beat on every headline line. Revenue was $14.5 billion, up 50% year on year; non-GAAP diluted earnings per share were $1.43, up 131%, above the company's own outlook range of $1.20 to $1.30 and above a consensus estimate of about $1.28. GAAP diluted earnings per share were $1.26. The company also raised its fiscal 2027 outlook to $59.4 billion to $63.1 billion in revenue and $5.05 to $5.35 in non-GAAP earnings per share.

The growth came from AI infrastructure. Cloud and AI revenue rose 68.6% to $11.3 billion, with server revenue nearly doubling, up 94.7% to $8.8 billion. The segment's operating margin doubled to 18.4% from 9.3% a year earlier. Networking revenue rose 12.3% to $3.1 billion, with a 22.1% operating margin, down from 23.4%. AI systems orders reached $4.5 billion in the quarter, up from $2.4 billion in the third quarter, and the AI backlog reached a record $10.5 billion.

The warning sits inside the guide. Chief Financial Officer Marie Myers said the gross margin rate would moderate as AI server volumes increase, including the ramp of its Helios systems, and management guided Cloud and AI operating margin to about 14% for fiscal 2027, down from 18.4% in the fourth quarter and 15.1% for fiscal 2026. Chief executive Antonio Neri added that supply of memory, NAND and many other critical components is expected to remain constrained to at least the second half of calendar 2028. Non-GAAP gross margin already fell to 38.2% in the quarter from 40.4% in the third quarter.

The market heard the margin line, not the beat. On Tuesday, November 24, HPE shares fell about 16% in afternoon trading, giving back most of Monday's 17.4% gain, while Dell Technologies fell about 12% after rising about 20% on Monday. Dell reports its own fiscal third-quarter results after Tuesday's close, with analysts expecting about $6.55 in adjusted earnings per share on about $49.3 billion in revenue, against Dell's own guidance of about $49.0 billion in revenue and $6.50 in non-GAAP earnings per share.

WHY IT MATTERSA record quarter priced as a margin problem.

The quarter is the strongest kind of beat: revenue up 50%, earnings up 131%, guidance raised, and a record $10.5 billion AI backlog. None of that moved the stock, because the margin math runs the other way. Every incremental AI server dollar carries a thinner margin than the last, component costs are rising faster than pricing, and management's own forecast puts Cloud and AI margin at about 14% next year, more than four points below the quarter just reported. For a company that just became, in large part, an AI server business, that is the story.

The read-across is why Dell fell harder than the broad market. Dell sells the same racks to the same customers, and its infrastructure margin is the number tonight's results have to defend. HPE's warning is not that demand is fading, it is that demand is arriving at a price. The question for Dell, and for the whole AI server trade, is whether the volume is worth the margin it costs.

THE WATCHPOINTTonight's Dell print is the test.

Dell reports after the close with its own AI server revenue expected at about $19.0 billion for the quarter, up from $16.4 billion in the second quarter. If Dell confirms HPE's margin pressure, the sell-off becomes a sector repricing rather than a single-stock reaction. If Dell holds its infrastructure margin near the 15.0% of last quarter and keeps its outlook intact, the HPE warning reads as company-specific mix, not an industry turn.

The durable watch is component supply. Neri's warning that memory and NAND stay constrained into the second half of 2028 means the margin squeeze is not a one-quarter event, and it connects directly to the contract repricing already underway across AI server vendors. The next data points are Dell's margin line tonight and whether hyperscalers accept the higher prices vendors are now pushing through.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe beat is real. The margin is the story.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: HPE's quarter is a genuine AI infrastructure beat, with server revenue nearly doubling and a record $10.5 billion AI backlog. But the market is right to price the warning over the beat. When a vendor tells you margins compress as volumes rise and components stay scarce into 2028, the growth is real and the profit on it is thinner. Dell's print tonight decides whether this is one company's mix problem or the moment the AI server trade starts paying for its own demand.
