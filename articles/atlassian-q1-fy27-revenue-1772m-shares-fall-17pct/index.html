Atlassian Beats on Q1 Revenue, Shares Fall 17% on Slower Subscription Outlook

Atlassian reported fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $1,772 million, up 24%, beating estimates, with cloud up 31% and adjusted EPS of $1.65, but shares fell more than 17% after hours as the company guided subscription growth to slow to about 18.5% by fiscal year-end.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Atlassian reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $1,772 million, up 24% year over year, beating the $1.71 billion analysts expected, according to the company's official results release published through Business Wire on November 5, 2026. Cloud revenue rose 31% to $1,302 million, subscription annual recurring revenue reached $6,812 million, up 23%, and adjusted earnings per share were $1.65, up from $1.04 a year earlier and above the $1.43 consensus.

Shares fell more than 17% in after-hours trading despite the beat. Investors sold on the outlook for subscription growth: Atlassian expects subscription annual recurring revenue growth to slow to about 18.5% by fiscal year-end, down from the 22.5% just reported. The stock had nearly doubled between the August report and late October, according to SiliconANGLE.

WHY IT MATTERSA beat punished for what comes next

Almost every number in the quarter was strong. Remaining performance obligations jumped 44% to $4,746 million, free cash flow climbed to $463 million, the company made money on an unadjusted basis for the second quarter running with net income of $91 million, and it returned $581 million to shareholders through buybacks. Second-quarter revenue guidance of $1,825 million to $1,835 million was slightly ahead of the $1.82 billion analysts forecast.

The market looked past all of it to a single guided number. Subscription growth is the metric Atlassian is valued on, and management said it would slow over the next three quarters as tougher comparisons arrive and the DX acquisition laps over. A 24% revenue beat that comes with a decelerating growth guide is, in this market, a sell signal.

WHY IT MATTERSAI is now the product story, not the side note

More than 87% of enterprise cloud customers now use Rovo, Atlassian's AI assistant. Upgrades to the Teamwork Collection bundle rose sevenfold from a year ago to more than 3,000 customers, and annual recurring revenue from the larger Service Collection bundle is now above $1 billion across more than 70,000 customers.

Chief executive Mike Cannon-Brookes used the release to argue enterprise AI has reached an inflection point: model power alone no longer sets anyone apart, and platforms that give people and AI agents shared context will pull ahead. Atlassian, he said, has never been better positioned to be one of them. The AI story is intact; the argument is about the price of the growth that carries it.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA beat the market refused to buy

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Atlassian's quarter shows how the market now reads software earnings. Revenue up 24%, cloud up 31%, a 44% jump in remaining obligations, and the stock fell 17% because one guided number slowed. After doubling into the print, Atlassian was priced for acceleration, and deceleration is the one thing this market will not forgive. The Rovo adoption numbers say the AI product story is real; the valuation argument is about how fast that story can keep compounding.
