Anthropic's Stabilization Support Is Gone as Morgan Stanley Closes Its Position

Anthropic said on Friday, November 20, 2026, that Morgan Stanley, its IPO stabilization agent, has closed the position meant to support the stock after its market debut, removing a potential source of buying support as the shares fell 11.5% to $86, with an intraday low of $85. The underwriters had already exercised the full 147-million-share greenshoe at the $83 IPO price, lifting aggregate gross proceeds to roughly $93.5 billion, and can no longer use open-market purchases to cover the overallotment if the shares fall below $83.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Anthropic said on Friday, November 20, 2026, that Morgan Stanley, the investment bank named as stabilization agent for its initial public offering, had closed the position meant to support the stock after its market debut. With the shares down 11.5% on the day at $86, after falling as low as $85 earlier in the session, one potential source of buying support is now unavailable.

The mechanics are the greenshoe. As stabilization agent, Morgan Stanley received an option to buy 147 million additional shares directly from Anthropic on behalf of the underwriting syndicate, or to buy shares in the open market to cover the overallotment, which can support the price. Anthropic's shares closed on Thursday at $97.17, about 17% above the $83 IPO price, and Morgan Stanley exercised the full greenshoe at the IPO price. The exercise brought aggregate gross proceeds from the IPO to roughly $93.5 billion.

With the full option exercised, Morgan Stanley can no longer use open-market purchases to cover any remaining overallotment if the shares fall below the $83 IPO price. Underwriters typically have up to 30 days after an IPO to exercise the greenshoe, and it is common to exercise when shares trade above the IPO price on the first day, since the additional shares can then be bought directly from the company at the offering price. Market participants have questioned the underwriters' timing given Thursday's market volatility, one person familiar with the matter said. Morgan Stanley declined to comment.

The read from the market is split. Carsten Hesse, London-based head of index events at Berenberg, said the development is positive for Anthropic as it increases the proceeds raised in the IPO. "However, it is less positive for investors, who are losing an important potential source of buying support," he said in a note to clients. "It seems like the bookrunners have given up their remaining stabilisation option already after 1 day just as the share price is getting closer to the IPO price."

WHY IT MATTERSThe support was spent on day one

The stabilization option is meant to be a shock absorber for the first month of trading, and Anthropic's was used up in a single day. That is the arithmetic of a greenshoe exercised in full at the IPO price: the underwriters took the cheap shares from the company instead of buying in the market, which maximized the money Anthropic raised and removed the buyer of last resort at the same time. The trade was rational while the stock sat 17% above the offer price. It looks different now that the shares are sliding toward $83 with the broader AI trade falling around them.

What follows is a test of whether the debut's demand was real or borrowed. Anthropic's roadshow and first-day pop had raised hopes that other AI companies would follow it to the public markets, with issuers looking to capitalize on renewed investor appetite. SB Energy, a data-center developer, updated its IPO prospectus with fresh financial information shortly after Anthropic priced. But that optimism was tested late on Thursday as a broader selloff in technology stocks gathered pace, and Friday's slide shows the debut premium evaporating faster than the stabilization window was designed to handle.

WHAT TO WATCHWatch the $83 line

The number that matters now is the IPO price itself. The shares were last down 11.5% at $86, with an intraday low of $85, and one intraday snapshot put the session low at $83.05, essentially at the offer price. A close below $83 would turn the largest IPO on record into a broken deal on its second day, and there is no stabilization bid left to catch it. The Friday close, due after US markets shut, is the next data point, and the following sessions will show whether the buyers who chased the debut return at the offer price or wait below it.

The second thing to watch is the pipeline the debut was supposed to open. If Anthropic holds near or above $83, the roadshow logic survives and the AI companies waiting behind it can still price. If it breaks the offer price with no support left, the lesson for the next issuer is that a record debut buys one day of goodwill, not a month of cover.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe debut bought one day, not a month

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the greenshoe was exercised exactly as designed, and that is the problem. Taking the full 147 million shares at $83 was the rational move on a day the stock closed 17% above the offer price, but it spent the entire stabilization budget before the second day's selloff arrived. Anthropic kept the extra $12.2 billion; investors lost the buyer that was supposed to stand under the stock for thirty days. The IPO arc is now a clean test of demand: if the shares hold $83 without support, the debut was real. If they break it, the largest IPO on record will have taught the next AI issuer that a first-day pop is not a floor.
