Cloud Doubled to $30.7 Billion, and Alphabet's Stock Still Fell

Alphabet's third-quarter total revenue was $127.6 billion, up 25% year on year. Google Cloud revenue was $30.7 billion, up 103%, with operating profit of $11.1 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Alphabet released third-quarter results after the close on October 28: total revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25% year on year. Google Cloud revenue was $30.7 billion, up 103%, with operating profit rising from $3.6 billion a year earlier to $11.1 billion and a cloud backlog of $561 billion.

The other side of the report was equally striking: capital expenditure of $51.4 billion in the quarter and free cash flow of negative $9.1 billion. The company maintained its full-year 2026 capex guidance of $195 billion to $205 billion. Search revenue grew 12%, below the previous quarter's roughly 17%, and the company said AI Mode search currently monetizes below conventional search.

Profit was dragged by a paper loss: Alphabet recorded a securities loss of about $8.7 billion on its equity investment in SpaceX, and GAAP EPS of $2.12 came in below the market expectation of $3.02. The stock fell about a tenth after hours, and closed down about 12.6% the next day.

CLOUD TAKES CENTER STAGECloud Becomes the Protagonist of Alphabet's Story for the First Time

Cloud revenue rose from about 15% of total revenue a year ago to about 24%, with growth accelerating for several consecutive quarters. TPU system sales were named by the company as an important contributor.

That means Alphabet's growth story is changing engines: from advertising-driven to compute- and AI-infrastructure-driven.

THE CASH BURNNegative Cash Flow Is the Price of Expansion

$51.4 billion of capex in a single quarter bought free cash flow of negative $9.1 billion. What the market is really asking is when the return on that spending converges.

By maintaining its full-year capex guidance, the company effectively told the market: this quarter's cash-flow profile will not change in the near term.

PAPER LOSS VS OPERATIONSRead Paper Losses and Operations Separately

The SpaceX equity loss is unrealized paper volatility and does not reflect the quarter's operating cash. Excluding such items, operating profit grew about 30% year on year.

The most common misreading of earnings season is treating GAAP profit swings directly as business quality.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISCloud Doubling Is Right, and So Is the Market's Worry

Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: cloud demand has been proven; the next question is whether annual capex at the $200 billion level can turn into sustained cash returns within a reasonable time.

The boundaries of this piece: the $8.7 billion is a securities investment loss, and adjusted EPS differs across definitions; backlog is contracted but unrecognized revenue, not a schedule of future cash receipts; the stock declines are readings from different points in time, after-hours and the next day's close.
