$700 Million Series A, Drone Maker UFORCE's Valuation Up Sixfold in Six Months

State funds entering is the real signal of this round; the valuation is only the result. To prove it is worth $6 billion, UFORCE needs a British or American procurement order, not a bigger round.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

UFORCE announced a Series A, at a $6 billion valuation. The round was led by US firm Valor Equity Partners and the UK National Wealth Fund, with the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund also participating. The UK National Wealth Fund put in £113 million, about $150 million, its largest defence investment to date.

The company is headquartered in London and founded by Ukrainians — co-founded in January 2025 by People.ai founder Oleg Rogynskyy and former Ukrainian prime minister Oleksiy Honcharuk — and brings together nine Ukrainian defence companies. The product line has four branches: the MAGURA maritime drone, the NEMESIS strike drone, the LIUT ground robot and the PREDATOR anti-drone turret.

The previous round was in March this year: $50 million, at a valuation just over $1 billion. In eight months the round size grew 14-fold and the valuation sixfold. The new money goes to scaling up drone-system production, with factories planned for the US, Europe, Asia-Pacific and the Middle East. The UK side committed that for every MV-series drone boat built in Britain, more than 70% of content will come from UK suppliers.

WHAT TO WATCHCombat Records Are Company Claims; Orders Are the Hard Currency

Tech.eu credits MAGURA with sinking more than a dozen Russian warships, and says NEMESIS drones have flown more than 300,000 sorties. Both figures come from the company, with no independent verification.

The UK government's announcement promised hundreds of high-skilled jobs and apprenticeships, but gave no specific numbers, factory locations or output, and announced no Royal Navy procurement order. Factories first, orders later — that is the bet the UK National Wealth Fund has placed.

WHY IT MATTERSGovernment Funds Are Rewriting Defence Financing

UFORCE's shareholder list now holds two government-backed funds, the UK National Wealth Fund and the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund. Former NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe Christopher Cavoli joins the board as a Valor partner, and former defence secretary Ben Wallace sits on the board too.

Defence startups used to rely on venture capital; now they rely on state capital. Government funds bring order expectations along with political constraints, and UFORCE's next question is turning government endorsement into government procurement.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

From venture capital to state capital

FROM BATTLEFIELD TO PROCUREMENT
From battlefield experience to the procurement list
MAGURA and NEMESIS earned their records on Ukraine's battlefields. UFORCE must now turn battlefield experience into a standard category on British and American government procurement lists.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISValuation Up Sixfold, Orders Yet to Follow

Tokenta Signal's judgment: UFORCE's $700 million Series A is a state-endorsed financing — the UK National Wealth Fund and the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund entering together shows drone systems have moved from battlefield experience to a category on government procurement lists. A sixfold valuation jump in half a year is easy to understand; the hard part is producing a solid military order before the next round. Without orders, $6 billion is a paper number.
