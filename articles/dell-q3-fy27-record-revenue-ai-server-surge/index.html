Dell Revenue Doubles to a Record $54.9 Billion as AI Server Orders Flood In

Dell Technologies reported record fiscal 2027 third-quarter revenue of $54.9 billion on November 24, 2026, up 103% year over year and far above the $49.3 billion consensus, with non-GAAP earnings per share of $9.45 against $6.60 expected. AI-optimized server revenue hit $22.0 billion, up 291%, and Dell closed the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI server backlog after $60.2 billion in new orders. Dell raised full-year guidance to $202 billion of revenue and $29.68 of non-GAAP earnings per share, and lifted its AI server revenue forecast to $78 billion. Shares rallied in extended trading.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Dell Technologies reported record fiscal 2027 third-quarter revenue of $54.9 billion on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, up 103% from a year earlier and well above the $49.3 billion analysts expected, the company announced after the market close. Diluted earnings per share reached a record $8.82, up 287%, while non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $9.45, up 265%, beat the $6.60 consensus by a wide margin.

The surge came from the infrastructure business. Infrastructure Solutions Group revenue nearly tripled to $42.0 billion, up 198%, and AI-optimized server revenue hit $22.0 billion, up 291% from a year earlier. Dell logged $60.2 billion in new AI server orders during the quarter and closed it with a record $132.3 billion AI server backlog. The PC business grew more slowly: Client Solutions Group revenue rose 3% to $12.9 billion, down from 20% growth the prior quarter.

Dell raised its full-year guidance for the third time this year, to $202 billion of revenue and $29.68 of non-GAAP diluted earnings per share, up from $192 billion and $25.50. It also lifted its annual AI-optimized server revenue forecast to $78 billion from $74 billion. For the current quarter Dell forecast $56.3 billion of revenue and $8.40 of non-GAAP earnings per share. Dell shares rallied in extended trading after the report, Investor's Business Daily reported.

The result lands a day after rival Hewlett Packard Enterprise warned that AI server margins would compress in fiscal 2027, sending Dell shares down 12% in Tuesday afternoon trading before the numbers arrived. Dell's quarter shows the demand side of the same story: customers are still ordering AI servers at a record pace, and the question now shifts from whether the orders exist to what margin they carry.

WHY IT MATTERSDemand is not the constraint. Backlog is the story.

A $132.3 billion AI server backlog, built on $60.2 billion of new orders in a single quarter, is the clearest evidence yet that enterprise AI infrastructure spending has not slowed. The orders are real, the revenue is recognized, and Dell's full-year revenue is now expected to rise 78% to $202 billion.

The contrast with HPE's margin warning a day earlier frames the open question for the whole AI hardware trade: growth is no longer in doubt, profitability is. Dell's operating income rose 251% to $7.4 billion in the quarter, which suggests the company is converting the surge into profit for now. Whether that holds as the backlog converts is what the next two quarters will decide.

THE OTHER SIDEThe PC business is now a footnote to the server story.

Client Solutions Group revenue grew just 3% to $12.9 billion, a sharp slowdown from 20% the prior quarter, while infrastructure revenue nearly tripled. Dell is increasingly a server company that also sells PCs, not the other way around.

That concentration is the risk. A $132.3 billion backlog is an asset only while customers keep paying for the systems, and the AI buildout's durability is the question every hardware maker now faces. For this quarter, though, the numbers answered the demand question emphatically.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe AI buildout is still accelerating. The margin question comes next.

Dell just posted the strongest quarter in its history on the back of a $132 billion AI backlog, and it raised guidance for the third time this year. That settles the demand question that hung over the sector after HPE's margin warning a day earlier. What it does not settle is the profitability question: HPE's warning was about margins, not orders, and Dell's ability to keep converting this backlog at healthy margins is the test of the next two quarters. For now, the orders are real, the revenue is recognized, and the buildout rolls on.
