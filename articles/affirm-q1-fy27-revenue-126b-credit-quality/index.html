Affirm Q1 Revenue Jumps 35% to $1.26B, but Delinquencies Push Shares Down

Affirm reported fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $1.262 billion, up 35%, with earnings of $0.47 beating the $0.35 consensus, but 30-plus-day delinquencies rose to 2.9% and shares fell more than 9% after hours.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Affirm Holdings reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of $1.262 billion, up 35% year over year, beating the roughly $1.213 billion analysts expected, according to reports on the company's results released after the market close on November 5, 2026. Earnings were $0.47 per share, up 104% from a year earlier and above the $0.35 consensus, while gross merchandise volume rose 34% to $14.4 billion, ahead of the roughly $14 billion estimate.

The beat came with a blemish. Loans 30 or more days past due rose 35 basis points sequentially to 2.9%, and shares fell more than 9% in after-hours trading to $63.10, after an 8% fall in the regular session amid the broader tech rout. The company forecast second-quarter gross merchandise volume of $17.9 billion to $18.2 billion, above the roughly $17.74 billion analysts expected.

WHY IT MATTERSA beat with a blemish

On growth, the quarter was clean: revenue up 35%, gross merchandise volume up 34%, and earnings more than doubling year over year. Interest income rose 44.9% to $658.1 million, and merchant network revenue grew 18.2% to $296.9 million. The consumer is still borrowing and buying through Affirm at a rapid pace.

The market sold the stock anyway because of credit quality. Delinquencies 30 or more days past due climbed 35 basis points in a single quarter to 2.9%, and for a lender that is the number that decides whether growth is profitable. The after-hours fall of more than 9% says investors are weighing the loan book more heavily than the top line this quarter.

WHY IT MATTERSGuidance says the demand is still there

Management's outlook points to demand holding up into the holiday quarter. Second-quarter gross merchandise volume guidance of $17.9 billion to $18.2 billion sits above the roughly $17.74 billion analysts expected, a signal that the company does not see the delinquency uptick as a demand problem.

The open question is whether delinquencies keep rising. If the 2.9% level stabilizes, the quarter reads as a buying opportunity on a growth stock caught in a market rout. If it climbs again next quarter, the credit cycle, not the growth rate, becomes the Affirm story.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISGrowth beat, credit quality decided the trade

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Affirm delivered the growth quarter and got punished for the loan book. Revenue up 35%, earnings more than doubling, and holiday-quarter guidance above estimates would normally be a clean beat. The 35-basis-point rise in delinquencies to 2.9% changed the read: in a lender, credit quality is the growth story, and the market sold first and asked questions later. Watch the next delinquency print; if it stabilizes, this selloff looks like a rout casualty, and if it climbs, the credit cycle takes over the narrative.
