Intuit Sells Mailchimp for $3.68 Billion After Beating Q1 Estimates; Shares Fall 12%

Intuit reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $4.354 billion, up 12%, with non-GAAP earnings of $2.91 a share, beating LSEG estimates of $2.70 on $4.31 billion, and agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, roughly a third of the $12 billion it paid in 2021. Shares fell about 12% after hours after full-year revenue guidance was cut to $23.08-23.37 billion on a Credit Karma change.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Intuit reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 results after the close on November 19, 2026, for the quarter ended October 31. Total revenue rose 12% to $4.354 billion, and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 56% to $2.91, beating the $2.70 a share and $4.31 billion in revenue that analysts polled by LSEG expected, according to CNBC. GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 63% to $2.59, and GAAP operating income rose 63% to $868 million. Global Business Solutions revenue rose 15% to $3.1 billion, with Online Ecosystem revenue up 20% to $2.4 billion and QuickBooks Online Accounting up 19% to $1.4 billion. Credit Karma revenue rose 9% to $708 million, Consumer revenue rose 7% to $958 million, and Mailchimp revenue fell 1% to $313 million.

Alongside the results, Intuit said it has entered into a definitive agreement to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for approximately $3.68 billion, made up of $3.31 billion in cash and $368 million in Bending Spoons stock. The deal is expected to close in early calendar 2027, subject to regulatory approvals and other customary closing conditions, and Intuit said it intends to use the cash proceeds to repurchase shares in fiscal 2027. The price is about a third of the roughly $12 billion Intuit agreed to pay for Mailchimp in September 2021. Intuit said selling Mailchimp sharpens its focus on AI-native experiences and lead-to-cash capabilities for small businesses.

The market looked past the beat. Intuit shares fell about 12% in after-hours trading, to around $272 from a $310.02 close, after the company cut full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $23.081 billion to $23.365 billion, growth of 8% to 9%, on a change to Credit Karma. The cut is roughly $173 million at the midpoint, all attributed to Credit Karma, whose full-year growth is now guided at 3% to 7%, down from 11% to 13%. Operating income and earnings guidance were reiterated, including non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $22.88 to $23.12. For the second quarter, Intuit guided to revenue of $4.931 billion to $4.954 billion and non-GAAP earnings per share of $3.17 to $3.22.

WHY IT MATTERSA beat that the guidance cut erased

The quarter itself was clean: revenue and profit both beat the top end of Intuit's own guidance, and the beat against Wall Street was comfortable on both lines. The stock still fell because the number that moved was the one that prices the next twelve months, not the one that closed October 31. A guidance cut concentrated in a single business, Credit Karma, told the market the softness is specific and visible to management, not a rounding error.

Credit Karma is the swing factor in the Consumer segment, and the new 3% to 7% growth guide is a sharp step down from the 11% to 13% it carried into the quarter. Intuit held its profit guidance, which says the shortfall is a revenue timing and mix problem rather than a margin problem, but a market that had priced in a recovery in consumer credit products read it as the opposite of a beat.

WHAT TO WATCHMailchimp, from $12 billion to $3.68 billion

The divestiture is the bigger strategic statement. Intuit bought Mailchimp in 2021 to own the marketing layer for small businesses; five years later it is selling the unit for about a third of the purchase price and returning the cash to shareholders through buybacks. The $313 million of quarterly revenue Mailchimp still produces is flat to shrinking, and Intuit's framing is explicit: the future is AI-native lead-to-cash tools, not email marketing.

Watch the close, expected in early calendar 2027, and whether regulators clear it on schedule. Then watch the earnings call for what Intuit says about Credit Karma, the one business that turned a beat into a sell-off, and whether the $1.5 billion of buybacks in the quarter, up 73% from a year earlier, plus the Mailchimp proceeds, are enough to steady a stock down more than 50% this year.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe quarter was fine; the outlook was not

Tokenta Signal's judgment: this is a guidance story wearing an earnings beat as camouflage. Intuit delivered on the quarter and still lost an eighth of its value in an hour, because the only forecast that changed moved the wrong way and it moved in the business investors were already worried about. The Mailchimp sale is the honest part of the release: a clean exit from a 2021 bet at a third of the price, with the cash going back to shareholders. The question now is whether Credit Karma's slowdown is a one-quarter reset or the start of a longer drag on the consumer business, and the next call has to answer it.
