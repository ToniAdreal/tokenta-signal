€805 Million: Italy Fines Meta Over 20 Million People's Photo Galleries

The real signal is not the amount but the remediation order. A feature that was on by default must switch to off by default, and permissions already given must be sought again. Once this playbook stands in Italy, other European regulators can copy it directly.

The Garante's largest fine on record, about $887 million and 670 million pounds.

The fine as a share of Meta's global revenue; the amount is absorbable, the remediation order is the pressure.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE FEATURE — The Facebook Android gallery-suggestions feature was on by default, reading photos, videos and metadata.
  • 02THE LAW — Under Article 122 of the Italian Privacy Code, implementing the EU ePrivacy Directive, an app-level permission plus a vague prompt does not constitute valid consent.
  • 03THE ORDER — Switch to off by default within two months, seek separate consent again from already-authorized users, and report progress at three milestones.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The feature at issue is called 'Get suggestions from your gallery when you browse Facebook,' offered with the Facebook Android app since 2023 and on by default; in the Italian interface it reads 'Ottieni suggerimenti dalla galleria quando navighi su Facebook.' It reads photos, videos and metadata from the phone's gallery, covering about 20 million Android users in Italy. Users had given a single app-level permission plus a vague prompt, which the regulator ruled does not amount to valid consent.

The legal basis is Article 122 of the Italian Privacy Code, implementing the EU ePrivacy Directive. The case began in July 2025, triggered by a complaint from the Bolzano Consumer Protection Centre, after which the Garante carried out its own technical analysis.

The remediation order requires the feature to switch to off by default within two months, with users who already gave permission asked again for separate consent. Meta must report progress at 30, 60 and 90 days and submit its remediation measures within the same window. Meta called the fine disproportionate and says it will appeal.

WHAT TO WATCHThe Consent Bar Has Just Been Raised

An app-level permission plus a prompt used to be standard practice for many products. The Garante's ruling declares that sensitive operations like reading the gallery can no longer ride along. Consent must be separate, specific and provable.

This standard will not stop at Italy. Other European data protection authorities hold the same legal tools, and Meta's remediation plan will be studied word for word. Copying costs less than inventing.

WHY IT MATTERSThe AI Training Question Was Not Touched This Time

This case did not examine whether gallery content was used to train AI, and Meta denies such use. The Garante reserved the right to assess it separately, targeting the cloud-based gallery processing feature launched in 2026.

That means the fine is only the first half. If the assessment of the cloud processing feature begins, the dispute escalates from consent procedure to data purpose, and Meta will need a different explanation.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM BUNDLED PERMISSION TO SPECIFIC CONSENT
From bundled permission to specific consent
One app-level permission plus a vague prompt used to carry a whole data-reading setup. The Garante drew a line: reading a photo gallery, a sensitive operation, must be asked again separately and specifically.
FROM FINE AS COST TO ORDER AS PRECEDENT
From fine as cost to order as precedent
The fine is a small share of revenue and will not scare a large platform. What peers will copy is the combination of default-off plus staged reporting. Other European regulators can pick it up directly.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISConsent Cannot Be Assembled by Default

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Default-on gallery access turned user consent into a formality, and Italy fined a default-on product habit. The amount is only the volume that makes everyone listen. The fine is 0.47% of Meta's global revenue and can be absorbed. The real pressure sits in the remediation order: once the default flips to off, reading the gallery again requires a separate, specific consent. The appeal can take its time; the product logic has to change now.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

Whether gallery content was used for AI training is outside the scope of this case; Meta denies such use, and the Garante reserves the right to assess separately the cloud-based gallery processing feature launched in 2026. This does not constitute investment advice.
