California AG Subpoenas Nvidia Over Whether Chips or Secrets Reached China

California Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia on November 19, 2026, asking whether its advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public, after former CEO Jensen Huang resigned over an affair with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive US intelligence suspects of ties to China's Ministry of State Security. The SEC is also reportedly probing Nvidia's disclosures, and the House Foreign Affairs Committee the same day disclosed about 879,000 H200 and MI325X chips approved for China.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

California Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia on Thursday, November 19, 2026, the California Department of Justice said. The subpoena is tied to reports of former chief executive Jensen Huang's affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. The state is asking Nvidia whether its advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China, and whether the company misled investors and the public about the risk.

"The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors," Bonta said. "My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information, and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law." The information gathered will help the department evaluate whether Nvidia's actions violated California law.

Huang resigned on November 12 after admitting to an affair with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data company under investigation over suspicions it diverted restricted Nvidia chips to China. US intelligence agencies are trying to determine the extent of Alice Huang's ties to China's Ministry of State Security and what information she may have passed to Beijing, according to Reuters. China has rejected allegations of an espionage operation targeting Jensen Huang. Nvidia previously denied that Huang had an unusual relationship with her, according to the Wall Street Journal. Huang also admitted to affairs with two Nvidia employees. Nvidia's board has ruled out bringing him back as chief executive.

The subpoena lands alongside two parallel developments. The US Securities and Exchange Commission is also reportedly probing Nvidia after Huang's resignation, examining whether its investor disclosures accurately described its business. And on the same day, the House Foreign Affairs Committee released Commerce Department records showing the Trump administration approved shipments of nearly 900,000 advanced Nvidia H200 and AMD MI325X chips to Chinese companies between December 2025 and May 2026, although it was unclear how many were ultimately shipped. Nvidia said the approvals were subject to strict conditions and limited volumes and that it has stopped shipping H200 chips to China. Nvidia did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the subpoena.

WHY IT MATTERSA subpoena, not a lawsuit, and two questions at once

An investigative subpoena is a fact-finding instrument, not an accusation in court. No charges have been filed, and the attorney general's office framed the move as an evaluation of whether California law was broken. What makes the subpoena notable is that it asks two different questions at the same time: whether advanced chips or confidential information reached China, and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public about that risk.

The second question is the one with teeth. The first is an export-control and national-security matter that sits mostly with Washington. The second is a corporate-securities question that California can pursue on its own, and Bonta's statement leaned on it directly: companies cannot mislead their investors. That framing turns a scandal about a former chief executive into a disclosure case about the company, which is a much larger exposure for Nvidia than the affair itself.

WHAT TO WATCHThree fronts, one week

The subpoena is the third front to open on Nvidia in the same week. The SEC is reportedly examining the company's disclosures. The House Foreign Affairs Committee published the most detailed public record yet of H200 and MI325X licences for China, about 879,000 chips approved out of 3.4 million requested. And the company is already operating under an interim chief executive, Colette Kress, who told investors on the November 17 earnings call that licensed Hopper sales to China have stopped.

Watch first for Nvidia's response to the subpoena, which had not come by Thursday evening. Bonta told Politico that other subpoenas might follow depending on what the office receives from Nvidia. Then watch whether the SEC probe becomes formal, whether any of the approved chips are shown to have actually shipped, and whether the December 1 House interview and December 3 Senate Banking hearing with Huang produce anything on the record.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA scandal becomes a disclosure case

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the affair is the hook, the disclosure question is the story. A state attorney general does not need to prove espionage to hurt a public company; it needs only to show investors were told less than they should have been about a risk the company knew. Nvidia now faces that question from California, a parallel probe from the SEC, and a public licence record from the House, all in the same week, while an interim chief executive runs the company and a former chief executive prepares to testify before Congress. The subpoena itself proves nothing about chips reaching China. What it proves is that the post-Huang investigation has moved from headlines into formal process, and formal process is where the numbers get checked.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The subpoena and the attorney general's statements come from the California Department of Justice press release of November 19, 2026, read in full. The Reuters report, published 22:49 UTC the same day, corroborates the subpoena and adds the background on Huang's resignation, the intelligence assessment of Alice Huang, and the SEC probe. The affair with Alice Huang is admitted; her alleged ties to Chinese intelligence are suspected, not proven, and China has rejected the espionage allegation. No charges have been filed; an investigative subpoena is a fact-finding tool, not a finding of wrongdoing. This does not constitute investment advice.
