A Chain-Tracking Firm Lost Every User's Passwords and Tokens

Late on November 4, Chainalysis admitted in an incident notice that attackers had taken every user's name, username, password hash and live login token. A week earlier it said nothing had leaked.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01FIRST NOTICE — On October 30, the company first confirmed the incident, saying no credentials had been exposed.
  • 02WIDER EXPOSURE — On November 2, the company found a wider exposure; Reactor and other products went offline.
  • 03THE REVERSAL — On November 4, the notice was revised: password hashes and live tokens of all users had been exposed.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The attack was described as an on-path attack. The company named no attacker and said nothing about the tools used or where they landed. The notice said only that the attack vector has been addressed and monitoring strengthened.

The timeline is itself the news. Denial on October 30, wider exposure found on November 2, admission on November 4, phased restoration starting November 6. Each update was worse than the one before.

The company's business is helping police and banks trace money flows on-chain. Now it has to trace what was lost from its own systems first. The customer to-do list is concrete: reset passwords, rotate keys, and send trusted IP addresses to the company for allowlisting.

TWO REVERSALS IN A WEEKThe Story Flipped Twice in a Week

The first notice, on October 30, said there was no evidence that investigation data, credentials or session tokens had been touched. A late-night update on November 4 took that back, admitting that names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens covered all users.

The company has not published a user count, nor said whether each data type touched every user. The exposure is described as covering all users; the headcount is still unknown.

LIVE TOKENS, LIVE RISKLive Tokens Are Worse Than Password Hashes

A password hash is a scrambled password: it has to be cracked before it can be used. A live token needs no cracking. Holding it is enough to act as a signed-in user.

That is why the company is asking every user to reset passwords and rotate API keys. Once tokens leak, changing a password alone is not enough. The keys have to change too.

TOOLS DOWN, DATA HELDCompliance Tools Down First, Investigation Data Said Safe

Products including Reactor, the KYT interface and API, and address screening went offline entirely, leaving compliance teams without the tools they use to screen transactions for days. Some products returned on November 7. Data Solutions stayed offline.

The company drew a line, saying there is currently no evidence that investigation data was accessed and that attribution data was not altered. That line is the company's own word, not yet independently verified.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA Business Built on Trust Lost Its Credentials First

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the speed of the reversal matters more than the attack itself. First it said nothing happened, then it said everything was lost. Customers can only prepare for the worst.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The company has not disclosed a user count, nor said whether each data type covered every user. That investigation data was not accessed and attribution data was not altered remains the company's own account, not independently verified. This does not constitute investment advice.
