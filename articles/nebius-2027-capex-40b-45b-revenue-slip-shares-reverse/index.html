Nebius Plans $40-45 Billion of 2027 Capex as Revenue Slips, Shares Reverse

Nebius Group plans $40-45 billion of capital expenditures in 2027, far above the $28.77 billion FactSet consensus, and raised its 2026 spending plan to $27 billion, hours after reporting third-quarter revenue of $980.8 million, up 571% year on year. Full-year 2026 revenue is now expected at the low end of the $3-3.4 billion range, with some December revenue slipping to February. Shares reversed an early premarket gain and fell 8.3% to $141.90, according to Barron's.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Hours after Nebius Group reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $980.8 million, up 571% year on year and ahead of estimates, the market's verdict turned on the spending plan, not the print. The Amsterdam-based AI cloud company plans $40 billion to $45 billion of capital expenditures in 2027, far above the $28.77 billion FactSet consensus, and raised its 2026 capital spending plan to $27 billion from $20-25 billion. The figures come from founder and CEO Arkady Volozh's quarterly letter to shareholders, published alongside the results.

The revenue outlook moved the other way. Volozh said full-year 2026 revenue is now expected at the low end of the $3 billion to $3.4 billion range, with some revenue expected in December now likely to arrive in February instead. He described the $12-13 billion 2027 sales projection as reflecting a conservative approach to the pace of the build-out. Shares swung sharply in premarket trading, giving up an early gain to fall 8.3% to $141.90, according to Barron's, with other outlets reporting declines of about 10%.

The scale is the point. A $40-45 billion spending year would run at roughly three and a half times the $12-13 billion of sales Nebius projects for the same year, and the 2026 plan of $27 billion already exceeds the $23.85 billion FactSet estimate. Volozh's answer to the funding question is the prepayment line: customers are paying in advance, and the letter says prepayments plus investment-grade contract asset-backed financing are expected to cover 75% to 85% of 2027 capital spending. Whether that coverage holds as the build accelerates is the question the earnings call now has to answer.

WHY IT MATTERSThe beat stopped mattering within hours

A 571% revenue jump was priced in before the release: the print beat the $907.5 million consensus by 8%, yet the shares fell. The market is grading Nebius on the financing of the next build, not the last quarter, and the $40-45 billion figure landed well above what analysts had modeled. The read-across to the rest of the neocloud group is immediate: CoreWeave told analysts on Tuesday it would keep parent-level debt financing below $5 billion through 2027, a deliberately different answer to the same question.

The December-to-February slip matters because the 2026 guide now rests on timing, not demand. With $1,962.1 million banked in nine months, the low end of the $3-3.4 billion range implies about $1.04 billion in the fourth quarter. A slip that pushes December revenue into February does not change the annual total much, but it tests the claim that capacity is coming online on schedule, and it leaves the fourth quarter with less room for further slippage.

WHAT TO WATCHWhat the call has to answer

Watch the earnings call, which began at 08:00 ET, for the contracted share of the $12-13 billion 2027 sales projection and the timetable for the capacity behind it. Nebius has said its large second-quarter deals come online late in 2026 and help 2027 more than 2026; the call is the first chance to test that against the December slip.

Watch the financing detail behind the 75-85% coverage figure: how much is customer prepayments, how much is asset-backed borrowing against investment-grade contracts, and what remains to be raised in debt or equity. A $3.5 billion secured facility from Citi, signed in October, covers only a fraction of a $40-45 billion year.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe buildout is the story now

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Nebius won the quarter and lost the morning. A $40-45 billion spending plan against a $12-13 billion sales guide means the company is asking the market to fund a buildout roughly three and a half times its own revenue forecast, and the December revenue slip hands skeptics a timing question on top of a financing question. The prepayment line remains the defense, and it is a real one: customers are still paying before the data centers exist. But prepayments covered the last quarter's bill, and the 2027 bill runs about six times larger. Until the call spells out how much of the 2027 guide is contracted and how the rest of the spending gets funded, this is a growth story repriced as a financing story.
