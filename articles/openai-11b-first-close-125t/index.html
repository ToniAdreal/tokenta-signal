$11 Billion Lands First as OpenAI's Valuation Settles at $1.25 Trillion

Bloomberg reported on November 6 that the first roughly $11 billion of OpenAI's funding round has landed at a $1.25 trillion valuation. The round's overall target has been cut from $30 billion to about $20 billion, with the rest expected over the coming weeks.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Bloomberg reported on November 6 that the first roughly $11 billion of OpenAI's latest funding round has landed, at a $1.25 trillion valuation. Reuters cited the same figures the same day, saying SoftBank and Abu Dhabi sovereign fund MGX took part in the round.

This money is only the first close. Bloomberg's account has the round's overall target cut from an initial $30 billion to about $20 billion, with the rest expected to arrive over the coming weeks. OpenAI has not publicly confirmed the round and did not respond to requests for comment.

The previous round was in March this year: $122 billion in committed funding at a post-money valuation of $852 billion. Eight months on, the valuation is up about 47% while the fundraising target has shrunk by a third. The market is still handing over money at the same speed; it has simply grown more selective.

THE FIRST CLOSEThe $11 Billion That Arrived First

The $11 billion is already in the bank, not an intention and not a commitment. Bloomberg's sources said the money had arrived before Friday, November 6.

The first close has a very practical benefit. Compute bills do not wait for a roadshow, so the first tranche plugs the immediate gap while the rest is negotiated at leisure.

THE SHRINKING TARGETFrom $30 Billion Down to $20 Billion

The Financial Times wrote on October 30 that the target had been cut from $30 billion to about $20 billion, because some large investors had cooled. The November 6 wire confirms that judgment.

With the target cut by a third, the valuation also fell from the $1.5 trillion initially discussed to $1.25 trillion. Asked about it on CNBC, OpenAI's chief financial officer did not directly acknowledge a price cut.

THE OTHER SIDE OF THE WEEKThe Other Side of the Same Week

The same day, Bloomberg also reported that prospective buyers of Anthropic had pushed valuation expectations down to a $1.1 trillion to $1.3 trillion range, far below the $2 trillion discussed in early October. The two leading labs were repriced in the same week.

The Nasdaq had just suffered its deepest single-day fall in 19 months on November 5. The funding news landed at that moment, and $11 billion in the bank was itself an answer.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Money Landed; the Market Set the Price

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the $11 billion landing is a fact, and $1.25 trillion is the price the market offered in a cooling period. The raise did not fail; the bargaining power simply changed hands.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The figures come from Bloomberg sources and have not been confirmed by OpenAI. Reports differ on whether the $1.25 trillion valuation is pre-money or post-money. The full investor list has not been confirmed, and whether the remaining roughly $9 billion will arrive depends on later reporting. This does not constitute investment advice.
