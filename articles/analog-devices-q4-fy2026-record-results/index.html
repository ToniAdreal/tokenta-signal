Analog Devices Posts Record Quarter as AI Demand Meets a Broad Industrial Rebound

Analog Devices reported record fiscal fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results on November 24, 2026: fourth-quarter revenue of $4.37 billion, up 42% year on year, and adjusted earnings per share of $4.06, up 80%, both beating consensus. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $15.18 billion, up 38%. The first-quarter fiscal 2027 guide of about $4.4 billion in revenue and $3.82 in adjusted earnings per share was read as mixed, and the stock fell.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Analog Devices reported fiscal fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results before the market opened on November 24, 2026. Fourth-quarter revenue was $4.37 billion, up 42% year on year, and adjusted diluted earnings per share were $4.06, up 80%. Both beat consensus estimates of about $3.90 per share and about $4.33 billion in revenue. GAAP diluted earnings per share were $3.40, up 113%.

Fiscal 2026, which ended October 31, 2026, was a record year: revenue of $15.18 billion, up 38% from $11.02 billion in fiscal 2025, with adjusted diluted earnings per share of $13.05, up 68%, and GAAP diluted earnings per share of $10.21, up 124%. Operating cash flow was $6.2 billion and free cash flow $5.5 billion for the year, and the company returned $5.4 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks.

Growth was led by data center and industrial demand, but it was broad. In the fourth quarter, industrial revenue rose 55% to $2.19 billion, half of total revenue; communications rose 81% to $706.8 million; automotive rose 21% to $1.04 billion; consumer rose 7% to $429.4 million. Chief executive Vincent Roche said AI infrastructure investment is driving tremendous growth in the data center and automated test equipment businesses, while more than two-thirds of fiscal 2026 growth came from non-AI-centric market opportunities.

For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, Analog Devices forecast revenue of about $4.4 billion, plus or minus $150 million, with adjusted diluted earnings per share of $3.82, plus or minus $0.20, and reported earnings per share of $3.02, plus or minus $0.20. Investor's Business Daily described the outlook as mixed and reported that the stock fell on the news. The earnings call is scheduled for 10:00 ET (15:00 UTC) on November 24, 2026.

WHY IT MATTERSA record quarter that is not only an AI story.

The headline numbers are strong, but the mix is the signal. Industrial revenue grew 55% in the quarter and 51% for the full year, and communications grew 81% in the quarter and 74% for the year. Those are the two largest growth engines in the quarter, and the chief executive's own framing puts more than two-thirds of the year's growth outside AI-centric markets. For a chipmaker often read as a proxy for AI infrastructure spending, that breadth matters: the cycle is not resting on one end market.

Margins moved with the volume. Adjusted operating margin reached 52.3% in the quarter and 49.5% for the full year, up from 43.5% and 41.9% a year earlier, and free cash flow of $5.5 billion funded $5.4 billion returned to shareholders. A record year built on volume, margin expansion and cash generation is a different kind of strength from a record year built on one hot product line.

THE WATCHPOINTThe guide is the test.

The market's first verdict was not celebration. The first-quarter revenue guide of about $4.4 billion is roughly flat against the $4.37 billion just reported, and the adjusted earnings guide of $3.82 sits below the $4.06 of the fourth quarter. That is why the beat did not lift the stock. The question for the 15:00 UTC call is whether management frames the guide as normal seasonality or as a pause in the industrial and data-center momentum that carried the year.

The durable watch is order quality. Data-center and automated test equipment demand tied to AI infrastructure is lumpy and capital-cycle driven; industrial demand is broader but slower to turn. If the call confirms that the industrial rebound has backlog behind it, the record year reads as a base for fiscal 2027. If the guide reflects softening orders, the record becomes a peak to defend.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA record quarter with a flat guide — the market heard the second half.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the quarter itself is the strongest kind of beat, broad-based growth with expanding margins and $5.5 billion of free cash flow. But a record is a rear-view number, and the guide is flat against it. The honest read is that Analog Devices proved the demand is real across industrial, communications and data center, and the market is now asking whether that demand keeps compounding or pauses. The 15:00 UTC call decides which reading wins: backlog and order color would turn the flat guide into seasonality, while softening orders would turn the record into a peak. Until then, the beat belongs to the quarter and the doubt belongs to the outlook.
