Europe's Strongest Model Debuts on Cyber-Attack Capability

Europe's strongest 'sovereign AI' card happens to be the world's first trillion-parameter model to publicly sell cyber-attack capability as its headline feature. Open weights plus a low-guardrail government edition — in the name of 'digital sovereignty', Europe is issuing the world a double-edged sword.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 6, 2026, France's Mistral AI released Mistral Large 4 in public preview, officially nicknamed 'Le Chonk'. The specs: a 1.05 trillion-parameter MoE with 49 billion active per token, natively multimodal, trained from scratch on 3,800 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in its own European data centres — the training infrastructure entirely European.

The biggest selling point is not parameters. It is cybersecurity: 93% on Cybench, a global-best 82% on the CyberGym-E2E vulnerability-reproduction-and-patching task, and confirmation from Artificial Analysis as the number one open-weight model outside China. Mistral itself acknowledges that several closed frontier models score close to zero on such tasks — because they simply refuse to answer offensive security prompts.

THE PITCHSelling cyber-attack capability: a first in history

No mainstream model vendor has ever dared to put 'offensive cybersecurity capability' centre stage at a launch. Mistral not only did; it gave numbers: Cybench 93%, CyberGym-E2E 82%, a global best, ahead of MiMo-V2.6-Pro and GPT-6 Luna. Agentic coding on DeepSWE at 61.7% beats DeepSeek V4 Pro; on the Harvey legal benchmark, 15% beats GPT-6 Astra.

The subtext of this play is blunt: while US models add guardrails and Chinese models compete on Chinese-language scenarios, Europe chose a differentiation track nobody dared touch — 'we do not refuse to answer dangerous questions'. In the politically correct AI-safety climate of 2026, that is heresy — and precise positioning.

THE TWO TIERSTwo tiers: fewer guardrails in the government edition

One detail of the preview deserves attention: vetted cybersecurity partners and national institutions receive a version with 'reduced moderation and expanded cyber capability' — in Reuters' wording, 'fewer safety guardrails' — while the public version carries more. Mistral states plainly that it is privately red-teaming with cybersecurity partners.

In other words: the same model, with the government unlocking full attack capability while the public gets a neutered edition. This is the ultimate form of sovereign AI — the state customises not just the model, but the model's 'danger level'. When France hands Le Chonk to an ally's cyber forces, whether it is handing over a weapon or a product depends on who is looking.

THE OPEN-WEIGHT FUTUREOpen-weight futures: the weights ship at the end of October

The API is live, but the weights are only promised for the end of October, and the licence is still unpublished. As aidailypost put it: 'API is live now... but the weights do not ship until end of October, so self hosting is not yet possible.' For now, 'open source' is a futures contract.

The pricing is calculated too: preview pricing of $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 per million output tokens, half price for the first two weeks. This is not selling an API; it is seizing ecosystem position — locking developers into Mistral Cloud before the weights open. And the hardware bar for self-hosting the full 1.05 trillion weights is something Mistral has still not mentioned: once truly open, how many organisations could actually run it?

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISIn sovereignty's name, a double-edged reality

When France writes 'we do not refuse dangerous questions' into its national AI strategy, it is betting that the geopolitical leverage of offensive capability outweighs the accountability of proliferation risk. History repeatedly shows that once a weapon is open-sourced, the first to lose control of it is its inventor. Le Chonk's bulk was fed by a hundred kitchens; what is left after the guardrails come off is its lean side.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

If the end-of-October open-weights commitment slips, or the weights ship with restrictive licence terms, the title of 'number one open-weight model outside China' becomes an empty phrase. And the bigger question: when offensive cyber capability is published with the weights, the first party held to account will not be the user but the publisher. Europe's double-edged sword has its own name engraved on the handle.
