Kakao Mobility and Nvidia Sign Level 4 Self-Driving Pact, Starting on Kia PV5

Kakao Mobility announced on November 9, 2026 that it signed a memorandum of understanding with Nvidia to co-develop a Level 4 autonomous driving system, pairing its end-to-end driving AI and mobility platform with Nvidia's computing, sensing and safety technology. The system will run on a computing unit with two Nvidia DRIVE AGX Thor processors, using the Hyperion reference architecture and the Halos safety system, with first integration on Kia PV5 purpose-built vehicles. The memorandum is non-binding, and no timeline, fleet volume or investment figure was disclosed.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Kakao Mobility announced on Monday, November 9, 2026 that it has signed a memorandum of understanding with Nvidia to co-develop a Level 4 autonomous driving system. The two sides signed at a ceremony at The Westin Seoul Parnas hotel in Seoul's Gangnam district, on the sidelines of Nvidia AI Day Seoul 2026, the chipmaker's two-day event at COEX. Kim Jin-kyu, vice president of Kakao Mobility's Physical AI division, and Gaurav Agarwal, senior director of Nvidia's automotive division, signed for the two companies.

The plan pairs Kakao Mobility's end-to-end autonomous driving AI model and mobility platform with Nvidia's high-performance computing, sensing and safety technology, aiming at a Level 4 system that meets global standards. Kakao Mobility plans to run its driving software on an autonomous-driving computing unit built around two Nvidia DRIVE AGX Thor processors, use Nvidia's Hyperion sensor and computing reference architecture, and apply Nvidia's Halos integrated safety system to develop and validate the software. First integration work will be done on Kia's purpose-built vehicles, with the Kia PV5 as the initial model, building on a partnership Kakao Mobility struck with Kia in July 2026 to develop PBVs for autonomous-driving services.

WHY IT MATTERSA ride-hailing operator, not a carmaker, is Nvidia's Korean vehicle

Kakao Mobility is Korea's dominant ride-hailing operator, and that is what makes this pact different from a conventional automaker deal. Nvidia supplies the compute and the safety architecture; Kakao Mobility supplies the demand side, the fleet operator that would actually run a driverless service, plus driving software it says has already been validated in urban driving in Seoul's Gangnam district. The combination is a full stack from silicon to dispatch, assembled without a traditional carmaker at the centre.

The Kia PBV gives the project a production vehicle base rather than a retrofitted test fleet. The PV5 is a purpose-built vehicle designed for commercial service, so the integration work is about fitting Kakao Mobility's software and Nvidia's Thor-based computing unit into a vehicle already meant to carry passengers for a living. The open question is the one the companies did not answer: the memorandum is non-binding, with no date for commercial service and no investment figure attached.

WHY IT MATTERSSigned on Nvidia's stage, on Nvidia's terms

The venue is part of the message. The signing took place on the sidelines of Nvidia AI Day Seoul, the same event where LG AI Research opened its K-EXAONE 2.5 API for free and Nvidia named the finalists in Korea's government-backed sovereign AI project. Nvidia is using its Seoul stage to assemble a Korean physical-AI ecosystem in public, one partner at a time, and Kakao Mobility is the mobility piece.

The technology choices bind Kakao Mobility tightly to Nvidia's stack. Two DRIVE AGX Thor chips for onboard compute, the Hyperion reference architecture for sensors and networking, and Halos for safety validation mean the Level 4 system is designed, from the start, around Nvidia's hardware and software. That buys Kakao Mobility a proven safety framework and a fast path to a validated system, and it buys Nvidia a flagship Korean deployment for Thor, Hyperion and Halos in a single deal.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISNvidia is assembling Korea's physical-AI stack in public

Tokenta Signal's judgment: this is a distribution deal for both sides, and the venue says as much. Kakao Mobility gets Nvidia's Thor compute and Halos safety validation without building a car, and Nvidia gets Korea's largest ride-hailing operator as the deployment arm for its full autonomous-driving stack. The substance to watch is not the signing ceremony but the integration on the Kia PV5: turning Kakao Mobility's Gangnam-validated driving software and Nvidia's hardware into a certified Level 4 system is the hard step, and the memorandum commits neither side to a date or a dollar figure. Signed on Nvidia's Seoul stage, on the first day of its AI Day event, the pact reads as one more piece of an ecosystem Nvidia is assembling in the open.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The facts in this report rest on Kakao Mobility's own announcement of November 9, 2026, as reported the same day by Korea JoongAng Daily (Yonhap), Aju Press and Seoul Economic Daily, and on a same-day explainer by Unite.ai. The memorandum of understanding is non-binding, and the companies disclosed no timeline for commercial service, no fleet volumes and no investment figures. The Level 4 designation refers to driving without human intervention inside defined operating conditions and geographic zones; no vehicle has been certified or deployed under this agreement.
