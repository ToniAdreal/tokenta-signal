Coherent Q1 FY27: Revenue Up 50% to $2.37B as AI Optics Hit 82% of Sales

Coherent reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $2.37 billion, up 49.9% year over year, with non-GAAP EPS of $2.09, up 80%, as Datacenter and Communications revenue rose 79% to $1.95 billion. The company guided second-quarter revenue to $2.55 billion to $2.75 billion and expects fiscal 2027 pro forma growth above 60%.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Coherent reported first-quarter fiscal 2027 results after the market close on November 4, 2026, for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Revenue was $2.37 billion, up 49.9% year over year and 56% on a pro forma basis, beating the roughly $2.31 billion analysts expected. GAAP diluted EPS was $1.90, up from $1.19 a year earlier, and non-GAAP diluted EPS was $2.09, up 80% from $1.16.

The growth came from AI infrastructure demand. The Datacenter and Communications segment reached $1.95 billion, up 79% year over year and 82% of total revenue, with datacenter revenue up 85% and communications revenue up 84% according to the company's investor presentation. The Industrial segment declined 14.8% to $418.6 million. Non-GAAP gross margin was 40.6%, up 184 basis points year over year, and non-GAAP operating margin reached 23.2%.

The outlook was the bolder part of the print. Coherent guided second-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue to $2.55 billion to $2.75 billion, with a $2.65 billion midpoint above the $2.54 billion consensus, and non-GAAP EPS of $2.28 to $2.48. Management expects fiscal 2027 pro forma revenue growth above 60%, a first $3 billion revenue quarter by the end of fiscal 2027, a first $4 billion quarter in fiscal 2028, and a first quarter above 45% non-GAAP gross margin before the end of calendar 2028. StockStory reported shares rose 4.8% to $204.92 immediately after the release.

WHY IT MATTERSOptics is where the AI build shows up as revenue

Coherent's quarter is the photonics version of the AI infrastructure story told this week by chipmakers: the bottleneck is moving from compute to connectivity. CEO Jim Anderson framed it directly, saying AI datacenters are transitioning from copper to optical connectivity, and Coherent's platform and manufacturing scale position it to meet rising bandwidth and efficiency needs.

The segment mix proves the point. Datacenter and Communications is now 82% of revenue, while Industrial shrinks. When one segment grows 79% and the other falls 14.8%, the company is no longer diversified in any meaningful sense; it is an AI connectivity supplier with a legacy business attached.

WHY IT MATTERSThe guidance ladder is unusually long

Most earnings prints guide one quarter ahead. Coherent guided a quarter, a fiscal year, a $3 billion quarter, a $4 billion quarter, and a gross-margin milestone two years out, all in one release. That is a company asking to be valued on a multi-year ramp, not on a single beat.

The test is the same as for every AI infrastructure supplier this season: whether the guided ramp survives contact with customer concentration, capacity, and pricing. The $2.55 billion to $2.75 billion second-quarter guide is the first checkpoint, and it already sits above consensus.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe AI trade's next bottleneck is the fiber between the racks

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Coherent's print matters because it moves the AI infrastructure story one layer down the stack, from chips to the optical links between them. A segment growing 79% to 82% of revenue, plus a guidance ladder stretching to a $4 billion quarter, makes photonics a first-class AI trade rather than a supplier footnote. The watchpoint is delivery against that ladder, starting with the $2.55 billion to $2.75 billion second-quarter guide.
