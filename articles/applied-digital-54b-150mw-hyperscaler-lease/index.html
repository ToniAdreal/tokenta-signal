WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Applied Digital announced on November 23, 2026, that it has signed an 18-year lease with a tier-one investment-grade hyperscaler for 150 MW of critical IT capacity. The company did not name the tenant. The lease provides for approximately $5.4 billion in base-term contracted revenue, or about $12.7 billion if all renewal options are exercised over a 33-year total term.

With the new lease, Applied Digital's total contracted base-term lease revenue exceeds $41 billion, or about $100 billion with all renewals exercised. Its portfolio now stands at more than 1.6 GW of total contracted critical IT load and 2.2 GW of grid-connected peak load. About 75 percent of contracted base-term revenue is now tied to investment-grade customers.

The company said the lease carries improved commercial terms over its prior leases, including higher pricing, and that the 18-year duration gives it greater long-term revenue visibility. Delivery of the capacity is expected to begin in the first half of 2028, subject to development milestones, utility arrangements and regulatory approvals. The build will be financed with first-in equity from the balance sheet and through a $5 billion programmatic equity partnership with Macquarie Asset Management, followed by project financing backed by the lease. Applied Digital also closed a $300 million senior secured bridge facility on the same day to support development of its Delta Forge 1 campus in Louisiana.

WHY IT MATTERSThe backlog keeps compounding, and the terms keep improving.

The new lease follows a 210 MW, 15-year lease worth about $5.2 billion that Applied Digital signed in June 2026 at its Delta Forge 2 site. Two leases in five months, each above $5 billion in base-term revenue, show a builder converting hyperscaler demand into long-dated contracted cash flow rather than speculative capacity.

The pricing detail matters as much as the size. Applied Digital said the new lease carries higher pricing than its prior leases, and the 18-year term is longer than the 15-year standard of its earlier deals. Hyperscalers are paying more, for longer, to lock in power and cooling for AI workloads. With about 75 percent of contracted revenue now from investment-grade tenants, the backlog reads less like a bet on one customer and more like a diversified infrastructure book.

THE FINANCING CHAINThe financing chain is the part to watch.

Applied Digital plans to fund the build with equity already on its balance sheet and its $5 billion Macquarie Asset Management partnership, then project financing supported by the lease itself. It expects to complete the remaining project finance for Delta Forge 1 and Polaris Forge 3 in the first quarter of 2027, followed by Delta Forge 2 in the second quarter.

The structure is the standard one for this buildout: long-term lease in hand, then debt raised against it. The risk is execution, not demand. Only a fraction of the contracted 1.6 GW is live today, and delivery of the new capacity is not expected before the first half of 2028. The project-finance closings in early 2027 are the next checkpoints.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISHyperscalers Are Paying More, for Longer, to Lock In AI Power.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the headline number is the $41 billion backlog, but the signal is the pricing. A longer term at higher prices, from an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler, says the constraint in AI infrastructure is still power and delivery, not demand. The exposure is the build itself: most of the contracted capacity is not live yet, and the financing chain has to close in early 2027 before the first half of 2028 delivery. If the project finance lands on schedule, the backlog converts into the most durable revenue stream in the AI buildout.
