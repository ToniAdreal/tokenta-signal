Meta's Chief Strategy Officer Leaves for a $17.5 Billion Inference Startup

On November 7, Meta chief strategy officer Dave Wehner confirmed he was leaving to become chief financial officer of AI inference company Fireworks. He spent 14 years at Meta, eight as chief financial officer.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The inference layer is becoming the new cloud. Training models costs more and more, yet the business of running models is seeing revenue first. The size of Fireworks' Series D shows capital is already pricing the inference layer.

On Meta's side, the strategy officer's seat is now empty. The company has not commented publicly on the departure and has not named a successor.

Wehner is not the first to move from a big tech company to an inference startup. Competition in inference has shifted from model quality to cost, speed and financial discipline. The man who kept Meta's books is arriving to cover exactly that.

FOURTEEN YEARSWehner's Fourteen Years

Wehner joined the then-Facebook in 2012, after two years as chief financial officer of Zynga. He became CFO in June 2014; Susan Li succeeded him in November 2022, and he became Meta's first chief strategy officer.

He announced the move himself on X. Fireworks chief executive Lin Qiao said Wehner's job is to scale the company's finance and operations.

WHAT FIREWORKS DOESWhat Fireworks Does

Founded in 2022, Fireworks is led by founder Lin Qiao, formerly head of Meta AI infrastructure, with a team drawn largely from Meta and Google. The company runs an inference and model-serving platform, helping enterprises customize and run open models.

Customers include Cursor, Perplexity and Uber. The company says annualized revenue reached $1 billion by mid-2026, processing more than 40 trillion tokens a day.

NEXT ROUND, DOUBLEThe Next Round Could Double the Valuation

Fireworks closed a $1.505 billion Series D in July this year at a $17.5 billion post-money valuation, led by Atreides Management, Index Ventures and TCV.

Reports say the company is in talks for a new round targeting a $30 billion to $40 billion valuation. That figure is unconfirmed, and neither Wehner's start date nor Meta's successor for the strategy role has been announced.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the first half of the race was about model quality; the second half of inference is about who can keep the books straight. Wehner's resume is exactly what Fireworks is buying.
