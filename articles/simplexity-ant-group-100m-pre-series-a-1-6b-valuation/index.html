WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On November 23, 2026, Bloomberg reported that Chinese physical AI startup Simplexity Robotics received $100 million in a pre-Series A funding round led by Jack Ma-backed Ant Group, valuing the company at $1.6 billion. Chairman and co-founder Wang Kai told Bloomberg News the figures directly. Existing investors Tencent Holdings, HSG and Lanchi Ventures joined the round.

Simplexity was founded in July 2025 in Hangzhou by three former Li Auto executives, including Wang Kai as chairman and Jia Peng as chief executive. The company is developing embodied AI and robots that perform tasks in physical environments. Its latest product, a wheeled humanoid called iX, was unveiled on Monday, November 23, with demonstrations including preparing and delivering drinks, fetching objects and guiding visitors.

Simplexity says it is pursuing service tasks that require a combination of perception, navigation and manipulation, bringing its automotive engineering background into competition with established humanoid developers. Bloomberg noted that China's humanoid robotics industry accounted for 97% of the machines shipped in the first three quarters of 2026, an early lead over American rivals.

WHY IT MATTERSA $1.6 billion valuation for a 16-month-old humanoid startup says the race is about capital velocity.

Simplexity is barely over a year old and has already reached a unicorn-scale valuation on the strength of a founding team, a demo and a cap table full of China's largest technology companies. That is not a valuation of shipped robots. It is a valuation of position: Ant Group, Tencent, HSG and Lanchi Ventures are buying into the team they believe can convert automotive manufacturing experience into humanoid production before the market consolidates.

The round also shows where the humanoid race is being financed. Bloomberg's shipment figure, 97% of humanoids shipped in the first three quarters of 2026 coming from China, frames the funding as part of a national industrial push rather than a single startup's milestone. The question for the rest of the field is whether Western humanoid companies can match that combination of state-scale demand, manufacturing depth and now, with Ant Group, fintech-scale capital.

WHAT TO WATCHWhether iX moves from demo floor to paying service deployments.

The demonstrations so far are service tasks in controlled settings: drinks delivered, objects fetched, visitors guided. Those are the right tasks for a wheeled humanoid, which trades the complexity of bipedal walking for stability and payload on flat floors. But the gap between a demo and a deployment is measured in uptime, safety certification and unit economics, none of which the funding announcement addresses.

Watch for Simplexity's first named customer deployments and any production volume figures. A second signal is whether Ant Group's backing extends beyond capital into distribution, for example placing iX units in Ant's own retail, logistics or hospitality partners. If the robots stay on demo stages, the $1.6 billion valuation will look like a bet on a team. If they start working shifts, it will look like a bet on an industry.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM CARS TO ROBOTS
From car makers to robot makers
Simplexity was founded by former Li Auto executives and says it is bringing automotive engineering discipline to humanoids. The bet is that building cars at scale teaches the supply chain, manufacturing and systems integration that robots now need.
FROM CUSTOMERS TO BACKERS
From tech giants as customers to tech giants as backers
Ant Group joins Alibaba, Tencent and other Chinese technology groups putting capital directly into humanoid startups. The funding round is as much a signal about who is placing the bets as it is about the company receiving them.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Valuation Is a Bet on the Team and the Supply Chain, Not on Shipped Robots

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Simplexity has raised a large round on a thin public record, one product unveiling and no disclosed customers or revenue. That is defensible only if the thesis is right: that the humanoid race will be won by whoever industrialises fastest, and that former car executives with the backing of China's biggest technology groups are the fastest industrialisers available. The $1.6 billion price tag is a bet that the bet is right. The proof will be deployment numbers, not demos.
