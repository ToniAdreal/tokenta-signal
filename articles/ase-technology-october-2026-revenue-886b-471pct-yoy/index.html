ASE October Revenue Jumps 47.1% as Chip Packaging Demand Keeps Climbing

ASE Technology reported October 2026 consolidated revenue of NT$88.615 billion (US$2.779 billion), up 47.1% year over year, with its core packaging and testing business up 57.7% to NT$56.840 billion. The print extends a third quarter in which packaging and testing revenue grew 51.0%, and management has guided fourth-quarter revenue to grow 6 to 8 percent sequentially.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

ASE Technology Holding reported unaudited consolidated net revenues of NT$88.615 billion (US$2.779 billion) for October 2026, up 47.1% year over year from NT$60.231 billion in October 2025 and up 0.6% from NT$88.130 billion in September 2026. In US dollar terms, revenue rose 40.4% year over year and 0.1% sequentially.

The growth was led by the company's core assembly, testing and material (ATM) business, which generated NT$56.840 billion (US$1.783 billion) in October, up 57.7% year over year from NT$36.039 billion and up 7.9% from NT$52.674 billion in September. In US dollar terms, ATM revenue rose 50.5% year over year and 7.4% sequentially.

The October print extends a run that was already visible in the third quarter, when ASE's ATM revenue reached NT$151.49 billion, up 51.0% year over year, and group revenue reached NT$244.16 billion, up 44.8%. Management's outlook, given with the October 29 earnings release, projects consolidated fourth-quarter revenue to grow 6 to 8 percent quarter over quarter in NT dollar terms.

WHY IT MATTERSThe back end of the AI build-out is still accelerating

ASE packages and tests the chips that the AI build-out runs on, so its monthly revenue is a direct read on how much silicon is actually moving through the supply chain. A 57.7% year-over-year rise in packaging and testing revenue, on top of a 51.0% rise in the third quarter, says the bottleneck has not moved upstream to demand: customers are still ordering more advanced packaging and test capacity, and ASE is still shipping it.

The sequential picture matters as much as the annual one. Consolidated revenue was nearly flat month over month, up only 0.6%, while the ATM segment grew 7.9% in the same month. The mix is shifting toward the higher-value packaging and testing work, which is where AI accelerators and high-bandwidth memory stacks consume capacity. That is the segment ASE's customers are paying a premium for.

WHY IT MATTERSA monthly print, not a quarter, and the bar for the rest of the year

Monthly revenue is a noisy signal by design: it is unaudited, it covers one month, and a single large customer's shipment schedule can move it. What makes this print worth more than a single month is the sequence. September consolidated revenue was already up 45.5% year over year; October held that level and the ATM segment accelerated from 50.5% to 57.7%. Two months of prints at this pace, plus a third quarter that grew 44.8%, is a trend, not a spike.

The test now is the fourth quarter. Management guided consolidated revenue to grow 6 to 8 percent quarter over quarter, which would put the quarter well above the third quarter's NT$244.16 billion. If the November print holds the October pace, the guidance looks conservative; if it fades, the October number will read as a pull-forward. The next monthly release is the check.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe packaging bottleneck is still the story

Tokenta Signal's judgment: This is a supply-chain print, not a product launch, and its meaning is in the mix. Consolidated revenue was nearly flat month over month, but the packaging and testing segment grew 7.9% in the same month and 57.7% year over year, which says the AI build-out is still pulling capacity toward the back end of the line. The honest check is the November print and the fourth-quarter guide: management expects 6 to 8 percent sequential growth, and two months at this pace make that look reachable rather than ambitious. A monthly figure is unaudited and can swing on one customer's schedule, so the number to watch is whether the segment keeps outgrowing the group.
