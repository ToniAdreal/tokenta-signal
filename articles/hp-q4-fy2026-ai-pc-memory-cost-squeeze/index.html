HP Beats on AI PCs as Memory Costs Bite, Free Cash Flow Set to Fall

HP Inc. reported fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 revenue of $16.0 billion, up 9.3% year over year and above the $15.4 billion analysts expected, with non-GAAP earnings of $0.83 a share against a $0.75 consensus. AI PCs reached nearly 60% of shipments and commercial PC revenue rose 23%. Memory and storage cost pressures cut the non-GAAP operating margin to 6.2% from 8.0% a year earlier, and HP guided fiscal 2027 free cash flow to $2.9 to $3.1 billion, below fiscal 2026's $3.3 billion. The dividend was raised to $0.31 a share.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

HP Inc. reported fiscal fourth-quarter 2026 results after the market closed on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, and the numbers beat expectations on both lines. Revenue reached $16.0 billion, up 9.3% from a year earlier and above the $15.4 billion analysts expected. Non-GAAP earnings were $0.83 a share, against a $0.75 consensus and above HP's own outlook of $0.69 to $0.79. GAAP earnings were $0.84 a share, flat from a year earlier. Shares rose after the report, according to Barron's.

The growth came from personal computers. Personal Systems revenue rose 14% to $11.8 billion, with commercial revenue up 23% while consumer revenue fell 7%. AI PCs reached nearly 60% of shipments in the quarter. Printing revenue was $4.3 billion, down 1%, with a 19.7% operating margin. For the full fiscal year, revenue was $60.5 billion, up 9.5%, and non-GAAP earnings were $3.33 a share, up 6.7%.

The cost side is where the quarter turns. Interim chief executive Bruce Broussard said HP took steps "to address memory and storage cost pressures," and the margin shows it: the fourth-quarter non-GAAP operating margin fell to 6.2% from 8.0% a year earlier, and quarterly free cash flow dropped to $0.8 billion from $1.49 billion. It is the same pressure that sent Hewlett Packard Enterprise shares down the day before, and the same question hanging over Dell's record server quarter.

HP guided fiscal 2027 non-GAAP earnings to $3.15 to $3.45 a share and free cash flow to $2.9 to $3.1 billion, below fiscal 2026's $3.3 billion. First-quarter fiscal 2027 non-GAAP earnings are guided to $0.66 to $0.76 a share. The board raised the quarterly dividend to $0.31 a share, payable January 6, 2027. HP also named Emma Walmsley as its next chief executive during November.

WHY IT MATTERSThe beat is real, and the squeeze is the story.

HP beat on revenue and earnings, and the driver is an enterprise AI-device refresh: commercial PC revenue up 23% and AI PCs at nearly 60% of shipments. That is demand, not accounting. But the non-GAAP operating margin fell 1.8 points year over year and quarterly free cash flow nearly halved, which says the memory bill is eating the beat as fast as HP books it.

HP is now the third hardware name in three days to report the same tension. HPE warned its Cloud and AI margin falls to about 14% in fiscal 2027. Dell doubled revenue on AI servers while the market asked about margins. HP's answer is that it can still grow and still pay a higher dividend, but its own forecast has cash flow going down next year. The industry's problem is no longer finding AI demand. It is paying for the memory inside the machines.

THE WATCHPOINTCash flow, not revenue, is the test for fiscal 2027.

The numbers to watch are the fiscal 2027 free cash flow range of $2.9 to $3.1 billion, against $3.3 billion in fiscal 2026, and the earnings range of $3.15 to $3.45 a share, against $3.33 this year. If memory and storage costs ease in the second half of fiscal 2027, HP's guidance will look prudent and the dividend raise will look like confidence. If costs keep rising, the guided cash flow decline becomes the start of a longer squeeze.

The next data points are HP's first-quarter fiscal 2027 report in February 2027 and, before that, whether component prices stabilize after the holiday quarter. Incoming chief executive Emma Walmsley inherits a company whose AI PC story is working and whose cost story is not yet solved.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA demand beat with a cost problem attached.

Tokenta Signal's judgment: HP's quarter is the third data point in three days that the AI hardware boom now has a cost problem, not a demand problem. Revenue beat, AI PCs are nearly three-fifths of shipments, and the dividend went up. But the operating margin fell 1.8 points and next year's free cash flow is guided down. HP is managing the squeeze better than HPE did, and the market rewarded the beat, but the company's own forecast says the memory bill keeps rising before it falls.
