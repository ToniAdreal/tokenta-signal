IREN AI Cloud Revenue Nearly Triples to $200M as $4B Target Slips to 2027

IREN reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of $228 million, up 66% sequentially, with AI cloud revenue nearly tripling to $200 million, but pushed its $4 billion annualized run-rate target to January 2027; shares fell 17% in the session, then rebounded 9.6% after hours.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

IREN reported fiscal first-quarter 2027 revenue of $228 million on November 5, 2026, up 66% sequentially from $137 million. AI cloud revenue nearly tripled to $200 million, up 182% from $71 million and now 88% of total revenue, while bitcoin mining revenue fell to $28 million from $67 million as the company continued its shift from mining to AI infrastructure. The net loss narrowed to $263 million from $684 million in the prior quarter, and adjusted EBITDA rose to $66 million from $19 million. AI cloud gross margin was 86%.

The release also carried a delay. IREN pushed back its target for more than $4 billion in contracted annualized run-rate revenue from the end of 2026 to January 31, 2027, with a possible extension to late February, citing a customer-requested design change and supplier timing. Revenue backlog rose 22% sequentially to $20.3 billion, about two-thirds with investment-grade counterparties. The company expects $15 billion of remaining capital spending this fiscal year, of which $9 billion is covered by cash, committed GPU financing and prepayments, while $6 billion depends on market conditions. Shares fell 17.0% in the regular session on November 5, closing at $18.03, then rebounded 9.6% after hours to $19.69.

WHY IT MATTERSThe AI cloud ramp is real; the $4 billion target slip is the story

AI cloud revenue of $200 million, up 182% in a single quarter, is the transformation IREN has been pitching: a bitcoin miner becoming an AI infrastructure provider, with a $9.7 billion Microsoft contract behind it and a $20.3 billion backlog. The mix shift is now decisive, with AI cloud at 88% of revenue against 12% for mining, and an 86% gross margin on the AI cloud segment.

The market's first reaction was to the delay, not the ramp. Pushing the $4 billion annualized run-rate target by one to two months, on a customer design change and supplier timing, is a small slip in calendar terms but a large one for a stock whose valuation rests on execution against a $15 billion capital plan. The 17% regular-session drop, followed by a 9.6% after-hours rebound, shows the market arguing with itself about whether this is a scheduling wrinkle or an early sign of delivery risk.

WHY IT MATTERSThe funding gap is the number to watch now

IREN disclosed that of the $15 billion of capital spending remaining this fiscal year, $6 billion depends on market conditions. That is the sentence that matters most in the release. The company has $9 billion covered, but the rest of the build-out assumes capital markets stay open to a business that just reported a $263 million quarterly loss and is spending at a pace its current revenue cannot fund.

The backlog, up 22% to $20.3 billion with two-thirds investment-grade counterparties, is the counterweight: contracted demand exists. The test through January and February, when the $4 billion target is now due, is whether the customer design change resolves cleanly and whether the remaining $6 billion of funding lands without dilutive terms. If both hold, the quarter reads as a ramp with a one-month slip; if either wobbles, the funding question becomes the story.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA real AI ramp with a funding question attached

Tokenta Signal's judgment: $200 million of AI cloud revenue, up 182% in one quarter with an 86% gross margin, is the transformation working. But the $4 billion target slip and the $6 billion of capital spending that depends on market conditions are the two facts the market will price until January. IREN is now an execution story, not a demand story: the backlog is there, and the question is whether the build-out and the funding arrive on schedule.
