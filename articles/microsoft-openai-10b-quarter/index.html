Microsoft Made $10 Billion From OpenAI in a Single Quarter

Microsoft's fiscal 2027 first quarter, ended September 30, posted total revenue of $92.3 billion, up 19% year on year. Azure revenue was $33.2 billion, and revenue from OpenAI commercial arrangements was $10 billion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Microsoft's quarterly filing, disclosed on October 29, laid out a set of numbers usually buried in earnings footnotes: in the quarter ended September 30, revenue from OpenAI commercial arrangements reached $10 billion, roughly 11% of total revenue of $92.3 billion. In the same quarter, Azure revenue was $33.2 billion, up 48% year on year, the first time Microsoft disclosed Azure's quarterly revenue in dollars.

The $10 billion came from three sources: OpenAI using Azure to train and run models, Microsoft reselling access to OpenAI models through Azure, and revenue sharing between the two. The filing does not split the three parts or disclose margins.

Receivables are growing too: at the end of September, OpenAI owed Microsoft $7.2 billion, up from $6 billion at the end of June. Microsoft's stake in OpenAI has been diluted from about 25% to 24%, and full-year fiscal 2026 revenue from OpenAI was $24.1 billion.

PARTNER AND RIVALPartnership and Competition, Written Into the Same Balance Sheet

Microsoft is both OpenAI's largest infrastructure supplier and shareholder, and a competitor for the same enterprise customers. The $10 billion quantifies that dual relationship.

The revenue-sharing agreement runs to 2030 with a cap, and OpenAI has meanwhile gained more freedom to use other clouds.

THE RECEIVABLESReceivables Are a More Honest Signal Than Revenue

$7.2 billion in receivables means part of the revenue has not yet turned into cash. OpenAI's balance owed rose by $1.2 billion in a single quarter; the cadence of cash payment is lengthening.

Reading this relationship, do not look only at the revenue line; the growth rate of receivables is equally a health indicator.

WHERE IT WAS DISCLOSEDWhere It Was Disclosed Is Itself the News

The $10 billion did not appear in the headline of the earnings release; it was buried in the related-party notes of the quarterly filing and only entered public view after media reporting pulled it together.

What a company chooses to put in the headline and what it puts in the footnotes is itself a narrative choice.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Microsoft-OpenAI Relationship Has a Quarterly Price for the First Time

Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: $10 billion shows the partnership is still making money; $7.2 billion in receivables shows the cash cadence of this relationship is worth continuing to watch.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The boundaries of this piece: the $10 billion covers Azure usage, model resale and revenue sharing; the filing does not break down the shares or margins; receivables are not new revenue; the filing does not state the reason for the stake dilution. This does not constitute investment advice.
