Five Countries Listed, US Closes the Remote-Compute Rental Route Too

The rule moves the target from the chip itself to the right to use compute. The window for Chinese companies to rent US compute through third countries is closing, and compliance costs will land before any technology blockade.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE FILING — An interim final rule, effective upon Federal Register publication, RIN 0694-AK50.
  • 02THE KNOWLEDGE TEST — The trigger for data center controls is the exporter knowing the end use, not where the chips themselves go.
  • 03THE CARVE-OUT — Chips shipped to companies headquartered in the US are not subject to the new license requirements.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The rule is formally titled 'Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors,' RIN 0694-AK50, submitted November 16 and set to take effect upon publication in the Federal Register on November 19. Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil are listed as countries with higher-than-normal diversion risk, and exports of advanced AI chips to them require a license.

A second layer of controls targets data centers themselves. Exporters that know chips will ultimately serve a data center providing more than the rule's threshold of total processing performance to a company headquartered in China, Macau or a Country Group D:5 nation must apply for a license. Country Group D:5 covers China, Russia, Iran, North Korea and 22 countries and regions in total.

The rule also rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, eliminating model-weight controls under ECCN 4E091, License Exceptions AIA and LPP, the country-tier system and the Data Center Validated End User program. The rule cites the 'superintelligence' concept from Executive Order 14434, updating the control language accordingly.

WHAT TO WATCHThe List Targets Diversion, Not Local Consumption

Singapore and Malaysia are regional data center hubs; the list's real target is the transit route through third countries, not local demand in those five countries.

Case-by-case licensing means compliance judgment moves up front. Exporters must prove the end user and end use before shipping, and deal cycles will stretch.

WHY IT MATTERSRescinding the Old Rule Is Not Deregulation

With the AI Diffusion Rule rescinded, the country tiers and license exceptions disappear with it, replaced by more direct license requirements. Every compliance path under the old rule is void.

Writing the superintelligence concept into the rule text shows the control target has extended from today's model performance to the next generation of capability, leaving wide room for interpretation on scope.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM SHIPPING CHIPS TO SELLING COMPUTE
From shipping chips to selling compute
Export controls used to watch goods crossing borders; now they watch whose hands the compute ends up in, and rental models fall within the scope.
FROM TIERS TO CASE-BY-CASE
From tiers to case-by-case
The AI Diffusion Rule's country tiers are gone, replaced by more direct license requirements, with certainty giving way to discretion.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Compute-Rental Back Door Is Closed

Tokenta Signal's judgment: This rule pushes the control boundary from chip shipments to compute usage, and the back door of renting US compute is now formally shut. The country list is only the first layer; the global data center performance threshold is the real weapon, forcing any hosting provider to run a compliance calculation before taking a Chinese customer. The interim final rule can still be revised after comments, but the direction is set, and the compute blockade will only tighten.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The rule is an interim final rule; details may change after the comment period, and license approvals are case by case. This does not constitute investment advice.
