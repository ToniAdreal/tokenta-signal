Core Scientific's Colocation Revenue Jumps 58% as the Miner Turn Becomes an AI Landlord

Core Scientific reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $232.9 million, ahead of the $196.36 million consensus, with colocation revenue up nearly 60% sequentially to $215.4 million, 485 billing megawatts, and adjusted EBITDA margins expanding to 57%, as the former miner's pivot to AI hosting nears completion.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Core Scientific reported third-quarter 2026 results before the market open on November 5, 2026, with total revenue of $232.9 million, up from $164.2 million in the second quarter and $81.1 million a year earlier, and ahead of the $196.36 million consensus. Colocation revenue, the company's high-density hosting business for AI workloads, reached $215.4 million, up nearly 60% sequentially from $136.7 million and up from $15.0 million a year earlier.

The company was billing for 485 megawatts of capacity as of September 30, 2026, up from 395 megawatts at the end of June, representing over $700 million in average annualized colocation GAAP revenue. Adjusted EBITDA more than tripled sequentially to $133.6 million, with margins expanding to 57% from 25%. Net income of $1.18 billion was driven by a non-cash gain in the fair value of warrants; operating income was $72.4 million, against an operating loss of $78.5 million in the prior quarter.

The pivot away from mining is now nearly complete in revenue terms: digital asset self-mining revenue fell to $12.3 million in the quarter, from $21.5 million in the second quarter and $57.4 million a year earlier. Core Scientific spent $985.1 million in capital expenditures during the quarter building out capacity, and held $1.74 billion in liquidity at quarter end. Chief executive Adam Sullivan said the company is nearing full delivery under its first colocation agreement while construction under its second is underway across five campuses.

WHY IT MATTERSA miner that now rents power to AI

Colocation is now 92% of Core Scientific's revenue, up from 18% a year ago, which makes the quarter a proof point for the whole miner-to-host conversion trade. The 485 billing megawatts and the $700 million-plus annualized run rate give the story a contracted base rather than a spot-market one, and the 57% adjusted EBITDA margin shows the hosting model can be profitable at scale once the megawatts are delivered.

The cost of that conversion is visible in the cash flow: $985 million of capital expenditure in a single quarter, with liquidity down to $1.74 billion from $2.60 billion at the end of June. The company is spending to deliver contracted capacity, and the next test is whether billing megawatts keep climbing without another large liquidity draw.

WHY IT MATTERSThe headline profit is not the operating story

The $1.18 billion net income looks like a blowout until the release's own footnote is read: it is mostly a non-cash warrant revaluation tied to the share price, the same mechanism that produced a $1.16 billion net loss in the second quarter. The operating line is the honest read, and it swung from a $78.5 million loss to a $72.4 million profit as colocation gross profit reached $123.3 million.

That swing is the number to carry forward. If colocation keeps scaling at this rate, the operating profit becomes structural; if delivery stalls, the warrant noise will dominate the headlines again. The 8:30 a.m. Eastern earnings call is the first chance to hear how much contracted capacity remains to be delivered.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe hosting pivot now has a profitable quarter behind it

Tokenta Signal's judgment: colocation at 92% of revenue with a 57% adjusted EBITDA margin turns Core Scientific's conversion story from a plan into a working business. The open question is funding, not demand: $985 million of quarterly capital expenditure against $1.74 billion of liquidity means delivery speed is now a balance-sheet question, and the earnings call should be judged on how much contracted capacity is left to build.
