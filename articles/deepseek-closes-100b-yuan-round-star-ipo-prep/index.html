WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Chinese AI developer DeepSeek has completed a second external funding round of about 100 billion yuan ($15 billion) as it prepares to pursue an initial public offering on Shanghai's STAR Market, Caixin Global reported on November 20, 2026. Business registration changes completed on November 17 show that a partnership representing the round's new investors took a roughly 17.3% stake in DeepSeek.

The Information put the round at about $15.8 billion from nearly 30 investors, valuing DeepSeek at about 615.5 billion yuan (about $89 billion), which it described as China's largest AI venture raise to date. A Tencent unit is the largest single holder at 9.57%, three funds tied to battery maker CATL together hold the same amount, and China Mobile's investment arm and CITIC Securities each hold 7.65%. State-owned investors supplied 27% of the round, up from 2% in the first round.

The first external round raised 50 billion yuan at a post-investment valuation of 350 billion yuan, with registration changes completed in July. Reuters reported on October 22 that the second round had reached about 100 billion yuan, twice its original 50 billion yuan target, with CATL and Tencent among the largest backers. DeepSeek has hired CITIC Securities to prepare the STAR Market listing and aims to start the process before the end of the year. Founder Liang Wenfeng is no longer a direct partner in the investor platform but still owns 99% of the company that manages it, keeping control.

WHY IT MATTERSState-anchored capital, not venture tourists

The cap table is the story. In June, state money was 2% of DeepSeek's first outside round; in this round it is 27%, and the largest holders are Tencent, CATL-linked funds, China Mobile and CITIC Securities, alongside vivo, Midea, Geely, Xiaohongshu, Meituan and NetEase. That is industrial and state capital buying into China's best-known AI developer, not a venture round in the usual sense.

The money also narrows the compute gap with the US labs. The round doubled from its original 50 billion yuan target while it was being raised, and one reason cited in reporting was the rising black-market price of Nvidia Blackwell B300 servers under US export controls. At roughly $15 billion, the raise is about twice the June round and roughly 40% of all AI funding raised by Asian startups in the third quarter.

The valuation tells the same story in miniature. Reuters reported a roughly 500 billion yuan pre-money tag in October; The Information's post-money figure of about 615.5 billion yuan is consistent with that plus the new cash. Against Anthropic's roughly $1.3 trillion market value, DeepSeek is still valued at a fraction, but it now holds the cash to keep building.

WHAT TO WATCHThe prospectus is the next real event

The next concrete marker is a filing. A STAR Market prospectus would give investors their first public look at DeepSeek's revenue and costs, which no reporting so far has disclosed. The company has not announced the financing itself, and the final size, per-investor amounts and terms were still described as unsettled in October.

Watch the listing window too. China's securities regulator used informal window guidance in September to slow humanoid robot listings after Unitree's shares fell 55% from their peak, and officials are questioning revenue tied to local-government-backed data-collection centers. DeepSeek's first-round investors gave up their voting rights and accepted a five-year lock-up, which stops them selling unless the company goes public, so the IPO is not optional for them.

The control question is settled for now. Liang Wenfeng kept control through the managing company, and the structure mirrors the June round. The open question is commercial: whether a company famous for open models can show the revenue to justify an $89 billion tag.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe cap table now matches the national-champion billing

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the size of the round is the least interesting number in it. What changed is who owns DeepSeek now: state money went from 2% of the first round to 27% of this one, and the largest holders are Tencent, CATL-linked funds, China Mobile and CITIC Securities. That is a national champion's cap table, and it means the company's next chapter is written as much by industrial policy as by model quality. The $89 billion post-money tag is still a fraction of Anthropic's market value, but the cash closes the compute gap for now. The real test is the STAR prospectus, which will show revenue and costs for the first time, and whether the listing window stays open after the regulator's warning to humanoid robot makers.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

DeepSeek has not itself announced the financing; the closing is established by business registry filings and reporting from Caixin and The Information. The two reported totals differ slightly: about 100 billion yuan per Caixin, about $15.8 billion per The Information. The valuation figures are pre-money (about 500 billion yuan, reported by Reuters in October) and post-money (about 615.5 billion yuan, reported by The Information). This flash covers the closing of the round, not the October reports, which covered a round still being raised. This does not constitute investment advice.
