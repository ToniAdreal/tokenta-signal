Reuters: OpenAI Staff Warned of Internal Security Lapses for Years; a Quarter of Production Engineers Shifted to Security in August

Reuters reported on November 3, 2026 that OpenAI employees had warned senior leadership and the board about internal security problems before ChatGPT's release, and that, according to five people familiar with the matter, management favored competitive speed. The two named gaps were broad monorepo access and the lack of regular security reviews of research sandboxes. In August 2026, Thibault Sottiaux moved roughly a quarter of production engineers to security infrastructure. OpenAI responded that its most sensitive work sits in separate repositories, the monorepo holds no model weights, regular reviews are now conducted, and — after Reuters sought comment — the company announced it is planning its next internal security sprint.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Reuters reported on November 3, 2026 that OpenAI employees had raised security concerns to senior leadership and the board well before ChatGPT's release in late 2022. According to five people familiar with the matter, some employees later concluded that management prized speed in competing with Google and Anthropic over security investment.

Sources said OpenAI's controls were at one point loose enough that outsiders, including foreign governments, could have gained relatively easy access to the lab's technical information. One specific mechanism was the monorepo: anyone with code access could see the technical details behind many projects, including sensitive projects unrelated to their own work. The monorepo's size and complexity may once have deterred some attackers, but sources said AI agents can now sift through such material quickly.

Another gap was the review of internal tools. According to three people familiar with the matter, OpenAI in the past did not conduct regular security reviews of internal tools such as the sandboxes researchers use to test new models. Employees routinely modified these tools for their work, and the changes did not necessarily undergo security testing, creating a potential route for external attack.

The shift came in August 2026. According to sources, Thibault Sottiaux, the executive responsible for product development, recently took over internal systems security and that month moved roughly a quarter of production engineers to improving security infrastructure. OpenAI responded that after Chief Information Security Officer Dane Stuckey took the role in October 2024, the company built a strong security system and now reviews internal tools regularly.

The immediate backdrop to the report is October's bugging-device incident. The Department of Justice said on October 13 that federal agents arrested Abdulrahman Alfozan, then an OpenAI engineer, on charges of planting a hidden recording device in an executive meeting room. Reuters reported that after it sought comment from OpenAI for this story, the company said in a November 2 blog post that it is planning its next internal security sprint.

WHY IT MATTERSAn old speed-versus-security ledger, reopened on the eve of an IPO

The news value of this report is not any single intrusion but the timeline. The warnings did not surface after the bugging incident; they existed before ChatGPT's release and ran all the way to board level. OpenAI, in other words, faces not a one-off accident but a long-standing organizational trade-off.

That trade-off is especially sensitive today. OpenAI and Anthropic are both preparing large public listings, and how well technical assets are kept confidential feeds directly into the valuation narrative. By placing the speed-versus-security tension in an IPO context, Reuters is making a clear point: investors will now ask whether security spending is a cost line or a precondition of the valuation.

WHY IT MATTERSThe monorepo and the sandbox are the real security boundary

The monorepo issue deserves to be singled out. The old defensive logic was that material was so voluminous and messy that attackers could not work through it. Agents can now read, search and summarize in bulk, so volume itself is no longer a barrier. OpenAI's response draws a line: the most sensitive work sits in separate repositories, and the monorepo contains no model weights. Whether that line holds depends on how the separate repositories' boundaries are drawn and how access is audited, not on how large the monorepo is.

The sandbox issue is parallel. Researchers modifying test tools to move faster is normal lab practice, but changes that skip security testing turn the experimental environment into an entry point. OpenAI says regular reviews are now in place; that claim needs verifiable follow-through — review frequency, coverage and remediation records — rather than a single blog statement.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe signal is an organizational trade-off, not a single intrusion

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the value of this report is that it pulls OpenAI's security problem back from a single incident to the organizational level. The bugging device is only the trigger. The real signals are warnings that existed for years, two named structural gaps in the monorepo and the sandbox, and August's redeployment of a quarter of production engineers. The verification points ahead are equally concrete: whether access controls on the separate repositories genuinely tighten, whether sandbox reviews become institutionalized, and what checkable results the next security sprint produces. Without those, a blog statement counts as a response, not a resolution.
