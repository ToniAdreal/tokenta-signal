New York Opens Registration: Frontier AI Labs Hand Over Their Safety Cards First

New York State opened registration under the RAISE Act on November 10. Large frontier developers with annual revenue above $500 million must register before January 1, 2027, publish a safety framework, and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours; a first violation carries a civil penalty of up to $1 million.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The registration threshold is written precisely. A large frontier developer is one whose annual revenue, including affiliates, exceeds $500 million and that has trained a frontier model with compute above 10^26 operations. The threshold gates on both money and compute, catching only the top labs.

Registration brings continuing duties. Companies must publish a safety framework on their website, report critical safety incidents within 72 hours, submit quarterly catastrophic-risk assessments, and produce a public model transparency report.

Penalties run per violation. A first violation carries up to $1 million; each subsequent violation up to $3 million, and the state attorney general may bring civil action. Registration fees are apportioned by share, so the regulated pay for the regulation themselves.

OFFICE CALLED DIGITThe New Regulator Is Called DIGIT

The enforcing body is a new Office of Digital Innovation, Governance, Integrity and Trust, DIGIT for short, housed inside the New York State Department of Financial Services, with rulemaking power.

DIGIT will publish the registration list but will not disclose each company's regulatory contact. The list itself is a roll call; anyone missing from it is visible at a glance.

NYCAIRR AT BINGHAMTONA Research Center Opened the Same Day

The governor also announced the New York Center for AI Responsibility and Research, based at Binghamton University, bringing together about 80 researchers to independently evaluate advanced AI systems.

The center has $55 million in donations and capital but no legal power to compel companies to accept audits. Its role is verifier, not enforcer.

SIGNED DEC 2025The Law Has a Long Backstory

The RAISE Act, short for the Responsible AI Safety and Education Act, was signed by the governor on December 19, 2025. Amendment S8828, signed March 27, 2026, moved the effective date to January 1, 2027.

November 10 opened the registration window, not the effective date. Fifty-two days separate opening from effectiveness, giving companies time to file.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM VOLUNTARY TO MANDATORY
From posting a safety framework to filing one
Posting a safety framework on a company website used to be voluntary; now registration, publication, regular assessments, and incident reporting form one linked set of statutory duties, and missing any one of them can draw a fine.
FROM SELF-RULE TO STATE LIST
From industry self-regulation to a state roll call
DIGIT will publish the registration list, and enforcement sits with the state attorney general. AI safety governance has a state-level roll call and penalty schedule for the first time.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISSafety Promises Get a Deadline for the First Time

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the RAISE Act's sharp edge is not the fines but the registration list. Safety frameworks used to be public-relations pages labs posted on their own websites voluntarily; now they are statutory duties with 72-hour incident reporting and quarterly assessments. New York has only opened the registration window; the real change arrives on January 1, 2027, when the law takes effect.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

New York has not yet published which companies fall within the registration scope, nor explained how the clock starts for incident reporting. As of November 10, no company had publicly responded and no fines had been issued. OpenAI and Anthropic, named by TechCrunch, are media examples, not an official list. The law takes effect January 1, 2027; until then only the registration duty applies. This does not constitute investment advice.
