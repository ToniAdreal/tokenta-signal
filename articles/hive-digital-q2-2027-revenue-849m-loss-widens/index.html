HIVE Digital Posts $84.9M Q2 Revenue as $84.5M Swedish VAT Provision Drives $78.7M Loss

HIVE Digital Technologies reported fiscal Q2 2027 revenue of $84.9 million, down 2.7 percent year over year, with a net loss of $78.7 million driven by an $84.5 million Swedish VAT provision and impairment charges, while cash rose to $139.0 million after $245 million in exchangeable note offerings to fund its AI and high-performance computing buildout.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

HIVE Digital Technologies reported fiscal second-quarter 2027 revenue of $84.9 million on November 9, 2026, down 2.7 percent from a year earlier, as the company continued to post a net loss. Net loss attributable to common shareholders widened to $78.7 million, or -$0.29 per diluted share. Gross profit rose sharply to $30.2 million as cost of sales fell to $54.7 million, but operating profit remained deeply negative at -$43.1 million.

The loss was driven by items outside the operating run rate. A Swedish VAT provision of $84.5 million, together with impairment charges, weighed on the bottom line, while growth in high-performance computing partly offset lower mining revenue. Cash and equivalents climbed to $139.0 million after $245 million in exchangeable note offerings, funding an accelerating AI and high-performance computing buildout. Operating cash flow came in at $8.1 million, capital spending totaled $85.3 million, and total liabilities increased to $493.2 million.

Shares closed at $1.81, up 1.7 percent, before the results crossed after the market close. Management hosts its earnings conference call and webcast on Tuesday, November 10, at 8:00 AM EST, where the HPC pipeline and the path back to profitability are expected to take center stage.

WHY IT MATTERSThe loss is a provision story, not an operating collapse

Strip out the Swedish VAT provision and the impairment charges and the quarter reads differently. Gross profit rose sharply, cost of sales fell to $54.7 million, and the company generated $8.1 million of operating cash flow. The $78.7 million net loss is real money on the books, but it is dominated by regulatory and non-cash items rather than a collapse in the underlying mining or computing operations.

The watchpoint is whether the VAT dispute stays contained. An $84.5 million provision against a company with a market value near $500 million is material, and investors will want to hear on the November 10 call how management sizes the remaining exposure and whether further provisions are likely. Until that is answered, the provision, not the revenue line, is the number that sets the tone.

WHY IT MATTERSHPC is now the funding story

HIVE raised $245 million through exchangeable notes and ended the quarter with $139.0 million in cash, while capital spending of $85.3 million shows the buildout is already consuming real money. The company describes itself as a builder of renewable-powered data centers and sovereign AI cloud infrastructure, and the quarter's narrative is that high-performance computing growth is offsetting a softer mining business.

The question for the call is conversion: how much of that spending is already under contract, and how fast HPC revenue scales against the mining decline. If contracted HPC revenue ramps on schedule, the balance sheet gives HIVE room to keep building. If it lags, the company is funding a pivot from a loss-making quarter with debt, and the $493.2 million in total liabilities will draw closer scrutiny.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe quarter's real signal is the balance sheet, not the revenue line

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Revenue down 2.7 percent is a footnote; the story is a company funding a pivot with debt while a regulatory provision eats the quarter. The gross-profit improvement and $8.1 million of operating cash flow say the underlying operations are not collapsing, and $139.0 million in cash after a $245 million raise buys time to build the HPC business. But an $84.5 million VAT provision is large relative to a roughly $500 million market value, and the November 10 call has to answer two questions: how much more VAT exposure remains, and how much of the $85.3 million in quarterly capital spending is already contracted HPC revenue. Until both are answered, HIVE is a balance-sheet story with a mining business attached, not an earnings story.
