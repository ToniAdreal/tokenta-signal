Applied Materials Posts Record $10.41 Billion Quarter, Guides Higher

Applied Materials reported record fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 revenue of $10.41 billion, up 53% year over year, with record non-GAAP earnings per share of $4.16, up 92%, beating Wall Street expectations. Full-year revenue reached a record $34.45 billion. Applied guided first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue to $11.1 billion and non-GAAP earnings per share to $4.35, both above consensus. Shares were roughly flat immediately after the results.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Applied Materials reported fourth-quarter and fiscal year 2026 results on November 12, 2026, for the quarter ended October 25, 2026. Quarterly revenue reached a record $10.41 billion, up 53% year over year, beating the roughly $10.30 billion Wall Street consensus. GAAP earnings per share were $3.40, up 43%, and record non-GAAP earnings per share were $4.16, up 92% and about 2.6% above consensus. GAAP gross margin was 50.4% and non-GAAP gross margin was 50.6%, up 250 basis points year over year.

For the full fiscal year, Applied generated record revenue of $34.45 billion, up 21%, with record GAAP earnings per share of $12.62, up 46%, and record non-GAAP earnings per share of $12.89, up 37%. The company generated record quarterly operating cash flow of $3.30 billion and record quarterly free cash flow of $2.6 billion, and returned $2.42 billion to shareholders through $2.0 billion in share repurchases, its largest quarterly buyback in more than eight years, and $421 million in dividends.

Applied guided first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue to $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and non-GAAP earnings per share to $4.35, plus or minus $0.20, above the roughly $10.87 billion revenue consensus. President and chief executive Gary Dickerson said AI continues to fuel strong incremental demand for semiconductors and that Applied is on track to grow its Semiconductor Systems business more than 40% in calendar 2026.

THE QUARTERSemiconductor Systems carried the quarter

Semiconductor Systems revenue was $8.0 billion, with foundry and logic at 58% of the segment, DRAM at 33% and flash memory at 9%. The segment's non-GAAP operating margin reached 39.0%. Applied Global Services contributed $1.9 billion in revenue. Record operating income of $3.66 billion on a GAAP basis, or 35.1% of revenue, shows the operating leverage in a quarter where revenue grew 53%.

The demand backdrop is the AI build-out: chipmakers are buying more equipment for advanced logic, memory and packaging, and Applied's order book is the upstream read on that spending. The quarter's 53% revenue growth against a $6.8 billion year-ago quarter is the largest year-over-year jump in the company's recent history.

THE SIGNALThe EPIC Center and the 2027 outlook

Applied unveiled the EPIC Center in Silicon Valley, described as the world's largest facility dedicated to collaborative semiconductor process technology and manufacturing equipment research and development, and added four new research and development partnerships, bringing total announced EPIC engagements to 15. Intel joins as a founding partner, alongside KIOXIA, BE Semiconductor Industries and the UCLA Samueli School of Engineering.

The company also entered an accelerated share purchase agreement to raise its investment in Besi to about 20% of outstanding shares, and introduced the VeritySEM 20 metrology system for high-NA EUV patterning control. The first-quarter guide implies another record quarter, with revenue up about 58% year over year at the midpoint.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISChip equipment is the cleanest AI demand signal in the print

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Applied's quarter is the upstream confirmation that AI chip demand is still accelerating, with revenue up 53% and the next quarter guided higher still. The stock's muted reaction is the tell: shares had already fallen about 35% in a month on fears of a data-center slowdown, and this print answers that fear with orders, not promises. The number to watch next is the China share of revenue and the fiscal 2027 framework from the earnings call, which will show whether the equipment cycle has further to run.
