Cognition Launches Devin Bots, Persistent Agents Start Managing Engineering Teams

The contest among coding agents shifts from single-task speed to who can carry a whole goal for the user.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
  • 01THE HANDOFF — A user hands a Bot an ongoing goal, and the tickets get split out by the Bot itself.

  • 03THE CHECK — Results pass automated tests and code review first, and only then merge in.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

A Bot takes on a goal, not a ticket. A user hands it an ongoing responsibility, such as keeping Android and iOS in sync. It breaks the work down itself, assigns it to multiple parallel Devin sessions, checks the results, and decides the next step. Context across sessions stays with the Bot; only decisions that need a human go into the Needs You inbox.

Cognition demonstrated it with its own Android app. On September 28, an engineer created an Android Bot and put it in charge of the app end to end. 44 days later the app was done. Along the way the Bot merged 1,134 PRs, wrote 344,651 lines of Kotlin code, and closed 1,217 tickets. The engineer sent 183 messages in total, and went 22 days without sending one.

The customer-side numbers come from the vendors' own accounts too. Bilt's VP of Engineering said its Bot merged about 1,600 PRs in a week. A one-person game studio runs about 20 Bots across development, releases and testing, and its weekly PR volume rose from about 50 to 1,900. Cognition itself merges more than 2,000 PRs a week.

WHY IT MATTERSCoordination Is the Real Bottleneck

Writing code is already fast enough. The time goes to spotting problems, choosing approaches, checking results and deciding what comes next, work that used to sit entirely on engineers. That is the layer the Bot takes over.

Once coordination can be handed off, the number of goals one engineer can track at once changes. The shape of the org changes with it, and management span is no longer set by headcount.

WHAT TO WATCHPR Counts Say Nothing About Quality

Every striking number comes from Cognition and its customers, with no independent verification. Merged PRs measure output; they say nothing about code quality or whether the changes were worth making.

Review load is the other side. The faster a Bot produces, the more a human has to review, and review may become the new bottleneck. Teams adopting early should work out that cost first.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM TASKS TO OUTCOMES
From Taking Tasks to Owning Goals
The unit of assignment changes from a ticket to an ongoing responsibility. The agent no longer waits for instructions; it finds the next step itself.
FROM CODER TO MANAGER
From Writing Code to Managing a Team
The Bot sits more like a front-line manager, splitting work, assigning it and checking it. It does not write every line for the human; it watches the whole line for them.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISFrom Single-Task Speed to Owning the Whole Goal

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the contest among coding agents shifts from single-task speed to who can carry a whole goal for the user. The Bot takes over coordination; the Devin sessions underneath still write the code. Whoever steadies that layer first wins the daily entry point into engineering teams.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

All key figures come from Cognition's official blog and customer accounts, without independent verification. Pricing and plans have not been published, and the company has not said who beyond existing Devin customers can use Bots, or at what limits. PR counts and lines of code measure output, not quality. This does not constitute investment advice.
