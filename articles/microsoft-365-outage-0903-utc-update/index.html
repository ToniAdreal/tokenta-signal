WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Microsoft's global Microsoft 365 outage passed 16 hours and 46 minutes with still no fix declared, and its newest update tempered the confidence of the one before it. At 09:03 UTC on November 13, 2026, Microsoft posted a fresh service-health update for incidents EX1550543 and MO1550821: recovery efforts are ongoing across impacted infrastructure, with early signs of stabilization, but service capacity has not yet returned to normal levels and health remains variable as traffic is carefully reintroduced. Engineering teams are cycling affected infrastructure through traffic drain, restart, remediation, and staged return-to-service while monitoring closely, and Microsoft is evaluating additional mitigation options to improve recovery speed and consistency. As part of the effort, Microsoft is extending the email timeout window to 48 hours to help protect delayed messages while service recovery continues. It said it is not aware of any malicious activity causing the incident, and that once mitigated it will publish a full post-incident report with the root cause and defense-in-depth actions. The next update is due by 10:00 UTC.

The wording is a step back from the 08:09 UTC update, which said Microsoft had confirmed stability across the portions of infrastructure to which it had applied the upstream authentication and routing configuration component patch, and was expanding the patch systematically to regions currently within or entering peak business hours. Confirmed stability has become early signs of stabilization, with capacity explicitly not yet normal and health variable as traffic returns. The incident opened at 16:17 UTC on November 12, when Exchange Online users lost mailbox access, and widened at 18:26 UTC into a Microsoft 365 advisory covering Outlook, Teams, SharePoint Online, Copilot, Defender, Purview, Planner, To Do, Intune and Power BI. Microsoft has twice reported servers returning to service and then becoming unresponsive again once they started taking traffic, at 00:00 UTC and again at 04:22 UTC. No root cause has been published, no region has been reported recovered, and no time for full recovery has been given.

The 09:03 UTC update matters because it is the first update to name a concrete protection for users' mail rather than only a repair for Microsoft's servers: the 48-hour email timeout extension is aimed at preserving delayed messages while recovery drags on, which is an admission that delayed mail remains at risk. The public status page, read in the live browser at 09:32-09:34 UTC, now shows the same update text, so users checking Microsoft's own public page are finally seeing the newest news, though the page carries no separate per-incident entries and timestamps it 9:14:20 AM UTC. The outage also continues to reach beyond Microsoft's own assistant: Reuters reported it hit the connectors OpenAI and Anthropic use to reach Microsoft 365.

WHY IT MATTERSFrom confirmed stability to early signs, the repair verdict softens

Every update since 04:22 UTC has carried the same shape: a mechanism named, a mitigation described, and no sustained recovery to point at. The 09:03 UTC update breaks the pattern in the other direction from its predecessor: at 06:57 UTC the grade was marginal success for patches applied after reboots; at 07:29 UTC it was positive results for an authentication and routing patch; at 08:09 UTC it was confirmed stability across the patched portions; forty minutes later the grade is early signs of stabilization, with capacity explicitly not yet normal and health variable as traffic is carefully reintroduced. The change is in language and in plan, not in declared recovery, and it lands more than sixteen hours after the first mailbox failures.

For the AI stack the exposure is unchanged and still open. Copilot Chat may not load or answer and may not reach email, calendar or files, and the OpenAI and Anthropic Microsoft 365 connectors remain affected, Reuters reported. Companies that layered AI assistants on top of Microsoft 365 are still waiting on the same mailbox layer as everyone else. A patch confirmed stable on part of the infrastructure is not the same as a restored service, and the next update will show whether the staged return-to-service holds under Friday morning traffic or repeats the pattern of the two recoveries that froze again at 00:00 and 04:22 UTC.

WHAT TO WATCHThe 10:00 UTC update, then the post-incident report

The next fact is the 10:00 UTC update: whether the traffic drain, restart, remediation and staged return-to-service cycle produces sustained recovery in any region, or whether the update repeats the same watching brief with a new synonym for stabilization. Watch whether Microsoft names the traffic influx as a cause or a symptom, and whether it gives a time for full recovery.

The post-incident report will carry the lasting answers: why servers failed their health checks, why restored servers froze again under traffic, what the network configuration change under review actually changed, and what the traffic influx was. Until it lands, treat every cause as unconfirmed. The earlier Teams incident TM1550488 was resolved the same day, with a preliminary post-incident report promised within two business days.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISSixteen hours in, confirmed stability softens to early signs, and Microsoft moves to protect delayed mail

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Sixteen hours and forty-six minutes in, Microsoft has walked its own verdict on its repairs back from confirmed stability to early signs of stabilization, with capacity explicitly not yet normal and health variable as traffic is carefully reintroduced. That is a candid downgrade, and it fits the pattern of this outage: two earlier partial recoveries froze again under traffic. The new concrete step is the 48-hour email timeout extension, aimed at preserving delayed messages while recovery drags on, which is an admission that delayed mail remains at risk. What is still missing is everything that would end the incident: a root cause, a recovered region, and a time for full restoration. Until the 10:00 UTC update shows sustained recovery under Friday morning traffic, treat the outage as ongoing.

The facts in this update rest on the University of Pennsylvania's mirror of Microsoft's Service Health admin-console feed, crawled at 09:32 UTC on November 13, 2026, which carries the newest update for MO1550821 at 09:03:36 UTC and for EX1550543 at 08:06:25 UTC, and on a live browser read of Microsoft's public status page at 09:32-09:34 UTC returning the identical current-status text. The public page shows one combined Microsoft 365 incident without separate per-ID entries, and its feed timestamps the update at 9:14:20 AM UTC, which is the public feed's publication time for the same 09:03 UTC admin-feed update. The incident IDs, start times, and the next-update time of 10:00 UTC come from the admin feed. Downdetector counts are user-submitted reports, not a count of affected accounts. The root cause is unconfirmed: Microsoft has described failing health checks, an overwhelmed secure-connection process, a network configuration change under review, a surge in outbound call volume from its routing services, machines stuck in a bad state, and a traffic influx it is still working to explain, and has said there is no indication of malicious activity.
