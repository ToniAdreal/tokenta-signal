$100 Million to Buy a Factory Run by Robots

Foundry Robotics announced an oversubscribed $100 million Series A: it builds and operates AI-robot autonomous assembly factories, delivering defense and commercial hardware as manufacturing-as-a-service, with the Emeryville plant reaching 130,000 square feet.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 28, Foundry Robotics announced an oversubscribed $100 million Series A led by Marlinspike Partners, with participation from Khosla Ventures, Red Glass Ventures and others. The company builds and operates autonomous factories: AI-driven robotic cells perform complex assembly, which is then sold to customers as manufacturing-as-a-service.

The key to the model is not selling robots but selling capacity: customers do not need to build their own production lines. Foundry owns the plant, deploys robotic cells and the FoundryOS system, and goes from design to production in weeks, with no new line investment or expensive changeover costs.

The company also announced a 20-fold expansion of its manufacturing footprint: its Emeryville, California plant reaches 130,000 square feet, serving defense and commercial customers, with products spanning drones, autonomous-driving subassemblies, wire harnesses, underwater vehicles, satellites and robot compute boxes, among other high-value complex electromechanical products.

THE FACTORY AS A SERVICEFactory as a Service: Production Lines Turn From Assets Into Subscriptions

The traditional barrier in manufacturing is the production line: building one takes months, and switching products means investing again. Foundry turns the line itself into a service: customers buy finished hardware and delivery timelines, not equipment.

That matters most for defense and robotics customers: orders come in small batches, models change quickly, and building their own lines does not pay. The reprogrammability of robotic cells lets the same factory assemble drones today and satellite components tomorrow.

THE TEAMThe Founding Team: Experience at Both Ends, Robotics and Mass Production

Founder Adarsh Kulkarni was employee No. 4 at Ghost Robotics and later Scale AI's first head of robotics; co-founder Emmanuel Lamacchia ran the Model Y program at Tesla, with annual output of roughly 1.5 million vehicles.

The team composition is itself the product narrative: people who understand robots run the cells, people who understand mass production run the factory. Factories punish inexperienced decisions; one wrong line layout can waste months.

THE RESHORING BETA New Player in the US Manufacturing Reshoring Story

Foundry's raise comes at the height of the US manufacturing reshoring narrative: defense orders, robotics capacity and supply-chain security have simultaneously become themes for policy and capital. The size of the $100 million Series A shows capital is willing to bet early on the "autonomous factory" category.

The real test is delivery: whether a 130,000-square-foot plant can ship steadily on a weekly cadence, and whether robotic cells can hold yield and traceability on real orders.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISFor the First Time, the Factory Is Sold as a Service, Not an Asset

Robotic cells can be reprogrammed, so for the first time a production line can switch by order. Tokenta Signal's judgment is unchanged: the autonomous factory race will be decided by yield and delivery cadence on real orders, not by the size of the raise.
