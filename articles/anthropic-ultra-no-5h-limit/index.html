The US$200 Ultra Finally Stops Watching the Five-Hour Clock

On October 30, 2026, Anthropic renamed Max 5x and Max 20x to Max and Ultra, with prices unchanged. Verified Ultra accounts lose the rolling five-hour limit, while the weekly cap remains 10 times Pro.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

On October 30, 2026, Anthropic renamed its two top subscription tiers. Max 5x became Max, at US$100 a month. Max 20x became Ultra, at US$200 a month. Prices did not move, and existing users were renamed automatically with nothing to do.

The real change is inside Ultra. Verified Ultra accounts no longer have a rolling five-hour usage limit, rolled out first to most existing users. New accounts keep a temporary limit, lifted automatically once verification passes; Anthropic says this is an anti-fraud and anti-resale measure.

The weekly cap did not budge. Ultra's weekly usage is still 10 times Pro's, and Max is still 5 times on both the weekly and five-hour measures. Sessions are no longer cut by the five-hour window, but the week's total quota still stops work when it runs out.

THE RENAME IS NOT THE STORYThe Rename Is Minor; Verification Is the Gate

Both tier names got shorter, with prices mapping one to one. Max is US$100 a month, Ultra US$200 a month. The change subscribers feel most is a single one: long Ultra sessions no longer hit a wall.

Anthropic has turned verification into the new threshold. Verified accounts are unlocked first; unverified ones wait, with verification based on factors including account and payment history. Users can check whether the current-session bar has disappeared in settings to tell whether they have been verified.

LONG SESSIONS ARE THE PRODUCTLong Sessions Are Worth Money in Coding and Research

The five-hour window hurt long tasks most. A coding or research job spanning several hours would be interrupted when the window reset, forcing context and rhythm to be rebuilt. With the window gone, what Ultra sells is no longer more tokens, but a workflow that does not break.

The total quota, however, is unchanged, and an unbroken workflow is not the same as unlimited work. What users actually buy is the freedom to spend a week's quota at their own pace, not a bigger quota.

VERIFICATION AS ANTI-RESALEAnti-Resale Logic Is Becoming Standard for Subscriptions

Anthropic has not spelled out the verification criteria, saying only that they relate to fraud prevention. Market interpretation is consistent: the temporary limits target resale and account sharing. Unlocking long-standing users first, then verifying new accounts step by step, is a standard risk-control approach.

This will become more common. Model vendors no longer tier only by price; they tier by identity verification too. Paying US$200 is just the entry ticket; the full entitlement arrives only after verification.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Next Gate for Subscriptions Is Verification, Not Price

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the way vendors tier is changing. Prices are still US$100 and US$200, but the full entitlement now unlocks through verification. Identity has become the new tier.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The boundaries of this report: the weekly usage cap has not changed, and Ultra is not unlimited. New accounts keep a temporary five-hour limit until verification is complete, and Anthropic has not disclosed how long verification takes. This does not constitute investment advice.
