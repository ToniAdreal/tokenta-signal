PDD Revenue Stalls as Temu Fees Fall 12% in First Full EU-Duty Quarter

PDD Holdings reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of RMB108,341 million on November 19, 2026, essentially flat year over year and about RMB7.7 billion below the LSEG analyst average of RMB115.97 billion, a fifth consecutive quarterly revenue miss. Transaction services revenue fell 12% to RMB48,522 million in the first full quarter under the EU duty on low-value parcels, while online marketing services revenue rose 12% to RMB59,819 million. Operating profit rose 25% to RMB31,164 million on a 17% cut in sales and marketing spending; non-GAAP diluted earnings per ADS of US$3.14 beat the $2.77 consensus. Shares rose about 9% in pre-market trading.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

PDD Holdings reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of RMB108,341 million on November 19, 2026, essentially flat against RMB108,277 million a year earlier and about RMB7.7 billion below the LSEG analyst average of RMB115.97 billion cited by Reuters. It is the fifth consecutive quarterly revenue miss, extending a run that already stretched through the June quarter. The release states revenue of US$16.2 billion at the quarter-end exchange rate.

The split inside the flat top line is the story. Revenue from online marketing services and others rose 12% year over year to RMB59,819 million, while revenue from transaction services fell 12% to RMB48,522 million. The September quarter is the first full quarter under the European Union's fixed duty of about 3 euros per item category on parcels under 150 euros, in force since July 2026, and management had warned on the second-quarter call that it would have a considerable near-term negative impact on affected cross-border orders. PDD does not break out Temu separately; the transaction-services line is where the duty lands.

The profit line was rescued by the cost line. Total operating expenses fell 12% year over year to RMB31,934 million, driven by sales and marketing expenses down 17% to RMB25,296 million, while research and development spending rose 17% to RMB5,059 million. Operating profit rose 25% to RMB31,164 million, and non-GAAP operating profit rose 21% to RMB32,643 million. Net income attributable to ordinary shareholders was RMB29,541 million, up less than 1% year over year, and non-GAAP net income was RMB31,020 million, down 1%. Non-GAAP diluted earnings per ADS were RMB21.04, or US$3.14, above the $2.77 consensus.

The market's first reaction rewarded the cost discipline rather than punishing the revenue miss: PDD's US-listed shares traded about 9.12% higher in pre-market trading, at $84.51 against a $77.45 prior close, according to Finnhub. Net cash generated from operating activities was RMB43,019 million, down from RMB45,660 million a year earlier, and cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments stood at RMB455,410 million at September 30, 2026. The earnings call is scheduled for 12:30 UTC today, and it is the call, not the release, that has to answer the two open questions: whether the transaction-services decline is a one-quarter duty shock or a durable Temu reset, and how the RMB100 billion three-year first-party brand program is tracking.

WHY IT MATTERSA flat top line with a rising profit line

Revenue growth has now stalled outright: RMB108,341 million against RMB108,277 million a year earlier is a rounding error, not growth, and it is the fifth straight quarter in which PDD has come in below the analyst average. The mix explains why. Domestic advertising is still growing, up 12%, but the fees on completed sales, the line that carries Temu's cross-border volume, fell 12% in the first full quarter under the EU duty. That is the first clean read on what the end of duty-free low-value parcels costs the model.

The profit resilience is real but narrow. Operating profit up 25% on flat revenue is a cost story: sales and marketing spending cut by RMB5 billion year over year, with research and development the only expense line still growing. Non-GAAP net income actually fell 1%, and nine-month net income is down 7% year over year, so the quarter's earnings quality rests on spending discipline, not on the business getting bigger. The pre-market rally says investors, for now, accept that trade.

WHAT TO WATCHThe call, the duty, and the merchant bill

Watch the 12:30 UTC call for the Temu read-through. Management's language on cross-border order volumes, European traffic, and whether the duty hit is stabilizing will decide whether the transaction-services decline is a one-quarter adjustment or the start of a lower-growth regime for the international business. Any update on the RMB100 billion three-year first-party brand program, which has not yet disclosed revenue or loss figures, is the second tell on where the next growth is supposed to come from.

Watch the spending balance. Sales and marketing fell 17% while research and development rose 17%, which is the shape of a company choosing engineering over promotion. If the advertising line keeps growing at 12% while promotion spending stays down, the model holds; if the marketing cut starts to cost domestic share against Alibaba and JD.com, both of which reported the same morning, the trade reverses. Watch, too, whether the cash pile of RMB455.4 billion starts to move toward buybacks or stays parked.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA cost-cut beat, not a growth story

Tokenta Signal's judgment: the honest read on this quarter is that PDD's revenue engine has stalled and its cost discipline is doing the work instead. Flat revenue against a RMB115.97 billion estimate, a fifth straight miss, and a 12% fall in transaction services in the first full EU-duty quarter say the Temu model is absorbing a real regulatory cost. The 25% rise in operating profit is genuine, a RMB5 billion cut in sales and marketing spending bought it, but non-GAAP net income fell 1% and nine-month net income is down 7%, so this is not an earnings acceleration. The pre-market rally is a vote for the cost line, not the top line. Watch the 12:30 UTC call for whether the duty hit is stabilizing or still deepening.
