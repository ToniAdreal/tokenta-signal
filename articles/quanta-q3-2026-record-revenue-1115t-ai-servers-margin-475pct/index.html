Quanta Posts Record Revenue of NT$1.12 Trillion as AI Servers Take Over 80% of Sales

Quanta Computer reported record third-quarter revenue of NT$1.115545 trillion, up 125.2% year on year, with gross margin slipping to 4.75% from 6.85% a year earlier as AI servers took over the mix. Operating profit rose 75.3% to NT$32.204 billion and net profit climbed 73% to NT$28.422 billion, with EPS of NT$7.08. Servers made up more than 80% of revenue, AI servers 75% to 80% of server sales, and management guided servers to 85% to 90% of revenue in 2027.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Quanta Computer reported the biggest quarter in its history. Third-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue was NT$1.115545 trillion, roughly $34.1 billion, up 125.2% year on year and 7.6% from the second quarter. Revenue for the first nine months reached NT$2.961358 trillion, approximately $90.2 billion, up 99.4% from the same stretch a year earlier. Both the quarterly and nine-month figures set new company records.

The profit percentage kept sliding even as the absolute numbers rose. Gross margin landed at 4.75% in the third quarter, down from 5.02% in the second quarter and 6.85% in the year-ago period. Quanta pointed to three pressures: material costs for notebooks rose, notebook shipments fell, and the product mix tilted further toward pricier AI server racks. Operating profit still rose 75.3% year on year to NT$32.204 billion, net profit attributable to the parent company climbed 73% to NT$28.422 billion, and earnings per share came in at NT$7.08 for the quarter. Across the first nine months, earnings per share reached NT$20.03, another company record.

At an investor conference on November 11, 2026, management said servers made up more than 80% of third-quarter revenue, and AI servers accounted for 75% to 80% of that server revenue. Quanta forecasts that servers will account for 85% to 90% of total revenue next year, with AI servers expected to represent 80% to 85% of server revenue, and expects operations to keep accelerating in the fourth quarter, driven by AI servers. The company is also shifting 2027 capital spending toward power, the new battleground for AI server makers. Executives framed the strategy around absolute profit growth rather than percentage margins.

WHY IT MATTERSRecord revenue with a thinner slice is the AI-server trade

Quanta's quarter is the clearest read yet on how much AI infrastructure is actually being built. A contract manufacturer does not more than double revenue in a year unless customers are placing very large orders, and with servers above 80% of sales, Quanta's numbers are effectively a proxy for hyperscaler AI server demand. The 73% net profit growth shows the model works in absolute terms: earning 4.75% on a much larger base makes more money than earning 6.85% on a smaller one.

The economics are thin by design. Accelerators and other high-value parts carry most of a rack's price, so revenue swings with platform pricing while margins stay in single digits. From the third quarter of 2026 some high-value components on GPU and ASIC programs are moving to consignment, which lowers reported revenue but can lift percentage margins. The margin percentage, in other words, is becoming a less reliable guide to the health of the business than the absolute profit line.

WHAT TO WATCHThe watchpoints are margin stabilization and concentration

The first question is whether gross margin stabilizes or keeps edging down as AI servers take a bigger share toward 85% to 90% of revenue in 2027. Notebook material costs are the other pressure: if they ease, the legacy business stops dragging the blended margin; if they keep rising, the squeeze continues even as AI volumes grow.

The second question is concentration. With servers already above 80% of revenue and guided higher, Quanta's fortunes are increasingly tied to a single theme and a handful of cloud buyers, with smooth platform transitions such as Vera Rubin as a dependency. The fourth-quarter acceleration management expects is the near-term test; the 2027 mix guide is the longer one.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISQuanta's record quarter is a direct read on how much AI infrastructure is being built

Tokenta Signal's judgment: Quanta more than doubling revenue in a year is the strongest demand signal in the AI supply chain this quarter, and the 73% net profit growth shows the thin-margin model still pays in absolute terms. But the margin slide from 6.85% to 4.75% is the price of the mix shift: the value in each rack belongs to the accelerator, and Quanta keeps a small percentage of a much larger ticket. With servers heading toward 85% to 90% of revenue in 2027, the company is effectively a pure AI-server play now. The number to watch is whether the margin stabilizes as consignment programs begin, or keeps edging down as AI takes a bigger share.
