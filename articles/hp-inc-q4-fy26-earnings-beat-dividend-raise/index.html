HP Beats on Earnings and Raises Its Dividend as PC Revenue Grows 14%

HP Inc. reported fiscal 2026 fourth-quarter revenue of $16.0 billion on November 24, 2026, up 9.3% year over year and above the $15.4 billion consensus, with non-GAAP earnings per share of $0.83 beating the $0.75 estimate. Full-year revenue reached $60.5 billion, up 9.5%, with non-GAAP earnings per share of $3.33. HP raised its quarterly dividend to $0.31 per share. Shares were little changed after the report, with investors weighing the beat against weaker margins and a 45.9% drop in quarterly free cash flow.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

HP Inc. reported fiscal 2026 fourth-quarter revenue of $16.0 billion on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, up 9.3% from a year earlier and above the $15.4 billion analysts expected, the company announced. Non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $0.83, down 10.8% from a year earlier, beat the $0.75 consensus by $0.08. GAAP diluted earnings per share were $0.84, flat year over year.

For the full fiscal year, HP posted revenue of $60.5 billion, up 9.5%, with GAAP diluted earnings per share of $2.62 and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share of $3.33, up 6.7% and above the outlook HP had set at the start of the year. Both earnings figures include a $0.26 benefit from tariff refunds. HP also raised its quarterly dividend to $0.31 per share from $0.30.

Personal Systems revenue grew 14% in the quarter to $11.8 billion, led by commercial demand and record revenue in advanced compute solutions, while Printing revenue fell 1% to $4.3 billion. Fourth-quarter free cash flow dropped 45.9% to $0.8 billion as operating cash flow fell 42.6%. HP shares were little changed after the report, Barron's reported, with investors weighing a solid beat against a weak PC market.

WHY IT MATTERSA beat that the market shrugged at.

HP beat on both the top and bottom lines, raised its dividend, and closed the year above its own earnings outlook. In most quarters that would be a clean win. The flat share reaction says the market is looking past the beat to the structure underneath: non-GAAP operating margin fell to 6.2% from 8.0% a year earlier, and fourth-quarter free cash flow nearly halved.

The contrast with Dell's record print the same evening is the story of the hardware market right now. AI infrastructure is booming; the traditional PC business is growing on commercial demand but with thinner margins and weaker cash conversion. HP's quarter is solid. It is also, for the moment, not the part of the market investors are paying up for.

THE CAVEATTariff refunds flattered the year.

HP's full-year earnings include a $0.26 per-share benefit from tariff refunds, and $0.15 of it landed in the fourth quarter. Strip that out and the non-GAAP fourth-quarter figure would have been closer to $0.68, below the year-ago $0.93 by a wider margin. The refunds are real cash, but they are one-off, and the underlying margin trend is down.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISA solid beat in a market that is looking elsewhere.

HP did what a mature hardware company is supposed to do: it beat expectations, grew the top line, and raised the dividend. The market's flat reaction is not a verdict on the quarter but on the segment. The money and the multiples are in AI infrastructure this season, and HP's PC-led growth, with margins compressing and cash flow down, does not command them. The quarter is fine. The story it belongs to is not the one investors are paying for.
