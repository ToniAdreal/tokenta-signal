Novanta Beats on Revenue and Adjusted Profit as Bookings Jump 29%

Novanta reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $305.0 million, up 23.1 percent year over year and ahead of the $301.0 million consensus, with adjusted earnings per share of $1.05 against $0.97 expected, as customer bookings rose 29 percent and backlog reached $543 million. GAAP profit fell on acquisition-related items in the quarter it closed the largest deal in its history, while the company guided fourth-quarter revenue of $306 million to $312 million and 2027 revenue growth above 15 percent.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Novanta reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $305.0 million on November 9, 2026, up 23.1 percent from $247.8 million a year earlier, beating the $301.0 million consensus. Organic revenue grew 9.9 percent, while acquisitions added 13.3 percent. Adjusted diluted earnings per share rose 20.7 percent to $1.05, against $0.87 a year earlier and ahead of the $0.97 consensus, and adjusted EBITDA climbed 38.3 percent to $80.3 million, a 26 percent margin, up 290 basis points year over year.

The headline beat came with a split underneath it. GAAP net income fell to $2.0 million from $10.7 million a year earlier, and GAAP diluted earnings per share dropped to $0.05 from $0.30, even as the adjusted measures rose sharply, with acquisition-related items including purchase price allocation in the quarter the company closed the largest acquisition in its history, Riverpoint Medical. Operating cash flow told the healthier story: $39.3 million in the quarter against $8.5 million a year earlier, and $155.9 million year to date against $55.3 million.

The order book gives the quarter its forward weight. Customer bookings rose 29 percent, expanding the backlog to $543 million, and chief executive Matthijs Glastra said Riverpoint Medical is performing in line with expectations. For the fourth quarter, Novanta guided revenue of $306 million to $312 million, adjusted EBITDA of $76 million to $79 million, and adjusted diluted earnings per share of $0.91 to $0.96. For the full year, it expects revenue of $1,135 million to $1,140 million, and looking ahead to 2027, reported revenue growth of more than 15 percent. Shares rose 8.0 percent to $145.98 in Monday's regular session, ahead of the release after the close.

WHY IT MATTERSA beat built on bookings, not just accounting

The number that carries this quarter is not revenue, it is the backlog. Bookings up 29 percent and a $543 million order book mean the 23 percent revenue jump is backed by demand still arriving, not by a one-off shipment. Novanta sells photonics, precision motion control, and medical imaging components into equipment that other manufacturers build, so its bookings are an early read on capital spending in medical and advanced industrial markets.

The GAAP versus adjusted split is the part to read honestly. Adjusted EBITDA up 38 percent while GAAP profit fell says the underlying business is expanding and the drag sits in acquisition accounting, not operations. The test comes over the next two quarters: whether Riverpoint Medical converts its pipeline into the margin the adjusted figures imply, and whether the backlog converts into the fourth-quarter revenue Novanta just guided.

WHY IT MATTERSThe 2027 guide is the real announcement

Guiding more than 15 percent reported revenue growth for 2027, on top of roughly 16 percent in 2026, is a bolder statement than the quarter itself. It says management believes the demand behind the backlog is durable across both of its segments, Automation Enabling Technologies at $145 million and Medical Solutions at $160 million, both up double digits in the quarter.

The watchpoint is concentration in the cycle. Both segments grew together on the same capital-spending wave, and the fourth-quarter adjusted EPS guide of $0.91 to $0.96 implies flat to 5 percent growth, a sharp step down from the 21 percent just printed. If customers slow equipment orders, the backlog cushions the turn, but the growth rate would compress fast. For now, the company's own visibility, a record backlog and a 2027 guide above 15 percent, argues the cycle still has room to run.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISNovanta's beat is real, and the backlog, not the quarter, is the story

Tokenta Signal's judgment: The headline, revenue up 23 percent and adjusted earnings ahead of consensus, is a clean beat, but the number that carries the story is the backlog at $543 million after bookings rose 29 percent. A record quarter can be a spike; an order book expanding that fast, paired with a 2027 growth guide above 15 percent, is a trend. The honest caveat sits in the GAAP line: net income fell to $2.0 million while adjusted profit surged, and the gap is acquisition accounting from the Riverpoint Medical deal, not a collapse in demand. Novanta sits one layer beneath the equipment makers, selling the photonics and motion control their machines cannot ship without, so its order book is an early read on capital spending rather than a lagging one. The risk is the same concentration that powers the growth: both segments rose together, and they would slow together if equipment spending pauses. For now, the evidence points to durability, not a peak.
