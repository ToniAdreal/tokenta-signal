WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

The U.S. Justice Department did not conduct a formal investigation into Jensen Huang before President Donald Trump pushed for his return as Nvidia chief executive this week, Reuters reported on November 25, 2026, citing three people familiar with the matter. The finding stands despite active Justice Department probes into two Nvidia clients and allegations that Huang conducted an affair with a woman linked to Chinese intelligence.

Instead, Attorney General Todd Blanche concluded there was no evidence Huang had broken any laws, based on the Justice Department's ongoing investigations into Super Micro Computer and Megaspeed International, which are suspected of smuggling advanced microchips to China. That determination was crucial to Blanche telling Trump and Vice President JD Vance over the weekend that he had no evidence Huang posed a risk to national security, two of the people said. The probes found no evidence that Huang was acting as an agent of China or knowingly transferred sensitive semiconductor technology, and Nvidia was not a target of those investigations. Nvidia cooperated, providing documents, interviews and other data that helped lead to the conclusion.

There was also no counterintelligence probe of Huang himself. U.S. spy agencies have gathered information on Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive and alleged paramour of Jensen Huang suspected of working with Chinese intelligence, but the United States had no details that would warrant a probe into the Nvidia chief executive, two other people familiar with the effort said. The Justice Department, the FBI and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence did not respond to requests for comment. Huang is due to testify before Congress next week, where members of both parties say it is too soon to set aside questions about whether Nvidia or its chief executive endangered U.S. national security.

WHY IT MATTERSThe reinstatement rested on evidence from other people's investigations.

Blanche's determination was not the product of an inquiry into Huang. It was drawn from two investigations of Nvidia customers, Super Micro and Megaspeed, in which Nvidia was a cooperating witness rather than a target. The Wall Street Journal first reported that the assessment was based on evidence from those two related investigations, and Reuters' sources confirm the shape of it: no formal criminal investigation of Huang, and no counterintelligence probe of him either.

That distinction is what Congress is now seizing on. Members of both parties say the lack of any additional effort to examine Huang's role before Trump's intervention will likely fuel a push for more investigations on Capitol Hill. The administration's position is that the client probes answered the question; Congress's is that the question was never directly asked. Huang's testimony next week is the first place that argument gets tested in public.

THE SECOND MOVEThe pardon question is now moot in the administration's telling.

On the same day the Reuters report landed, Vice President JD Vance told reporters at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio that Huang does not need a pardon "at all" and that he is not under investigation. "I haven't seen any evidence that he committed a crime," Vance said, adding that he was told by the Justice Department and multiple officials that Huang was under no investigation. The remarks close the loop on flash 529, when Trump called a pardon "always an option."

Two loose ends remain on the administration's side. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on November 22 that the administration had requested compliance answers from Nvidia within ten days and had not received them after seven days, a clock that runs out around today with no public word of a response. And the FBI has given Nvidia leadership at least two defensive briefings about Chinese espionage tactics, Reuters reported, without saying whether Huang himself was briefed. The Justice Department is also conducting antitrust reviews of Nvidia business deals unrelated to Huang's personal behavior.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM PROBE TO NO PROBE
From an investigation of Huang to an investigation around him
The public assumption was that the Justice Department had examined Huang and cleared him. Reuters reports the opposite sequence: the department examined two Nvidia clients, found nothing implicating Huang, and never opened a formal investigation or a counterintelligence probe of the chief executive himself.
FROM PARDON TO TESTIMONY
From a pardon on the table to a pardon unneeded
Trump called a pardon for Huang "always an option" days ago. Vance now says Huang does not need one at all, because he is not under investigation and the attorney general has seen no evidence of a crime. The clemency question has been replaced by a congressional one.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe question was answered without ever being asked of Huang.

Blanche's conclusion may be right on the evidence, but its provenance is the story. The Justice Department determined Huang broke no laws by studying two investigations of other companies, then told the president, who told the board, which reinstated the chief executive. No formal probe of Huang, no counterintelligence probe of Huang, and a press conference where the attorney general declined to explain how the determination was made. Congress now gets its turn with Huang under oath next week, and the administration's other clock, Bessent's ten-day demand for compliance answers, runs out with no public answer. The reinstatement is settled. The record behind it is not.
