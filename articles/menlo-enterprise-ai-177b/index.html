Enterprise AI Spent $176.8 Billion, and the Payer Is No Longer Human

Menlo Ventures published its enterprise AI report on November 3: deployment spending reached $176.8 billion by September, up 5.4x year over year; agents generated 80% of tokens on OpenRouter; the top three model vendors took 69% of spending.

WHAT HAPPENEDWhat Happened

Menlo Ventures published its fourth annual enterprise AI report on November 3. Enterprise AI deployment spending reached $176.8 billion by September 2026, up from $33 billion a year earlier, a 5.4x increase, faster than the prior year's 3.9x pace.

The share of enterprises using AI rose only from 55% to 65%, while spending grew more than fivefold. The growth did not come from new customers entering the market; it came from existing users spending much more. Actual spending by surveyed enterprises averaged 4.7x last year's level, against a planned 1.9x at the start of the year.

The money is highly concentrated. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google together took 69% of deployment spending, about $122.7 billion, up from 50% a year earlier. Within enterprise large-model API spending, Anthropic held 55%, OpenAI 31%, and Google fell from 21% to 9%.

MACHINES AS CUSTOMERSOnce the Customer Is a Machine, the Billing Math Changes

The most telling line in the report comes from OpenRouter's data. Agents generated 80% of tokens, roughly 7x human usage. Since agent traffic overtook human traffic in February, agent consumption has grown 37x.

The logic of buying AI has changed with it. Companies used to buy tools for employees; now they buy compute for agents. Agents take no leave and keep no hours, so usage is limited only by task volume. Budget overruns are not a management failure; the caller has changed to a party that never tires.

CODING TAKES THE LEADCoding Ate the Biggest Slice First

Application-layer spending reached about $77 billion, up from $19 billion a year earlier. Coding took $34 billion, AI assistants and productivity tools $25 billion; together the two account for 76% of application spending.

The ranking shows enterprises spending first where the return is visible. Code that runs, tests that pass, tickets that close: those results can be counted the same day. More abstract use cases are still waiting in line until the earlier bills are settled.

READ THE ESTIMATE AS AN ESTIMATEAn Estimate Should Be Read as an Estimate

The report states its own method. The figures come from a Morning Consult survey of nearly 400 US enterprise decision-makers conducted September 8-26, who oversee more than $4 billion in AI spending, combined with public financial reports and third-party research in a model.

The report measures how much money was spent, not how much value came back. Whether $176.8 billion in spending bought a matching amount of productivity is a question the report does not answer. Reading spending growth directly as value growth is the easiest way this report can mislead.

THE SHIFTWhat Changed

FROM SEATS TO TASKS
From budgeting by headcount, to budgeting by task volume
Agents never rest; usage follows tasks alone.

SIGNAL JUDGMENT — ANALYSISThe Payer Is No Longer Human; the Budget Logic Has to Be Rewritten

Tokenta Signal's view: the payer of the enterprise AI bill has changed. Agent calls carry no ceiling; budgets now follow task volume, not headcount. Any enterprise still budgeting by headcount will find overspending is only a matter of time.

WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAINWhat Remains Uncertain

The boundaries of this piece: the $176.8 billion figure is Menlo Ventures' modeled estimate, built from a survey of about 400 US enterprise decision-makers plus a bottom-up market model; it is not an audited number. Menlo discloses that it invests in Anthropic and OpenRouter, so the share figures in the report should be read with that in mind. The 80% token share comes from a single channel, OpenRouter, not the whole industry. This does not constitute investment advice.
